## On-Campus Halls vs Renting Around Campus: How Big Is the Gap in 2024?

For students about to start at the Australian National University (ANU), or current students planning to move out of their current place, accommodation is always the single biggest fixed expense. Faced with the flat all-inclusive fees of on-campus residences like Davey Lodge and Kinloch Lodge, versus the flexible but bill-laden rentals in nearby suburbs like Braddon, Turner and Acton, many people find it hard to tell at a glance which option actually saves more money. This article analyses the differences in rent, utilities, commuting and day-to-day convenience between ANU on-campus residences (such as Davey Lodge and Kinloch Lodge) and off-campus rentals in the surrounding suburbs (such as Braddon, Turner and Acton), combines them with safety and convenience, and provides the latest 2024 data comparison and a room-hunting checklist, to help new students and budget-conscious readers make a rational decision.

## 1. The Full Picture of On-Campus Accommodation Prices (2024 Academic Year Data)

ANU's self-catered student residences on campus mainly include Davey Lodge, Kinloch Lodge, Warrumbul Lodge and Lena Karmel Lodge. All of these are managed by UniLodge, with standard 2024 contracts of around 44 or 52 weeks.

Taking the two Lodges most popular with undergraduates and coursework master's students as examples, the 2024 weekly rents are roughly as follows:
- **Davey Lodge**: standard single room in a shared apartment around A$314–329 per week, meals not included, self-catered; larger single rooms cost a bit more, around A$344–359 per week.
- **Kinloch Lodge**: single rooms around A$308–324 per week, while the newer Studio configuration can reach A$429–449 per week.

The standout advantage of on-campus residences is that the price is **all-inclusive**: electricity, gas, unlimited Wi-Fi, furniture, common-area cleaning and in-building security are all covered in the weekly rent. There is no need to set up accounts or worry about paying bills, and the budget is completely transparent. In addition, the 44-week contract matches one academic year, with the option to extend over the holidays — moderate flexibility.

That said, on-campus halls have their limitations: rooms are generally 12–16 square metres, and the kitchen and living room are shared with 3–6 flatmates. The social atmosphere is lively, but personal space is relatively limited.

## 2. Rent Levels in the Three Popular Off-Campus Suburbs

For students who want more privacy or hope to lower their weekly outlay, the areas around campus reachable on foot or by a short bus ride are the top choice. Braddon, Turner and Acton each have their own character.

**Acton**
Acton hugs the ANU campus, and from most streets it takes 5–10 minutes on foot to reach the teaching buildings. The area is mostly older apartments with a handful of new purpose-built student accommodation. In 2024, renting an entire ordinary two-bedroom apartment costs around A$550–650 per week, while a single room in a share house costs about A$270–340 per week. Studios in newer student apartments (not owned by ANU) often run to A$480–550 per week, approaching or even exceeding on-campus residence prices.

**Turner**
Turner sits on the western side of campus, one street away from Acton, with many detached houses and low-density units converted into student share houses. A shared single room ranges from A$220–310 per week depending on room size and condition; a master bedroom with ensuite may go for A$320–380 per week. Turner rents are usually slightly lower than Acton's, and with more supermarkets and cafés nearby, it is the value-for-money pick.

**Braddon**
Braddon is Canberra's dining and trend-life hub, with Lonsdale Street packed with restaurants, bars and boutique shops. For exactly that reason, Braddon has seen a wave of modern apartment construction in recent years, and rents have climbed accordingly. A shared single room typically runs A$280–360 per week, and a one-bedroom apartment usually costs A$500–600 per week or more. Life here is vibrant, but budget-conscious students should treat the premium with caution.

All of the above are **bare rents excluding bills**. Canberra's rental vacancy rate was still low in 2024, stock is tight in peak season (January–February), and good properties often need to be applied for several weeks in advance, usually with 4 weeks' bond and 2–4 weeks' rent paid upfront in one go.

## 3. Utilities, Internet and Hidden Costs: How Much Do the Bills Really Add Up To?

This is the part of off-campus renting that people most often underestimate. On-campus halls are all-inclusive, while off-campus you have to arrange your own service providers and pay bills every month.

Based on the 2024 Australian energy market, here are the per-person bill benchmarks for share houses in Canberra:
- **Electricity and gas**: if the house uses electric cooking and an electric hot-water system, expect around A$50–80 per person per month; in winter, if you use electric heating (Canberra winter nights can drop below zero), the electricity bill can climb to A$100–120 per month. Share houses usually split bills by headcount, but bills run higher if you end up with a wasteful flatmate or an old, poorly insulated house.
- **Water**: tenants generally only pay the usage component, with the owner covering the service charge. Around A$15–25 per person per month in a share house.
- **Internet**: an unlimited-data NBN plan averages A$70–90 per month, or about A$18–23 per person per month in a four-person share house.

All up, off-campus bills come to about A$90–160 per person per month (roughly A$23–40 per week), and can spike above A$50 per week in winter. This outlay may not look significant, but annualised it reaches A$1,500–2,500 — a key variable in your total budget.

On top of that, off-campus rentals usually require you to buy or take over furniture. If you are unlucky enough to take over an unfurnished place, a one-off purchase of bed, desk, fridge, microwave and cookware can cost A$1,000–2,000, a real strain on first-move-in cash flow.

## 4. Commuting Costs and Time Efficiency

From a commuting standpoint, on-campus halls have an absolute edge. Davey Lodge and Kinloch Lodge sit in the southeast corner and the middle of campus, and walking to the Chifley Library, the College of Business and Economics and the Coombs Building takes just 3–8 minutes. Zero commuting means an extra half-hour in bed every day, or staying in the library until closing time without worrying about the last bus — a real convenience for postgraduates with heavy class schedules or frequent lab visits.

![anu-wiki image](https://img.anu.wiki/留学-单校wiki/anu-accommodation-cost-comparison-2024-2026-1880x1253.jpg)

Measured commutes by suburb:
- **Acton**: 10–15 minutes' walk from the edge streets to the centre of campus, and about 20 minutes from the southern end near the lake. 5 minutes by bike.
- **Turner**: about 10–18 minutes' walk to ANU's northern teaching buildings, crossing the Barry Drive main road to enter campus. Bus routes R2, R3 and R7 also pass through; a single trip with the student card costs about A$1.6, with a weekly cap of A$8.8.
- **Braddon**: 20–30 minutes' walk to the centre of campus; most students cycle or take the bus instead. Cycling takes about 10–12 minutes; buses run frequently along Lonsdale Street, but you still need to factor in waiting time.

Assuming six round trips to campus a week, students who take the bus spend about A$10–15 per week on transport — and that is before the time cost: 40–60 minutes of travel a day, which easily breeds anxiety in deadline weeks and exam season. For time-sensitive students, on-campus halls or a short-term rental within walking distance in Acton or Turner are therefore more attractive.

## 5. Safety and Day-to-Day Convenience

As a planned city with a generally low crime rate, Canberra is broadly safe. Still, there are fine-grained differences between areas.

ANU on-campus residences are equipped with electronic access control, a front desk and night security patrols, and the whole building houses a single demographic (all students), so safety is without question the highest. Among off-campus areas, Acton is mostly research institutes, government office buildings and student apartments — quiet, with few pedestrians at night but good lighting; Turner is a mature residential neighbourhood whose residents are mainly long-term renting families and students, with a warm community feel; Braddon, concentrated as it is with dining and nightlife, draws big crowds on weekend nights and the occasional rowdy drunk, but overall violent crime remains low. Students choosing Braddon would do well to steer clear of low-rise apartments packed with bars and instead favour high-rise buildings or units on side streets.

On convenience: from on-campus halls, supermarkets, cafés and the post office are all within walking distance, though the choice is limited. Turner has the O'Connor and Civic precincts, with Coles, Woolworths, pharmacies and clinics within 10 minutes' walk — day-to-day life is extremely well developed. Braddon offers the best dining experience and suits students who dislike cooking, but eating out every day will visibly inflate living costs. Acton is relatively short on commercial facilities, and a big shopping trip still means heading to Civic.

## 6. ANU Room-Hunting Decision Checklist (2024 Edition)

Faced with the different accommodation options, work through the following checklist item by item to find the balance point that fits you best:

- **Does your budget ceiling include bills?** If an on-campus room costs A$320 per week all-inclusive, while an off-campus bare rent is A$280, adding A$35 per week in per-person bills leaves an actual gap of just A$5 — and you save the hassle of dealing with utilities.
- **Does the contract length match the academic year?** The on-campus 44-week contract aligns closely with the academic year and spares you the headache of holiday subletting. Off-campus leases are mostly 12-month contracts, so if you go home for the holidays you need to find someone to take over the lease yourself.
- **How much commuting can you tolerate?** If you are fine with 30 minutes of walking or cycling a day, Turner or Acton make little practical difference from living on campus; if you want zero commute, prioritise on-campus halls or student apartments on the campus edge.
- **How do you weigh sociability against privacy?** On-campus halls force shared spaces, which is great for quickly building a social circle; off-campus share housing may land you a roomier master bedroom with an ensuite for better privacy.
- **Do you have enough upfront cash for move-in?** Off-campus renting means bond + rent in advance + possibly buying furniture; the initial outlay is often 2–3 times that of an on-campus room.
- **How resilient is your risk buffer?** On-campus housing is managed uniformly by the university, with clear dispute channels; off-campus renting involves unpredictable factors like rent rises, end-of-lease disputes and flatmate conflicts, and a thin budget buffer can leave you cornered.

After weighing the checklist, most budget-conscious students will find that the "expensiveness" of on-campus halls is only skin-deep: once bills, commuting and energy costs are discounted in, more often than not there is no meaningful premium over a furnished, walkable off-campus share house.

## FAQ

**1. Are on-campus rooms easy to get? When do applications open?**
ANU accommodation is applied for online, with places for the first semester of the following year usually opening between September and October. Popular Lodges like Davey Lodge are fiercely competitive, so submit your application as soon as applications open, and list second and third preferences as backups.

**2. How do I find reliable off-campus listings?**
Use Australia's mainstream real-estate platforms, and also keep an eye on sublet postings in ANU student communities. Inspecting the property in person is a must; sign through a reputable agency where possible and avoid private-transfer scams.

**3. How do I set up electricity, water and internet after moving off campus?**
Electricity and gas can be set up on the websites of companies like ActewAGL and Origin; for internet, choose an NBN provider such as Aussie Broadband or TPG. Confirm the property already has the infrastructure before signing up; connections are usually completed within 1–3 business days.

**4. What are my options if I only want to rent for one semester?**
On-campus halls sometimes release short-term spots, and off-campus you can look for a lease takeover or short-term share housing — but the options are limited, and rents may run slightly above market.

**5. How can I cut winter heating electricity bills?**
An apartment with a reverse-cycle air conditioner is far more efficient than an old electric oil heater. Keep windows and doors shut, and make good use of electric blankets and thick duvets to keep the bill under control.

## Summary

After analysing the differences in rent, utilities, commuting and other costs between ANU on-campus residences (such as Davey Lodge and Kinloch Lodge) and off-campus rentals in the surrounding suburbs (such as Braddon, Turner and Acton), it is clear that on-campus halls deliver a worry-free, all-inclusive weekly rent and zero-commute convenience, suiting new students with stable budgets who value time efficiency. Among the surrounding suburbs, a shared single room in Turner or Acton can match or slightly undercut on-campus weekly spending if you keep winter electricity in check, while giving you more flexible living space. Braddon suits students with a bit more financial headroom who enjoy a lively scene. When you make the final call, work through the room-hunting checklist and line up every item against your real lifestyle and financial capacity — that is how you find the "home" that truly fits your Canberra student years.
