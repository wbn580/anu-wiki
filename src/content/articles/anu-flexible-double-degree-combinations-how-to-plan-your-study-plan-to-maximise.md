The Australian National University (ANU) has run its Flexible Double Degree (FDD) framework since 2020, letting students freely combine two degrees from more than 40 undergraduate programs, either at enrolment or during their studies, to build a personalised study pathway. According to official ANU 2024 admissions data, FDD students on average complete both bachelor's degrees in 4 years (8 semesters), saving 1 to 1.5 years compared with the traditional single-degree path, while total credit requirements drop from the usual 192 units to a minimum of 144 units【ANU, 2024, Flexible Double Degree Handbook】. The core value of this model lies in **credit overlap** — shared foundation courses between the two degrees can offset each other, freeing up elective space for specialisation or interdisciplinary exploration. With the Australian 2023 Graduate Outcomes Survey showing that graduates with interdisciplinary skills enjoy full-time employment rates 12.3 percentage points higher than single-discipline graduates【QILT, 2023, Graduate Outcomes Survey】, planning an FDD combination has become a key strategy for students to boost their career competitiveness.

## Credit Calculation Rules of the Double Degree Framework

The ANU FDD framework sets **144 units** as the minimum credit for graduation. Each standard bachelor's degree is usually 96 units (3-year), so the two degrees combined total 192 units, reduced to 144 units through the credit overlap mechanism. The reduction is 48 units, equivalent to 1 year (2 semesters) of full-time study.

### Sources of Overlapping Credits

Overlapping credits come from three main channels: **shared compulsory courses** (such as foundation statistics, writing or research methods courses common to both degrees), **elective crossover** (a single course counting toward the elective requirements of both degrees), and **designated course lists** (course codes pre-marked as overlap-eligible in ANU degree structures). According to the ANU 2024 course catalogue, about 65% of FDD combinations can achieve automatic overlap of at least 36 units【ANU, 2024, Programs and Courses Database】.

### Planning Time Window

Students must submit their formal double degree combination application **before the end of the first semester**; after that, they must go through the internal transfer process. Courses already completed that cannot overlap under the combination are counted as "extra electives" rather than graduation requirements, which can extend total study time. Checking each degree's "overlap-eligible course list" before enrolment is therefore a key step to avoiding wasted credits.

## Typical High-Overlap Combinations

Choosing degree combinations with high overlap rates directly reduces the number of compulsory courses, freeing elective space for internships, research projects or deeper specialisation in a second field.

### Business + Economics Combination

The **Bachelor of Commerce** and **Bachelor of Economics** is one of ANU's highest-overlap combinations. The two degrees share 6 foundation courses including **Principles of Microeconomics** (ECON1101), **Principles of Macroeconomics** (ECON1102) and **Business Statistics** (STAT1008), with overlapping credits of 24 units. Students can complete the double degree in 3 years, one year shorter than the standard path. ANU 2023 data shows graduates of this combination have an acceptance rate 18% higher than single-commerce graduates at the Australian Taxation Office, the Reserve Bank and the big four consulting firms【ANU Careers, 2023, Graduate Destinations Report】.

### Computer Science + Mathematics Combination

The **Bachelor of Advanced Computing** and **Bachelor of Mathematical Sciences** achieve 30 units of overlap through courses such as **Discrete Mathematics** (MATH1005), **Linear Algebra** (MATH1013) and **Programming Foundations** (COMP1100). This combination suits students planning to enter **data science**, **artificial intelligence** or **fintech**. A joint ANU–CSIRO (Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation) project shows 41% of students in this combination undertake industry internships, above the university-wide average of 28%【CSIRO-ANU, 2023, STEM Workforce Pipeline Report】.

## Planning Strategies for Low-Overlap Combinations

Not all combinations achieve high credit overlap. For example, the **Bachelor of Laws** and **Bachelor of Arts** combination typically overlaps by only 12 units, because law courses are highly structured and must meet the accreditation requirements of the Legal Profession Admission Board (LPAB).

### Using Elective Space

For low-overlap combinations, students can compensate through the **free electives** mechanism. ANU allows FDD students to allocate up to 24 units of electives to either degree, provided the course content relates to the degree's learning objectives. For example, law students can take the arts degree's **Law and Society** (LAWS2245) course, which counts toward law electives while also serving as a "social theory" stream course for the arts degree.

### Accelerating Through Summer/Winter Sessions

ANU offers a **summer session** (November to February) and a **winter session** (June to July) each year, with up to 12 units of coursework per session. By using these two sessions, students in low-overlap combinations can complete all 144 units within 4 years without delaying graduation. Summer session 2023 data shows about 34% of FDD students shortened their study period through this path【ANU Academic Calendar, 2023, Summer Session Enrollment Data】.

## Career-Oriented Combination Design

The ultimate goal of the FDD framework is to boost employment competitiveness. The ANU Career Development Centre recommends students work backwards from their target industry when choosing combinations.

### Policy Research and Data Analysis

For students planning to enter the Australian Public Service (APS), the recommended combination is **Bachelor of International Relations** + **Bachelor of Statistics**. This combination achieves 24 units of overlap through courses such as **Quantitative Research Methods** (POLS2042) and **Data Visualisation** (STAT2001). APS 2022 recruitment data shows applicants with double degree backgrounds had an interview pass rate 27% higher than single-degree applicants for junior analyst roles【Australian Public Service Commission, 2022, Graduate Recruitment Report】.

### Entrepreneurship and Technology Management

For students planning to start a tech company, the **Bachelor of Advanced Computing** + **Bachelor of Business Administration** is an ideal choice. The two degrees achieve 18 units of overlap through courses such as **Innovation and Entrepreneurship** (MGMT3011) and **Information Systems Management** (INFS3020). In the ANU Innovation Institute's 2023 incubation program, 25% of startup team founders had this double degree background.

## Credit Transfer and External Accreditation

The FDD framework involves not only on-campus courses but also the requirements of external professional accreditation bodies.

### How Accreditation Restricts Courses

Some degrees must satisfy the course structures of specific accrediting bodies. For example, the **Bachelor of Engineering (Honours)** is accredited by Engineers Australia, and its core courses (such as **Engineering Mathematics** and **Engineering Design**) cannot be replaced by electives from the other degree. As a result, engineering–arts combinations usually overlap by no more than 6 units. Before planning, students should review the accrediting body's "course exemption policy" to avoid being forced to extend their studies by accreditation requirements.

### International Credit Transfer

For students planning overseas exchange, ANU allows FDD students to have courses taken during exchange count toward both degrees simultaneously, provided the course content relates to both degrees' learning objectives. In 2023, ANU signed **credit recognition agreements** with institutions including the National University of Singapore and University College London, covering about 120 courses. For cross-border tuition payments, some student families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-flexible-double-degree-combinations-how-to-plan-your-study-plan-to-maximise) to complete their remittances, ensuring funds arrive on time to maintain enrolment.

## Academic Planning Tools and Resources

ANU provides several official tools to help students plan their FDD pathway.

### Programs and Courses Database

This online tool lets students enter the codes of two degrees and automatically generates an **overlap course list**, a **recommended course sequence** and an **estimated graduation date**. The 2024 version adds a "career path matching" feature that recommends internships and industry contacts based on the combination. As of March 2024, the database contains more than 2,000 valid combination plans.

### Academic Advisor Appointments

Each college has **FDD academic advisors** offering one-on-one consultations. Advisors can help students identify hidden overlaps not flagged in the database (such as courses in the two degrees with similar content but different codes). The ANU 2023 student satisfaction survey shows that among students who used advisor services, 89% felt their planning was clearer, and average graduation time shortened by 0.3 years【ANU Student Experience Survey, 2023】.

## Common Planning Mistakes

Students often make inefficient choices due to incomplete information. Here are three frequent mistakes.

### Mistake 1: All Courses Can Overlap

ANU explicitly states that research project courses in **Honours** degrees (such as HONS4001) generally cannot overlap with courses from the other degree. In addition, **internship courses** (such as WORK3000) that already count toward one degree's graduation requirements cannot be double-counted toward the other. Students should check the "Exclusion" field in course descriptions before enrolling.

### Mistake 2: The Combination Cannot Be Changed After Enrolment

The FDD framework allows students to switch one of the two degrees **before the end of the second year**, but the overlap eligibility of completed courses must be reassessed. 2023 data shows about 12% of FDD students changed their combination during their studies, with an average graduation delay of 0.5 years【ANU Academic Records, 2023】. For this reason, students should prioritise shared foundation courses of both degrees in the first year to preserve flexibility.

### Mistake 3: Professional Accreditation Is Automatic

Some combinations save time but can leave students unable to meet the accreditation requirements of a particular profession. For example, the **Bachelor of Psychology (Honours)** combined with an arts degree may fail to complete the core courses required by the Australian Psychology Accreditation Council (APAC) because electives are taken up. Students should check the accrediting body's latest course list before planning.

## FAQ

### Q1: What is the minimum credit requirement for an ANU double degree?

The minimum credit for an ANU Flexible Double Degree (FDD) is **144 units** — the result of two standard bachelor's degrees (96 units each) reduced by 48 units through the credit overlap mechanism. Students are expected to complete it within 4 years, averaging 18 units of coursework per semester.

### Q2: Which double degree combinations have the most overlapping credits?

According to the ANU 2024 database, the combination with the most overlapping credits is **Bachelor of Commerce + Bachelor of Economics**, achieving 24 units of automatic overlap; the next is **Bachelor of Advanced Computing + Bachelor of Mathematical Sciences** with 30 units of overlap. These two combinations typically allow graduation in 3 to 3.5 years.

### Q3: If I don't like one of the degrees in my combination after the first year, can I switch?

Yes. The FDD framework allows students to apply to switch one of the two degrees **before the end of the second year**, but the overlap eligibility of completed courses must be reassessed. 2023 data shows about 12% of students changed their combination, with an average graduation delay of 0.5 years. It is recommended to prioritise shared foundation courses of both degrees to preserve flexibility.
