The Australian National University (ANU) library system comprises 9 physical branch libraries and multiple digital platforms, serving more than 25,000 students and 5,000 staff each year [ANU Library Annual Report 2023]. According to 2024 statistics from the Council of Australian University Librarians (CAUL), ANU's collections total more than 2.5 million physical items and over one million ebooks, making it one of the most academically resource-dense library networks in the Southern Hemisphere. Yet most students only use its basic borrowing functions, leaving the hidden booking systems, special collections access channels and research support services underused. Drawing on official ANU policies and practical guides, this article lays out how to book study rooms efficiently, access special collections and get free research help, helping you save time and boost your academic output.

## Study room booking: how it works and the hidden rules

ANU libraries provide more than 1,200 study seats, of which about 400 are bookable individual or group study rooms. Booking runs through the ANU Library Room Booking platform, available online 24 hours a day. Users sign in with their ANU student or staff account (Uni ID) and can book up to 2 hours per session, with a daily cap of 4 hours. Bookings open 7 days in advance, and popular slots (such as the two weeks before exam period) are usually snapped up within 30 minutes of opening.

### How to improve your chances of getting a booking

**Key strategy**: book off-peak times (8:00-10:00 am or after 4:00 pm), which raises success rates by about 40%. **Fallback option**: if your target branch has no availability, the system automatically shows free slots at nearby branches (Hancock Library and Chifley Library, for example, are only a 3-minute walk apart). The 2023 ANU student experience survey found that 62% of users of the "waitlist" function got a released slot within 24 hours.

### Study room types and equipment

- **Individual quiet pods**: 12 pods on Level 3 of Hancock Library, equipped with noise-cancelling headphones and USB charging ports — ideal for writing tasks that need total focus.
- **Group collaboration rooms**: Chifley Library Level 2 has 8 rooms seating 4-6 people, with 55-inch interactive screens and wireless screen-casting. At least 2 people must check in together, or the booking is automatically cancelled after 15 minutes.
- **Accessible study rooms**: every branch has at least 1 room meeting Disability Discrimination Act standards; contact the library's accessibility team (access.library@anu.edu.au) in advance to confirm.

## Accessing special collections: manuscripts, maps and rare books

ANU Library's Special Collections hold more than 100,000 rare items, including Australian Indigenous manuscripts, Pacific Islands atlases and 16th-century European printed books. The collections are stored in a temperature- and humidity-controlled vault on the basement level of Menzie Library and are not open to direct browsing. Access requires submitting a request through the booking system, with a review period of usually 3-5 business days.

### Booking process and eligibility requirements

**Step 1**: fill in the application form on the ANU Library Special Collections page, noting the item numbers you need (searchable in the ANU Digital Collections database). **Step 2**: provide a statement of research purpose (100-200 words); undergraduate applicants must attach a supervisor's recommendation letter. **Step 3**: once approved, you will be admitted to the Reading Room at the scheduled time, where you must deposit all personal belongings (including phone, pens and bags) and may only use pencils and paper provided by the library. ANU Library 2024 data shows that about 35% of applications are returned because the research statement is incomplete.

### Digital access alternatives

For students who cannot visit in person, ANU Library has digitised about 15% of its special collections, freely accessible via the ANU Open Research Repository. **Key resources**: the ANU Pacific Islands Manuscripts Collection (more than 800 19th-century missionary diaries) and the ANU Rare Book Digital Archive (more than 200 scientific works from the 16th-18th centuries). Digital copies support PDF download and OCR text search, but may not be used for commercial publication.

## Research help services: from literature searching to data management

ANU Library offers multi-tier research support covering everything from undergraduate theses to PhD projects. In 2023, the library's research help team handled 4,200 one-on-one consultations, with an average response time of 2.3 business days. The service is completely free, but appointments must be booked in advance.

### Booking a Subject Librarian

Each discipline (anthropology, physics, law, for example) has 1-2 dedicated subject librarians who provide in-depth literature search strategies, database selection advice and citation management training. **How to book**: use the "Book a Librarian" function on the ANU Library website and pick your subject plus a 30- or 60-minute slot. **Practical use case**: when writing a literature review, subject librarians can help identify core journals and grey literature, cutting search time by about 50%. A 2024 internal ANU Graduate School report notes that students who used subject librarian services achieved on average 27% higher literature coverage at the thesis proposal stage.

### Data management support

For research-data-intensive projects (climate modelling, genomics, for instance), the library offers data management planning (DMP) tools and storage advice. **Key service**: the ANU Data Commons platform supports up to 500 GB of data storage and assigns DOIs for citation. You can book a "data management clinic" (held fortnightly), where library data specialists guide you on metadata annotation and backup strategies. Of ARC-funded projects in 2023, about 80% required a data management plan, and ANU Library's DMP template has been used by more than 1,200 researchers.

## Interlibrary loans and remote access

ANU Library's Interlibrary Loan service lets users borrow physical items from other Australian university libraries and access electronic resources from partner institutions worldwide. **How it works**: submit a request in the ANU Library system with the ISBN/ISSN or DOI of the item you need. Physical books usually arrive within 5-10 business days; electronic articles are emailed within 48 hours. **Cost**: completely free for ANU students and staff, with a cap of 50 requests per person per year. In 2023, ANU Library processed 8,700 interlibrary loan requests, 72% of them from postgraduate students.

### Setting up remote access

For students off campus or overseas, ANU Library offers two remote access methods: VPN and proxy server. **Setup steps**: download the Cisco AnyConnect client, sign in with your Uni ID, and you can access all subscribed databases (such as JSTOR, Scopus and Web of Science). **Notes**: some databases (Westlaw, for instance) cap concurrent users (ANU's subscription allows 50 concurrent users), so connection failures may occur during peak hours (10:00-14:00 Australian Eastern Time) — consider off-peak use. ANU IT Services 2024 data puts the average availability of the remote access system at 99.2%.

## Study skills workshops and academic writing support

In partnership with the Academic Skills Centre, ANU Library runs about 60 free workshops per semester covering time management, critical reading, essay structure, referencing formats and more. **How to register**: check the schedule and sign up on the ANU Library Events page; places usually open two weeks before each workshop. **Popular sessions**: "How to Write a Literature Review" (4 sessions per semester, averaging 120 registrations, 100% full) and "Advanced EndNote Techniques" (2 sessions per semester, bring your own laptop).

### One-on-one writing tutoring

For students who want individual guidance, the library offers 30-minute one-on-one writing tutoring (Peer Writing Tutor) delivered by trained senior PhD students. **How to book**: choose "Book a Writing Tutor" on the ANU Academic Skills website and fill in a thesis abstract and your specific question (such as "how to improve paragraph transitions"). **Outcome data**: a 2023 ANU student services evaluation found that students who used writing tutoring scored on average 8.2 points higher (out of 100) on final essays than those who did not, with 34% fewer citation errors.

## Opening hours and special schedules

Opening hours vary between ANU branches and are adjusted dynamically across semesters and holidays. **Regular hours**: Monday to Friday 8:00 am to 10:00 pm, Saturday and Sunday 10:00 am to 6:00 pm. **Exam period extensions**: during the exam weeks in June and November each year, Hancock Library and Chifley Library are open 24 hours (entry requires swiping your student card). **Holiday adjustments**: all branches close during the Christmas break (December 24 to January 2); from January 3 to mid-February hours are shortened to 10:00 am to 4:00 pm. ANU Library 2023 statistics show that 24-hour areas were used 3.2 times as much during exam weeks as at other times, with about 15% of seats still occupied between 2:00 and 4:00 am.

### Safety and conduct

All branches have 24-hour surveillance and emergency call buttons. **Key rules**: no eating or drinking in the library (except in designated rest areas), and no phone calls (silent mode is mandatory). Violations are recorded, and 3 accumulated violations suspend borrowing privileges for 30 days. In 2023, ANU Library issued 214 conduct warnings, the most common being eating in non-designated areas (62%).

## FAQ

### Q1: How can I find an empty study room without booking?

**A**: the ANU Library website offers a live seat availability map, updated every 5 minutes. Data shows that between 9:00 and 11:00 am on weekdays, Level 4 of Hancock Library has about 40-50 unbooked seats, but during exam weeks that number drops to 5-10. **Alternative**: use the "Walk-in" areas (such as the sofa area on Level 1 of Chifley Library), which need no booking but have an average wait of 15 minutes (2023 data).

### Q2: Can I photograph or photocopy special collections?

**A**: most special collections cannot be photographed or photocopied, but the Reading Room offers a free scanning service (max 20 pages per session). For rare books printed before the 19th century, only pencil transcription is allowed. **Exception**: if the material is for academic publication, you can submit a "digitisation reproduction request", with a review period of 10 business days and a fee of $5 per page (2024 rate). ANU Library approved 1,200 digitisation requests in 2023.

### Q3: What happens if I lose an interlibrary loan item?

**A**: if you lose a physical interlibrary loan book, report it to ANU Library immediately (response.library@anu.edu.au). The compensation amount is set by the lending library, usually 2-3 times the original price (about $150 on average). **Important timing**: if an item is more than 30 days overdue, it is automatically marked "lost" with fines accruing, and borrowing privileges are suspended until the account is settled. In 2023, ANU Library handled 87 lost interlibrary loan cases, with an average resolution time of 14 days.
