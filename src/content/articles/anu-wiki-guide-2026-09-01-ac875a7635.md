## The Reliance on International Student Fees

Australian universities receive billions of dollars in fees from international students, and some of the largest institutions depend on these payments for up to a third of their revenue. At some of the biggest universities, international students make up nearly half of the student population and contribute a disproportionately growing share of tuition income. Despite this, the number of international students in Australia continues to reach record levels.

## The English Language Question

For international students entering Australian universities, passing a certified English language test is a common requirement—most often the IELTS. However, some students arrive without having achieved the required score, and evidence suggests that only a portion of international students improve their English during their studies, while others actually see their language skills decline over time.

## The Funding Debate

A former government official involved in Australia's skilled migration policy, Abul Rizvi, noted that the underfunded education sector has long chased overseas students' tuition revenue at the expense of academic integrity. The Group of Eight universities described a proposed law as "harsh," while others accused the government of deliberately weakening the economy and treating international students as "cannon fodder in a politics-driven migration battle."

## Concerns from Students and Institutions

The Australian government's plan to cap foreign student numbers has caused panic among current and prospective international students. La Trobe University's vice-chancellor, Theo Farrell, said they support "transparent and moderate measures" to manage the growth of international students. A Monash University student, Vedant Gadhavi, mentioned that several friends in India who planned to study in Australia are now anxious about the policy changes. The ANU's international student director, Rishika Agrawal, noted that the proposals have triggered other concerns as well.
