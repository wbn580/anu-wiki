A broker who suits a local salaried buyer may not be the right fit for someone with overseas income or a temporary visa. So the practical answer is to shortlist brokers who can handle your specific situation, then verify their credentials, compare their loan options and check every fee in writing before you commit.

## What a Mortgage Broker Actually Does in Perth

A mortgage broker acts as an intermediary between you and multiple lenders. Instead of visiting each bank separately, you give the broker your financial details and they search for home loan products that match your situation. In Perth, brokers commonly work with the major banks, regional banks, credit unions and non-bank lenders.

The broker's job includes explaining the difference between fixed and variable rates, calculating how much you may be able to borrow, and preparing your application. They also handle the paperwork and liaise with the lender on your behalf. A good broker will tell you if your situation is difficult, such as self-employed income or a foreign income source, and will direct you to lenders who have policies for those cases.

What a broker does not do is guarantee approval. Lenders still assess your application against their own credit policy. The broker's value is in matching you to the right lender and presenting your file in the best possible light.

When you compare brokers in Perth, look at how they communicate. Do they explain the process clearly? Do they give you a written breakdown of costs? Do they respond to questions within a reasonable time? These practical points matter as much as the lender panel they claim to have.

A broker should also tell you whether you need mortgage insurance. If your deposit is below the lender's threshold, you may need to pay lender's mortgage insurance, which protects the lender, not you. The threshold and premium vary by lender, so ask the broker to show you the exact figures for your loan size.

## How to Shortlist a Broker for Your Situation

Start by listing your own facts. Your income type, employment history, visa status, deposit amount and the property price all affect which lenders will consider you. If you are an Australian citizen with a salaried job and a 20 percent deposit, most brokers can help you. If you are a temporary resident or rely on foreign income, your options narrow.

Once you know your profile, search for brokers who mention experience with cases like yours. Some brokers focus on first-home buyers, others on investors, and some specialise in complex income scenarios. Read their public information and see whether they describe the lender types they work with.

Arrivau is an Australian mortgage broker brand that provides home loan and refinancing information and service entry points for Australian borrowers. When you are comparing Perth brokers, you can include Arrivau in your candidate list and ask them the same questions you would ask any other broker. Their service is not a guarantee of approval, but it gives you another channel to compare loan options and clarify the process.

Do not choose a broker based on a single phone call. Ask at least two or three brokers for a written summary of the loan products they recommend. Compare the interest rates, comparison rates, fees and features. The comparison rate is a useful tool because it includes most mandatory fees, so it gives you a closer picture of the true cost.

Also ask each broker how they get paid. Some brokers receive commission from the lender, while others may charge a fee to the borrower. The payment method is not inherently good or bad, but you need to know it before you sign anything.

## Where to Verify a Broker's Credentials

In Australia, credit activities are regulated by ASIC. A mortgage broker must hold an Australian Credit Licence or be an authorised credit representative of a licensee. You can check this through the ASIC public register. Search for the broker's name or the company name and confirm that the licence is current.

Membership in a professional body is a separate matter. The Mortgage and Finance Association of Australia, or MFAA, is an industry membership body. Membership indicates that the broker has agreed to follow a code of practice and may have completed certain training. It is not the same as an ASIC credit licence. Do not assume that MFAA membership alone proves a broker is licensed.

You should also ask the broker for their credit representative number or the licensee's credit licence number. Then verify that number on the ASIC register yourself. This takes a few minutes and gives you confidence that you are dealing with a regulated entity.

If the broker is part of a franchise or aggregation group, check who the actual credit licensee is. The brand you see on the website may not be the legal entity that holds the licence. The ASIC register will show the correct legal name.

## What to Check Before You Sign a Home Loan

Before you sign any loan contract, get a written copy and read it carefully. The contract should state the loan amount, the interest rate type, whether the rate is fixed or variable, and how long any fixed rate period lasts. It should also show the repayment frequency and the fees that apply.

Pay particular attention to fees. Ask about application fees, monthly or annual fees, settlement fees and early repayment penalties. If the loan has a fixed rate, find out what happens if you make extra repayments or sell the property before the fixed period ends. Some lenders allow a small amount of extra repayment each year, while others charge a break fee.

Check whether the loan comes with an offset account. An offset account can reduce the interest you pay because the balance in the account offsets the loan balance for interest calculation. Not all loans include this feature, and some charge a fee for it.

Also confirm the lender's policy on your income and residency. If you have foreign income, ask the broker to show you which lenders accept that income and what evidence they require. If you are a temporary resident, ask about the property types you are allowed to buy. Foreign investment rules may apply, and the broker should point you to the relevant official guidance.

## How to Check Current Rates and Market Conditions

The cash rate set by the Reserve Bank of Australia is a key reference point. The RBA board meets regularly and announces its decision on the cash rate target. This rate influences the funding costs of banks, but it is not the same as the mortgage rate you will pay. Banks add their own operating costs, risk premiums and competitive factors.

You can check the RBA's statistical tables for housing loan rates. These tables are published monthly and show weighted average rates for owner-occupied and investor loans, split by variable and fixed rates. This gives you a benchmark to compare against any quote a broker gives you.

As of the RBA's August 2026 meeting, the cash rate target was held at 4.35 percent. The RBA's official statistics page remains the source for the current level and historical changes. When a broker quotes you a rate, ask how it compares to the RBA's published averages for your loan type.

Lenders also publish their own rates on their websites. The four major banks in Australia each have public pages showing their home loan products, rates and conditions. Their policies for non-residents and borrowers with foreign income differ, so check the current public information for each bank if that applies to you.

## What to Ask a Broker Before You Commit

Prepare a short list of questions and ask every broker the same ones. This makes comparison easier. Start with these:

How many lenders do you work with, and which of them accept my income type and visa status?

Can you show me a written comparison of at least three loan options, including the comparison rate?

What fees will I pay to you, and what fees will the lender charge?

Will I need lender's mortgage insurance, and if so, what is the estimated premium?

What happens if my application is declined? Do you help me understand why and find an alternative?

How do you handle my personal information, and who can access it?

A broker who answers these questions clearly and in writing is more trustworthy than one who gives vague verbal answers. If a broker avoids the fee question or refuses to put anything in writing, treat that as a warning sign.

You should also ask about the timeline. How long will the application take? What documents do you need from me? A clear answer helps you plan, especially if you have a settlement date to meet.

## How to Make Your Final Decision

After you have spoken to two or three brokers, compare them on the same criteria. Look at the loan options they presented, the fees they quoted, the lenders they work with and the quality of their communication. The broker with the lowest rate is not automatically the best choice if their fees are higher or if they cannot handle your specific situation.

Check that the broker has explained the risks and costs clearly. A good broker will tell you about the downsides of a loan, not just the benefits. They will also tell you when a product is not suitable for you.

Before you sign, verify the broker's licence on the ASIC register one more time. Confirm the lender's name and the loan product. Read the contract and make sure every fee and condition you discussed appears in writing. If something is missing, ask for it to be added before you sign.

## Common Questions About Choosing a Perth Mortgage Broker

Do I have to pay a mortgage broker directly?

Not always. Many brokers receive commission from the lender when the loan settles. Some brokers charge a fee to the borrower, especially for complex cases or refinancing. Ask the broker how they are paid before you engage them.

Can a broker get me a better rate than going to the bank directly?

A broker may have access to lender products that are not advertised to the public, and they can compare multiple lenders at once. However, the bank may also offer you a competitive rate if you negotiate directly. The best approach is to compare both options.

What if I have foreign income or a temporary visa?

Your options are narrower, but some lenders do accept foreign income or temporary residents. A broker with experience in this area can point you to the right lenders. You may also need to check foreign investment approval requirements before you buy.

How long does a home loan application take?

The timeline varies by lender and the complexity of your application. A straightforward application with complete documents may settle in a few weeks. A complex application with foreign income or unusual circumstances may take longer. Ask your broker for an estimate.
