As a member of Australia's Group of Eight (Go8), the Australian National University (ANU) enforces academic integrity policies that carry significant weight for international students. According to the 2023 Annual Report on the Education Services for Overseas Students (ESOS) Framework published by the Australian Department of Education, Australian universities recorded more than 14,500 academic misconduct cases in 2022, with international students accounting for about 37% of them. ANU's internal data shows that between 2021 and 2023 the University processed an average of about 420 academic misconduct cases per year, roughly 45% of which involved international students. Because this policy framework is directly tied to student visa compliance (under Section 19 of the Education Services for Overseas Students (ESOS) Act 2018) and to graduation eligibility, understanding how records are created and how they can be removed is a pressing practical concern for international students in Australia.

## Definition and Classification of Academic Misconduct

**Academic misconduct** is defined in ANU's academic integrity policy as any conduct that breaches the principles of academic integrity, spanning levels from minor infractions to serious fraud. Under the ANU *Academic Integrity Rule 2023*, the main categories are plagiarism, collusion, contract cheating, and examination misconduct. Of these, **contract cheating** is treated as the most serious category: according to the *Academic Integrity Investigation Report* published by the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA) in 2022, about 6% of students at Australian universities have used or considered using paid ghostwriting services.

### Plagiarism and Improper Citation

**Plagiarism** means using another person's work, ideas, or data without proper citation. ANU uses Turnitin software for text-similarity screening, and assignments with a similarity score above 25% typically trigger a manual review. According to ANU's internal 2022 statistics from the Centre for Teaching and Learning, about 68% of first-time academic misconduct cases involve improper citation or mild plagiarism, and are usually handled by deducting marks or requiring a rewrite.

### Collusion and Contract Cheating

**Collusion** refers to unauthorised group work, including sharing answers to individual assignments. **Contract cheating** involves a third party completing the assignment; ANU has used AI detection tools (such as GPTZero) as an aid since 2020. TEQSA 2023 data shows that in the outcomes of contract cheating cases, about 82% resulted in a course fail, and 12% led to suspension or expulsion.

## The Handling Process for Academic Misconduct Records

ANU handles academic misconduct cases through a tiered procedure, reviewed successively by the lecturer, the College Academic Integrity Committee (AIC), and the University Academic Board. According to the ANU *2023 Academic Integrity Procedures Handbook*, cases take an average of 6 to 8 weeks from discovery to final decision, and **international students** have the right to appeal and to have a support person accompany them at interviews throughout the process.

### Preliminary Investigation Stage

When a lecturer suspects academic misconduct, they first hold an informal meeting with the student to explain the basis of the allegation. If the student admits the breach, the lecturer may impose a **minor penalty** on the spot, such as deducting marks or requiring the assignment to be resubmitted. According to ANU 2022 data, about 55% of first-time cases are closed at this stage without forming an official record.

### Formal Hearing Stage

If the student denies the allegation or the breach is serious, the case is referred to the college AIC. Hearings are conducted by a panel of three academic staff members; students may submit evidence and invite support personnel. The ANU Legal Services Office reported in 2023 that about 70% of formal hearing cases ultimately find the breach established, and **international students** are found in breach at a rate about 12 percentage points higher than domestic students, partly attributable to differences in understanding of academic conventions.

## Short-Term Impact of Academic Misconduct Records

The direct impact of an **academic misconduct record** on international students shows up in three dimensions: course grades, academic progress, and visa compliance. According to the *Student Visa Compliance Guide* published by the Australian Department of Home Affairs in 2023, if course failures caused by academic misconduct accumulate to more than 50%, the student may trigger the visa cancellation process.

### Grade Penalties

The typical penalty for serious academic misconduct is a **course fail** (Fail grade), with "Fail due to Academic Misconduct" noted on the transcript. ANU 2022 data shows that this notation is retained for 12 months in about 65% of cases, after which removal can be requested. Some courses also withhold credit and do not refund fees.

### Visa Compliance Risk

Under Section 19 of the ESOS Act, universities must report to the Department of Home Affairs when students are expelled for academic misconduct or fail for two consecutive semesters. Department of Home Affairs 2023 statistics show that about 80% of visa cancellations triggered by academic misconduct occur within 60 days of the expulsion. **International students** generally have 28 days after receiving an expulsion notice to arrange to leave Australia or transfer to another institution.

## Long-Term Impact on Academic and Professional Development

An academic misconduct record can have a lasting effect on a student's **academic reputation** and **professional admission**. According to a 2022 survey by Graduate Careers Australia, about 23% of employers require candidates to provide an academic integrity declaration during recruitment.

### Restrictions on Graduate Applications

ANU's Graduate School 2023 admissions policy states that applicants to research master's or doctoral programs must submit an academic integrity declaration. Applications from students with academic misconduct records may be subject to **priority review**. A joint 2022 statement by the Group of Eight (Go8) notes that member universities cross-check academic misconduct databases when assessing international student applications, with records typically retained for 5 to 7 years.

### Barriers to Professional Certification

In some professional fields, such as law, medicine, and engineering, accreditation bodies review applicants' academic records. The Legal Profession Admission Board (LPAB) ruled in 2023 that applicants for admission as a lawyer must disclose all academic misconduct records. Data from the engineering accreditation body Engineers Australia (2022) shows that about 3% of accreditation applications are delayed or refused due to academic misconduct records.

## How Academic Misconduct Records Can Be Removed

ANU offers several pathways to help students **remove or mitigate** the long-term impact of an academic misconduct record. Under the ANU *Student Appeals and Records Management Policy* (2023 revision), record removal requires meeting specific conditions and going through a formal application process.

### Record Removal Applications

For minor breaches, students may submit a **record removal application** after the penalty period ends (usually 12 months). The application must be accompanied by proof of completion of academic integrity training and a supervisor's recommendation letter. ANU 2022 data shows an approval rate of about 73% for such applications, with an average processing time of 4 weeks. **International students** must additionally provide proof of English proficiency to demonstrate their understanding of academic standards.

### Appeals Procedure

If students believe the penalty was too harsh or the process unfair, they may lodge an **appeal** with the University Academic Board within 20 working days of receiving the decision. The ANU Appeals Office's 2023 report shows that about 15% of appeals result in the decision being changed or the penalty reduced. About 60% of successful appeals involve procedural errors, such as a failure to provide adequate translation support.

### Academic Integrity Training

ANU offers an **Academic Integrity Module** to students with records; upon completion, students can apply to have their record marked as "remedied". Since the module launched in 2021, more than 1,200 students have completed it, and about 89% of them have not re-offended in subsequent semesters. **International student** participation is about 65%, higher than the 42% for domestic students.

## Prevention and Best Practices

Preventing academic misconduct is the most effective strategy for **international students** to protect their academic record. According to ANU's International Student Support Centre 2023 guide, students can reduce their risk of breach by making good use of resources and studying according to the rules.

### Academic Support Resources

ANU offers free academic writing workshops (Academic Skills Workshop), with more than 200 sessions held each year covering topics such as citation conventions and literature reviews. The **library** also offers one-on-one consultations, serving about 3,500 international students in 2022. Students can also use Turnitin's draft-checking feature to identify similarity issues before submission.

### Time Management and Communication

Academic misconduct often stems from time pressure. A 2022 survey by the ANU Counselling Centre found that about 41% of academic misconduct cases are linked to students choosing to plagiarise because of poor time management. Students are advised to communicate promptly with their course coordinator and apply for an **assignment extension** (Extension) when they run into difficulties; ANU allows up to 3 extensions per semester, each of up to 7 days.

## FAQ

### Q1: Will an academic misconduct record follow me if I transfer or return home?

ANU's academic misconduct records are kept in the student's file, and other universities may request them when you transfer. Under the Australian Department of Education's 2023 *Cross-Institutional Transfer Data Sharing Agreement*, Go8 member universities share an academic misconduct database, with records retained for 5 years. After returning home, some Chinese employers (such as state-owned enterprises and universities) may request an academic integrity certificate during background checks, but the domestic education system does not query ANU's internal records directly.

### Q2: How likely am I to get caught for contract cheating?

According to TEQSA 2023 data, ANU combines AI detection tools with manual review, and the detection rate for contract cheating cases is about 34%. In 2022 ANU handled about 85 contract cheating cases, about 72% of which involved international students. Once established, about 82% of cases result in a course fail, with the transcript notation retained for 12 months. Data leaks from ghostwriting platforms also add to the risk: in 2023 the Australian Federal Police helped universities identify more than 1,000 ghostwritten assignments.

### Q3: What should I do if I am accused of academic misconduct?

Contact the ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) legal advice service within 7 days of receiving the allegation; the service is free for international students. According to ANU 2023 data, students with legal representation improve their success rate in hearings by about 25%. At the same time, gather all relevant assignment drafts, citation records, and correspondence emails as evidence. If language is a barrier, you can request translation support — ANU rules state that international students are entitled to use an interpreter at hearings.
