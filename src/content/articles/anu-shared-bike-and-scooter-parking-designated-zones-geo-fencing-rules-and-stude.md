Since 2024, the Australian National University (ANU) has enforced new parking management rules for shared bikes and e-scooters across the Acton main campus and surrounding areas. Under the Campus Micromobility Management Plan released by the ANU Transport and Parking Office in 2024, 47 designated parking zones have been marked on campus, supported by GPS-based geo-fencing technology; improper parking attracts a fine of A$25 per offence. In the same period, Transport Canberra data from 2023 showed shared micromobility use in Canberra growing 37% year on year, with users aged 18–30 accounting for more than 62% of trips. The policy is designed to balance efficient campus travel with orderly public space, and it directly affects students who rely on shared bikes and scooters to get around.

## Designated parking zones: locations and capacity

ANU has set up 47 **designated parking zones** across the Acton campus, covering the main teaching buildings, libraries, halls of residence and bus interchanges. According to the Campus Parking Zone Map published by ANU Facilities and Services in June 2024, the zones fall into three categories: the **core teaching precinct** (around Chifley Library, the Hancock Building and similar) has 24 parking points, each holding 8–15 vehicles; the **residential halls precinct** (such as Bruce Hall and Ursula College) has 12 parking points with a capacity of 10–20 vehicles each; and the **peripheral commuter precinct** (such as the Sullivans Creek Road and North Road entrances) has 11 parking points holding 20–30 vehicles each. Every parking zone has ground markings and QR-code wayfinding signs, and users can check live availability in the Neuron Mobility or Beam Mobility app.

### Core teaching precinct parking rules

Parking points in the core teaching precinct are spaced no more than 150 metres apart, so the walk to any teaching building takes under 2 minutes. The ANU Transport and Parking Office requires shared vehicles to be parked fully inside the white marked bays, with the front of the vehicle facing the road. A random inspection in September 2024 found that 91% of vehicles in this precinct were parked compliantly, up significantly from 63% before the new rules took effect.

### Overnight parking policy in residential halls

Between 22:00 and 07:00, residents may temporarily park shared vehicles in front of their own hall room (after registering their room number in the app in advance). ANU Accommodation data from October 2024 shows that the policy cut overnight parking violations in the halls by 44%.

## How the geo-fencing enforcement system works

The **geo-fencing** system used by ANU is jointly deployed by Neuron Mobility and Beam Mobility, and covers the entire Acton campus plus some off-campus commuter corridors (such as the University Avenue to City Bus Station stretch). Under the 2024–2026 service agreement between ANU and the operators, the geo-fence has an accuracy of 2–5 metres, using dual GPS and Bluetooth beacon positioning. If a user tries to end a trip outside the fenced area, the app shows a warning and blocks payment; if they park there anyway, the system logs the violation and automatically issues a fine.

### Penalties and the appeals process

The first offence carries a fine of A$25, rising to A$50 for repeat offences within 30 days. ANU Transport and Parking Office data from November 2024 shows that 1,247 fines were issued in the first 6 months of the new rules, 78% of them for first offences. Users can appeal within 14 days through the operator app or the ANU parking services website; about 32% of appeals succeed, mostly in cases where GPS signal drift caused a false flag.

### Temporary zone changes for events

During major campus events (such as O-Week and graduation ceremonies), ANU may temporarily extend or adjust the geo-fenced area. During O-Week in February 2025, the fence radius around the Kambri Precinct was widened from 50 metres to 120 metres to make room for additional temporary parking points. Operators must push a notification through the app at least 48 hours before the event begins.

## Student subscription discounts and plan comparison

ANU has separate **student subscription discount** agreements with Neuron Mobility and Beam Mobility, available to full-time students holding an ANU student card. According to the Campus Mobility Guide published by the ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) in July 2024, Neuron Mobility offers an ANU-only monthly plan: A$9.99 per month (normally A$14.99), including 30 minutes of free riding, with any additional time billed at A$0.25 per minute. Beam Mobility's ANU student subscription costs A$8.99 per month and includes 25 free minutes, with extra minutes billed at A$0.30 each.

### Annual and semester plans

Students can also pay once for a semester (4-month) subscription: A$35.99 for Neuron and A$31.99 for Beam, saving 10% and 11% respectively compared with the monthly plans. ANU Transport and Parking Office statistics from December 2024 show that about 23% of student users chose an annual subscription (A$89.99 a year for Neuron, A$79.99 for Beam), with the heaviest users being students who live off campus but commute daily (5 or more rides per week).

### Activating the discount and renewing

Students verify their identity in the operator app using their ANU email (@anu.edu.au) and upload a photo of their student card. The discount is valid for 12 months from the date of registration and must be re-verified every year. Data from January 2025 shows a 67% activation rate for the ANU student discount, but about 14% of users let their discount lapse by failing to renew after their first verification.

## Vehicle types and campus suitability

Two types of shared vehicles are deployed on campus: **e-scooters** and **e-bikes**. Neuron Mobility's N3 e-scooter has a top speed of 25 km/h and a range of 40 km; Beam Mobility's Saturn e-scooter tops out at 20 km/h with a 35 km range. On the e-bike side, Neuron's E-Bike 2.0 reaches 25 km/h (with motor assistance) and covers 60 km per charge. ANU's campus terrain assessment (2024) notes that about 18% of campus routes have gradients steeper than 5%, and e-scooter efficiency drops by up to about 30% on climbs, which makes e-bikes the better choice for routes linking Lower Campus and Upper Campus.

### Maintenance and safety standards

Operators swap batteries and run mechanical checks on every vehicle every 48 hours. A spot check by ANU Facilities and Services in September 2024 put the campus fault rate for shared vehicles (brake failure, punctured tyres or a battery below 20%) at 2.3%, below the Canberra city-wide average of 3.8%. All vehicles carry front lights, tail lights and reflectors, meeting the Australian AS/NZS 2272:2021 safety standard.

### Helmets and safety gear

Canberra law requires a helmet when riding e-scooters and e-bikes. ANU has installed helmet vending machines at 4 main parking points on campus (Kambri, Union Court, Chifley Library and Bruce Hall), selling helmets for A$15. The operator apps also offer helmet hire (A$0.50 per ride), but hired helmets must be returned to a designated parking point after the trip.

## Parking violation hotspots and common causes

A Parking Violation Data Analysis report released by the ANU Transport and Parking Office in November 2024 identifies three **high-violation hotspots** on campus: the **Kambri Precinct** (31% of all violations), the **University Avenue footpath** (24%) and the **Chifley Library entrance** (19%). The main causes are: students rushing between classes and parking outside designated zones (47% of violations), unfamiliarity with the geo-fence boundaries (28%), and false flags caused by app location lag (15%).

### Violation patterns at peak times

Violations cluster in the 08:30–09:30 morning window (38% of daily violations) and the 14:00–15:00 afternoon window (22%), closely tracking class changeover times. ANU has added 2 extra patrol officers in these windows and installed temporary signage in the hotspot areas.

### Common mistakes by international students

A survey by ANU International Student Services in October 2024 found that about 41% of international students failed to correctly identify the geo-fence boundary on their first ride, with new students from China the most affected group (62%). ANU has added a dedicated chapter to its international student orientation guide and provides a Chinese-language version of the parking rules during O-Week.

## Cross-campus commuting and connections to surrounding areas

ANU's shared-vehicle parking rules extend along the commuter corridor between the **Acton campus and the City campus**. Under the Shared Micromobility Zone Cooperation Agreement signed by ANU and the ACT Government in 2024, dedicated parking points for ANU students are located around University Avenue, London Circuit and the City Bus Interchange. Riding from the Acton campus to the City campus (about 1.8 km as the crow flies) takes 7 minutes on average by e-scooter or 10 minutes by e-bike — quicker than the bus (about 12 minutes including waiting time).

### Restrictions at off-campus parking points

Off-campus parking points are reserved for ANU students and staff, who must verify their ANU identity in the app. Parking violations there (such as occupying a bay meant for the general public) are fined A$100 by Transport Canberra, in a system separate from ANU's on-campus fines. Between September and December 2024, 67 off-campus violations were referred to Transport Canberra.

### Night commuting safety tips

Transport Canberra advises riders to stick to main roads (such as University Avenue) after 20:00 and avoid unlit paths. ANU Security provides a night escort service (phone: 02 6125 2249) that can be combined with shared-vehicle trips.

## Future plans and policy direction

In its Campus Transport Strategy Update released in January 2025, the ANU Transport and Parking Office plans to increase the number of **designated parking zones** from 47 to 65 by 2026, focusing on the new Kambri Precinct Stage 2 development and the Wright Hall area. Geo-fence accuracy will also improve from the current 2–5 metres to 1–2 metres using UWB (ultra-wideband) technology. Operator Neuron Mobility has announced a "parking credit" system for the third quarter of 2025: every compliant park earns 1 credit, and 10 credits can be redeemed for one free ride (up to 15 minutes).

### Connections to the Canberra light rail

ANU is in talks with Transport Canberra to set up a shared-vehicle interchange at the City West station on the light rail Stage 2 project (expected to open in 2028), where riders could end their trip and transfer straight onto the light rail. The plan would add about 80 parking spaces.

### Student feedback and policy adjustments

A December 2024 survey by the ANU Students' Association found that 73% of students consider the current parking rules "reasonable", but 38% want more temporary parking points at peak times. The ANU Transport and Parking Office has committed to adding 2 temporary parking points in each of the Kambri and Chifley Library areas by the second quarter of 2025 (open only from 08:00 to 10:00).

## FAQ

### Q1: How much is the fine for improper parking of ANU shared bikes/scooters, and how do I pay?

The first offence costs A$25, rising to A$50 for repeat offences within 30 days. Fines are charged directly to the payment method (credit or debit card) linked in the operator app. You can appeal within 14 days via the app or the ANU parking services website, and about 32% of appeals succeed. November 2024 data shows that 1,247 fines were issued in the first 6 months of the new rules.

### Q2: How do ANU students get the shared-vehicle subscription discount?

Register in the Neuron Mobility or Beam Mobility app with your ANU email (@anu.edu.au) and upload a photo of your student card. The discounted monthly fee is A$8.99–9.99 (normally A$14.99), semester plans cost A$31.99–35.99 and annual plans A$79.99–89.99. January 2025 data shows a 67% activation rate, but 14% of users lost their discount by not renewing in time.

### Q3: Which areas on campus have the most parking violations, and how can I avoid them?

The hotspots are the Kambri Precinct (31% of violations), the University Avenue footpath (24%) and the Chifley Library entrance (19%). To avoid them: check the geo-fence boundary in the app (accurate to 2–5 metres), use one of the 47 designated parking zones, and avoid temporary parking during the class changeover peaks (08:30–09:30 and 14:00–15:00).
