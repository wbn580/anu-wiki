The Australian National University (ANU) requires all international students on student visas to hold Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) for the duration of their studies, which covers basic medical services. However, according to the OSHC Standard Cover Statement published by the Australian Department of Health in 2023, OSHC typically does not cover common services such as dental, optical, physiotherapy and the out-of-pocket component of prescription medicines, and policies carry an individual excess of between A$300 and A$750 per year. A 2022 survey by the International Education Association of Australia (IEAA) found that about 37% of ANU international students used at least one service not covered by OSHC during their studies, with average out-of-pocket costs of A$412. Choosing an add-on (OSHC Add-on or private extras cover) is therefore a key decision for keeping unexpected medical expenses under control.

## Where Basic OSHC Falls Short

Australian immigration regulations require international students on a Student Visa (Subclass 500) to hold OSHC, but the cover is designed for emergency hospitalisation and general practitioner (GP) visits, not everyday preventive care. According to the Private Health Insurance Annual Report published by the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) in 2023, standard OSHC plans typically do not cover: routine dental check-ups (about A$150-300 per visit), glasses (about A$200-500 per pair), physiotherapy (about A$70-120 per session) and the PBS co-payment for prescription medicines (about A$30 per prescription). Internal data from the ANU Health Service for 2023 shows that about 22% of international student consultations are for dental issues and 18% for optical needs — together accounting for 40% of non-urgent visits. In practice, students relying on basic OSHC alone can face hundreds to thousands of dollars in uncovered medical costs each year.

### The Impact of the OSHC Excess

OSHC policies usually carry an individual excess ranging from A$0 to A$750. Plans with lower premiums tend to have higher excesses. For example, **Medibank OSHC**'s 2024 standard plan has a A$300 excess, while **Allianz OSHC**'s basic plan has a A$500 excess. When hospital treatment is needed, the excess is paid first and the insurer covers the rest. According to Private Health Insurance Association of Australia (PHIAA) data from 2022, about 65% of international students choose the lowest-premium plan, but 28% of them face financial strain within the following two years because of high excesses.

### Common Uncovered Services and Indicative Costs

The median prices below are for common self-funded medical services in the ACT in 2023 (source: ACT Health Cost Database, 2023): dental clean (A$120 per visit), filling (A$180-350 per tooth), glasses (A$350 per pair, frames and lenses included), physiotherapy (A$85 per session) and counselling (A$150-200 per session, without a GP referral). Add-ons typically reimburse these services up to an annual limit — for example, a dental claims cap of A$500-1,000 per year.

## What an OSHC Add-On Covers

An add-on is supplementary cover sold alongside OSHC, usually by the same insurer, designed to fill the gaps in the basic plan. According to 2023 guidance from the Australian Government Department of Health and Aged Care, the main areas an add-on covers are: **general dental** (check-ups, cleans and fillings, with annual limits of A$500-1,500), **optical** (eye tests and glasses, A$200-400 every two years), **physiotherapy** (10-20 sessions per year, with 50-80% reimbursed per session) and **prescription medicines** (reducing the PBS co-payment to A$10-20 per prescription). Some add-ons also cover chiropractic, naturopathy and ambulance services (OSHC usually excludes ambulance, which costs about A$400-1,000 per trip). ANU Global Programs advised in 2023 that add-on premiums typically run A$200-500 per year — far less than the average out-of-pocket cost of uncovered services.

### Add-Ons vs Private Health Insurance

An add-on is not private health insurance. Private health insurance is available to Australian residents and covers a much broader range of hospital and medical services, but international students cannot buy private health insurance as a substitute for OSHC. Add-ons exist to provide "extras" on top of OSHC, not to replace the hospital cover OSHC provides. Under the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) 2023 rules, add-on premiums do not qualify for the Private Health Insurance Rebate, but they can be claimed as medical expenses on a tax return.

## Comparing Add-Ons from ANU's Major Partner Insurers

ANU officially recognises four OSHC providers: Medibank, Allianz Care, Bupa and nib. Each offers add-on cover at different levels. According to the Student Health Insurance Comparison Report published by the Private Health Insurance Association of Australia (PHIAA) in January 2024, here are the key parameters of each company's basic add-on:

- **Medibank OSHC Plus**: A$1,000 annual dental limit, A$300 optical every two years, 60% reimbursed per physiotherapy session with a A$600 annual cap. Premium about A$350 per year.
- **Allianz Care OSHC Add-on**: A$800 annual dental limit, A$250 optical every two years, 50% per physiotherapy session with a A$500 annual cap. Premium about A$280 per year.
- **Bupa OSHC Extras**: A$1,200 annual dental limit, A$400 optical every two years, 70% per physiotherapy session with an A$800 annual cap. Premium about A$450 per year.
- **nib OSHC Plus**: A$900 annual dental limit, A$350 optical every two years, 55% per physiotherapy session with a A$550 annual cap. Premium about A$320 per year.

### Waiting Periods and Limitations

All add-ons have waiting periods, typically: 2 months for general dental, 12 months for major dental (such as root canal treatment), 2 months for optical and 2 months for physiotherapy. Under the Australian Consumer Law, insurers must clearly state waiting periods in the policy documents. ANU international students can buy an add-on immediately after arriving in Australia, but should note that services used during the waiting period are not reimbursed.

## Choosing an Add-On by Student Profile

Choosing an add-on comes down to your medical needs, budget and length of study. According to an IEAA 2023 survey of ANU international students, three groups show typical patterns of need:

**Long-term students (3-4 years)**: Bupa OSHC Extras is the recommended pick, as it has the highest annual dental and optical limits, which pays off over multiple routine visits. Assuming two dental cleans a year (A$240) and one pair of glasses (A$350), Bupa reimburses about 70%, saving roughly A$1,200 over three years.

**Short-term students (1-2 years)**: Medibank OSHC Plus or nib OSHC Plus is recommended for shorter waiting periods and moderate premiums. For students on a one-year master's program, Medibank's 2-month waiting period means reimbursements can start mid-way through the program.

**Students with specific medical needs**: for example, contact lens wearers or students who need regular physiotherapy should prioritise plans with higher caps on the relevant items. Allianz Care's physiotherapy reimbursement is lower, but its prescription co-payment drops to A$15 per prescription, which suits long-term medication users.

## When and How to Buy an Add-On

You can add an add-on when you purchase your OSHC, or upgrade at any point during your studies. Under 2023 immigration policy, an add-on is not a visa requirement, but buying one within 30 days of arriving in Australia is recommended to avoid delaying your waiting period. The purchase process usually involves the following steps:

1. Log in to the OSHC provider's website and select "Add Extras" or "Upgrade Plan".
2. Fill in your personal details, including passport number, visa subclass and ANU student ID.
3. Choose your add-on level and confirm the waiting periods and annual limits.
4. Pay the premium (usually annually or per semester).
5. Download the electronic Certificate of Insurance and save it on your phone or in the cloud.

ANU Health noted in 2023 that add-on premiums can be claimed directly through the Medicare rebate system, but you must provide your bank account details to the insurer within 30 days of purchase.

## Making a Claim: Process and Things to Watch

Add-on claims work much like OSHC claims, with online submission and offline reimbursement options. According to the PHIAA 2023 guidelines, you will need: medical receipts (with service date, item description and cost), a GP referral letter (where applicable) and your insurance membership number. **Online claims** are usually submitted through the insurer's app or website and take 2-5 working days to process. **Offline reimbursement** means visiting the insurer's Canberra branch or submitting paper documents through an on-campus agent (such as the ANU Student Centre).

Common reasons for rejected claims include: services used during the waiting period, no GP referral (physiotherapy requires one) and exceeding the annual limit. According to HCCC 2022 data, about 12% of add-on claims are rejected for incomplete documentation, so check whether a service is covered before you book it.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can ANU international students buy only OSHC and skip the add-on?

Yes. Immigration requirements only mandate OSHC; the add-on is voluntary. But according to the IEAA 2022 survey, students without an add-on spend an average of A$412 per year out of pocket on medical costs, while add-on premiums run A$200-500 per year. For students planning to use dental or optical services, an add-on typically saves 30%-60%.

### Q2: When does the add-on waiting period start?

The waiting period starts on the policy effective date (i.e., the purchase date). For example, if you buy an add-on on 1 February 2024, the general dental waiting period ends on 1 April 2024. Under the Australian Consumer Law, services used during the waiting period are not reimbursed, though emergency dental treatment (such as acute toothache) may be an exception with a GP certificate.

### Q3: Does my add-on transfer automatically if I switch OSHC providers?

No. The add-on is tied to your OSHC policy; switching OSHC providers means buying a new add-on and starting the waiting period again. Per PHIAA 2023 advice, check the waiting period on the new policy before switching to avoid a gap in cover. ANU international students usually switch at the end of a semester to make the most of their existing policy's claim limits.
