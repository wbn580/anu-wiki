If you're planning to apply to the Australian National University through an education agent, the first practical question you'll hit is: does the agent charge at all, and if so, how much? There is currently no officially published standard fee — different education agents vary widely in how they structure their services and charges. Some agents don't charge students extra for the application assistance itself, while others bill separately for university selection advice, document coaching or visa assistance. Because there is no "standard price", checking each item against fee disclosure, written agreements and open questions before you sign matters more than comparing specific numbers.

## Official Advice: Four Principles of Fee Disclosure

Study Australia's guide to choosing an education agent for international students offers several tips you can put to use right away. First, check the agent list published on the official website of each institution you're applying to. This step isn't about comparing prices — it's about confirming whether the agent you're talking to is part of the university's authorised channels. If they're not on the list, you need to be more careful about verifying any fee commitments. Second, before you commit, ask exactly what services are included and how the fees are made up. Don't just ask for one total figure — find out which services cost money and what each one actually involves. Third, insist on a written agreement that pins down what you will pay and the specific services those fees cover. Verbal promises are hard to keep on record and easy to argue about later. Fourth, actively compare service fees across different agents. Even for the same program at the same university, different agents may quote completely different prices and package structures. Comparing several isn't about finding the cheapest — it's about understanding the market's typical fee range and what a reasonable breakdown looks like.

## Confirm These Fee Questions in Writing Before You Sign

Here is a checklist you can take straight to your agent and work through item by item. Every question comes from Study Australia's advice on fee transparency, with the goal of shrinking the grey areas to a minimum before you sign.

- Does the scope of application assistance explicitly cover the Australian National University, and all the ANU courses you intend to apply for?
- What is the total service fee? If you're charged in stages, what specific deliverables does each stage correspond to?
- Do the fees include actions like communicating directly with ANU, supplying additional documents, and following up on application progress?
- If visa application assistance is involved, is it charged extra, or already covered by the total fee?
- Are there any services that look free but are actually only available through bundling with other paid services?
- Besides what you pay the agent directly, what third-party fees might arise during the application process? Who bears these costs, and what is the payment path?

The last point above deserves special attention. Many expenses that are easy to overlook in an application — such as notarised translation of academic documents, English test score delivery fees, and medical examination costs — aren't necessarily included in an agent's quote. If you haven't seen the relevant terms in a written agreement, don't assume the agent will handle them, and don't wait until you see the bill to ask.

## What Still Needs Written Confirmation

Even if the agent can answer most of the questions above on the spot, a few categories of matters only count once they're on paper and written into the agreement. First, refunds. Whether the agent offers refunds at all, under what circumstances, at what rate, and within how long — all of this should become a black-and-white commitment. Second, the relationship between application outcomes and service fees. For example, if ANU doesn't make an offer, what happens to the fees you've already paid? Third, how already-paid fees are settled if the service changes or is terminated. If these aren't written down, verbal promises are likely to be impossible to verify afterwards. Fourth, whether any fees exist that didn't appear at the quoting stage but could be added during the service.

There's also a continuity point: if the fee structure, service catalogue or refund terms change, how will the agent notify you, and do you have the option to accept or withdraw? This is another detail worth clarifying up front. Until you see written documents, all communication is only part of the negotiation process — don't treat it as a final decision.
