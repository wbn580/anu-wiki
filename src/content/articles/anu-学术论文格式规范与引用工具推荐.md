As a member of Australia's Group of Eight, the Australian National University (ANU) ties its academic paper formatting standards directly to students' academic integrity scores and graduation eligibility. According to the Academic Integrity Guide published by the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA, 2023), improperly cited plagiarism accounts for 67% of academic misconduct cases at Australian universities. ANU's official Academic Writing and Citation Handbook (2024 edition) requires all undergraduate and postgraduate students to follow one of the APA 7th, Chicago 17th or Harvard citation systems, and every reference entry in each paper must be precise down to the DOI or a stable URL. This guide systematically sets out ANU's current formatting standards, citation tools and common violation scenarios, helping students build a compliant academic writing workflow before the 2025 academic year begins.

## Choosing a Citation System by Discipline

ANU colleges have clear preferences when it comes to citation systems, and choosing the wrong one can see a paper sent straight back for revision. According to statistics from ANU's Academic Skills Centre (2024), roughly 58% of courses university-wide require APA 7th, mainly across the social sciences, business and health sciences; in the humanities and arts, meanwhile, take-up of Chicago 17th (Notes-Bibliography format) is as high as 82%.

**APA 7th** requires in-text citations to include the author's surname, year of publication and page number, e.g. (Smith, 2023, p. 45). ANU Library rules state that APA reference lists must be ordered alphabetically by author surname, and electronic sources must include a DOI (Digital Object Identifier). If a paper uses AI-assisted tools (such as ChatGPT), the tool's name, version and date of use must be stated in the methods section or an appendix.

**Chicago 17th** uses footnotes or endnotes, with every citation requiring full publication details on first use. In its Discipline Writing Guide updated in 2024, ANU's School of History and Classics stresses that Chicago footnote numbering must run continuously, and that subsequent citations of the same source should use "ibid." or a shortened form.

## Citation Management Tools and Official Support

ANU offers students free or discounted citation management software that automatically generates reference entries and cuts down on formatting errors. According to the annual report of ANU Information Technology Services (ITS, 2024), about 74% of postgraduates university-wide use Zotero, while undergraduates lean towards EndNote (usage rate of roughly 51%).

**Zotero** is the open-source tool ANU Library recommends first; it supports Chrome and Firefox browser extensions and can capture bibliographic records directly from ANU Library databases. ANU runs Zotero workshops twice a month (Academic Skills Centre, 2024), covering group sharing and attachment management. When writing group papers, students can sync references through Zotero groups to avoid version conflicts.

**EndNote**'s web version is free for ANU students, and its Cite While You Write plugin embeds directly into Microsoft Word. In an internal survey conducted in 2023, ANU's College of Health and Medicine found that students using EndNote made about 42% fewer reference formatting errors. Note, however, that EndNote's default output styles need to be manually switched to the APA 7th or Chicago version ANU requires.

## Paper Structure and Layout Requirements

ANU's Academic Writing and Citation Handbook imposes strict rules on the physical layout of papers, and breaches can cost marks. Every paper submitted to the ANU Turnitin system must use 12-point Times New Roman, double spacing, and 2.54 cm (1 inch) margins. The body text must include continuous page numbers, and the title page must state the course code, student name and student ID.

**Heading hierarchy** uses decimal numbering (e.g. 1.0, 1.1, 1.1.1), although some colleges, such as the ANU College of Law, require the traditional combination of Roman numerals and letters. The ANU Academic Skills Centre recommends that H2 headings (such as "Literature Review") be bold and centred, and H3 headings left-aligned and italicised. Each paragraph's first line is indented 1.27 cm (0.5 inches), with no extra blank lines between paragraphs.

**Tables and figures** must be numbered separately and placed after the body text or in an appendix. In its Data Presentation Guide updated in 2023, ANU's statistics discipline requires table captions to sit above the table and figure captions below the figure, and every table and figure must state its data source. If third-party figures are used, copyright permission must be obtained and the permission statement attached.

## Reference List Formats and Common Mistakes

Formatting errors in reference lists are the most common cause of lost marks among ANU students. In a sample check of 2,000 papers from the 2023 academic year, ANU Library (2024) found that about 35% of references were missing a DOI and 22% of entries had the author's name order wrong. APA 7th requires hanging indents in reference lists (first line flush left, subsequent lines indented 0.5 inches), and journal articles must include the volume, issue and page range.

**Book citations** follow the format: Author surname, Initial(s). (Year of publication). *Title* (edition). Publisher. For e-books, add the DOI or URL after the publisher. ANU Library warns in particular that Google Books preview links cannot be used as stable URLs — the permanent links in ANU Library databases must be used instead.

**Journal articles** must include: Author surname, Initial(s). (Year). Article title. *Journal name*, *volume*(issue), page range. DOI. ANU updated its policy in 2024 to require that all citations use the latest version, and where an article has both a preprint and a formal version, the formal version must be cited.

## Academic Integrity and the Consequences of Citation Violations

ANU takes a zero-tolerance approach to citation violations, and violation records stay in the academic file even after graduation. According to the Academic Misconduct Procedures published by ANU Student Affairs (2024), a first minor violation (such as one or two missed citations) may only cost marks and result in a mandatory academic integrity workshop; systematic plagiarism (such as an entire passage with no source attribution), however, results in a zero grade for the course and a record on file.

**Turnitin** is ANU's only designated similarity-checking system, with a similarity threshold under 15% treated as the safe zone. But the ANU Academic Skills Centre stresses that Turnitin cannot detect paraphrasing plagiarism — replacing words with synonyms while keeping the original sentence structure. Students should use Turnitin's draft function to self-check before submission and revise any paragraphs with similarity above 5%.

**AI-generated content** had its citation requirements updated in March 2024. ANU stipulates that if tools such as ChatGPT are used to draft a paper, this must be stated in the "Methods" section at the start of the paper, with the full conversation log attached in an appendix. Papers flagged by Turnitin's AI detection feature for undeclared AI use are treated as plagiarism.

## Cross-Disciplinary Differences and Special Cases

Different disciplines have special citation requirements, and students should confirm these in their course outlines. In its Technical Report Writing Guide updated in 2024, the ANU College of Engineering and Computer Science requires all code snippets to state their source, and citations to use the IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers) system rather than APA.

**Law** uses the 4th edition of AGLC (Australian Guide to Legal Citation). The ANU College of Law requires case citations to include the case name, year, volume, report abbreviation and starting page, e.g. *Smith v Jones* (2023) 280 CLR 1. Students should use the AGLC quick-reference card provided by the ANU Law Library to avoid missing report abbreviations when citing High Court of Australia cases.

**Medicine and health** use the Vancouver citation system, with in-text citations numbered in square brackets. In an internal audit in 2023, the ANU Medical School found that about 18% of students failed to include the ethics approval number when citing clinical trial data — an error that can get a paper rejected by journals.

## Best Practices for Managing References

Building a personal reference library is an effective way to avoid citation errors. The ANU Academic Skills Centre recommends that students create a corresponding Zotero folder at the start of each course and immediately import every item from the course reading list. As each paper is read during the semester, notes and tags (such as "methodology", "core argument") should be added in Zotero so items can be quickly located when writing later.

**Version control** is a common pitfall. In its Reference Management Guide published in 2024, ANU Library stresses that students should regularly sync Zotero data to the cloud (such as the ANU-provided OneDrive) to avoid losing references if local files are corrupted. Before final submission of each paper, students should use Zotero's "Refresh" function to update all fields, making sure DOIs and URLs are the latest working links.

**Backup strategy** includes exporting the Zotero database to BibTeX format weekly and storing it on the ANU personal network drive (H: drive). Students using EndNote should manually save the reference library (.enl file) after every edit to a Word document, because EndNote Web's autosave has a 15-minute delay.

## FAQ

### Q1: What are ANU's minimum requirements for paper citation format?
ANU requires all papers to use one of the APA 7th, Chicago 17th or Harvard citation systems, and the reference list must contain at least 5 sources (undergraduate) or 15 sources (postgraduate). According to data from the ANU Academic Skills Centre (2024), about 89% of courses require page numbers in in-text citations, even when citing the overall argument of a whole book.

### Q2: What Turnitin similarity rate counts as safe?
ANU officially advises that a Turnitin similarity below 15% is the safe range, but similarity to any single source must not exceed 3%. According to statistics from ANU Student Affairs (2024), papers with similarity above 25% have a 73% probability of being flagged for manual review. Students should use Turnitin's draft function to self-check early and revise any passages that match the source text for 5 consecutive words or more.

### Q3: Do I need to declare the use of ChatGPT when writing a paper?
Yes. ANU's AI Use Policy, updated in March 2024, stipulates that if AI-generated content is used in a paper (including ChatGPT, GrammarlyGO, etc.), the tool name, version, date of use and specific purpose must be stated in the methods section or an appendix. Papers flagged by Turnitin's AI detection feature for undeclared use are treated as academic misconduct, and a first violation costs 30% of the marks.
