The Australian National University (ANU) Winter Session usually runs from late June to July each year, lasting about 4 to 6 weeks, and gives current students the opportunity to earn credit points in a concentrated period. According to the ANU official academic calendar, the 2025 Winter Session runs from 30 June to 25 July and covers about 30 undergraduate and postgraduate courses【ANU 2025, Academic Calendar】. This mechanism lets students use the break to accelerate their academic progress or make up credits lost through failed courses or program transfers. According to 2023 statistics from the Australian Government Department of Education, about 12% of Australian university undergraduates take at least one intensive session course during their studies, and ANU's Winter Session has drawn particular attention for its compact courses and flexible credit transfer【Australian Government Department of Education 2023, Student Data Summary】. For students who want to complete their credit requirements before graduation or get an early start on internships or research, the Winter Session is a clear time-efficient option.

## Winter Session course types and credit structure

ANU Winter Session courses are characterised by **short, intensive delivery**. Each course typically runs for 3 to 4 weeks, with 2 to 3 sessions of 3 hours of face-to-face or online teaching per week, plus independent study and assignment time. Most courses are worth **6 credit points** (the ANU standard course credit), equivalent to regular semester courses, and can count toward the elective or compulsory credit requirements of a degree. According to the ANU course database, Winter Session courses fall into three main categories: core general education courses (such as philosophy, history and cultural studies), discipline-advanced courses (such as business, computer science and international relations), and language and cultural immersion courses (such as intensive Japanese, French and Chinese classes). Each course involves about 130 to 150 hours of total study time, including class discussion, reading assignments and final assessment. Students should confirm before enrolling that a course counts toward their program's credit requirements; some courses may require faculty approval.

### Credit transfer and academic records

The Winter Session **credit transfer mechanism** is the same as for regular semesters. After completing a course, the grade is recorded on the official ANU transcript, and the GPA is calculated in the same way as for regular semester courses. Under ANU academic policy, Winter Session courses do not offer a pass/fail option; all grades are recorded as letter grades (HD/D/CR/P/N). Students enrolling in courses outside their own college must submit a **credit approval application** to their home college student centre in advance; processing typically takes 5 to 10 working days. The ANU International Student Office advises overseas students on student visas to make sure their total Winter Session enrolment does not exceed the 24-credit-per-semester cap, to avoid visa compliance issues.

### Course timing and delivery modes

Winter Session courses use a **blended delivery model**, including in-person classes, live Zoom sessions and asynchronous recordings. About 60% of 2025 Winter Session courses offer an in-person option, with the remainder fully online or blended. Timetables are dense — a 3-week course, for example, might run Monday, Wednesday and Friday mornings from 9 am to 12 pm. Students should check the course timetable before enrolling to avoid clashes with internships, research or part-time work. ANU libraries and IT services maintain normal opening hours during the Winter Session, so students can use campus resources for assignments and group projects.

## Popular course recommendations: arts and social sciences

The ANU Winter Session offers several high-demand courses in the **arts and social sciences**, which students tend to favour for their compact content and clear assessment methods. For example, **PHIL1004: Logic and Critical Thinking** is a popular undergraduate elective running for 4 weeks and covering propositional logic, fallacy analysis and argument construction. It has no prerequisites; final assessment consists of two online quizzes (40%) and a 1,500-word essay (60%). According to ANU Philosophy Department 2024 course feedback, the course has an average pass rate of 92% and a student satisfaction score of 4.3/5.0.

### HIST2223: International Relations during the Cold War

Another recommended course is **HIST2223: International Relations during the Cold War**, aimed at second- and third-year undergraduates. The course focuses on US–Soviet confrontation, nuclear deterrence and regional conflicts from 1947 to 1991, using case seminars and primary archive analysis. It runs for 3 weeks with three 2-hour seminars per week; final assessment comprises a 2,000-word research essay (50%) and a group debate (50%). The course coordinator is Associate Professor Dr. Sarah Johnson from the History Department, whose research focuses on security architecture in Cold War Asia. The course is usually **limited to 30 students**, so early enrolment through the ANU ISIS system is recommended.

### LING1001: Language and Mind

**LING1001: Language and Mind** is an introductory course from the Linguistics Department, suitable for students interested in cognitive science and the philosophy of language. The course runs for 4 weeks and covers linguistic relativity, syntactic structure and semantic analysis. Assessment includes weekly online discussion posts (20%), a mid-term quiz (30%) and a final essay (50%). There are no prerequisites, and the grade can be used to satisfy the "cultural understanding" category of ANU undergraduate general education requirements. In the 2024 Winter Session the course enrolled 45 students, about 30% of them international students.

## Popular course recommendations: business and computer science

For students in **business and computer science**, the Winter Session offers several courses that count toward core or elective credit. **BUSN1001: Principles of Business** is a classic introductory course from the College of Business and Economics, running for 3 weeks and covering business structures, marketing fundamentals and financial analysis. Final assessment is a closed-book exam (60%) and a group business plan (40%). According to ANU College of Business and Economics 2024 data, the Winter Session grade distribution for this course was: HD (high distinction) 15%, D (distinction) 30%, CR (credit) 35%, P (pass) 18% and N (fail) 2%.

### COMP1730: Introduction to Python Programming

**COMP1730: Introduction to Python Programming** is one of the most popular Winter Session courses from the School of Computing, aimed at students with no programming experience. The course runs for 4 weeks with two 3-hour lab sessions per week, covering variables, loops, functions and basic data structures. Assessment consists of three programming assignments (20% each) and a final project (40%). The grade counts toward elective credit in computer science or engineering. According to 2023 statistics from the ANU College of Engineering and Computer Science, the Winter Session enrolment was 120 students, about 25% of them from other colleges (such as science or law).

### FINM2001: Corporate Finance

**FINM2001: Corporate Finance** targets senior business undergraduates and requires the prerequisite course FINM1001. The course runs for 3 weeks with three 2-hour lectures and one 1-hour tutorial per week, covering capital budgeting, capital structure and dividend policy. Final assessment includes two online quizzes (30%) and a closed-book exam (70%). The course is usually **limited to 40 students** in the Winter Session to maintain interactive small-class quality. ANU's finance department recommends confirming that you meet the prerequisite before enrolling — otherwise you may not earn credit.

## Language and cultural immersion courses

The ANU Winter Session offers **language and cultural immersion courses** for students who want to improve their foreign language skills or meet graduation language requirements. These courses are usually worth **6 credit points** and are intensive: students complete the equivalent of a full semester of language study in 4 weeks. For example, **JAPN1003: Elementary Japanese II** is for students who have completed JAPN1002, covering kanji, grammatical structures and everyday conversation, with five 2-hour classes per week plus a daily 1-hour language lab session. Assessment includes weekly listening quizzes (40%), a final oral exam (30%) and a written exam (30%).

### FREN1002: Elementary French II

**FREN1002: Elementary French II** is the French Department's Winter Session course for students who already know basic French grammar. The course runs for 4 weeks and uses an immersion teaching approach, with classes conducted mainly in French and a weekly cultural workshop (such as French film appreciation or cooking activities). Assessment includes daily vocabulary quizzes (20%), two writing tasks (20% each) and a final comprehensive exam (40%). The grade can be used to satisfy the ANU undergraduate "language and culture" general education requirement. According to 2024 data from the ANU School of Literature, Languages and Linguistics, the course's average Winter Session pass rate is 88%.

### CHIN2002: Intermediate Chinese

**CHIN2002: Intermediate Chinese** is for students with two years of Chinese study experience, covering news reading, argumentative writing and topic discussion. The course runs for 4 weeks with three 2-hour classes per week plus a weekly language partner activity. Assessment includes weekly dictations (25%), a mid-term speech (25%) and a final project presentation (50%). The course is usually **limited to 25 students** in the Winter Session to ensure every student gets ample speaking practice. ANU's Chinese Department recommends completing a placement test in advance to confirm the appropriate course level.

## Enrolment process and key dates

The Winter Session **enrolment process** is similar to a regular semester, but the time window is shorter. According to the ANU academic calendar, the 2025 Winter Session course selection system typically opens in early May, with the enrolment deadline in mid-June. The key dates are as follows: 1 May — course selection opens; 15 May — priority enrolment deadline (for high-demand courses); 10 June — standard enrolment deadline; 20 June — final add/swap day; 25 July — courses end; 10 August — results released. Students must enrol through the ANU ISIS system and pay course fees. For international students, Winter Session fees are the same as regular semesters; in 2025, a 6-credit course costs about AUD 4,500 to 5,000 (depending on course level). When paying tuition across borders, some study-abroad families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%86%AC%E5%AD%A3%E5%B0%8F%E5%AD%A6%E6%9C%9F%E8%AF%BE%E7%A8%8B%E6%8E%A8%E8%8D%90%E7%9F%AD%E6%9C%9F%E9%9B%86%E4%B8%AD%E4%BF%AE%E5%AD%A6%E5%88%86%E5%A5%BD%E6%9C%BA%E4%BC%9A) to complete the currency exchange.

### Course selection strategy and credit planning

When choosing Winter Session courses, students should prioritise **courses that match their degree progression**. The ANU Student Centre recommends enrolling in at most 2 courses (12 credit points in total) per Winter Session to avoid overload. For students planning to graduate early, the Winter Session can add 12 to 18 extra credit points per year, allowing a three-year bachelor's degree to be completed in 2.5 years. For students who need to retake courses, the Winter Session is an effective way to close credit gaps from failed courses or program transfers. Students can consult their college student advisor before enrolling for personalised course planning advice.

### Withdrawal and refund policy

The Winter Session **withdrawal policy** is relatively strict. Under ANU financial policy, withdrawing before the enrolment deadline (10 June) qualifies for a full refund; withdrawing between 10 June and 20 June incurs an administrative fee of 50% of the course fee; withdrawing after 20 June receives no refund and the transcript shows a "W" (withdrawal) notation. Students withdrawing for medical or family emergencies can submit a special circumstances application to the Student Centre with supporting documents; processing takes 10 to 15 working days.

## Learning support and campus resources

ANU provides **comprehensive learning support services** during the Winter Session to help students adapt to the short, high-intensity pace. The Academic Skills Centre runs free workshops during the Winter Session covering time management, essay writing and exam techniques. Workshops are typically held 2 to 3 times a week, one hour each, and can be attended online or in person. **Library study spaces** also maintain normal opening hours during the Winter Session: the Chifley Library on the main campus is open until 9 pm Monday to Friday and until 5 pm on weekends. Students can book group study rooms or use the 24-hour study area.

### Tutoring and advisory services

Winter Session courses usually have **teaching assistants (tutors)** who run 1 to 2 tutorial sessions per week, in formats such as group discussion, assignment Q&A and mock tests. Students can view tutorial schedules and book sessions through the ANU Wattle platform (the course management system). For students who need extra help, the ANU Student Centre offers **one-on-one academic tutoring** in 30-minute sessions, bookable with 48 hours' notice. Mental health support services continue to run during the Winter Session; students can call the ANU counselling hotline (02 6125 2442) or arrange a session through the online booking system.

### Dedicated support for international students

The **ANU International Student Office** provides visa compliance reminders and cross-cultural adaptation workshops during the Winter Session. International students must hold a valid student visa and keep their course enrolment status compliant with visa requirements throughout the Winter Session. The office also organises a weekly "coffee social" to help international students connect with local students. Students who need to extend their visa should submit their application at least 8 weeks before the Winter Session starts, to avoid disruption to course enrolment.

## Advantages and cautions for the Winter Session

The **core advantage** of the Winter Session is time efficiency: students can complete in 4 weeks a course that takes 16 weeks in a regular semester, freeing up an entire semester for internships, research or part-time work. According to 2024 data from the ANU Careers Centre, about 35% of students who took Winter Session courses secured an internship in a subsequent semester, higher than the 22% among non-participants. Winter Session courses also tend to have **smaller class sizes** (20 to 30 students on average), allowing more interaction between students and instructors, which helps with deeper understanding of the material. For students enrolling across colleges, the Winter Session offers flexible course combinations.

### Potential challenges and coping strategies

The **high-intensity pace** of the Winter Session is the main challenge. Students must absorb a large amount of content in a short time, with daily self-study time possibly reaching 4 to 6 hours. Poor time management can lead to fatigue or declining grades. Students are advised to plan their study schedule in advance and reserve at least half a day of rest each week. For online courses, make sure you have a stable internet connection and test the Zoom or Echo360 platforms in advance. Winter Session courses are also typically **assignment-heavy**; some require weekly submissions or quizzes, so you should avoid enrolling in too many courses at once.

### Course selection advice

When choosing Winter Session courses, students should prioritise **courses that fit their academic goals**. For students planning to apply to graduate or doctoral programs, courses with high distinction rates (such as PHIL1004 or LING1001) are recommended to boost your GPA. For those looking to broaden their interdisciplinary horizons, introductory courses outside your major — such as a computer science student taking HIST2223 — are worth trying. Before enrolling, check the course outline (usually published in early May) to understand the assessment methods and reading load, and avoid courses that don't match your study style.

## FAQ

### Q1: Are Winter Session courses easier than regular semester courses?

Winter Session courses cover the same content as regular semester courses, but the timeline is compressed to 3 to 4 weeks, so the study intensity is higher. According to ANU Academic Skills Centre data, Winter Session students study about 30 hours per week on average, compared with 12 to 15 hours in a regular semester. Assessment standards are identical, and grade distributions show no significant difference from regular semesters. It is advisable to choose carefully based on your own time management ability.

### Q2: Does taking Winter Session courses affect an international student's visa?

International students on Australian student visas who enrol in Winter Session courses must ensure their total enrolled credit does not exceed the 24-credit-per-semester cap. Winter Session courses count toward that semester's credit total. Under Australian immigration policy 2024, students must enrol in at least 12 credit points (full-time) each semester to maintain visa compliance. During the Winter Session, students may enrol in 1 to 2 courses (6 to 12 credit points) without affecting their visa status.

### Q3: Do Winter Session grades count toward GPA?

Yes. Winter Session grades count fully toward the official ANU GPA and are calculated in the same way as regular semester courses. Grades are recorded as letter grades (HD/D/CR/P/N) and affect your weighted average mark (WAM). Under ANU academic policy, Winter Session grades are marked "Winter Session" on the transcript, with no special weighting or exemption. Students can view their transcripts in the ANU ISIS system.
