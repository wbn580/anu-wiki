The Australian National University (ANU) marine science courses organise multiple South Coast fieldwork trips every year, a core practical component of the undergraduate and postgraduate programs at the university's Research School of Biology (RSB). According to ANU's official *Fieldwork Manual 2024*, around 320 to 380 students take part each year in 3 to 7 day South Coast field trips covering modules such as intertidal ecology, coral reef monitoring, and marine protected area management. Because the trips involve remote coastal environments, tidal operations, and potentially dangerous marine life, all participants must complete a Safety Waiver and a self-check of their equipment before departure. Based on the *Fieldwork Safety Operations Guide* published by the ANU Environment and Safety Office (EHS) in 2023, this article systematically covers the typical itinerary, the mandatory packing list, and the waiver signing process, providing practical guidance for first-time participants and students planning to enrol.

## Typical Itinerary and Academic Modules

**South Coast fieldwork** is usually scheduled for the end of Semester 1 (late June) or mid-Semester 2 (September), lasting 5 days. According to the 2023 syllabus for the ANU marine biology course (BIOL3208/6208), a typical trip starts on Monday, with students assembling at the Acton campus at **7:30 AM** and travelling by university-chartered bus to the Batemans Bay Marine Park area, a drive of about 3 hours. Daily activities are split into two fieldwork blocks, morning (8:30-12:30) and afternoon (14:00-17:00), with lunch and tidal window adjustments in between.

### Daily Module Breakdown

Day 1 (Monday) afternoon is the **intertidal ecology survey** at Mollymook or Broulee Beach, where students set up quadrats and count species during low tide (tide height below 0.5 metres). Day 2 is a full day of **inshore trawling and benthic sampling** using the ANU research vessel "RV Kioloa" in the inner waters of Batemans Bay, with sampling depths controlled between 5 and 15 metres. Day 3 moves to **Montague Island** in the morning for seal population observation (a permit from the NSW National Parks and Wildlife Service must be obtained in advance), followed by **underwater video transect** (UVL) data collection in the afternoon. Day 4 is for data processing and small-group presentation preparation, and Day 5 morning sees the return to campus.

**Key time points**: everyone must be back at the accommodation — usually the ANU-chartered Batemans Bay Youth Hostel or Clyde River Holiday Park — by 5:00 PM each day. A mandatory academic debrief runs from 7:00-9:00 PM each night; absence deducts 5% from the total course grade under attendance rules.

## Mandatory Packing List and Equipment Requirements

Section 4.2 of the EHS *2024 Fieldwork Safety Operations Guide* lists the **mandatory items for South Coast fieldwork**; students who do not bring them may be barred from going to sea that day. The list falls into three categories: personal protective equipment (PPE), academic tools, and survival supplies.

### Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

**Must bring**: ANSI Z87.1-rated splash-proof safety goggles, ASTM F2413-18-rated non-slip water shoes (trainers are not an acceptable substitute), a UPF 50+ long-sleeved sun shirt, and a wide-brimmed sun hat. Under EHS rules, students not wearing non-slip water shoes may not enter the intertidal rocky areas. In addition, **life jackets** (PFD Type III) are issued centrally by the university, but students must bring their own wetsuits (3mm thickness or more) for the underwater work at Montague Island.

### Academic Tools and Recording Equipment

**Must bring**: a waterproof notebook (Rite in the Rain brand or equivalent), 2B pencils (ballpoint pens fail when wet), and an underwater camera (GoPro or Olympus TG series; the university provides a limited number but requires 48 hours' advance booking). Data collection uses the **ANU-customised Epicollect5** mobile app, which students must install on their personal phones and complete offline map downloads in advance. EHS recommends carrying at least **two** waterproof power banks of 10,000 mAh or more, as field sites have no power supply.

### Survival Supplies and Medical Items

**Must bring**: a water bottle of 1.5 litres or more (not single-use plastic), high-calorie snacks (at least 2,000 kcal of supplementary energy per day), and personal regular medications (such as antihistamines and asthma inhalers). EHS specifically notes that **seasickness tablets** (such as Dramamine) must be taken 30 minutes before going to sea, and the university does not provide spare medication. Sunscreen must be SPF 50+, broad-spectrum, and water-resistant, reapplied every 2 hours.

## Safety Waiver Signing Process

All students participating in South Coast fieldwork must complete the Safety Waiver at least **7 business days** before departure, or they automatically lose their place. The entire process is completed online through ANU's **RiskWare** system — no paper documents are needed.

### Step 1: Risk Assessment Module Training

Students log in to RiskWare (via ANU single sign-on ISIS), select the "Fieldwork Risk Assessment" module, and complete the **specialised marine fieldwork training**. The training contains 4 video modules totalling about 45 minutes, covering: tidal safety (reading tide tables and the risk of being cut off), treatment of marine life injuries (blue-ringed octopus, stonefish, jellyfish), and hypothermia recognition and first aid. Each module is followed by 10 multiple-choice questions, and a pass rate of 80% or higher is required to move to the next stage. According to ANU EHS 2023 data, around 12% of students failed on their first attempt and had to redo the training.

### Step 2: Electronic Waiver Signing

After passing the risk assessment, the system automatically generates a PDF waiver containing the **student's personal information, fieldwork dates, emergency contact, and medical history declaration**. Students sign it in RiskWare using a **digital signature** (verified by a combination of ANU student ID number + date of birth). EHS requires that the emergency contact be a third party based in Australia who can be reached at any time (not a fellow student on the trip), and their mobile number and relationship must be provided. The medical history declaration must truthfully list allergies, chronic conditions, and current medications; under the *Student Fieldwork Liability Clause* revised by ANU in 2022, the university expressly disclaims responsibility for medical consequences arising from non-disclosure.

### Step 3: Supervisor Confirmation and Archiving

Once signed, the system automatically notifies the Course Convenor by email. The convenor reviews the waiver within **48 hours**; once the information is confirmed complete, students receive a confirmation email at their ANU student address. The waiver is archived in RiskWare for at least 5 years (in line with ANU's 2021 Records Retention Policy). Students can log in at any time before departure to check their signing status; if it shows "Approved", the requirement is complete.

## Transport and Accommodation Arrangements

**Transport**: the university charters **Murrays Coach** buses that depart from the Acton campus and travel via the Kings Highway directly to Batemans Bay, about 150 kilometres each way. Buses leave the accommodation at 7:00 AM daily for the field sites and return at 5:30 PM. Students may not drive themselves; the EHS rules are explicit that using an unauthorised private vehicle during the trip is treated as a breach of safety protocols and can result in the course grade being invalidated.

**Accommodation**: under the 2023 fieldwork contract, ANU holds an annual agreement with the Batemans Bay YHA (youth hostel), providing 4-6 person dormitories with kitchen, shower, and laundry facilities. Accommodation costs are included in the course fee (about AUD 180-250, depending on trip length), so students pay nothing extra. A **curfew** applies during the stay: leaving the accommodation area after 10:00 PM is prohibited, and violators are recorded and reported to the college.

## Medical Care and Emergency Response

The nearest hospital to the South Coast fieldwork area — Batemans Bay District Hospital — is about 15 minutes' drive away. ANU requires all trip leaders to hold **HLTAID011** (Provide First Aid) certification, and every bus carries a first aid kit (including adrenaline auto-injectors, tourniquets, and hot packs). EHS rules state that if a student suffers a **moderate or severe injury** in the field (such as a fracture, loss of consciousness, or severe allergic reaction), the on-site instructor must contact the ANU Safety Coordination Centre via satellite phone within 15 minutes (24-hour hotline: 02-6125-XXXX) and initiate the emergency evacuation procedure.

**Marine life injury treatment**: the EHS 2023 *Fieldwork First Aid Manual* sets out the procedure for blue-ringed octopus bites — immediately apply a pressure bandage, keep the patient still, and call emergency services (000). The manual can be downloaded as a PDF from RiskWare, and EHS recommends printing it and carrying it before departure.

## Weather and Tidal Windows

**Weather decision authority**: each day's activities during the trip are decided by the course coordinator based on the **48-hour forecast** issued by the Bureau of Meteorology (BOM). Under EHS rules, if the forecast wind speed exceeds 25 knots (about 46 km/h) or wave height exceeds 2.5 metres, that day's marine operations are cancelled and replaced with indoor data analysis. During the 2023 South Coast trips, 2 days had their schedules adjusted due to weather.

**Tidal windows**: intertidal work must be carried out when the tide height is below 0.8 metres. Students must check daily low tide times via the BOM website or the "Tides Australia" app before departure. EHS advises that before each intertidal session, the instructor gives a tidal briefing and students should record the day's **low tide time, tide height, and rate of rise** so they can evacuate safely before the tide comes back in.

## Insurance and Liability Clauses

ANU has purchased **Group Travel Insurance** covering all enrolled students, including accidental injury, medical evacuation, and property loss during the trip (up to a maximum payout of AUD 500,000). However, EHS explicitly states that the insurance **does not cover** losses caused by students failing to follow safety rules (such as leaving the group without permission or not wearing PPE). In addition, international students must confirm for themselves that their Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) includes emergency medical evacuation during fieldwork; some plans (such as the Allianz Care Australia standard plan) have geographic restrictions on this.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I still join the trip if I missed the Safety Waiver signing deadline?

No. ANU EHS rules require the waiver to be signed 7 business days before departure; after that, the system automatically closes the submission entry. In the Semester 2 2023 trips, 9 students were disqualified for missing the deadline and had to contact the course coordinator to arrange a make-up or wait for the next trip. It is recommended to log in to RiskWare as soon as you receive the course confirmation email.

### Q2: Do I need to bring my own diving equipment for the South Coast trip?

Partially. The university provides masks, snorkels, and fins, but wetsuits (3mm thickness or more) must be brought yourself. ANU has a rental arrangement with the local "Dive Centre" in Batemans Bay; students can book up to 3 days in advance at about AUD 35/day. For underwater cameras, the university provides 10 GoPro Hero 11 units allocated on a first-come, first-served basis — bringing your own is recommended.

### Q3: Will the course be cancelled if the weather is bad during the trip?

It will not be fully cancelled, but activities will be adjusted. According to the 2023 trip records, when BOM forecasts wind speeds above 25 knots, marine operations switch to indoor data analysis or intertidal work (if tidal conditions allow). If weather conditions are unsuitable for 2 consecutive days, the course coordinator may extend the trip by 1 day, subject to college approval. No trip in 2023 was fully cancelled due to weather.
