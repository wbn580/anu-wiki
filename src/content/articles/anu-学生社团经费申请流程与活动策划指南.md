The Australian National University (ANU) has more than 180 registered student societies covering academic, cultural, sporting and interest areas. According to the ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) 2023 annual report, the association allocated approximately 350,000 AUD in total to student societies that year, with individual event grants ranging from 200 AUD to 5,000 AUD. Meanwhile, the ANU Postgraduate and Research Students' Association (PARSA) also provided a dedicated activity fund totalling more than 100,000 AUD in 2024. For students hoping to hold events on campus or grow their societies, mastering the standard funding application process and event planning methods is key to ensuring projects are approved and executed smoothly. Based on ANU official policy documents and historical funding data, this article systematically maps out the complete operational path from application to project closure.

## Funding Sources and Eligible Applicants

Funding for ANU student societies comes mainly from three channels: **student association grants**, **University Student Life Office funding** and **external sponsorship**. The application conditions, funding caps and approval timelines differ significantly across channels.

### Student Association Grants (ANUSA and PARSA)

Undergraduate societies apply to ANUSA, and postgraduate societies apply to PARSA. In the first funding round of 2024, ANUSA received 47 applications and approved 34, an approval rate of about 72%. Applicants must submit forms at least 4 weeks in advance, and the society must be in "active" registration status — that is, having held at least 3 public events in the past 12 months [ANUSA 2024, *Clubs & Societies Funding Guidelines*]. The cap for a single event is 2,000 AUD, with an annual cumulative cap of 5,000 AUD.

### University Student Life Office

For large cross-faculty or university-wide events (such as cultural festivals and academic conferences), societies can apply for the **Student Life Office Event Fund**. The fund had a total budget of 180,000 AUD in 2023, with a maximum single grant of 5,000 AUD. Applications must be co-signed by the society president and treasurer, and must include an event risk assessment report [ANU Student Life 2023, *Event Funding Policy*]. The approval cycle is usually 6-8 weeks.

## Preparing Application Materials

Regardless of the channel chosen, applications must include the following core documents: **event proposal**, **detailed budget sheet**, **risk assessment form** and **society registration certificate**. Of these, the budget sheet is the section the review committee scrutinises most closely.

### Key Elements of the Budget Sheet

The budget must list all expected income (tickets, sponsorship, other grants) and expenses (venue, equipment, catering, promotion, etc.). ANUSA explicitly requires that expense items must not include a "contingency" category; every cost must be supported by a clear supplier quote or historical data. Of the applications not approved in 2023, 41% were returned because the budget was unreasonable [ANUSA 2024, *Funding Application Rejection Analysis*]. It is recommended to use the Excel template provided by ANUSA (downloadable from its website) and attach quotes from at least 3 suppliers.

### Filling in the Risk Assessment Form

ANU requires all events to complete a **risk assessment** before application, classified into three levels: low, medium and high. Low-risk activities (such as indoor film screenings) only require the standard form; medium- and high-risk activities (such as outdoor hikes and the sale of alcoholic drinks) require an additional emergency plan. Under 2024 PARSA rules, events involving alcohol must attach the name and contact details of a Responsible Service of Alcohol (RSA) certificate holder [PARSA 2024, *Event Risk Management Policy*].

## Application Submission and Approval Process

After submission, applications go through three stages: **preliminary review by the society committee**, **recheck by the student association treasurer** and **final vote**. The entire process takes about 2-4 weeks, depending on the number of applications and the scale of the event.

### Preliminary Review Stage

ANUSA's Clubs Council meets once a month to collectively review applications. The meeting schedule is published at the start of each first semester; applications that miss the deadline are automatically deferred to the next round. In the first semester of 2024, there were 5 rounds of meetings, each processing an average of 8-10 applications. The preliminary review mainly checks the completeness of materials, with a pass rate of about 85%.

### Treasurer Recheck and Vote

During the treasurer recheck stage, the budget is verified against actual market prices. If irregularities are found, the application is flagged as "pending clarification" and the society must provide supplementary explanation within 5 working days. The final vote uses a simple majority system; in the event of a tie, the association president casts the deciding vote. Voting results are emailed to the society contact within 48 hours after the meeting.

## Event Planning and Execution Standards

After funding approval, societies must complete the following items **14 days before the event**: confirm venue booking, purchase public liability insurance, and report to ANU Security. Failure to complete these on time may result in the funding being withdrawn.

### Venue Booking and Insurance Requirements

On-campus venues can be booked through the **Eventbrite ANU system** or by contacting faculty administrative offices directly. Outdoor venues (such as University Avenue and Union Court) require an additional temporary use permit, usually costing 50-150 AUD per day. Public liability insurance is purchased centrally by ANUSA or PARSA, covering personal injury and property damage during the event, but excluding alcohol-related incidents. In 2023, ANUSA paid about 12,000 AUD in premiums for this [ANUSA 2023, *Annual Financial Report*].

### Promotion and Materials Management

Event promotion must comply with the ANU brand usage guidelines; all posters and social media posts must state "Funded by the ANU Students' Association". Promotion materials must be submitted to the Student Life Office for review 7 days before the event; materials that have not been reviewed may not be posted. Original receipts must be kept for materials purchases; electronic payment records are also acceptable.

## Reimbursement and Closing Reports

After the event, societies must submit reimbursement applications and closing reports within **30 days**. Those who submit late will be placed on the "suspended funding list", and their applications in the following year will be restricted.

### Reimbursement Process

Reimbursement must be claimed using the **reimbursement form** designated by ANUSA or PARSA, attached with all original receipts (electronic versions must be in PDF format; single receipts over 200 AUD require a bank statement screenshot). Reimbursements are usually paid by bank transfer within 10-15 business days. In 2023, ANUSA processed 210 reimbursement applications, with an average processing time of 12 days.

### Closing Report Content

Closing reports must include an **event summary** (number of participants, event photos, feedback summary), a **final financial statement** (actual expenditure compared with the budget) and **improvement suggestions**. ANUSA requires at least 5 on-site event photos, of which 2 must clearly show the event banner or backdrop. For societies that fail to submit closing reports twice in a row, ANUSA has the right to cancel their registration eligibility for the following year.

## Common Problems and Handling Strategies

During the application and execution process, societies often encounter three types of problems: **budget overruns**, **insufficient attendance** and **returned materials**, for which contingency plans should be prepared in advance.

### Handling Budget Overruns

If actual expenditure exceeds the budget by more than 10%, the society must immediately notify the ANUSA or PARSA treasurer in writing, explaining the reason for the overrun and applying for additional funding. The cap on additional funding is 50% of the originally approved amount, and applications must be submitted 7 days before the event. In 2023, there were 18 additional funding applications, of which 11 were approved, with an average additional amount of 680 AUD.

### Remedying Insufficient Attendance

If event registrations are more than 30% below expectations, the society may apply to convert the in-person event to online 3 days before the event (subject to the venue manager's consent), or adjust the scale of the event to reduce expenditure. Unused funding must be returned in the closing report. Under 2024 PARSA rules, for events cancelled due to insufficient attendance, societies must return all unused funding within 7 days.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I appeal if my funding application is rejected?
Yes. Rejected societies may submit a written appeal to the **appeals committee** of ANUSA or PARSA within 14 days of receiving the notice, attaching new supporting materials. In 2023, the appeal success rate was 23%, with an average processing time of 10 working days. Appeals require a 50 AUD deposit, which is fully refunded if the appeal succeeds.

### Q2: Can a society apply for funding from both ANUSA and PARSA?
No. A single event can apply to only one association for funding. If a society has both undergraduate and postgraduate members, the application channel should be chosen based on the event's primary target audience. In 2024, PARSA explicitly prohibited "dual-channel applications"; violators will have their application eligibility cancelled for the next 12 months.

### Q3: How soon after the event must I submit for reimbursement?
Reimbursement applications must be submitted within 30 days after the event. Late submissions are automatically downgraded to "second priority", with processing time extended to 25-30 working days. Two consecutive late submissions result in a 6-month suspension of funding eligibility. In 2023, 12 applications were downgraded for lateness.
