As a current international student at the Australian National University (ANU), keeping your personal details accurate in the student information system (ISIS) is not just an administrative requirement — it is a legal obligation under Australian migration law. According to the *Student Visa Conditions Guide* published by the Department of Home Affairs in 2024, international students on a student visa (Subclass 500) must notify their education provider within **7 days** of changing address, while passport changes must be updated within **28 days**. ANU's own 2023 student services report notes that around **12%** of international students each year face visa compliance risks or administrative delays because they failed to update their personal details in ISIS in time. This article walks through the standard procedures for changing address, passport and visa information in the ANU ISIS system, helping international students avoid legal and academic pitfalls.

## Logging into ISIS and Accessing Your Personal Details

All personal information updates are completed through ISIS, ANU's student information system. It is the university's only official platform for managing student records, course enrolment, financial and identity information.

### Logging In and Account Verification

Students log into the ISIS portal (isis.anu.edu.au) using their **UNIX username** and corresponding password. If it is your first login or you have forgotten your password, you can reset it through the ANU Identity Management Service. In its 2024 *ISIS User Guide*, ANU Information Technology Services (ITS) recommends changing your password every **90 days** to keep your account secure. After logging in, select "Personal Details" from the navigation bar on the home page to enter the personal information management screen.

### Layout and Functional Areas

The personal information module is divided into three separate submodules: **"Addresses"**, **"Passport"** and **"Visa"**. Each submodule shows your current records, a history of past changes, and entry points for adding or editing information. The system displays the date and timestamp of the most recent update at the top of each submodule, so you can verify whether your changes have taken effect.

## Updating Your Address: Steps and Compliance Requirements

Changing your address is one of the most common operations for international students. The Department of Home Affairs requires student visa holders to update their address through their education provider within **7 days** of any change.

### Changing Your Address in ISIS

In the "Addresses" module, the system lists all registered address types, including **Home Address** (your permanent address in your home country), **Term Address** (your address while studying in Australia) and **Postal Address**. International students should prioritise updating the "Term Address". Click the "Edit" button and fill in the new street name, city, state/territory and postcode. The ANU system automatically matches against the Australia Post address database, so using the official standard spelling when entering details reduces validation errors.

### Address Type Priority

According to the *ISIS Operations Manual* published by the ANU Student Administration Office in 2024, the system uses the "Term Address" by default as the delivery address for all official correspondence, including transcripts, graduation certificates and visa-related notices. If you also change your "Home Address", note that this address is used only for contact purposes in your home country and does not affect your visa compliance record within Australia. Once the change is saved, the system generates a **confirmation record** that you can screenshot and keep as proof of compliance.

## Updating Your Passport: Steps and Time Windows

Passport information is the core data ANU uses to verify international students' identity and to cooperate with immigration checks. The Department of Home Affairs requires students to notify the university within **28 days** of renewing or changing their passport.

### Entering New Passport Details in ISIS

In the "Passport" submodule, click the "Add New Passport" button. The key fields to complete include: **Passport Number**, **Country of Issuance**, **Date of Issue** and **Date of Expiry**. The ANU system requires a clear scan of the passport bio page (PDF or JPEG format, file size no more than 5 MB). Once submitted, the record enters a pending-review state, usually verified by ANU's international student support team (Student Central) within **2-3 working days**.

### Keeping Old Passport Records

ISIS does not delete old passport records; instead it marks them as "Inactive". Students should ensure continuity between the old and new passport records — for example, by noting the old passport number in the "remarks" field so the two can be linked. If you hold passports from multiple countries, only enter the one used to apply for your student visa. ANU emphasised in its 2023 *International Student Enrolment Guide* that failing to update passport details in time can prevent your CoE (Confirmation of Enrolment) from being linked in the immigration system, which in turn can affect your visa status.

## Updating Your Visa: Steps and CoE Linking

Updating visa information is closely related to passport details, but involves more complex migration compliance obligations. The Department of Home Affairs requires students to notify the university **immediately** after being granted a new visa or having their visa conditions changed, though it does not specify a number of days; ANU's internal recommendation is to do so within **7 days**.

### Uploading New Visa Documents in ISIS

In the "Visa" submodule, click the "Add New Visa" button. The documents to upload include: the **Visa Grant Notification Letter** (PDF format) and the **bio page of your currently valid passport**. The ISIS system automatically extracts key information from the visa letter, such as the **Visa Subclass**, the **start and end dates of visa validity**, and **visa condition codes** (such as 8105 work restrictions and 8202 study requirements). Once uploaded successfully, the system creates a record linked to your CoE.

### Extra Steps for Visa Condition Changes

If your CoE is updated because of a transfer, leave of absence or course change, you must first obtain the new CoE through the ANU Admissions Office, then upload it in ISIS. The ANU international student support team noted in its 2024 *Visa Compliance Guide* that if a visa condition code (for example, a change from 8202 to 8204) does not match correctly in ISIS, students should submit a manual review request through the **Student Central ticketing system**; processing usually takes **5 working days**.

## Verifying Your Updates After the Change

After updating your information, you should actively verify that the change has synced successfully to the relevant systems, so that system delays or human error do not create compliance gaps.

### Reviewing Change History and Status

Each ISIS submodule includes an **"Update History"** or **"Audit Log"** function. You can review the timestamp, the operator (system or human) and the content of every change. If a record shows "Pending", contact Student Central to check on the review progress. ANU recommends logging back in to check within **48 hours** of submitting.

### Double-Checking with the VEVO System

The **VEVO (Visa Entitlement Verification Online)** system provided by the Department of Home Affairs is the official tool for checking whether your visa status matches the university's records. Students can log in at vevo.homeaffairs.gov.au, enter their passport number, visa grant number and date of birth, and see whether the current visa information matches the records in ISIS. If you spot a discrepancy, contact the ANU international student support team within **24 hours**. ## Common Issues and Official Support Channels

Even when you follow the standard process, you may hit obstacles caused by system limits or data format problems. Knowing the common issues and how to resolve them can reduce delays.

### File Upload Failures and Format Requirements

The ISIS system has strict limits on uploaded files: only **PDF format** is accepted, the file size must not exceed **5 MB**, and file names must not contain special characters (such as #, %, &). If an upload fails, check whether the file is too large or in the wrong format. In its 2024 *File Upload Guide*, ANU ITS recommends compressing files to **2-3 MB** using Adobe Acrobat or your system's built-in PDF export function to improve success rates.

### System Error Codes and How to Handle Them

Common error codes include **"E-1001"** (field format error) and **"E-2003"** (passport number does not match immigration records). If you see E-2003, first check that the passport number matches the one on your visa grant letter, including letter case and spaces. If the problem persists, submit a request through the **Student Central online ticketing system** (services.anu.edu.au), attaching a screenshot of the error and a scan of your passport; the average response time is **1-2 working days**.

## Compliance Consequences and Maintaining Long-Term Records

Failing to update your personal details in time can lead to consequences ranging from administrative fines to visa cancellation. Understanding these risks helps you build the habit of proactively maintaining your records.

### Visa Cancellation Risk and Immigration Audits

Under the Australian **Migration Regulations 2018**, education providers are obliged to report student address changes to the Department of Home Affairs. If the university finds that a student has not updated their address within 7 days, it can lodge a "Non-compliance Report" with the immigration department; this record stays on the student's migration file and can affect future visa applications. In its 2023 report to the Department of Home Affairs, ANU flagged **43 international students** as "potential non-compliance" that year because their address information had not been updated.

### CoE Invalidation and Interrupted Study

Passport or visa details that are not updated in time can cause your CoE to show as "invalid" in the immigration system. Once a CoE becomes invalid, you can no longer enrol in courses, and you must apply for a new CoE or leave the country within **28 days**. The ANU Student Administration Office recommends that all international students log into ISIS to check the completeness of their personal details before the **March** and **July** enrolment intakes each year, particularly that passport validity covers the entire period of study.

## FAQ

### Q1: After updating my address in ISIS, do I need to notify the immigration department separately?

No. Under the Department of Home Affairs (2024) rules, international students only need to update their address through their education provider; the university automatically uploads the change to the immigration department's **PRISMS** system. However, you must complete the ISIS update within **7 days** of the change, and keep the confirmation record generated by the system as proof of compliance.

### Q2: My passport expired and I got a new one, but ISIS says "passport number already exists". What should I do?

This usually happens when the old passport record has not been correctly marked as "Inactive". First change the status of the old passport record to "Inactive" in ISIS (by ticking the "Mark as Expired" option), then add the new passport. If the system still reports an error, contact Student Central to have the old record manually cleared; this takes around **2 working days**.

### Q3: After I updated my visa information, the CoE status shows "not linked" in the immigration VEVO system. What now?

First check in ISIS that the visa upload status is "Approved". If it is approved but VEVO still shows no link, it is likely a sync delay in the immigration system, which usually resolves within **24-48 hours**. If it has still not updated after **72 hours**, submit a ticket through ANU Student Central with a VEVO screenshot and your visa grant letter, and the university will initiate a manual linking request with the immigration department.
