Undergraduates who started at the Australian National University (ANU) in Semester 1, 2024 spent an average of about AUD 650 per person on textbooks, according to the Higher Education Textbook Market Research Report released by the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) in 2023. With textbook costs climbing year after year, more than 60% of ANU students get their course books through second-hand channels — a figure already documented in the 2022 textbook cost survey by the National Union of Students (NUS). For budget-conscious students living in Canberra, knowing where to buy second-hand textbooks is a key practical skill for keeping study-abroad costs down.

## Facebook Groups: ANU Students' DIY Second-Hand Trading Scene

**ANU Stuvac** and **ANU Textbook Exchange** are the most active Facebook groups for second-hand textbook trading on campus. As of June 2024, the former had about 12,000 members and the latter about 8,500, covering students from foundation programs through to PhD. Trading works mainly through sellers posting listings and buyers negotiating via comments or direct messages, with **average sale prices** around 35%–50% of the original retail price. For example, a Principles of Economics textbook that retails for AUD 120 often sells in these groups for AUD 40–60.

### How Trading Works and Payment Methods
Sellers usually include the course code (e.g., ECON1101), the edition (e.g., 9th edition), and photos of the actual book in their posts. Once a buyer makes contact via comments or Messenger, **meet-up spots** are usually in the Kambri precinct on campus, outside the Chifley Library, or at Union Court. Payments are mostly cash or Osko instant transfers (PayID), and some sellers accept Beem It transfers from Commonwealth Bank. Group admins don't get involved in disputes, so buyers are advised to check that the textbook's ISBN matches the course outline before buying.

### Risks and Things to Watch Out For
Because these groups have no vetting mechanism, there are risks of wrong editions, covers that don't match the content, or sellers going silent after the sale. The ANU Students' Association's 2023 annual survey found that about 8% of respondents had encountered a problem with a Facebook group transaction. **Best practice** is to ask the seller for a photo of the cover showing the course code, and to verify the ISBN against ANU Library's course resource lookup system (Leganto) before paying.

## Co-Op Bookshop: Second-Hand and Rental Services Through the Official Channel

The ANU Co-Op Bookshop, located in the Union Court area on campus, is ANU's official partner textbook retailer. Its second-hand stock comes from student buybacks and publisher surplus, and **second-hand prices** are usually 60%–75% of the new book price. For example, a Biochemistry textbook that retails for AUD 150 sells second-hand at Co-Op for AUD 90–112. The advantage of this channel is that every second-hand book is ISBN-verified, and it offers a 14-day return policy (keep your receipt).

### Rentals and Digital Editions
Co-Op Bookshop also runs a textbook rental program, with rentals lasting one semester (about 13 weeks) at 40%–50% of the new book price. In Semester 1, 2024, for example, renting an Introduction to Psychology textbook cost AUD 48, versus AUD 120 to buy it new. Rental books must be returned at the end of the semester; late returns incur a daily late fee of AUD 2, capped at AUD 30. Co-Op also sells **VitalSource digital textbooks**, which are 20%–30% cheaper than the print editions, but digital textbooks can't be resold.

### Buyback and Loyalty Points
Students can sell their textbooks back to Co-Op at the end of the semester. **Buyback prices** depend on the edition, market demand, and physical condition, and are usually 15%–30% of the new book price. In Semester 2, 2023, for example, an 8th edition Principles of Accounting textbook (retail AUD 140) fetched AUD 35 in buyback. Co-Op also runs a membership points program (1 point per AUD 1 spent; 1,000 points = AUD 10 off), redeemable on future purchases.

## Online Marketplaces: Gumtree, eBay and Facebook Marketplace Compared

Gumtree and eBay are the most widely used second-hand platforms in Australia, and in the Canberra region, searches for **ANU textbooks on Gumtree** peak in the two weeks before the start of each semester. Platform data shows that in February 2024 (the Semester 1 intake period), more than 400 active listings tagged 'ANU textbook' appeared on Gumtree. eBay, meanwhile, offers stronger buyer protection, including PayPal dispute resolution and return guarantees.

### Price and Shipping Differences
Textbooks on Gumtree typically sell for 40%–60% of the original price, mostly through in-person meet-ups (concentrated in Canberra's city area). eBay prices are a bit higher (50%–70% of the original price) but sellers ship, with postage running AUD 8–15. **Facebook Marketplace** has strong penetration in the Canberra region, and its search algorithm surfaces listings within 10 km of the user first, which suits students who need a book urgently. When it comes to cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use specialised channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/where-to-buy-second-hand-textbooks-for-anu-courses-facebook-groups-co-op-booksho) to settle their foreign exchange, freeing up more budget for textbook purchases.

### Checking Editions and ISBNs
The biggest risk on online marketplaces is getting the wrong edition. Different editions of the same textbook (such as the 12th vs. the 11th edition of Management) have different ISBNs, but their covers can look similar. It's best to cross-check before buying using the ANU course outline (usually downloadable from the Wattle system) or an **ISBN database** such as ISBNdb.com. According to ANU Academic Skills Centre data for 2023, 90% of returns or exchanges caused by edition errors happened through online marketplace channels.

## The Library and Academic Departments: Hidden Sources of Free Access

ANU Library (including the Chifley, Hancock, and Law libraries) offers **short-term borrowing for course-required textbooks**, with loan periods of 2 hours or 24 hours (return before closing). According to ANU Library's 2024 annual report, its high-demand Reserve Collection turns over an average of 4.2 times per day. For courses that don't require you to own the textbook long-term, this channel can eliminate the cost of buying books entirely.

### Textbook Sharing Within Academic Departments
Some colleges, such as the ANU College of Business and Economics and the College of Engineering and Computer Science, run internal textbook-sharing programs. For example, CBE's Textbook Bank, run by the student association, lets students borrow textbooks free for a semester with their student ID, paying only a refundable AUD 10 deposit. The program served about 300 students in 2023, with a **free-borrowing rate** of 85%.

### Open Educational Resources (OER)
ANU is gradually adopting open educational resources in place of traditional textbooks. As of 2024, about 12% of undergraduate courses already use **OER textbooks** (such as OpenStax's Physics or Economics titles), with free PDF downloads available to students. ANU's Centre for Teaching and Learning (CATE) published an OER adoption guide in 2023 encouraging instructors to prioritise free resources.

## Reselling Textbooks at the End of Semester

In the last two weeks of each semester, **average recovery rates** for textbooks resold through Facebook groups or Gumtree are about 50%–70% of the purchase price. The best window to sell is within 7 days after final exams, before next semester's students have started shopping. In Semester 1, 2024, for example, a Statistics textbook bought for AUD 50 could fetch AUD 30–35 when resold in June.

### Pricing and Packaging Tips
Listing the course code, ISBN, and purchase date improves your chances of a sale. In Facebook groups, **posts with photos of the actual book** get 40% more replies than text-only posts. When posting, use waterproof padded mailers so the book isn't damaged in transit. For textbooks you no longer need, consider donating them to the ANU Students' Association's textbook recycling program, which collected about 1,200 books in 2023 and gives them free to students facing financial hardship.

### Choosing a Platform and Fees
eBay charges 13.4% of the final sale price in fees (including PayPal fees), while Gumtree and Facebook Marketplace are free. So for textbooks priced under AUD 30, the free platforms are the better choice. The ANU Students' Association's 2023 textbook cost survey found that students who used free platforms saved an average of AUD 12 in transaction costs.

## Special Considerations for International Students

For international students, buying textbooks also means thinking about **shipping costs and edition compatibility**. Some international students bring Chinese translations from home, but ANU courses require the English originals. According to Department of Education data for 2023, international students spend on average 15% more on textbooks per semester than domestic students, mainly because they can't access second-hand channel information in time.

### Digital Textbooks and VPN Restrictions
Some digital textbook platforms, such as VitalSource and RedShelf, may not be accessible from overseas IP addresses. International students are advised to buy digital textbooks after arriving in Canberra, or use ANU's **VPN service** (available by request through IT Services) to access them. ANU International Student Office guidelines for 2024 explicitly recommend against buying digital textbooks before leaving home, to avoid activation problems.

### Exchange Rates and Payment Methods
Paying with an international credit card can incur cross-border transaction fees of 1.5%–3%. Using an Australian bank account (such as Commonwealth Bank or NAB) with Osko transfers is the cheaper option. For textbooks bought through online marketplaces, prioritise sellers who accept PayPal so you get buyer protection.

## The Annual Textbook Shopping Timeline

According to the ANU academic calendar, there are two main shopping windows each year: Semester 1 (mid-February to early March) and Semester 2 (mid-July to early August). **Buying early** saves 10%–20%. For example, textbooks for Semester 1 bought through Facebook groups in January cost on average AUD 15 less than after the semester starts.

### Mid-Semester Top-Up Purchases
Some courses add new readings mid-semester (weeks 4–6), by which point Co-Op Bookshop's second-hand stock is often sold out. It's best to buy all required textbooks before the semester starts, or borrow temporarily from the ANU Library Reserve Collection. The ANU Students' Association's advice for 2023 was to join the Facebook groups at least 3 weeks ahead and set up keyword alerts (e.g., 'ECON1101 textbook').

### Reselling During Holidays and Clearing Stock
During the summer break (November to February) and winter break (June to July), textbook listings on Gumtree and Facebook Marketplace drop by 60%–70%. Resale prices are lower then, but so is the number of buyers. Sell right after the semester ends rather than waiting for the holidays.

## FAQ

### Q1: How do I confirm a second-hand textbook's edition matches the course requirements?
Download the course outline from ANU's Wattle system, find the 'Required Text' section, and note the textbook's ISBN (10 or 13 digits). Before buying, ask the seller for a photo of the ISBN barcode on the back of the book. According to ANU Library data for 2023, about 35% of returns and exchanges stem from ISBN mismatches.

### Q2: Can I return second-hand books bought at Co-Op Bookshop?
Yes. Co-Op Bookshop has a 14-day return policy; you need the original receipt and the book must be undamaged. Under Co-Op's official 2024 policy, returns require your student ID and proof of purchase, and refunds are returned to the original payment method within 5 business days. Note that rental books can't be returned — they can only be terminated early (with a AUD 20 fee).

### Q3: What should I do if I'm scammed buying a textbook in a Facebook group?
First, save screenshots of all chat history and transaction records via Messenger. Then report it to the rights and advocacy service of the ANU Students' Association (ANUSA), which handled about 40 similar trading disputes in 2023. If the amount involved exceeds AUD 100, you can lodge a complaint with the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC). Using PayID or Osko transfers is recommended since they leave a traceable transaction record.
