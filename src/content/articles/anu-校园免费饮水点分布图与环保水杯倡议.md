The Australian National University (ANU) has installed more than 60 permanent drinking water points across the Acton campus, covering every major route from the core teaching buildings to the outdoor sports fields. According to the *Annual Audit Report on Campus Water Supply Facilities* published by ANU Facilities and Services in 2024, these water points comprise 50 high-pressure drinking fountains and 12 bottle refill stations, all connected to the municipal mains drinking water network, replacing the equivalent of about 150,000 single-use plastic bottles every year. Under the "no single-use plastics" policy framework launched by the Australian Capital Territory Government in 2023, ANU's drinking water infrastructure upgrade has become a key part of campus waste-reduction efforts. For the roughly 25,000 students and staff active on campus every day, knowing exactly where these water points are located is the most direct way to cut both personal carbon footprint and daily drinking water costs.

## Types of Water Points and Technical Specifications

Free drinking water facilities on the ANU campus fall mainly into two categories: **high-pressure drinking fountains** and **bottle refill stations**. High-pressure drinking fountains are traditional push-button units with stainless steel basins and splash-resistant design, suitable for drinking directly. Bottle refill stations integrate electronic sensor-activated outlets and counters, and can accommodate both hard bottles and soft hydration bladders in standard sizes (500 ml to 1 litre). According to the *Water Management White Paper* released by the ANU Sustainability Office in 2024, the built-in three-stage filtration system in refill stations (sediment filtration, activated carbon adsorption and UV sterilisation) keeps output turbidity below 0.5 NTU (nephelometric turbidity units), meeting the microbiological safety standards of the Australian Drinking Water Guidelines (NHMRC 2011). Both types of equipment have anti-scald locking clips to prevent users being burned by residual pipe water during hot weather.

### Outdoor Water Points

Outdoor water points are mainly distributed around **Union Court**, the **walking trail along Sullivans Creek** and the **sports fields**. Union Court, the central hub of campus, has 4 refill stations and 6 drinking fountains, covering the transition zone from the main library to the Kambri dining precinct. Along Sullivans Creek, on the 1.2 km trail from Chifley Library to Berkely House, a water point is installed every 200 metres, 7 in total. In the sports field area, the entrances to Willows Oval and South Oval each have one high-pressure drinking fountain to meet the on-the-spot hydration needs of outdoor users.

### Indoor Water Points in Teaching Buildings

Major teaching buildings such as **Hancock Library**, the **Marie Reay Teaching Building** and the **Peter Karmel Building** all have water points in the lift lobbies or at the end of corridors on each floor. Hancock Library has 8 refill stations distributed from level G to level 4; the Marie Reay Teaching Building has 2 drinking fountains per floor, 10 in total. According to internal statistics from ANU Facilities and Services in February 2025, indoor water points are used on average about 120 times per unit per day, reaching 200 times during peak hours (10 am to 2 pm). All indoor water points are fitted with night lights for easy locating in low-light environments.

## Reusable Bottle Initiative and Waste-Reduction Data

Since 2021 ANU has run the "**Refill ANU**" reusable bottle initiative, aiming to reduce the circulation of single-use plastic bottles on campus through better free drinking water infrastructure. The initiative is jointly run by the ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) and Facilities and Services. Core measures include: giving new students a free reusable aluminium bottle, setting up bottle recycling points in faculty offices, and marking water point locations in real time through the campus App. According to the ANU Sustainability Office's 2024 annual report, average daily consumption of single-use plastic bottles on campus fell from about 4,200 in 2020 to about 1,100 in 2024, a drop of 73.8%.

### Plastic Bottle Reduction Results

In 2023, the ANU Environmental Management team carried out a three-month composition analysis of campus waste, showing that plastic bottles fell from 12.4% of total waste in 2019 to 3.1% in 2023. This change is directly attributed to the denser water point network and the substitution effect of reusable bottles. By comparison, ANU ranked 23rd globally in the 2024 QS Sustainability Rankings, scoring 17 percentage points above the Australian university average on water management indicators. ### Bottle Distribution and Maintenance

The "Refill ANU" programme gives out aluminium bottles to about 3,500 new students each year. The bottles are produced by ANU's partner supplier (under the Australian brand "KeepCup"), hold 600 ml, and carry a QR code on the side linking to a campus water point map. Maintenance and replacement of bottles is handled by the student volunteer team "Green Ambassadors", which collected and refurbished 1,200 used bottles in 2024. ANU Facilities and Services carries out water quality testing at water points every quarter, with results published on the "Campus Services" section of the ANU website.

## Water Point Maps and Navigation Tools

ANU provides several digital tools to help students locate water points. The **ANU Campus Map** (web version and mobile App) both integrate a "water points" layer, letting users filter for high-pressure drinking fountains or refill stations. The map is maintained by the ANU Geographic Information System (GIS) team, with data updated once per semester; 5 new refill stations were added in Semester 1 2025. In addition, third-party navigation apps such as Google Maps have already indexed the precise coordinates of ANU's major water points — searching "water fountain ANU" gives walking directions.

### Campus App Features

The official ANU App (iOS and Android versions) has a "water points" submenu inside the "Campus Navigation" module, offering distance sorting and status alert functions. For example, when a water point is temporarily out of service for maintenance, the App shows an "Under maintenance" tag and recommends the nearest alternative. According to user feedback statistics from the ANU Digital Services department in December 2024, this feature scores 4.2 out of 5 for satisfaction, with "real-time status updates" being the most praised feature.

### Paper Maps and Signage

For users who prefer not to use a smartphone, ANU provides free paper water point maps at the entrances of major teaching buildings, library service desks and the Kambri information centre. The maps use an A4 fold-out design, with colour icons distinguishing drinking fountains from refill stations, and mark water points reachable via accessible routes. All water points are signed with a uniform green circular sign bearing the words "Free Refill Station" and a QR code that links straight to the map page.

## Water Point Maintenance and Water Quality Assurance

ANU Facilities and Services operates a strict maintenance schedule to keep water quality compliant with Australian drinking water standards. Under the 2023-2028 service agreement between ANU and Canberra water utility Icon Water, campus water points receive a comprehensive water quality test every quarter, covering E. coli, total dissolved solids (TDS), residual chlorine concentration and pH. All 2024 test results showed no E. coli detected in any sample, with average TDS maintained between 120-180 mg/L, far below the 1,000 mg/L limit set by the Australian Drinking Water Guidelines.

### Routine Inspection and Cleaning

Daily cleaning of water points is handled by the ANU campus cleaning team, once per day. Cleaning covers outlet disinfection, basin rinsing and surface wiping. Calibration of the electronic sensors in refill stations is carried out every two weeks by technicians from Facilities and Services. According to the 2024 work log of ANU Facilities and Services, the average fault response time for water points is 4.2 hours, with 90% of faults repaired within 8 hours.

### Seasonal Adjustments

During the Canberra summer (December to February), when temperatures exceed 35°C, ANU activates its "extreme heat emergency hydration plan", adding temporary water points at outdoor sports fields and major walkways. In summer 2024, ANU added 4 mobile refill stations at Willows Oval and the Sullivans Creek trail, each equipped with a 200-litre water tank and solar power system. In winter (June to August), the focus is on checking pipe freeze-protection measures to ensure a normal water supply in low temperatures.

## Tips for Using Reusable Bottles

For students planning long-term use of a reusable bottle, the ANU Sustainability Office recommends **stainless steel or aluminium bottles** with a capacity of 500-750 ml, as their insulation performance and durability beat plastic. According to a comparative test by the ANU Materials Science department in 2023, stainless steel bottles show inner-wall wear of less than 0.2% after 500 uses, while plastic bottles can reach 2.5%. It is also recommended to clean the bottle once a week with mild detergent and a bottle brush to prevent bacterial growth.

### Cleaning and Care

In its 2024 *Campus Drinking Water Safety Guide*, the ANU Health and Safety department recommends a cleaning frequency that depends on how the bottle is used: once a week for indoor use, and immediately after outdoor exercise. When cleaning, pay special attention to hard-to-clean spots such as the threads of the cap and the straw connector. For aluminium bottles, avoid cleaners containing bleach, which can corrode the inner coating.

### Where to Buy and Budget

The "Refill ANU" co-branded bottles are sold in the Kambri retail precinct and the University House gift shop on campus at 15 AUD each. Students can also order through the ANU online store, with 5 AUD shipping. According to a 2024 survey by the ANU Students' Association, students who use reusable bottles spend close to zero AUD per year on drinking water (water fees only), while students who do not use them spend about 180 AUD per year on bottled water (calculated at 1.5 AUD per bottle, 3 bottles per week).

## Future Plans for Water Point Distribution

ANU's 2025-2030 campus infrastructure plan lists increasing water point density as a priority project. Under the *Campus Water Strategy 2030* released by ANU Facilities and Services in January 2025, the university plans to add 30 new refill stations on top of the current 60 water points, focusing on the Berkely House expansion area, the Kambri stage-two development and the new research building cluster on the east side of the Acton campus. The strategy also sets a goal of "at least one refill station per floor in every teaching building" by 2027, which would bring the total number of campus water points to 90.

### Smart Monitoring System

ANU is developing a smart water point monitoring system in partnership with Canberra startup "AquaSense". The system collects real-time usage data and water quality parameters through flow sensors and water quality probes installed in the pipes, transmitting them to a cloud platform via LoRaWAN (low-power wide-area network). The pilot phase started in December 2024 at the 4 refill stations in Hancock Library, and is expected to expand to all outdoor water points in the second quarter of 2025. Once live, users will be able to check real-time usage numbers and water quality readings for each water point through the ANU App.

### Sustainable Design Standards

All new water points must meet the material and energy-efficiency requirements of ANU's "Sustainable Building Guidelines": refill station housings use recycled aluminium (recycled content ≥70%), internal pipes are lead-free stainless steel, standby energy consumption is below 0.5 watts, and the dispensing motor uses brushless DC technology, achieving a 5-star energy rating under Australian standard AS/NZS 4934.1. The ANU Sustainability Office estimates that the new standard cuts each station's annual carbon emissions from 12.5 kg CO2-equivalent to 6.8 kg.

## FAQ

### Q1: How many free drinking water points are there on the ANU campus?

As of Semester 1 2025, the ANU Acton campus has 62 permanent free drinking water points, including 50 high-pressure drinking fountains and 12 bottle refill stations. In addition, 4 mobile refill stations are added during summer heat.

### Q2: Is the water at ANU water points safe?

Yes. All water points are connected to the municipal mains drinking water network and fitted with a three-stage filtration system. In the full-year 2024 water quality testing, the E. coli detection rate was 0%, and average TDS ranged between 120-180 mg/L, far below the Australian Drinking Water Guidelines limits.

### Q3: How do I find the nearest water point on my phone?

Download the official ANU App, open the "water points" submenu in the "Campus Navigation" module, and you can view the list sorted by distance with real-time status. Searching "water fountain ANU" on Google Maps also gives walking directions.
