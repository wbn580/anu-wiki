In its ANU Security Annual Report 2023, the Australian National University (ANU) recorded more than 420 emergency and non-emergency incidents involving international students handled on campus that year, with about 15% of cases requiring coordination with external agencies such as embassies or consulates. For the roughly 8,000 international students studying in Canberra (source: Department of Education International Student Data 2023), knowing how to quickly reach their embassy or consulate and the university's internal crisis support systems in a moment of crisis is essential to protecting personal safety and rights. This article systematically sets out the official emergency channels available to ANU international students, covering embassy liaison, university crisis response, mental health support, and legal assistance, all based on the latest publicly available official guidance as of 2024.

## ANU On-Campus Crisis Support Systems

ANU operates two parallel response mechanisms — **ANU Security** and **ANU Wellbeing & Support Services** — covering immediate safety threats and ongoing psychological or life crises respectively.

### 24/7 Campus Security Patrols and Emergency Hotline

The ANU Security team operates year-round. More than 200 **emergency help points** are distributed across campus in teaching buildings, residential areas, and car parks. Dial campus extension **+61 2 6125 2249** or call **+61 2 6125 3333** from a mobile to reach the control centre directly. According to the official ANU Security page (updated 2024), average response time in the campus core is 4-7 minutes. The hotline also handles fires, medical emergencies, theft reports, and reports of suspicious activity.

### ANU Wellbeing & Support Services

For crises that are not an immediate threat to life (such as academic stress, relationship conflicts, or cultural adjustment difficulties), students can book an appointment or walk in to the **ANU Wellbeing Centre** (Building 152, Childers Street). The centre offers free, confidential short-term counselling, with more than 80 bookable slots per week (source: ANU Wellbeing 2024 service overview). In an emergency, call the **ANU Crisis Support Line: +61 2 6125 2444** (weekdays 9:00 am to 5:00 pm); outside working hours, calls are transferred to external professional hotlines.

## Contacting Chinese Diplomatic Missions in Australia

For Chinese international students, the first priority in cases of lost passports, serious safety incidents, legal disputes, or major natural disasters is to contact the **Embassy of the People's Republic of China in Australia** or the **Consulate-General in Sydney** (depending on the consular district).

### Embassy of China in Australia (Canberra Consular District)

The Chinese Embassy in Australia is located in Canberra and directly covers the ANU area. The consular protection and assistance emergency line is **+61 2 6228 3948** (for emergencies only, not passport or visa enquiries). According to the 2024 Consular Protection and Assistance Guide published by China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the number is answered by staff on Australian Eastern Time weekdays from 9:00 am to 5:00 pm; at other times calls are transferred to the Ministry's global consular protection hotline **+86 10 12308**. The embassy also provides a 24-hour automated voice enquiry service.

### Consulate-General in Sydney (New South Wales Consular District)

Students in Sydney or elsewhere in New South Wales should contact the Consulate-General in Sydney. Its consular protection and assistance line is **+61 2 9550 5519**. Important: the consular district is determined by where the incident actually occurs, not by where you are enrolled. For example, if you run into trouble during a short-term internship in Sydney, call the Sydney consulate number. China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs 2024 data notes that Chinese diplomatic missions in Australia handled more than 600 consular protection cases involving Chinese students in 2023, about 30% of which involved lost property or fraud.

## Embassy and Consulate Contacts for Other Major Countries

ANU's international student body comes from more than 100 countries. Below are the official emergency contact channels for several countries with large student numbers, based on 2024 public information from each country's foreign ministry or embassy in Australia.

### Embassy of the United States in Australia

US citizens in an emergency in Australia can call **+61 2 9373 9200** (Canberra embassy) or **+61 2 8265 5000** (Sydney consulate-general). The US State Department's **Overseas Citizens Services** can assist with passport replacement, medical assistance liaison, and emergency fund transfers. According to the US State Department Bureau of Consular Affairs 2024 report, more than 10,000 emergency medical evacuations involving US citizens were handled worldwide in 2023.

### British High Commission in Australia

British citizens can call **+61 2 6270 6666** (Canberra) or **+61 2 9247 7521** (Sydney). The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) 2024 guidance confirms that British citizens in Australia whose passports are stolen or lost can apply online for an emergency travel document, usually issued within 2 working days.

### High Commission of India in Australia

Indian students can contact **+61 2 6273 3999** (Canberra). Indian Ministry of External Affairs 2024 data shows Australia is the third most popular study destination for Indian students, with more than 120,000 Indian students in Australia. In an emergency, the High Commission can provide temporary travel documents, legal assistance referrals, and support in contacting family.

## Mental Health and 24-Hour Crisis Hotlines

Beyond university services, Australia's national crisis hotlines offer multilingual support to international students with no appointment needed.

### Lifeline Australia

**Lifeline** provides 24-hour crisis support and suicide prevention services on **13 11 14**. According to Lifeline's 2023-2024 annual report, the hotline answers more than 1 million calls a year, about 12% from people with non-English-speaking backgrounds. Lifeline offers free translation services — ask for the "Translating and Interpreting Service (TIS National)" when you call; TIS supports more than 150 languages.

### Beyond Blue

**Beyond Blue** focuses on mental health issues such as anxiety and depression. Call **1300 22 4636**, available 24 hours a day. Its website offers information pages in Chinese, Arabic, Vietnamese, and other languages. A 2024 Beyond Blue survey found that about 35% of 18-25-year-olds had sought mental health support due to academic stress.

### 13YARN (Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander Crisis Support)

For students of Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander background, **13YARN** (**13 92 76**) provides crisis support answered by Indigenous professionals, available 24 hours a day. Funded by the Australian Government Department of Health, the service handled more than 8,000 calls in 2023.

## Legal and Financial Emergency Assistance

When international students face legal disputes, visa problems, or financial hardship, the following organisations provide free or low-cost support.

### ANU Legal Service

Run by the ANU College of Law, the **ANU Legal Service** offers free legal advice to current students covering tenancy disputes, domestic violence orders, and small claims. The service is open on Tuesday and Thursday afternoons by appointment. According to ANU Legal Service 2024 service data, more than 300 student cases were handled in 2023, 40% of which involved tenancy issues.

### Legal Aid ACT

Legal Aid ACT provides free legal representation to people on low incomes. Call **1300 654 314**. International students who meet the income test (single, pre-tax income below about 600 AUD per week) can apply for criminal or civil legal aid. Its 2023-2024 annual report shows more than 4,500 civil legal cases handled.

### ANU Emergency Grant

ANU provides **Emergency Grants** of up to 2,000 AUD for international students facing sudden financial crisis. Applications require bank statements and evidence of the crisis (such as hospital bills or police reports). According to the ANU Scholarships Office 2024 data, more than 150 emergency grants were approved in 2023, with an average payout of 1,200 AUD.

## Natural Disasters and Public Crisis Response

Australia is prone to natural disasters such as bushfires, floods, and extreme heat. ANU has a dedicated **Crisis Management Framework** and issues alerts by SMS, email, and campus broadcast during public crises.

### The ANU Alert System

All current students and staff are automatically enrolled in the **ANU Alert** SMS notification system. During the 2020 Canberra bushfires, the system sent 6 emergency evacuation instructions to more than 40,000 registered numbers within 24 hours (source: ANU Crisis Management After-Action Report 2020). Students should make sure their mobile number is up to date in the ANU ISIS system to receive real-time alerts.

### Emergency Evacuation and Shelters

ANU has several **emergency shelters** on campus, including the **Kambri Precinct** and **Union Court** areas, which meet the disaster-resilience standards of the Australian Building Code (BCA 2022). During extreme weather events, the university opens **Llewellyn Hall** and **Manning Clark Hall** as temporary refuge centres, with capacity for more than 1,000 people.

### Government Disaster Assistance

Through the **Disaster Recovery Allowance**, the Australian Government provides short-term financial support to students who lose income because of a natural disaster, paying up to the equivalent of the Australian minimum wage per week (23.23 AUD per hour in 2024). The allowance is administered by Services Australia; applications require proof of loss from the police or an insurer.

## FAQ

### Q1: On the ANU campus in an emergency, should I call campus security first or 000?

**A1:** Assess the situation first. If there is an **immediate threat to life** (such as a fire, cardiac arrest, or armed attack), call **000** immediately for police, fire, or ambulance. For **non-life-threatening** situations (such as theft, suspicious persons, or minor injuries), call **ANU Security: +61 2 6125 2249**. ANU Security 2023 data shows they transfer about 80 cases a year to 000 emergency services.

### Q2: If I lose my passport in Canberra, how quickly can I get a replacement? What documents are needed?

**A2:** Through the Chinese Embassy in Australia, a **standard replacement** normally takes 15 working days (China Consular Service website 2024 standard). If you need to return to China urgently, you can apply for a **travel document**, which can be issued in 3-5 working days when documents are complete. Required documents include: a passport copy or national ID, a police loss report (available from ANU Security or ACT Police), 2 white-background passport photos, and an online application form.

### Q3: If an international student has a tenancy dispute, does ANU offer free legal assistance?

**A3:** Yes. The **ANU Legal Service** provides free legal advice, but you need to book an appointment (Tuesday and Thursday afternoons). In addition, the ACT's **Tenants' Union ACT** (**+61 2 6247 1026**) specialises in tenancy disputes and offers free telephone advice and mediation. In 2023 it handled more than 1,200 tenancy-related cases, about 25% of which involved international students.
