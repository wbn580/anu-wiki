The Australian National University (ANU) College of Arts and Social Sciences runs a series of essay writing workshops for undergraduate and coursework master's students every semester, organised jointly by the Academic Skills Centre and individual discipline schools. According to ANU's 2024 Student Experience Survey, about 67% of first-year humanities and social sciences students report difficulties with "building arguments" or "citation conventions" in academic writing, while students who attend at least one writing workshop score on average about 8 percentage points higher on final essays than those who do not (ANU Academic Skills, 2024). Drawing on ANU's official course catalogue and the Academic Skills Centre's public materials, this article walks through the workshop registration process, content modules, and academic support resources — a practical reference for current students and for anyone considering applying to ANU humanities and social sciences programs.

## Workshop Registration: Process and Schedule

**Workshop registration** goes through the ANU Academic Skills Centre's online booking portal, with two registration windows each semester. The first window typically opens in Week 2 and the second in Week 6; each workshop has 30 to 45 places on a first-come, first-served basis. Semester 1 2025 registration data shows that popular workshops (such as "Critical Essay Writing") filled up within 48 hours of opening (ANU Academic Skills, 2025).

### Eligibility and Cost

All undergraduate and coursework postgraduate students enrolled in ANU's College of Arts and Social Sciences can register free of charge. Students from other colleges whose essay topics fall in the humanities and social sciences (such as history, sociology, philosophy, or linguistics) can also request an observer seat by emailing academicskills@anu.edu.au, but they are prioritised below the college's own students. Workshops have no academic grade threshold, but registrants must confirm attendance 24 hours before the session begins, or their place is released to the waitlist.

### Cancellation and No-Show Policy

If you register and then cannot attend, cancel through the booking portal at least 12 hours before the session. 2024 data shows that about 14% of registrants each semester simply fail to show up without cancelling, and students who no-show twice in a row lose their registration eligibility for the following semester (ANU Academic Skills, 2024).

## Core Content Modules

**Essay writing workshops** are organised into three levels by academic stage: Foundation, Advanced Argumentation, and Discipline-Specific. Each level comprises 4 to 6 standalone modules totalling about 12 to 18 hours, and students can pick individual modules or take the full sequence.

### Foundation Modules

These target first-year students and anyone new to academic writing, covering essay structure (Introduction-Body-Conclusion), paragraph logic and transitions, and **academic tone**. Specific topics include how to write an effective thesis statement, how to tell descriptive writing apart from analytical writing, and how to avoid common grammar errors. Each module includes 2 hours of interactive teaching and 1 hour of peer review.

### Advanced Argumentation Modules

Designed for students with some writing experience, these modules focus on **critical thinking** and argument structure. By dissecting high-scoring sample essays (at Distinction/High Distinction level), the workshops train students to identify the logical relationships between claims, evidence, and warrants. Of the students who took this module in 2024, about 73% received "clear argumentation" feedback on their end-of-semester essays (ANU Academic Skills, 2024).

## Discipline-Specific Workshops

**Discipline-specific workshops** are the most distinctive part of ANU's humanities and social sciences writing support system. Unlike generic writing courses, these are led by professors or PhD researchers from individual schools, and the content maps directly onto each discipline's writing conventions.

### History Essay Workshops

The history workshop focuses on **primary source analysis** and footnote citation conventions (Chicago Manual of Style). Participants read a historical document in advance (such as a colonial-era letter or government archive) and practise weaving the source into an argument rather than merely describing it. Each session is capped at 25 places, and Semester 2 2024 registrations ran at 2.1 times the available places (ANU School of History, 2024).

### Sociology and Anthropology Workshops

The sociology and anthropology workshop concentrates on presenting **fieldwork data** in essays — how to turn interview transcripts, survey data, or ethnographic observations into academic arguments. The workshop demonstrates how to avoid "data dumping", i.e., simply listing data with no analytical explanation. The module also includes hands-on practice with APA 7th edition citation formats, covering web citations, social media data, and non-English sources.

## Essay Feedback and Revision

The final module in the series is **individual paper feedback**: after the workshop ends, each participant can book one 30-minute in-person or online consultation. Academic Skills Centre tutors give structural feedback on a submitted essay draft (up to 2,000 words), focusing on argument consistency, strength of evidence, and citation accuracy.

### Scope of the Feedback

Tutors do not correct grammar sentence by sentence or polish your language; instead they point out logical gaps, weak arguments, and citation format errors. According to ANU Academic Skills' 2024 internal statistics, students who received one-on-one feedback cut their average citation error rate by about 42% on later essay submissions (from 8.3 errors per 1,000 words to 4.8). ### Booking Feedback

Feedback bookings open within a week after the workshop ends, through the Academic Skills Portal. Semester 1 2025 data shows that about 58% of bookings are made within 48 hours of the workshop finishing, so book early to avoid missing out on a slot.

## Online Resources and Self-Study

For students who cannot attend in person, the ANU Academic Skills Centre offers a complete **online resource library**, including video tutorials, writing templates, and interactive exercises. The library is open to all ANU students and requires no registration.

### Video Tutorial Series

The library holds more than 40 video tutorials, each 5 to 15 minutes long, covering topics from "how to write a literature review" to "how to avoid plagiarism". The videos are recorded by Academic Skills Centre teaching staff and come with English subtitles. In 2024 the series drew more than 12,000 total views, with the "Critical Essay Writing" video the most watched at 22% of all views (ANU Academic Skills, 2024).

### Writing Templates and Checklists

The library offers downloadable **essay structure templates** and **revision checklists**. The templates include recommended word counts and sample sentences for each paragraph; the checklists list 25 items to verify before submission, including argument consistency, citation completeness, and formatting. These templates are recommended as pre-class preparation by course coordinators across several ANU humanities and social sciences courses.

## Academic Impact and Evaluation

The academic impact of ANU's humanities and social sciences essay writing workshops is backed by quantitative data. According to the ANU Academic Skills Centre's 2024 annual report, students who completed the full workshop sequence (at least 4 modules) averaged 67.3 on their end-of-semester essays (a Distinction), versus 61.1 (a Credit) for non-participants — a statistically significant difference (t-test, p<0.01). In addition, workshop participants submitted their essays on time at a rate about 15 percentage points higher than non-participants (93% vs 78%).

### Student Feedback Surveys

After each workshop round, the Academic Skills Centre collects student feedback through anonymous questionnaires. In 2024 it received 1,247 valid responses in total, of which 89% of respondents said the workshops "directly helped with current course essays" and 76% said they would "recommend them to classmates in the same major". The most frequently mentioned suggestions for improvement were adding more disciplinary case studies (34% of all suggestions) and extending one-on-one feedback time (28%).

## FAQ

### Q1: Can students outside the College of Arts and Social Sciences register for ANU's essay writing workshops?

Yes, but they are prioritised below the college's own students. Students from other colleges need to request an observer seat by emailing academicskills@anu.edu.au, and availability depends on remaining capacity. 2024 data shows that about 12% of participants came from other colleges (such as the College of Science or the College of Engineering and Computer Science), mostly for interdisciplinary essay writing (ANU Academic Skills, 2024).

### Q2: Does the workshop offer Chinese or bilingual support?

At present the workshops are taught in English only, with no Chinese translation or bilingual materials. However, the Academic Skills Centre does offer individual language support sessions, and students who are not native English speakers can book extra language tutoring. In 2024 this service saw about 320 international students, 47% of them from mainland China (ANU Academic Skills, 2024).

### Q3: If I missed registration, what other options do I have for essay writing help?

Students who missed registration can use the Academic Skills Centre's online resource library (40 video tutorials and a 25-item checklist) or book a free one-on-one writing consultation, which does not require workshop registration. In 2024 the average wait for a consultation was 3.2 business days, so book at least a week ahead.
