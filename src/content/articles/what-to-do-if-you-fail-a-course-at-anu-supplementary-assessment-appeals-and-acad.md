Among ANU's undergraduate cohort in 2023, approximately 12.5% of course enrolments ended with a fail grade, according to the 2023 Student Progress Report published by the ANU Planning and Performance Measurement Office. For international students, failing a course can trigger the notification obligation for unsatisfactory academic progress under the student visa provisions of the ESOS Act 2000, which in turn can affect their visa status. Understanding ANU's supplementary assessment, academic appeals, and the long-term impact of a fail on your academic record is essential knowledge for any student dealing with academic setbacks.

## Eligibility for Supplementary Assessment

ANU's supplementary assessment policy does not automatically apply to every failed course. Under ANU's Assessment and Supplementary Assessment Policy (2024 revision), students must meet one of the following conditions to be eligible for a supplementary assessment.

### Final Mark in the 45–49 Band

When the final overall mark for a course falls between 45 and 49 (out of 100), the Course Convener has the discretion to grant one supplementary assessment opportunity. The supplementary mark is usually capped at 50 (the pass mark), meaning that no matter how well you actually perform in the supplementary assessment, the recorded mark will not exceed 50. This policy is clearly set out in clause 42 of ANU's Bachelor Degree Rules (2023).

### Special Consideration

If you were unable to complete the scheduled assessment tasks because of **a health condition, a major family emergency, or other force majeure**, you can submit a Special Consideration application to Student Central. If approved, you may receive a deferred exam or an alternative assessment rather than a direct supplementary assessment. ANU requires such applications to be submitted within 3 working days after the assessment deadline, with third-party evidence attached (such as a medical certificate or police report). In 2023, ANU Student Central processed about 2,400 Special Consideration applications, of which approximately 68% were approved in part or in full.

## The Supplementary Assessment Process and Key Dates

Understanding the application window and exam scheduling for supplementary assessments helps students avoid missing critical deadlines.

### Application Window and Notification

Supplementary assessment eligibility is usually communicated by the Course Convener through the ANU student information system (ISIS) within 5 working days after final results are released. Students do not need to apply proactively, but must confirm whether they will sit the assessment within 3 working days of receiving the notification. Those who do not confirm are deemed to have forfeited their eligibility. Supplementary assessments are usually held during the 'Supplementary Exam Period' in the two weeks before the start of the next semester, with exact dates announced by the ANU Examinations Office at the beginning of each semester.

### Format and Marking of Supplementary Assessments

The format of a supplementary assessment may differ from the original exam. For example, a course originally assessed by group work may have a supplementary assessment in the form of an individual essay or an oral exam. The supplementary result is recorded only as 'Pass' (50) or 'Fail' (below 50), and it replaces the original fail grade on the official transcript. If you fail the supplementary assessment, you must retake the course; the original fail remains on your transcript but is not counted in your GPA calculation.

## The Formal Route for Grade Appeals

If you believe the final grade involved a calculation error, unfair assessment, or procedural breach, you can initiate ANU's three-level appeals mechanism.

### Level 1: Informal Appeal (Course Level)

Students should first contact the Course Convener or the lead lecturer to request a re-check of the mark. No written form is required at this stage, but it is advisable to send the email from your ANU account and copy in the course administrator. The Course Convener must respond within 10 working days. According to ANU's internal statistics for 2022, about 40% of informal appeals are resolved at this stage.

### Level 2: Formal Appeal (College Level)

If the informal appeal does not produce a satisfactory outcome, you can submit a formal appeal to your college's College Student Progress Committee (CSPC) within 20 working days. The appeal requires completing the Academic Appeal Form and attaching supporting materials such as the marking criteria, the original assessment task, and communication records. The CSPC will make a decision within 30 working days, which may include re-marking, granting a supplementary assessment, or upholding the original grade.

### Level 3: External Appeal (University Level)

If the college's decision is still not accepted, you can request an external review from the ANU Appeals Office. This stage only reviews whether procedures were followed correctly; it does not re-assess academic judgement. The outcome may require the college to re-process the case, but the office cannot directly change the grade. In 2023, ANU received 47 Level 3 appeals, of which 12 (about 25.5%) were partially upheld and referred back to the college for reconsideration.

## The Long-Term Impact of a Fail on Your Academic Record

A fail affects more than just the current semester — it can have knock-on effects on your GPA, degree classification, and even your visa status.

### GPA and Degree Classification

ANU uses a 7-point GPA scale (High Distinction=7, Distinction=6, Credit=5, Pass=4, Fail=0). A failed course counts as 0 points directly and can pull your GPA down significantly. For example, if a student takes 4 courses (each worth 6 units) and fails one of them, the GPA can drop from 5.0 (a Credit average) to about 3.75, close to the Pass boundary. Degree classifications (Honours Class I/IIA/IIB) usually require an overall GPA of at least 5.0 or 5.5, so a fail can cost you your honours eligibility.

### Academic Progress and Visa Risk

ANU assesses each student's **academic status** at the end of every semester. If a student's fail rate exceeds 50% for two consecutive semesters, or their GPA falls below 2.0, they are placed on the 'Academic Warning' list. For international students, ANU must report the warning to the Department of Home Affairs under section 19 of the ESOS National Code 2019, which can trigger student visa cancellation proceedings. In 2023, ANU issued academic warnings to about 320 international students, of whom 18 ultimately had their visas cancelled by the Department for unsatisfactory academic progress.

## Retaking a Course After a Fail

Retaking the course is the most direct way to recover from a fail, but watch out for enrolment strategy and costs.

### Enrolment Restrictions for Retakes

ANU allows students to retake a failed course in the next semester or the next academic year, but be aware of course capacity limits. Popular courses (such as ECON1101 Microeconomics) can fill up within hours of enrolment opening. Students should prioritise enrolling in their retakes, because ANU's system reserves a small number of places for students retaking a course. The retake grade replaces the original fail in the GPA calculation, but the original fail will still appear on your transcript marked as 'Fail (R)'.

### Financial Cost and Time Impact

Tuition for each standard 6-unit course varies by program. In 2024, international students paid about AUD 5,400 per business course (based on an annual tuition of AUD 48,000 divided by 8 courses). A retake means paying this fee again, and it can delay your graduation. If a fail prevents you from completing your degree on time, you will need to apply for an extension of your student visa, which involves an additional visa application fee (AUD 2,500 as of 1 July 2026) and renewed health insurance costs.

## Alternatives Beyond Supplementary Assessment and Retakes

In some cases, students have other options to soften the impact of a fail.

### Withdrawing from a Course and Withdraw Without Failure (W)

Withdrawing from a course before the Census Date (usually the fourth week of the semester) means the course will not appear on your transcript. If you withdraw after the Census Date but before final results are released, you can apply for a 'Withdraw Without Failure' (W) grade, which appears on the transcript but is not counted in the GPA. W-grade applications require proof of special circumstances, and each student may use this option at most 4 times during their undergraduate studies. In 2023, ANU approved more than 1,100 W-grade applications.

### Cross-Institutional Credit Transfer

If you fail the same ANU course twice in a row, you can consider taking an equivalent course at another Australian university and transferring the credit back to ANU. You must obtain prior approval from the ANU Course Convener, and transferred credit is usually not counted in the GPA. This route is common for foundational courses in languages or mathematics.

## Mental Health and Support Resources

The psychological impact of academic setbacks should not be underestimated. ANU offers a range of free support services.

### On-Campus Counselling Services

ANU's Health and Wellbeing Centre provides up to 6 free counselling sessions for enrolled students (2024 standard). Appointments can be booked online through the student portal, with waiting times usually 1–2 weeks. In an emergency, you can call the 24-hour student support line at 1800 572 736.

### Academic Skills Support

ANU's Academic Skills and Learning Centre offers one-on-one tutoring, workshops, and online resources focusing on time management, exam techniques, and essay writing. Students who have failed a course can book an 'Academic Recovery Consultation', where a professional adviser develops a personalised study plan. The service supported more than 600 students in 2023, of whom about 75% improved their GPA by at least 0.5 points in a subsequent semester.

## FAQ

### Q1: If I fail the supplementary assessment again, how many fails will appear on my transcript?

After failing a supplementary assessment, your transcript keeps the original fail grade (e.g., 45) and adds a supplementary fail record (usually marked 'Supplementary Fail'). This means the same course can show two fail records. After you pass a retake, the original records remain, but the GPA calculation uses the new grade instead.

### Q2: How soon after a fail does the Department of Home Affairs need to be notified?

ANU completes its academic progress assessment within 30 working days after the end of the semester, and submits a Section 19 Notice (report of unsatisfactory academic progress) to the Department of Home Affairs within 10 working days after the assessment. Students usually receive formal notification from ANU within 2–3 weeks after the assessment. Once notified, students have 28 days to explain or provide evidence to the Department.

### Q3: Will a fail affect my scholarship renewal?

Most ANU scholarships (such as the ANU Chancellor's International Scholarship) require a GPA of at least 5.5 each semester. If a fail drops your GPA below this threshold, the scholarship will be suspended from the next semester. Students can apply once for a 'Scholarship Reinstatement Waiver', but must demonstrate that the fail was caused by factors beyond their control and that their GPA recovered to above 5.5 in a subsequent semester. In 2023, about 30% of waiver applications were approved.
