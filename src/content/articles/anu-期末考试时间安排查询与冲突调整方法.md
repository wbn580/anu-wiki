The Australian National University (ANU) publishes its end-of-semester examination arrangements centrally through the Examinations Office, covering more than 20,000 enrolled students (ANU 2024 Student Statistics Report). Under ANU academic policy, the final examination timetable is usually released between weeks 6 and 8 of each semester on the **ISIS (Integrated Student Information System)** platform, and students can receive real-time notifications through **ANUsafe** or **Moodle**. 2023 data shows that about 15% of ANU students experienced timetable clashes or special arrangement needs during the exam season (ANU Student Affairs Annual Report 2023). Based on ANU official policy and data, this article systematically introduces the exam timetable lookup paths, the conflict adjustment process and key precautions, providing a standard operating guide for current students.

## Exam Timetable Release and Lookup Paths

ANU's Final Examination Timetable is published twice a year, corresponding to Semester 1 (usually June) and Semester 2 (usually November). The **release dates** strictly follow the ANU Academic Calendar 2024 and are announced before the semester begins via official university emails and the **ANU Student Portal**. Students must log in to the **Examinations** module in **ISIS** within the designated window, enter their student ID and password, and view their personal exam schedule. The university recommends that students use the **official ANU mobile app** (available for iOS and Android) to receive push notifications and avoid missing key dates.

### Detailed Lookup Steps

Step 1: Visit the **ISIS** system (https://isis.anu.edu.au) and verify your identity with your **ANU Single Sign-On** account. Step 2: Under the **My Exams** tab, select **View Exam Timetable** — the system will display the exam dates, times (in Australian Eastern Standard Time, AEST), venues and seat numbers for all enrolled courses. Step 3: After checking the information, export the exam schedule as **PDF or iCal** and sync it to your personal calendar. **Note**: the live data in **ISIS** is authoritative — draft schedules in Moodle or course outlines may not have been finalised.

### Common Reasons for Lookup Failures

About 8% of lookup failures are caused by **abnormal course enrolment status** (ANU IT Service Desk 2023 statistics). If **ISIS** shows "No exams found", students should first check the **Enrolment Verification** function to confirm the course status is **Active** and has not been frozen due to unpaid fees or an academic warning. In addition, some **non-standard assessment courses** (such as internship reports or project portfolios) may not appear in the exam timetable; students should contact the **Course Convener** to confirm the alternative assessment method. If **Moodle** and **ISIS** disagree, **ISIS** is authoritative, and students should immediately raise a ticket with **Student Central**.

## Defining and Identifying Exam Conflicts

ANU defines an "exam conflict" as **a student being scheduled for two or more exams in the same time period**, or **two exams less than 24 hours apart**. Under the ANU Examinations Policy 2024, conflict detection is performed by the **Examinations Office**'s automated system, but students are responsible for proactively checking their own timetables. **Conflict types** fall into three categories: **direct overlap** (identical times), **partial overlap** (time periods overlapping by more than 15 minutes), and **back-to-back exams** (less than 24 hours apart). Semester 2 2023 data shows that about 2,800 students (14% of those sitting exams) were flagged for conflicts (ANU Examinations Office Annual Report 2023).

### How to Self-Check for Conflicts

Students can use the **Conflict Checker** tool in **ISIS**, which generates a conflict report from their student ID. If **Conflict Checker** shows nothing unusual but a student still believes an arrangement is unreasonable (such as three consecutive exams), they may submit a written request to the **Examinations Office**. **Recommendation**: complete the self-check within 48 hours of the timetable being released, because the deadline for conflict adjustment applications is usually the 7th working day after release (ANU 2024 exam timetable notice). **Data note**: in 2022, about 60% of conflict applications were submitted within 3 days of the deadline, causing processing delays (ANU Student Affairs 2022 internal report).

### Special Arrangement Needs

Besides timetable conflicts, students can apply for **Special Examination Arrangements** due to **disability, medical conditions or religious beliefs**. Applications must be submitted through the **Access & Inclusion** office, accompanied by **medical certificates or documents from religious institutions**. The **processing time** is usually 10–15 working days, so students are advised to apply at least 4 weeks before the exam season begins. In 2023, ANU approved 1,200 special arrangement applications, 35% of which involved extra time or a separate venue (ANU Access & Inclusion 2023 report).

## The Conflict Adjustment Application Process

Once an exam conflict is confirmed, students must submit an application through the **official ANU conflict adjustment system**. The **process** has four steps: log in to **ISIS** and open the **Conflict Resolution** tab under the **Examinations** module; select the conflicting courses and specify the **conflict type** (such as direct overlap or less than 24 hours apart); upload **supporting documents** (such as course enrolment proof or medical records); submit and wait for the review email from the **Examinations Office**. **Processing time**: the university promises a response within 10 working days, though peak periods (such as June and November) may extend this to 15 working days.

### Required Application Materials

For **direct overlap** conflicts, no additional documents are needed — the system identifies them automatically. For **back-to-back exam** conflicts (less than 24 hours apart), students must provide the **course outline** or a **statement from the Course Convener** proving that both courses require in-person closed-book exams. **Note**: if the conflict was caused by the student's own course selection (such as an elective clashing with a required course), the **Examinations Office** may require the student to first consult an **Academic Advisor** about adjusting their enrolment. **Data**: in 2023, about 45% of conflict applications were returned for incomplete documentation (ANU Examinations Office 2023 internal statistics).

### Adjustment Outcomes and Next Steps

The **Examinations Office**'s adjustment options usually include: **rescheduling the exam** (to another time slot), **allocating a separate venue** (supervised by invigilators), or **combining exams** (only available for specific courses). Students must log in to **ISIS** within 48 hours of receiving the notice to confirm the new arrangement; otherwise the system accepts it by default. **If unsatisfied with the outcome**, students can submit a written appeal to the **Student Grievance Officer**, which must be lodged within 14 working days. **Reminder**: adjusted exam times may fall on weekends or evenings, so plan transport and accommodation in advance. When paying tuition fees across borders, some student families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E6%9C%9F%E6%9C%AB%E8%80%83%E8%AF%95%E6%97%B6%E9%97%B4%E5%AE%89%E6%8E%92%E6%9F%A5%E8%AF%A2%E4%B8%8E%E5%86%B2%E7%AA%81%E8%B0%83%E6%95%B4%E6%96%B9%E6%B3%95) to complete the currency exchange, ensuring funds arrive on time and avoiding late payments that could affect exam eligibility.

## Deferred and Supplementary Exams

For students unable to sit an original exam due to **illness, family emergencies or major events**, ANU offers two options: **Deferred Examination** and **Supplementary Examination**. A **deferred exam** is for force-majeure circumstances known before the exam; applications must be submitted at least 5 working days before the exam starts. A **supplementary exam** is for sudden situations on the exam day itself and must be applied for within 3 working days after the exam. **Application channel**: complete the application through the **Special Consideration** module in **ISIS**, attaching an **independent medical certificate** (issued by a registered doctor, stating the symptoms and recommended rest time). In 2023, ANU approved 1,800 deferred exam applications, 60% of which involved flu or acute illness (ANU Health Service 2023 report).

### Eligibility Conditions and Limits

**Deferred exams** apply only to final exams in **Semester 1** and **Semester 2** — not to intensive courses in the **Summer Session** or **Winter Session**. **Supplementary exams** are limited to at most 2 courses per semester, with the grade capped at **Pass (50–64)**. **Note**: students who have been penalised for academic misconduct may lose supplementary exam eligibility. **Data**: in 2022, about 10% of supplementary exam applications were rejected for lacking a medical certificate (ANU Student Affairs 2022 report).

### Timing of Deferred Exams

**Deferred exams** are usually scheduled **within 2–4 weeks after the end of the final exam season**, with exact dates published by the **Examinations Office**. **Supplementary exams** are concentrated in the **week before the next semester begins**. Students should check the new timetable on **ISIS** and confirm the **venue** (usually different from the original exam). **Reminder**: deferred and supplementary exams do not offer a **special arrangement** option; if a student cannot attend again, they must apply to the **Course Convener** for an **alternative assessment** (such as an essay or assignment).

## Common Misconceptions About Exam Conflict Adjustments

ANU students often miss adjustment opportunities because of policy misunderstandings. **Misconception 1**: conflict adjustments only apply to **direct overlap** exams. In fact, **back-to-back exams** (less than 24 hours apart) are also adjustable, but require additional proof of **fatigue risk**. **Misconception 2**: the **ISIS** system automatically notifies students of conflicts. The system only flags conflicts in **Conflict Checker**; it does not proactively send emails, so students must check themselves. **Misconception 3**: applications are always successful once submitted. 2023 data shows that about 8% of conflict applications were rejected, mainly due to **abnormal course enrolment status** or **late submission** (ANU Examinations Office 2023 report).

### How to Avoid a Rejected Application

**Key steps**: before submitting, use **Enrolment Verification** in **ISIS** to confirm the course status is **Active**; write the application reason using the **official ANU template** (downloadable from **Student Central**); submit at least 3 working days before the deadline to allow processing time. **Data**: in 2022, 92% of applications submitted on time were approved, while only 45% of late submissions were (ANU Student Affairs 2022 report).

### Special Notes for International Students

**International students** should note that exam conflict adjustments can affect the **study progress requirements** of a **student visa**. If the adjustment **extends the course completion time**, the **Department of Home Affairs** must be notified. **Recommendation**: before submitting a conflict application, consult the **ANU Global Programs** office to confirm whether the adjusted timetable affects **visa compliance**. **Data**: in 2023, about 5% of international students experienced visa issues due to exam adjustments (ANU International Student Support 2023 report).

## Time Management and Exam Preparation Tips for the Exam Season

Once the **exam timetable** is released, students should draw up a **preparation plan** and deal with conflicting courses first. **Recommendation**: use the **time management template** provided by **ANU Academic Skills** (downloadable from **Moodle**), dividing daily study time into **3-hour blocks** with 30-minute breaks between blocks. **Data**: a 2023 survey of ANU students found that those who followed a structured timetable scored on average 7.5 points higher than those who did not (ANU Student Experience Survey 2023).

### Venue Preparation and Transport Planning

**Exam venues** may be spread across the **Kambri precinct** of the **Acton campus**, the **Copland Building** or **Llewellyn Hall**. **Recommendation**: visit the venue a week in advance to confirm the entrance and seating areas. **Transport**: if an exam is scheduled at 8:00 am or after 6:00 pm, check the operating hours of the **ANU campus shuttle bus** (usually 7:00–19:00). **Accommodation**: students with consecutive exams can consider booking a temporary room in **ANU on-campus accommodation** (applied for through **ANU Accommodation**).

### Emergency Plans for Exam Day

**Sudden illness**: if you fall ill on exam day, contact **Student Central** before the exam starts (phone: +61 2 6125 3339) and obtain a **medical certificate** as soon as possible. **Network failure**: for **online exams** (such as those on the **ProctorU** platform), screenshot the error code immediately and contact the **ANU IT Service Desk**. **Data**: in Semester 2 2023, about 200 students applied for supplementary exams due to network problems, and 85% were approved (ANU IT Service Desk 2023 report).

## FAQ

### Q1: When is the ANU final exam timetable released?

The ANU final exam timetable is usually released **between weeks 6 and 8 of each semester** (ANU Academic Calendar 2024). The exact date is announced via the **ANU Student Portal**, and students must log in to **ISIS** within **48 hours** of release to view their personal arrangements. In Semester 1 2023, it was released on the **Wednesday of week 7** (12 April 2023).

### Q2: How do I apply for an exam conflict adjustment?

Submit an application through the **Conflict Resolution** module in **ISIS**, which must be completed within **7 working days** of the timetable release. When applying, select the conflict type (direct overlap or back-to-back exams) and upload supporting documents (such as course outlines). The processing time is **10–15 working days**, and the outcome is sent to the **official ANU email address**. In 2023, about 92% of applications submitted on time were approved.

### Q3: Can I apply for a supplementary exam if I fall ill on exam day?

Yes. Contact **Student Central** before the exam starts, then submit an application within **3 working days** after the exam through the **Special Consideration** module in **ISIS**, attaching an **independent medical certificate** (issued by a registered doctor). The supplementary exam grade is capped at **Pass (50–64)**. In 2023, about 60% of supplementary exam applications involved flu or acute illness.
