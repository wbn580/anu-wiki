The Australian National University (ANU) Bachelor of Computer Science is known for its high academic standards and stringent attrition rates. According to 2023 data from the Australian Government's Quality Indicators for Learning and Teaching (QILT), the six-year completion rate for ANU computer science undergraduates is 74.3%, below the national average of 79.1% for comparable programs — and course difficulty is one of the core factors affecting completion. The program ranked 40th globally in the 2024 QS World University Rankings by Subject, with a curriculum spanning four major streams: algorithm theory, software engineering, artificial intelligence and data science. Course selection strategy directly determines whether students can complete the degree within 96 units (three years). Based on ANU's official course outlines (2024 edition) and student academic progress data, this article maps the difficulty tiers of courses at each year level and offers evidence-based course selection advice.

## Program Structure and Credit Points

The ANU Bachelor of Computer Science requires 24 courses (6 units each): **compulsory core courses** account for 48 units (8 courses), **program electives** for 48 units (8 courses), and a further 48 units (8 courses) of **ANU general electives**. Under ANU's official classification, course difficulty is divided into three levels: 1000-level (introductory), 2000-level (intermediate) and 3000/4000-level (advanced). According to ANU Registrar's Office statistics for 2023, around 62% of computer science students had at least one 1000-level course below 60 (i.e., below Credit) in their first year; that proportion rises to 78% for 2000-level courses.

### 1000-Level Courses: The Entry Hurdle

There are four compulsory 1000-level courses: COMP1100 (Introduction to Programming), COMP1110 (Data Structures and Algorithms), MATH1005 (Discrete Mathematics) and MATH1013 (Calculus I). **COMP1100 uses the Haskell functional programming language**, which differs markedly from common procedural/object-oriented languages — the course's failure rate was 12.4% in 2023 (ANU Course Evaluation Report, 2023). MATH1005 covers set theory and graph theory in discrete mathematics; students who never encountered formal proof at high school need on average an extra 6 hours per week of self-study (ANU Mathematics Department, 2023 student survey).

### 2000-Level Courses: The Core Challenge

Compulsory 2000-level courses include COMP2100 (Software Engineering Methods), COMP2120 (Operating Systems), COMP2300 (Computer Organisation and Architecture) and COMP2400 (Theory of Computation). The final project in **COMP2120 Operating Systems** requires implementing a simplified memory management unit in C; the course's average mark in 2022 was 58.3 out of 100, below the ANU average of 63.1 for all 2000-level courses (ANU Office of Educational Quality, 2023 report). COMP2400's theory of computation covers automata and computability, and is the first key turning point where students shift from hands-on programming to theoretical thinking.

## 3000/4000-Level Courses: Specialisation

Advanced courses are grouped into four streams of 3-4 electives each; students must pick at least two streams to complete 24 units. According to the ANU College of Engineering and Computer Science 2024 course guide, difficulty varies considerably across streams.

### Artificial Intelligence Stream

Core courses in the AI stream include COMP3620 (Machine Learning), COMP3630 (Deep Learning) and COMP3640 (Natural Language Processing). **COMP3620 Machine Learning** requires students to complete an end-to-end project within the semester, covering data cleaning, model training and an evaluation report; in 2023 the course had the college's highest average weekly workload at 12.3 hours of homework (ANU Student Experience Survey, 2023). Prerequisites include COMP2600 (Probability and Statistics) and COMP1110 — missing either one will cause the enrolment system to reject the course outright.

### Data Science Stream

The data science stream includes COMP3710 (Data Mining), COMP3720 (Big Data Processing) and COMP3730 (Data Visualisation). **COMP3710 Data Mining** uses Python's scikit-learn and the Spark framework, and its final project requires analysing a real dataset of at least 10 GB. ANU Library data laboratory statistics for 2023 show that students in this course average 47 hours per semester of high-performance computing cluster time. The stream is run jointly with ANU's statistics department, and students may additionally take STAT3008 (Advanced Statistical Modelling) as a complement.

## Course Selection Strategy and Time Planning

Drawing on the ANU Academic Skills Centre's 2023 Computer Science Degree Planning Guide, students are advised to follow a "2-2-2" enrolment rhythm: each semester take **2 computer science courses, 2 general education courses and 2 mathematics/statistics courses**. This pattern keeps the average semester load within 24 units (4 courses) and reduces the risk of timetable clashes.

### Year One: Building Foundations

Year one should prioritise completing all four compulsory 1000-level courses. ANU 2023 enrolment data shows that **students who took COMP1100 and MATH1005 in their first semester** had an 18.3 percentage-point higher pass rate in COMP1110 the following semester than those who deferred them (ANU Student Administration, 2023). A sensible pattern is 4 courses (24 units) per semester: 2 computer science core courses, 1 mathematics core course, and 1 ANU general education course (e.g., PHIL1004 Logic, which supports the understanding of theory of computation).

### Year Two: Transition and Branching

Year two requires completing the four compulsory 2000-level courses and two program electives. **COMP2120 and COMP2400 should not be taken in the same semester**, since one demands heavy programming practice and the other heavy theoretical derivation; combined, the average weekly homework load exceeds 20 hours (ANU student forum self-reported data, 2023). It is advisable to pair COMP2120 with a general elective, and COMP2400 with MATH2015 (Linear Algebra), the follow-on to MATH1005, to balance the workload. For cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E8%A8%88%E7%AE%97%E6%A9%9F%E7%A7%91%E5%AD%B8%E5%B0%88%E6%A5%AD%E8%AA%B2%E7%A8%8B%E9%9B%A3%E5%BA%A6%E5%88%86%E7%B4%9A%E8%88%87%E9%81%B8%E8%AA%B2%E5%BB%BA%E8%AD%B0) to settle foreign exchange, ensuring fees arrive on time and avoiding enrolment eligibility issues caused by payment delays.

### Year Three: Advanced Courses and the Capstone Project

Year three requires four 3000/4000-level electives and two general education courses. The **capstone project COMP3900 (Computer Science Project)** is the most mandatory of all: students form teams of 3-4 and must build a complete software system within 12 weeks. In 2023 the average team size was 3.6, but 31% of teams experienced members dropping out or uneven contributions (ANU College of Engineering and Computer Science, 2023 project evaluation). It is advisable to approach potential teammates at the end of year two and pick a topic aligned with your own stream to reduce team-forming friction.

## Common Obstacles and How to Tackle Them

The main obstacles facing ANU computer science students are weak programming foundations, difficulty transitioning to university mathematics, and poor time management. An ANU Academic Skills Centre report for 2023 notes that **67% of students who failed the introductory programming course COMP1100 did not attend the weekly tutorials**, whereas students who did attend had a failure rate of only 4.8%. The university offers a free Peer-Assisted Learning service (2 hours per week) that requires advance booking.

### Mathematics Bridging Support

For students with weaker mathematics backgrounds, ANU's Mathematics Department offers MATH1003 (Foundations of Mathematics) as a non-credit preparatory course. In 2023, 142 computer science students took it, and 89% subsequently achieved Credit or above in MATH1005 (ANU Mathematics Department, 2023 internal report). Enrolment opens two weeks before the start of each semester, and the course is free of charge.

### Wellbeing and Time Management Resources

The ANU Health and Wellbeing Centre offers free counselling; in 2023, bookings by computer science students made up 14.7% of the university-wide total, representing 22% of the school's students (ANU Health Centre Annual Report, 2023). The university also runs time management workshops six times per semester; students are advised to attend at least one in semester two, when the course load is heaviest.

## FAQ

### Q1: Which course has the highest failure rate in the ANU computer science program?

According to the ANU College of Engineering and Computer Science 2023 course evaluation report, the highest failure rate belongs to COMP2400 (Theory of Computation) at 18.7%, followed by COMP2120 (Operating Systems) at 15.2%. Both are compulsory 2000-level courses; students are advised to ensure a mark of at least 65 (Credit level) in COMP1110 before taking them to lower the risk.

### Q2: Can I enrol in 3000-level courses across semesters?

Yes, provided prerequisites are met. Since its 2024 update, the ANU enrolment system automatically checks prerequisite completion. For example, COMP3620 (Machine Learning) requires passing both COMP1110 and COMP2600 (marks of 50 or above), and COMP2600 must be completed in the semester before COMP3620 starts. In 2023, 23 students were automatically rejected by the system at enrolment because prerequisites were incomplete (ANU Registrar's Office, 2023 data).

### Q3: What is the minimum credit load for international students each semester?

Under the Australian Department of Home Affairs 2024 student visa regulations, international students must enrol in at least 24 units (4 courses) per semester, otherwise they risk breaching visa condition 8202. ANU permits requests to reduce the load to 18 units in special circumstances (e.g., medical reasons), but a doctor's certificate and college approval are required. In 2023 the computer science school approved 47 load-reduction applications, or 8.2% of its international students.
