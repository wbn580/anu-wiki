Since 2020, the Australian National University (ANU) Accommodation Services has formally enforced a unified **Quiet Hours Policy** across all its on-campus residential buildings, including more than 10 residential halls and colleges such as Bruce Hall and Burgmann College. The policy designates **10:00 pm to 7:00 am the next morning** from Sunday to Thursday, and **12:00 midnight to 8:00 am** from Friday to Saturday, as the official quiet periods. According to the Residential Handbook released by ANU Accommodation Services in 2023, over 72% of noise complaints fall within these periods, and about 15% of complaints involve repeat violations. The policy draws on the residential noise restrictions in the Australian Capital Territory (ACT) *Environment Protection Act 1997*, and aims to balance the study, rest and social needs of about 6,000 residents. Based on ANU official documents, student residential agreements and complaint handling records, this article systematically sets out the policy's designated times, the complaint process and the consequences of violations.

## Definition and Scope of Quiet Periods

The ANU Quiet Hours Policy divides the week into two quiet period patterns, corresponding to weekdays and weekends. On weekdays (Monday to Thursday), quiet hours run from **10:00 pm** to **7:00 am** the next morning — a total of **9 hours**; on weekends (Friday to Saturday), quiet hours shift to start at **12:00 midnight** and end at **8:00 am** — a total of **8 hours**. Sunday follows the weekday evening rule (starting at 10:00 pm) to protect preparation for Monday morning classes.

**Scope** covers the common areas and private rooms of all residences (Lodges) directly managed by ANU Accommodation Services, as well as affiliated Colleges. The policy explicitly prohibits the following during quiet hours: playing audio equipment at volumes exceeding **40 decibels** (measured 1 metre outside the room door), using household appliances such as vacuum cleaners or washing machines, talking loudly in corridors or stairwells, and holding gatherings of any kind. ANU Accommodation Services stressed in the 2022 Residential Policy Update that this standard is consistent with the ACT government's baseline for night-time noise.

### Exceptions to Quiet Hours

The policy allows two categories of exceptions. The first is **academic emergencies**, such as late-night study needs during the final exam period (as officially designated by ANU). Students may apply to the Residential Advisor (RA) 48 hours in advance and, once approved, use low-volume devices in designated study rooms. The second is **cultural or religious activities**, such as night-time prayers during Ramadan, which require written approval from the head of the residential college. According to 2023 statistics from ANU Accommodation Services, **47 exception applications** were received across the year, of which **36 were approved** — an approval rate of about 76.6%.

## Formal Noise Complaint Procedure

When a student or staff member identifies a noise violation, they must follow the three-level complaint procedure prescribed by ANU Accommodation Services. Level 1 is **immediate intervention**: affected persons can remind the offender on the spot or contact the RA for their floor. After receiving a complaint, the RA must arrive on site within **15 minutes**, record the noise source, the decibel reading (using ANU-issued noise meters) and a timestamp, and give the offender a verbal warning. According to 2023 data, about **68%** of noise complaints are resolved at Level 1.

### Level 2: Formal Written Complaint

If Level 1 intervention is ineffective or the complainant prefers anonymity, a **formal written complaint** (Formal Complaint Form) can be lodged with the residential college office. The form requires the complainant's name, room number, time of the violation, a description, and at least two witnesses (optional). Within **3 business days** of receiving the complaint, the residential college office sends a written notice to the person complained about, requiring a written response **within 7 days**. The ANU Accommodation Services Complaint Handling Guidelines (2022) require all electronic records to be retained at this stage, including email copies and photos of noise meter readings.

### Level 3: Accommodation Services Hearing

If the written complaint is not resolved, or the violation is serious (such as a night-time gathering of more than 20 people), the case is escalated to an **ANU Accommodation Services Residential Conduct Hearing**. The hearing panel consists of one Accommodation Services manager, one student representative and one independent staff member. The person complained about may bring one support person (such as an ANU Students' Association member). Hearings are usually held **within 14 days** of the complaint being lodged, and a written decision is issued. In 2023, ANU Accommodation Services held **23 hearings** of this kind, of which **17 found the violation established**.

## Classification of Violations and Penalty Standards

ANU classifies Quiet Hours violations into three levels with corresponding penalties. **Level 1 violation** (minor): a first offence with noise lasting no more than **15 minutes**; the penalty is a verbal warning recorded on file and retained for **6 months**. **Level 2 violation** (moderate): a second offence within 12 months, or noise lasting more than 30 minutes; the penalty is a written warning, mandatory attendance at a **noise awareness education course** (2 hours), and payment of a **AUD 50** course administration fee.

### Level 3 Violations and the Eviction Process

**Level 3 violation** (serious): a third offence within 12 months, or any conduct causing injury to others, property damage or serious nuisance (such as hosting a party at 3 am). Penalties include a **AUD 200 fine**, community service (such as 10 hours of cleaning common areas), and **eviction from the residence**. The eviction process requires a decision at an Accommodation Services hearing, and the evicted student must vacate **within 48 hours**. The ANU Accommodation Services 2023 annual report shows **5 evictions** for the year, of which **3** resulted directly from repeated Quiet Hours violations. All penalty records remain in the student's residential file and may affect future eligibility to apply for on-campus housing.

## Legal Force of the Residential Agreement

The core legal basis of the Quiet Hours Policy is the **ANU Residential Agreement** signed by every resident at check-in. Clause 8.2 of the agreement explicitly requires residents to comply with all policies made by Accommodation Services, including the quiet hours rules. Violating the policy constitutes a **breach of contract**, and ANU Accommodation Services is entitled to take action under Clause 12 (breach clause) of the agreement.

### Relationship with ACT Local Law

ANU's policy is directly linked to **Section 24 (noise control) of the ACT Environment Protection Act 1997**. The Act provides that night-time (10:00 pm to 7:00 am) noise in residential areas must not exceed **45 decibels** (A-weighted). ANU's internal standard (40 decibels) is stricter than the local law. Where a violation also breaches local law, ANU Accommodation Services refers the case to the **ACT Police** or the **Environment Protection Authority**. In 2022, **1 case** — noise exceeding 60 decibels sustained for 3 hours — was reported by ANU Accommodation Services to the ACT Police, and the offender was ultimately fined **AUD 1,200**.

## Adjustments for Special Periods and Exam Weeks

During ANU's **final exam periods** (usually the last two weeks of June and November) and **mid-semester exam periods** (April and September), the Quiet Hours Policy is temporarily extended. Under the ANU Academic Calendar 2024 edition, quiet hours during exam weeks apply **24 hours a day** — noise above **35 decibels** is prohibited at any time. This adjustment must be notified to all residents by email and noticeboard **7 days** before the exam period begins.

### Aggravated Penalties During Exam Weeks

Violations during exam weeks are escalated one level. For example, an act that would normally be a Level 1 violation (first minor offence) is treated directly as a Level 2 violation during exam weeks, requiring mandatory attendance at the education course and payment of a **AUD 100** administration fee. During the June 2023 exam period, ANU Accommodation Services recorded **18 violations**, of which **11** were escalated. This measure received the support of **73%** of surveyed students in the ANU Student Representative Council (SRC) 2023 survey.

## Practical Challenges and Data in Policy Enforcement

Despite the clear policy framework, ANU Accommodation Services faces several challenges in enforcement. **Language barriers** are a prominent issue: 2023 complaint records show that about **22%** of complaints involved international students, some of whom — non-native English speakers — did not fully understand the policy details. In response, ANU launched **multilingual policy summaries** in 2024 (including Chinese, Hindi and Vietnamese) and made attendance at a **30-minute** policy briefing compulsory for all new residents at check-in.

### Complainant Anonymity and Retaliation Risk

Another challenge is **complainant anonymity**. ANU Accommodation Services allows anonymous complaints, but if a case reaches the hearing stage, the person complained about has the right to request disclosure of the complainant's identity. In 2023, **4 cases** were withdrawn because complainants refused to reveal their identity. In response, ANU introduced a **third-party complaint channel** in 2024: students can submit complaints through the ANU Student Safety Team, which forwards them as an intermediary, protecting the complainant's information throughout.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I use equipment in common areas if I need to study during quiet hours?
Yes, but specific restrictions apply. During quiet hours, the **study rooms** in residential buildings are exempt from the quiet policy, but wearing headphones is mandatory. If you need to use a printer or scanner, ensure the device noise stays below **35 decibels** and do not use it for more than **30 minutes**. Breaching this rule is also treated as a noise violation. ANU Accommodation Services 2023 statistics show that about **12%** of noise complaints came from study room areas.

### Q2: How many days do I have to appeal a fine?
After being fined or penalised with an education course, students may submit a written appeal to the Accommodation Services manager **within 14 days**. The appeal must include new evidence or an explanation of procedural error. The manager must respond in writing **within 10 business days**. If the appeal is dismissed, students can escalate to the **ANU Student Appeals Committee**, which consists of three staff members and two student representatives. In 2023, there were **8 fine appeals**, of which **2** were partially or fully overturned.

### Q3: Can I request a room change if my roommate makes noise during quiet hours?
Yes, but specific conditions must be met. First, you must address the issue through the RA or the formal complaint procedure. If **3 or more** valid complaints occur **within 30 days** (as recorded by ANU Accommodation Services), you can submit a **Room Transfer Request**. ANU Accommodation Services assesses the request **within 5 business days** and prioritises moving you to another room in the same building. In 2023, ANU received **47 room transfer requests** caused by noise issues, of which **31** were approved, with an average processing time of **3.2 days**.
