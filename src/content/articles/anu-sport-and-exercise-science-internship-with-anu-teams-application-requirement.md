The ANU Sport and Exercise Science Internship with ANU Teams is a credit-bearing practical course from the ANU College of Health and Medicine, designed for senior undergraduates and postgraduates. The internship embeds students directly in the sport science and conditioning systems of ANU's representative teams, where they complete at least 120 hours of field work under accredited coaches and exercise physiologists. According to the 2023 University Internship Standards Guide from Exercise & Sports Science Australia (ESSA), only three universities across the Group of Eight (Go8) offer internships embedded in high-performance sport — and ANU is the only one that ties the internship directly to its on-campus teams, which span 12 sports including Australian rules football, soccer, basketball and tennis. The 2024 ANU course catalogue lists the internship as SPOR3006 (undergraduate) and SPOR8006 (postgraduate), with 20 places per semester and an application competition ratio of about 3.5:1.

## Eligibility and prerequisites

Applicants must clear hard academic thresholds and specific course requirements. The official ANU 2024 course outline requires undergraduate applicants to have completed at least 96 credit points (roughly the end of second year), with a mark of at least 65 (Credit) in both **SPOR2001 Exercise Physiology** and **SPOR2002 Exercise Biomechanics**. Postgraduate applicants need a mark above 70 (Distinction) in **SPOR6001 Advanced Exercise Science** to enter the selection pool.

**English language proficiency** is the other hard requirement. Applicants whose first language isn't English must provide an IELTS score of 7.0 overall (no band below 6.5) or the equivalent PTE Academic score (65 overall, no skill below 58), and the test must have been taken within two years of the application date. The ANU College of Health and Medicine 2023 admissions brief notes that this is higher than the ANU postgraduate entry benchmark (IELTS 6.5), because the internship involves real-time communication with athletes and giving instructions in emergency situations.

Applications must also include a **personal statement** (no more than 500 words) explaining why you've chosen an ANU representative team as your internship setting, along with any prior practical experience in sport science. The college internship coordinator interviews shortlisted candidates within two weeks of the application deadline; the interview pass rate is about 60% (ANU internal data, 2023).

## Time commitment and semester structure

The internship runs on the semester calendar, with each semester lasting 14 weeks. The official ANU course description specifies **8 to 10 hours of on-site work per week**, totalling at least 120 hours. Schedules must align with ANU representative team training, typically Tuesday and Thursday afternoons (14:00–17:00) and Saturday mornings (09:00–12:00).

**Semester availability**: undergraduate SPOR3006 runs only in S1 (Semester 1, February to June) and S2 (Semester 2, July to November), with no Summer Session option. Postgraduate SPOR8006 additionally offers a Winter Intensive module outside S1 and S2 (6 weeks, 20 hours per week). The ANU Sport annual report for 2023 shows that about 70% of internship students choose S1, to line up with the high-intensity training cycle of the Australian rules football season (March to September).

Students must complete a **mandatory induction** in the two weeks before the semester starts, covering sports injury prevention protocols, emergency response procedures (such as using an AED if an athlete suffers sudden cardiac arrest) and ANU sports facility safety rules. The induction runs about 6 hours; missing it disqualifies you from the internship.

## Placement types and team matching

ANU Sport manages 12 high-performance representative teams: **Australian rules football (men's/women's), soccer (men's/women's), basketball (men's/women's), tennis, athletics, cross country, swimming, volleyball and squash**. Interns don't choose their team; the internship coordinator matches them based on the skills and preferences in their personal statement and the teams' current needs.

Matching happens in two rounds. Round one is a **skills profile assessment**: within two weeks of the interview, the college sends students a skills matrix questionnaire covering sport physiology testing (such as VO₂max measurement), biomechanics analysis (such as motion-capture data collection), nutrition intervention design and psychological support. Round two is **team needs alignment**: before the season, each ANU team's head coach submits a "sport science support needs form" stating what the team needs most (recovery monitoring, injury risk assessment or training load quantification). The coordinator combines both sets of information and publishes the matches one week before the semester starts.

2023 internship data shows that the men's and women's Australian rules football teams together took 40% of interns (8 students); soccer and basketball each took 15% (3 each); the remaining teams shared 30% (6 students). Unmatched students can join a waitlist, but ANU doesn't guarantee a slot in the following semester.

## Core work and skill development

An intern's day-to-day duties span three main areas. **Athletic performance monitoring** is a core task: using GPS tracking systems (such as the Catapult Vector S7) to record running distance, high-speed sprint counts and heart-rate variability (HRV) during training, and producing standardised reports after every session. The ANU Sport Science Laboratory's 2022 technical white paper notes that interns must independently complete at least 15 such reports during the internship.

**Injury prevention and recovery management** is another key module. Under accredited exercise physiologists, interns run Functional Movement Screen (FMS) tests to identify athletes' weaknesses and design personalised warm-up and recovery programs. ANU representative team data from the 2023 season shows that athletes using recovery programs co-designed by interns saw their soft-tissue injury rate during the season fall 12.7% versus the previous year (ANU Sport internal statistics).

**Data collection and analysis** requires interns to be proficient in at least one sport science software package, such as **Kinovea** (a free video analysis tool) or **Dartfish** (a paid sports analysis platform). At the end of the internship, students submit a case analysis report based on data they collected themselves (2,500–3,000 words), which is the main basis for the course grade (60% of the total mark). When paying cross-border tuition fees, some student families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-sport-and-exercise-science-internship-with-anu-teams-application-requirement) to complete the transfer.

## Mentorship and assessment

Each intern is assigned a **field supervisor** (usually the head or assistant coach of an ANU representative team) and an **academic supervisor** (a professor or senior lecturer in sport science at the College of Health and Medicine). This dual-supervisor setup ensures guidance on both hands-on practice and academic reflection.

The field supervisor runs a **weekly feedback meeting** (about 30 minutes each) focused on professional skills: data collection accuracy, effectiveness of communication with athletes and speed of emergency response. The academic supervisor hosts a monthly **case seminar** (90 minutes each) where students present their data findings and field questions from peers and supervisors. The ANU course assessment criteria (2023 edition) allocate 25% of the final grade to supervisor feedback.

**Assessment has three components**: the internship log (15% of the final grade, submitted weekly, recording daily work and reflections), the case analysis report (60%) and a final oral defence (25%). The defence is chaired by two academic supervisors and one external examiner (from the Australian Sports Science Association, ASSA) and runs 30 minutes. Among 2023 interns, 85% earned Distinction (70 or above) or higher, and no student failed.

## Career pathways and industry accreditation

Completing the internship earns **120 hours of ESSA-recognised sport science practice**, one of the requirements for applying for ESSA's Accredited Exercise Scientist (AES) credential. The ESSA 2023 accreditation guide notes that AES applicants need at least 500 hours of supervised practice, of which this internship contributes about 24%.

The ANU College of Health and Medicine 2022 graduate employment survey shows that 78% of students who completed the internship found sport science-related work within 6 months of graduating, versus 52% of same-degree students who didn't do the internship. Common employers include the Australian Institute of Sport (AIS), state institutes of sport (such as NSWIS and VIS) and professional sports clubs (such as the Sydney Swans and Melbourne Victory).

The internship can also feed into the research project for an **ANU Honours degree**: some interns expand their case analysis report into an honours thesis. College data shows that 3 interns in 2023 published peer-reviewed journal papers based on their internship data (source: the International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance, IJSPP).

## FAQ

### Q1: Do I need to contact ANU team coaches before applying?

No. All matching is handled centrally by the internship coordinator — you don't contact coaches yourself. The official ANU application process (2024) explicitly forbids students from communicating directly with representative team coaches before the match results are announced, to keep the process fair. Breaking this rule can disqualify your application.

### Q2: Can I switch teams mid-internship if it's not a good fit?

Yes, but with strict limits. Within the first two weeks of the internship (the "trial period"), you can submit one request to change teams to the internship coordinator, with a stated reason (such as a skills mismatch or a health issue). In 2023, 2 students (about 10%) successfully switched teams during the trial period. No changes are allowed after two weeks, and a switch means re-accumulating the 120 internship hours.

### Q3: Is the internship open to international students? Do I need an extra visa?

Yes, it's open. ANU College of Health and Medicine data for 2023 shows that international students made up 35% of internship applicants, with a 20% acceptance rate (slightly below the 25% for domestic students). International students need a valid student visa (Subclass 500), and since the internship is part of the course requirements, no separate work visa is needed. Note that the work counts as "course-related practice": it can't be counted separately as part-time work hours (student visas generally allow 40 hours of part-time work per fortnight, but internship hours don't count toward that cap).
