The Australian National University (ANU) registers more than 8,000 international students each academic year, and about 15% of them apply to change their academic supervisor at some point during their studies, for reasons such as shifting research interests, mismatched supervision styles, or a supervisor's departure. According to the *Graduate Research Policy and Procedures Handbook* published by ANU in 2023, a supervisor change is not an immediate process but a formal procedure that requires faculty coordination, supervisor consent, and filing with the Academic Board. With the launch of ANU's new student management system (the upgraded ISIS) in 2024, the way documents are submitted and approvals are tracked has also changed structurally, making an understanding of the current process and the common reasons for rejection a key prerequisite for international students to avoid disruption to their studies.

## Eligibility and When a Change Applies

**Academic supervisor changes** cannot be initiated at any time by any student. The ANU *Research Student Supervision Policy* (2023 edition) explicitly lists three **applicable scenarios** for a supervisor change: the supervisor can no longer continue supervision due to retirement, resignation, or extended leave; the student's research direction has shifted significantly and the original supervisor's expertise no longer matches; and a breakdown of the supervisory relationship as determined by the college's Dean of Graduate Research (such as communication difficulties or academic ethics disputes).

### Exclusions and Time Limits

Coursework master's students are generally not eligible for the supervisor change process, because their academic supervision is handled centrally by course coordinators. Doctoral and research master's students may only submit a change application after 6 months of enrolment, and colleges will in principle no longer accept change requests when fewer than 12 months remain before the final thesis submission. ANU's internal 2022 statistics show that about 23% of change applications are returned outright for not meeting the timing requirements.

### Special Requirements for Cross-College Changes

If the new supervisor belongs to a different college or research centre, applicants must additionally submit a *Cross-College Supervision Agreement*, signed by the heads of both colleges. The *Interdisciplinary Research Guidelines* updated by ANU in 2024 require such applications to include proof of the new supervisor's supervision capacity (i.e., currently supervising no more than 8 students).

## Application Document Checklist

The **core application form** is ANU Form 12A (*Supervision Arrangement Change Request*), which must be signed by four parties: the student, the current supervisor, the new supervisor, and the Dean of Graduate Research. The form can be downloaded from the ANU Graduate Research Office (HDR Office) website and has supported electronic signature submission since March 2024.

### Additional Supporting Documents

In addition to the form, applicants must prepare the following: a written statement of 300-500 words explaining the reasons for the change and the basis for choosing the new supervisor; a brief written consent letter from the new supervisor (stating the intended supervision commitment period); and a handover plan from the current supervisor (including arrangements for transferring unfinished experimental data and thesis drafts). If laboratory resources are to be transferred, a *Research Facilities Access Change Form* must also be submitted.

### Document Submission Channels

All documents must be uploaded via the ANU ISIS system to the "Supervision Change" subsection under the "Degree Management" module. Since January 2024, paper submission is only permitted when the system is down, and prior notice to the HDR Office is required. When paying tuition fees across borders, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payment](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%9B%BD%E9%99%85%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E5%AD%A6%E6%9C%AF%E5%AF%BC%E5%B8%88%E6%9B%B4%E6%8D%A2%E7%94%B3%E8%AF%B7%E6%B5%81%E7%A8%8B%E4%B8%8E%E6%B3%A8%E6%84%8F%E4%BA%8B%E9%A1%B9) to complete the foreign exchange transfer, though tuition matters are not directly related to the supervisor change application process.

## Approval Process and Timeline

The **approval cycle** is usually 4-6 weeks and has three stages. Stage 1 is the college preliminary review (1-2 weeks), in which the Dean of Graduate Research checks the completeness of the documents and the applicant's eligibility; Stage 2 is supervisor confirmation (1-2 weeks), in which the current and new supervisors separately confirm the handover arrangements and the supervision plan; Stage 3 is filing with the HDR Office (1 week), which completes the update of the system records.

### Expedited Processing and Exceptions

ANU allows expedited processing (24-48 hours) in specific circumstances, including: the supervisor has a sudden health problem and can no longer supervise (medical evidence required); or the student's visa is about to expire and a supervisor change is needed to complete their studies (a letter from the Department of Home Affairs required). Expedited requests must be made directly to the Head of the HDR Office; such applications accounted for about 8% of the total in 2023, with a 76% approval rate.

### Common Reasons for Approval Delays

ANU's 2023 internal audit report shows that about 34% of applications are delayed because the new supervisor's supervision capacity is full; 28% because the current supervisor fails to sign the handover plan within the required time (14 working days); and a further 12% are returned because the student did not provide a complete cross-college agreement. International students should pay particular attention to the potential impact of visa condition 8105 on research progress — if a change causes a study interruption of more than 60 days, students may need to update their CoE status with the Department of Home Affairs.

## Academic Transition After the Change

**Transition management** is the critical phase after a successful change. ANU requires the new supervisor and the student to jointly develop a *Revised Research Plan* within 30 days of the change taking effect, setting out new milestones and the supervision frequency (formal meetings at least fortnightly are recommended). The plan must be uploaded to the ISIS system and copied to the Dean of Graduate Research.

### Transfer of Resources and Access

Laboratory access, library borrowing privileges, and IT accounts are usually migrated automatically to the new supervisor's project group within 5 working days of the change taking effect. For cross-college changes, students must re-apply for access permissions at the new college; the ANU Information Technology Services (ITS) reported in 2024 that such permission transfers take an average of 8.2 working days.

### Psychological Support and Academic Coaching

ANU Student Central offers free psychological counselling to students going through a supervisor change; 142 graduate students used the service in 2023. In addition, the ANU Academic Skills Centre runs a "Supervisory Relationship Management" workshop 3 times per semester, helping students develop effective communication strategies with their new supervisor.

## Common Reasons for Rejection and Coping Strategies

The three main reasons for **application rejection** were quantified in ANU's 2023 student satisfaction survey: insufficient justification (41%), insufficient supervision capacity of the new supervisor (29%), and the current supervisor's objection without college support (18%). International students in particular should avoid "personal preference" grounds — for example, "personality clash with the current supervisor" is generally not accepted as a valid reason.

### Appeals Channel

If the application is rejected, students may submit a written appeal to the College Academic Committee within 10 working days. The appeal must cite section 5.3 of the ANU Policy Handbook (*Student Rights and Responsibilities*) and attach new evidence (such as communication records or a third-party mediation report). In 2023 the appeal success rate was 31%, with an average processing time of 21 days.

### Alternatives

When a formal change cannot be achieved, ANU allows students to apply for "co-supervision" as an alternative, i.e., keeping the current supervisor while adding a co-supervisor. According to 2024 data from the ANU Graduate Research Office, about 22% of change applications ultimately convert into co-supervision arrangements; this path does not require the full four-party signing process, only the consent of the new supervisor and the Dean of Graduate Research.

## Special Considerations for International Students

**The visa-CoE link** is the area international students need to watch most closely. According to 2024 data from the Australian Department of Home Affairs, student visa condition 8202 requires students to maintain progress in their enrolled course while in Australia. If a supervisor change alters the research plan by more than 12 months, students may need to apply for a new CoE (Confirmation of Enrolment), which in turn can affect their visa validity. ANU International Student Services (ISS) recommends verifying visa conditions with a migration adviser before applying for a change.

### Language and Cultural Barriers

ANU's 2023 *International Student Experience Report* notes that about 37% of international students have experienced language or cultural misunderstandings in supervisor communication. For the written statement in the change application, it is recommended to use the template provided by the ANU Academic Writing Centre and avoid informal expressions (for example, "I feel uncomfortable" should be replaced with "The supervisory relationship does not align with the research methodology requirements").

### Impact on Tuition and Scholarships

A supervisor change usually does not affect scholarships already awarded (such as the ANU International Postgraduate Research Scholarship, AIPRS), but if the change moves the research field outside the scope of the scholarship, students may need to re-apply for funding. The ANU Scholarships Office's 2024 guidelines make clear that such changes must be submitted together with a scholarship adjustment request in the change application; otherwise the scholarship may be at risk of suspension.

## FAQ

### Q1: How soon can I re-apply after a supervisor change application is rejected?

ANU policy requires at least 6 months (183 days) between re-applications for a supervisor change involving the same student and the same supervisor combination. 2023 data shows that about 58% of students rejected the first time were approved on their second application after adding supporting evidence. If the reason for the change changes materially (e.g., the current supervisor leaves), the 6-month limit does not apply.

### Q2: Will changing supervisors affect my PhD thesis submission date?

According to the ANU *Research Degree Timeline Guide 2023*, a supervisor change delays the thesis submission date by an average of 4.2 months. About 60% of the delay stems from the new supervisor requiring experiments to be redesigned or the literature review framework to be revised. Students may apply to the Dean of Graduate Research for an extension of the degree candidature within 30 days of the change taking effect; the approval rate for such applications was 84% in 2023.

### Q3: Do international students need to notify the Department of Home Affairs of a supervisor change?

No direct notification is needed, but if the change alters the CoE status (e.g., the research area changes by more than 12 months or the student moves to a different college), ANU International Student Services will update the CoE information with the Department on the student's behalf. According to 2024 departmental data, there were 7 cases of visa cancellation caused by failure to update the CoE in time after a supervisor change, accounting for 9% of ANU's total visa cancellations that year.
