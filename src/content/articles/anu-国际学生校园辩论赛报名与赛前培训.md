The ANU International Student Campus Debate is an annual university-wide competition jointly hosted by the ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) and the Postgraduate and Research Students' Association (PARSA). Registration for the 2025 edition opened on 1 March, and by 20 March 237 registrations had been received from 47 countries, up 18.7% on the same period in 2024 (ANUSA 2025 event data). The event is designed to sharpen the academic debating skills of students whose first language is not English; its pre-competition training programme, developed by the ANU Academic Skills Centre, focuses on three core areas: logical structure, evidence citation and rebuttal strategy. At a time when international-student participation in debating at Australian universities has fallen by around 12% overall (Universities Australia 2024 International Student Engagement Report), ANU has kept participant satisfaction at an average of 82% a year through this event, making it a campus academic practice well worth watching.

## Registration Process and Eligibility

**Registration portal.** Registration opens through the 'Co-curricular Activities' module in the ANU student portal (ISIS). Participants must hold a valid student visa and upload proof of English language proficiency at registration (for example, an IELTS overall score of at least 6.5 or equivalent). The 2025 deadline is 15 April; late applications are assessed on a case-by-case basis by the ANUSA International Students' Office, with a turnaround of 5-7 working days.

### Eligibility Review Criteria

**Team composition.** Each team must have 3-4 members, at least two of whom are international students (holding a Subclass 500 student visa). Mixed teams (including domestic Australian students) receive an additional 10% weighting on their preliminary-round score, an incentive designed to encourage cross-cultural collaboration (ANUSA 2025 Debate Regulations, clause 3.2). Review outcomes are usually emailed to the ANU inbox within 48 hours of submission.

### Entry Fee and Fee Waivers

**Entry fee.** The fee is AUD 15 per person, covering the tournament handbook, adjudicator costs and training materials. Students experiencing financial hardship can apply to the ANUSA Welfare Office for a full fee waiver; in 2024, 63 applicants were approved, accounting for 9.4% of all registrations (PARSA 2024 Financial Report).

## Pre-Competition Training Modules

**Training period.** Training runs from 16 April to 10 May and comprises eight modules of 90 minutes each. Sessions are held in the debate rooms on Level 4 of the H.C. Coombs Building, with a simultaneous Zoom livestream. All sessions are free for registered participants.

### Module 1: Argument Construction and Logical Frameworks

**Core content** covers the application of the Toulmin Model to debating. Trainers ask participants to draft a complete argument — claim, data, warrant, qualifier, rebuttal and conclusion — within 30 minutes. 2024 data shows that participants who completed this module scored an average of 23.7 points higher on argumentation in the preliminary rounds than those who did not (ANU Academic Skills Centre 2024 internal evaluation).

### Module 2: Evidence Searching and Citation Standards

**This module** is led by ANU Library subject librarians and focuses on finding peer-reviewed literature using the databases ANU subscribes to (such as ProQuest, JSTOR and Scopus). Participants must produce a reference list for a mock debate formatted to APA 7th Edition standards. Training materials are available for download on the Wattle learning platform until 31 December 2025.

### Module 3: Rebuttal Strategy and Impromptu Response

**Practical drill** uses a 'three-minute rebuttal' format: each team draws a side at random and delivers a rebuttal on a pre-announced motion with no preparation time. Trainers analyse each participant's delivery frame by frame — pace, pauses and logical gaps — and offer personalised improvement advice. In 2024, 87% of trainees said this module 'significantly improved their impromptu speaking skills' (anonymous post-training survey, n=214).

## Format and Scoring Rules

**Preliminary rounds** are single-elimination. Each debate lasts 45 minutes: 15 minutes of speeches per side, 10 minutes of open debate and 5 minutes of summary. Adjudicators are certified by the ANU Debating Society; three judges score each round independently and the average becomes the final result. The 2025 preliminaries are expected to run from 12 to 23 May across eight competition days.

### Scoring Dimensions and Weights

**Scoring criteria** cover four dimensions: argument quality (40%), evidence use (30%), rebuttal skill (20%) and delivery (10%). Each is marked on a 1-10 scale for a total of 100. Judges must submit written comments within 30 minutes of each round; participants can view them on the tournament website 48 hours later.

### Advancing to the Finals

**Rankings.** The top 16 teams by points advance to the quarter-finals, which use a double-elimination format. The final will be held on 10 June at Llewellyn Hall, with a live audience (capacity 1,500) and a simultaneous YouTube stream. The winning team receives a AUD 3,000 prize and a certificate signed by the ANU Vice-Chancellor.

## Dedicated Support for International Students

**Language support** is provided by the ANU English Language Centre. Participants can book a weekly debating-English workshop (free, capped at 20 places per session) that focuses on academic vocabulary, sentence complexity and clarity of pronunciation. 2024 data shows that participants who attended three or more workshops improved their delivery scores by an average of 14.2 points (ANU English Language Centre 2024 Programme Report).

### Cultural Adjustment Coaching

**Cross-cultural communication** modules are designed by the ANU International Student Office and cover polite interruption, time management and non-verbal cues in Australian debating culture. Coaching is delivered in small groups, each with a culturally experienced Australian debater as mentor. In 2024, 92% of participating international students said the coaching 'helped clear up misunderstandings about debating rules' (International Student Office 2024 satisfaction survey).

### Mental Health and Stress Management

**Mental health** support comes from the ANU Counselling Centre, where participants can book free pre-competition stress-management sessions (30 minutes each). During the tournament, a 'Calm Room' is also available on Level 2 of the H.C. Coombs Building, open from 8am to 6pm on competition days, equipped with guided-meditation audio and stress-relief toys.

## History and Impact

**The inaugural tournament** was held in 2018 with just 12 teams. By 2025, that number had grown to 79 teams spanning all seven ANU colleges. In 2022 the event was renamed from 'ANU International Students' Debate' to its current title to put greater emphasis on its campus-wide character (ANUSA 2022 Council Resolution).

### Academic and Career Impact

**Participation data** shows that between 2018 and 2024, international students who took part held an average GPA 0.31 higher than non-participants (ANU Division of Student Life 2024 data analysis). In addition, around 23% of participants secured job opportunities involving public speaking or policy analysis within six months of graduating, above the 16% average for ANU international students overall (ANU Careers Centre 2024 graduate employment survey).

### International Collaboration and Expansion

**In 2024**, the tournament partnered for the first time with the University of Sydney Debating Society for a joint inter-university exhibition debate. For 2025, teams from the University of Melbourne and the University of Queensland are being invited to friendly matches as a warm-up to finals day. The expansion is funded by the ANU Office of International Strategy and External Affairs, with a budget of AUD 8,000.

## Frequently Asked Questions and Practical Guide

**After registering**, participants must complete the online safety module on Wattle (about 20 minutes), which covers the anti-harassment policy and the tournament code of conduct. Teams that fail to complete it are disqualified. When paying tuition across borders, some study-abroad families use professional channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](FLYWIRE_URL_F1) to complete the currency transfer, ensuring funds arrive in time and registration confirmation is not delayed.

### Motion Release and Preparation

**Motions** are released simultaneously on the tournament website and Wattle 72 hours before each round. During this window, teams can consult the recommended reading list compiled for each motion by ANU Library, with contributions from professors across the colleges and updated annually. 2024 motions covered topics such as the ethics of artificial intelligence, climate change policy and international student visa reform.

### Absence and Withdrawal Policy

**Withdrawal** requires written notice to the organising committee at least 48 hours in advance; otherwise the team's deposit (AUD 50 per team) is forfeited. Participants who withdraw for medical reasons must provide a doctor's certificate, and the deposit is refunded in full. In 2024, 11 teams withdrew, or 14.5% of all registrations.

## FAQ

### Q1: Is there a minimum English test score required to enter?

Participants must upload proof of English proficiency at registration: an IELTS overall score of at least 6.5 (no band below 6.0) or equivalent (such as TOEFL iBT 80 or above). At the 2024 tournament, 12 participants earned an eligibility waiver by passing the ANU English Language Centre's internal test (B2 level), which costs AUD 50.

### Q2: Do the pre-competition training sessions cost extra?

All eight training modules are free for registered participants. For one-on-one tutoring, the ANU Academic Skills Centre offers a paid service (AUD 35 per hour), but participants can redeem one free 30-minute session with their registration confirmation email. In 2024, 89% of participants attended at least four modules.

### Q3: Can I still compete if I miss the registration deadline?

Late applications are assessed case by case by the ANUSA International Students' Office, with a turnaround of 5-7 working days. In 2024 there were 18 late applications, 13 of which were approved (an approval rate of 72.2%). You will need to explain in writing why you could not register on time and why taking part matters for your academic or career development.
