The Bachelor of Nursing program at the Australian National University (ANU) requires students to complete a total of **880 hours** of clinical placement, spread across the six semesters of the three-year degree. Under the rules of the Australian Nursing and Midwifery Accreditation Council (ANMAC, 2023 accreditation standards), all placement rostering is allocated centrally by the ANU Clinical Coordination Office, and individual students cannot directly choose their department or time slot. According to official data from the ANU School of Nursing for 2024, about **240** students take part in placement rostering each semester, and the average processing time for a shift swap application is **7 working days**. This article systematically sets out the placement rostering rules for the ANU nursing program, the shift swap application process, priority assessment criteria and common dispute-resolution paths, with data current to Semester 1 2025.

## Core rostering rules

ANU nursing placement rostering follows the principle of **combining rotation with random allocation**. The Clinical Coordination Office publishes a draft roster 6 weeks before the start of each semester, and students must confirm or raise objections within **10 working days** of receiving notice. Rostering is based on the credits a student has completed, pass status in prerequisite courses and the availability of supervising resources at placement sites.

### Rotation departments and duration

Each student must complete rotations through at least **5 core departments** within three years: internal medicine, surgery, mental health, community nursing and aged care. Each department rotation is fixed at **160 hours**, usually arranged as **4 weeks × 40 hours/week**. The remaining 80 hours are elective placements, where students can choose specialty departments such as emergency, paediatrics or oncology from the list approved by the Clinical Coordination Office.

### Shift types and time distribution

Placement shifts are divided into morning shifts (07:00–15:30), afternoon shifts (13:00–21:30) and night shifts (21:00–07:30). ANU rules cap consecutive night shifts at **3** per placement cycle per student, with at least **48 hours** between rounds of night shifts. ANU internal statistics for 2024 show that about **68%** of placement shifts were morning shifts, 22% afternoon shifts and 10% night shifts.

## Conditions for submitting a shift swap application

Shift swap applications are not open-ended. The ANU Clinical Coordination Office explicitly lists **4 acceptable categories**: medical emergencies (with a doctor's certificate), major events involving immediate family (such as a funeral or critical illness), registered religious holidays (with evidence from a religious organisation submitted 14 days in advance) and timetable clashes with other confirmed courses. Unacceptable reasons include personal preference, transport difficulties or part-time work arrangements.

### Application windows and deadlines

Each semester has two shift swap application windows: the **initial adjustment period** (within 10 working days of the roster being published) and the **emergency adjustment period** (from 7 days before the placement starts to 3 days after it starts). The initial adjustment period accepts all acceptable categories; the emergency adjustment period accepts only medical or family emergencies. Semester 2 2024 data shows that applications submitted in the initial adjustment period accounted for **73%** of all shift swap applications, with an approval rate of **41%**.

### Required supporting documents

Shift swap applications must be submitted through the ANU School of Nursing's **InPlace system** with the following documents: a completed shift swap application form (PDF format), supporting evidence (such as a doctor's diagnosis, death certificate or letter from a religious organisation), and a written statement of **no more than 300 words** explaining the reason for the swap and its impact on academic progress. Applications missing any of these documents are automatically returned, and the deadline for resubmission is not extended.

## Priority assessment of shift swap applications

The Clinical Coordination Office uses a **three-tier priority system** to assess shift swap applications. First priority (P1) covers cases involving life-threatening situations or serious health risks, such as the student's own hospitalisation or a critically ill immediate family member; second priority (P2) covers religious obligations or legal guardianship responsibilities; third priority (P3) covers course timetable clashes. P1 applications are usually answered within **3 working days**, while P2 and P3 take **7–10 working days**.

### Statistics on shift swap approval rates

According to the ANU School of Nursing 2024 annual report, **1,872** shift swap applications were received across the year, of which **634 (about 33.9%)** were approved. The success rate for P1 applications was **89%**, for P2 **52%**, and for P3 just **18%**. Of the unsuccessful P3 applications, about **76%** were advised to resolve the clash through course enrolment adjustments rather than a shift swap.

### Waiting list mechanism

For applications that are not approved but have reasonable grounds (mainly P2 and P3), students can apply to join a **waiting list**. The list is ordered by submission time; when a student withdraws from a placement or a swap frees up a spot, the office allocates it in list order. In 2024, the average wait on the waiting list was **12 days**, and about **31%** of waitlisted students eventually got their swap.

## Alternative paths after a failed swap

When a shift swap application is rejected, students still have **3 alternative paths**. The first is to apply for a **placement deferral**, postponing the current placement to the next semester, provided this does not breach ANU's **maximum study duration** (usually 6 years full-time equivalent). The second is to apply for a **placement site change**, looking for vacancies at other partner hospitals in the Canberra region (such as Canberra Hospital or Calvary Public Hospital). The third is to apply for a **placement intensity adjustment**, switching from full-time placement (40 hours/week) to part-time mode (20–24 hours/week), with total placement hours unchanged.

### Impact of placement deferral

Choosing a placement deferral means the student may not graduate on time. ANU rules state that each deferred placement course pushes back graduation by **at least one semester**. In 2024, **47** students chose to defer a placement, and **32** of them eventually completed their studies one semester later. During the deferral period, students must keep their student visa valid, and international students should note that visa **condition 8105** (no more than 48 hours of work per fortnight) is not affected by the placement deferral.

### Feasibility of site changes

There are **4** main partner hospitals in the Canberra region that accept ANU nursing placement students. A site change application must be negotiated between the Clinical Coordination Office and the target hospital and usually takes **2–4 weeks** to confirm. In 2024, the success rate for site change applications was **23%**, with the main limiting factor being that the target hospital's supervising nurse quota was full. When handling cross-border tuition payments, some international student families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payment](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E6%8A%A4%E7%90%86%E4%B8%93%E4%B8%9A%E4%B8%B4%E5%BA%8A%E5%AE%9E%E4%B9%A0%E6%8E%92%E7%8F%AD%E8%A7%84%E5%88%99%E4%B8%8E%E8%B0%83%E7%8F%AD%E7%94%B3%E8%AF%B7) to complete the transfer.

## Special rules for international students

International students holding an Australian student visa face additional restrictions on placement rostering and shift swaps. Under the Australian Department of Home Affairs rules (2024 student visa conditions), international students must maintain **full-time enrolment status**, i.e. at least **24 credit points** per semester (equivalent to 4 standard courses). If a shift swap creates a clash between placement hours and class time, students cannot unilaterally reduce their credit load; they must first obtain written permission from the International Student Support Office.

### Visa conditions and placement hours

Student visa **condition 8104** restricts international students to no more than 48 hours of work per fortnight, but clinical placement hours do not count towards this limit. Data from the ANU International Student Affairs Office for 2024 shows that about **15%** of international students had to adjust their work arrangements because placement hours after a swap overlapped with their part-time work hours. Students are advised to confirm scheduling flexibility with their employer before submitting a shift swap application.

### Language proficiency and swap review

If an international student's shift swap application involves reasons related to **clinical communication skills** (for example, requesting a change of supervising nurse), they must additionally submit an English proficiency assessment report issued by the ANU Language Centre. In 2024 there were **28** such applications, of which **9** were approved; all successful applicants were placed in departments with bilingual supervising nurses.

## Appeals and dispute resolution

Students dissatisfied with a shift swap outcome can submit a written appeal to the ANU School of Nursing Academic Committee within **5 working days** of receiving the rejection notice. The appeal must cite specific policy provisions, such as clause 4.2 or clause 5.1 of the ANU Clinical Placement Management Policy 2024. The Academic Committee issues its decision within **14 working days** of receiving the appeal.

### Appeal success rate

A total of **67** shift swap appeals were received in 2024, of which **22 (about 32.8%)** were upheld in full or in part. Among the upheld appeals, the most common grounds were that the office had not properly assessed the validity of medical evidence (accounting for **55%**) or had not considered religious holiday clashes (**27%**). Of the unsuccessful appeals, **68%** failed because the supporting documents submitted did not meet ANU's prescribed format requirements.

### External arbitration paths

If the Academic Committee upholds the original decision, students can lodge a complaint with the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA, 2024 student complaints handling guidelines). TEQSA does not accept individual shift swap requests, but it will investigate whether ANU breached its publicly committed placement management policy. In 2024, TEQSA received **3** complaints related to ANU nursing placements, none of which progressed to a formal investigation.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I choose my own hospital for ANU nursing placements?

No. The ANU Clinical Coordination Office allocates placement sites centrally based on the student's residential address, academic progress and hospital supervising resources. 2024 data shows that about **85%** of students were allocated to Canberra Hospital, with the rest placed at Calvary Public Hospital or community health centres. Students can state a site preference in their swap application, but the office does not guarantee that it will be met.

### Q2: Can I resubmit a shift swap application after it is rejected?

Yes, but you must wait at least **14 days** and provide new supporting documents. Semester 2 2024 data shows that the approval rate for second submissions was only **12%**, dropping to **4%** for third or later submissions. Students are advised to prepare the strongest possible evidence for the first submission.

### Q3: Will taking leave during placement affect graduation?

Yes. ANU rules state that absence exceeding **20%** of total placement hours (i.e. more than 32 hours out of a 160-hour placement) results in a fail grade for that placement course, which must then be repeated. In 2024, **18** students had to repeat a placement due to excessive absence; the repeat fee is **AUD 4,800** per course (international student rate).
