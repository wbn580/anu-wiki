The Australian National University (ANU) has offered the undergraduate double degree (Flexible Double Degree, FDD) system since 2000, allowing students to complete two bachelor's degrees in four years and graduate with two separate degree certificates. As of 2025, ANU offers more than 60 undergraduate single-degree programs, which combine into more than 800 possible double degree pathways. According to the Times Higher Education World University Rankings 2025, ANU ranks 67th globally, with its humanities and social sciences, natural sciences and politics all in the global top 30. For students planning to complete 192 units of credit in four years, choosing a complementary degree combination and planning credit allocation directly affects graduation timing and academic depth. Based on ANU's official course handbook (2025 edition) and the Australian Department of Education's Higher Education Student Data Annual Report (2023), this article systematically sets out recommended double degree combinations and credit planning strategies.

## Basic Rules of the Double Degree Structure

ANU's **double degree programs** require students to complete 192 units in total before graduation, usually spread across 8 semesters (4 years). Each single degree has a minimum requirement of 96 units, though some programs (such as engineering and medicine) may require more. Students must complete at least 48 units of compulsory courses from each of the two degrees; the remaining units can be filled with electives or courses shared across the two degrees.

**The credit recognition mechanism** is central to double degree planning. When the two degrees contain overlapping courses, students can apply for up to 48 units of cross-credit, avoiding duplicated study. For example, a student doing both economics and statistics can have the econometrics course shared by both programs counted toward both degrees' credit requirements, reducing the actual study load from 192 to 144–168 units. ANU rules require each degree to include at least 24 units of standalone advanced courses (Level 3000+) to ensure degree depth.

**The course selection timeline** follows the ANU academic calendar: each academic year has two semesters (Semester 1 and 2), with students normally taking 4 courses (24 units) per semester. Students must submit their double degree registration application within the first semester of enrolment; late applications may require extra semesters to complete.

## Recommended Double Degree Combinations with High Complementarity

### Bachelor of Economics & Bachelor of Statistics

This combination spans **quantitative analysis** and **economic modelling**, suiting students planning careers in finance, policy analysis or data science. ANU's economics degree requires 96 units, with core courses including Microeconomics (ECON1101), Macroeconomics (ECON1102) and Econometrics (ECON3101); the statistics degree also requires 96 units, covering Probability (STAT1008), Statistical Inference (STAT2001) and Advanced Regression Analysis (STAT3032). Three courses are shared between the two degrees (ECON3101, STAT2001, STAT3032) and can be cross-credited, leaving 168 units of actual study.

According to the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS, 2023) Labour Force Survey, the full-time employment rate of economics and statistics double degree graduates within 6 months of graduation is 89.5%, above the ANU undergraduate average (83.2%). For **credit planning**, this combination is best approached by concentrating on foundation courses (such as calculus and linear algebra) in the first two years and taking advanced courses in the last two, to avoid timetable clashes.

### Bachelor of Advanced Computing (Honours) & Bachelor of Psychology (Honours)

This combination pairs **artificial intelligence** with **cognitive science**, suiting students interested in human-computer interaction, computational neuroscience or clinical technology. ANU's Advanced Computing (Honours) degree requires 144 units (including a 48-unit honours research project), and the Psychology (Honours) degree requires 96 units. With a total of 240 units, students need to apply for up to 48 units of cross-credit (for example, the cognitive science courses PSYC2006 and COMP3720 can be shared), bringing actual study down to 192 units.

ANU School of Psychology (2024) data shows students in this combination take on average 4.5 years to graduate, though Summer Session courses can accelerate this to 4 years. **Timetable clashes** commonly occur when laboratory sessions overlap; students are advised to check with ANU's Timetable Planner tool before selecting courses each semester.

### Bachelor of International Relations & Bachelor of Laws (Honours)

This is one of ANU's most prestigious pathways. The Law (Honours) degree requires 192 units and the International Relations degree requires 96 units, for a total of 288 units. Through the credit recognition mechanism (such as International Law LAWS4210 and INTR2020), students can reduce this to 240 units, usually taking 5 years to complete. ANU College of Law (2024) reports that graduates of this combination have a 34% higher hiring rate at the Australian Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) and international organisations than single law degree graduates.

**Key credit planning points**: law core courses (such as Contracts LAWS1201 and Criminal Law LAWS1202) must be completed in the first year, while international relations courses can be spread across later semesters. Students are advised to concentrate their electives on international law topics to maximise cross-credit benefits.

## Core Credit Planning Strategies

### Prerequisites and Scheduling

Each degree has **prerequisite requirements** — without completing them, students cannot enrol in advanced courses. For example, an economics double degree student must complete ECON1101 and STAT1008 in the first semester, or they cannot take econometrics in the third semester. The ANU course handbook (2025 edition) states that more than 70% of advanced courses require at least 2 prerequisite courses, so the order of course selection in the first four semesters directly affects graduation progress. Students are advised to use ANU's Program Planner tool to generate a personalised timetable; it automatically detects clashes and recommends alternative courses.

### The Leverage of Electives

Electives are the most flexible part of a double degree, accounting for 24–48 units. Students can use them for **interdisciplinary exploration** (such as a computer science student taking design courses) or for **deepening degree specialisation** (such as a law student taking a special topic in international human rights law). ANU allows students to take up to 24 units of "non-award electives" before graduation — these do not count toward either degree's credit requirements but do count toward the overall total. For example, an economics and statistics double degree student could take 24 units of financial mathematics electives to strengthen their competitiveness in investment banking recruitment.

### Summer Session and Accelerated Graduation

ANU offers two summer sessions a year (Summer Session A and B), each worth 6–12 units. According to ANU Academic Registrar data (2024), about 18% of double degree students use summer sessions to shorten their time to graduation. Summer session courses cost AUD 1,200 per unit (2025 rate), about 14% higher than the regular semester rate (AUD 1,050 per unit), but can save a semester of living costs. Note: summer sessions generally do not offer the independent research projects required for honours degrees, so they are only suitable for foundation courses or electives.

## Changing Programs and Credit Transfer in Practice

### Changing Your Degree Combination On Campus

ANU allows students to change their double degree combination before the end of their first academic year, provided they meet the new degree's entry requirements. For example, moving from "Economics and Statistics" to "Economics and Finance" requires at least 48 units completed and a GPA of no less than 5.0 (out of 7.0). The Australian Department of Education's Higher Education Student Data Annual Report (2023) shows that about 12% of ANU undergraduate double degree students change their combination after enrolment, adding on average 0.3 years to graduation time. Change applications must be submitted through ANU's ISIS system and take 2–4 weeks to process.

### Overseas Credit Transfer

ANU has exchange agreements with more than 40 universities worldwide (including Tsinghua University and the London School of Economics), allowing students to earn up to 48 units overseas. Credit transfer requires prior approval from the ANU Academic Committee, and course content must match the double degree curriculum. For example, an international relations double degree student who takes an "International Security" course at the University of Oxford can have it converted to credit for INTR3006. ANU Global Programs (2024) reports that about 22% of double degree students participate in exchange programs, and 85% of them successfully transfer credit.

When paying tuition fees across borders, some student families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E6%9C%AC%E7%A7%91%E5%8F%8C%E5%AD%A6%E4%BD%8D%E9%A1%B9%E7%9B%AE%E7%BB%84%E5%90%88%E6%8E%A8%E8%8D%90%E4%B8%8E%E5%AD%A6%E5%88%86%E8%A7%84%E5%88%92) to complete the currency exchange, ensuring funds arrive on time with transparent exchange rates.

## Career Prospects and Industry Recognition

**The employment advantage of double degree graduates** is significant in the Australian job market. According to the Graduate Outcomes Survey (GOS, 2024), ANU double degree graduates have a full-time employment rate of 87.6% and a median annual salary of AUD 68,000, 7.9% higher than single degree graduates (AUD 63,000). In specific industries such as consulting, fintech and international organisations, a double degree background is listed as a preferred hiring condition. For example, McKinsey & Company's 2024 recruitment data shows that 73% of its ANU hires hold double degrees.

**Industry accreditation requirements** need attention: some professions (such as registered psychologists and practising lawyers) require additional internships or exams beyond the double degree curriculum. ANU's Psychology (Honours) degree is accredited by the Australian Psychology Accreditation Council (APAC, 2024), but graduates still need 1 year of supervised internship to register. Law double degree graduates must pass the Australian Legal Profession Admission Examination (LPAB) before they can practise.

## Common Misconceptions and How to Avoid Them

**Misconception 1: a double degree always takes 4 years to complete.** In reality, about 35% of double degree students need 4.5–5 years due to timetable clashes or insufficient credit (ANU Academic Registrar, 2024). Combinations involving honours degrees in particular usually require an extra semester for the research project.

**Misconception 2: all courses can be cross-credited.** ANU rules allow cross-credit only when two courses overlap in content by more than 70%, and each degree can be reduced by at most 48 units. Students must submit course outlines for review by the college, a process that takes 2–6 weeks.

**Misconception 3: a double degree is always more competitive than a single degree.** A survey by the Australian HR Institute (AHRI, 2023) shows that employers value relevant internship experience more than the number of degrees. If a double degree drags a GPA below 5.0, it can be counterproductive.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I combine programs from two different colleges in an ANU double degree?
Yes. ANU allows cross-college combinations, such as a philosophy degree from the College of Arts and Social Sciences (CASS) with a computer science degree from the College of Engineering and Computer Science (CECS). Note that different colleges' academic calendars may differ slightly, but the course selection system is unified. 2024 data shows cross-college combinations account for 41% of double degree registrations.

### Q2: Will both programs be listed on my double degree certificates?
Yes. ANU issues two separate degree certificates, each stating the corresponding degree name. For example, graduates receive both a "Bachelor of Economics" and a "Bachelor of Statistics" certificate. The Academic Transcript lists all courses taken and marks the double degree status.

### Q3: How does failing a course affect graduation time in a double degree?
After failing, you must retake the course, and the failure may affect prerequisites for later courses. ANU rules allow at most two retakes per course, and failed credit does not count toward GPA. 2023 data shows the double degree failure rate is about 8.7%, adding on average 0.4 years to graduation time. Students are advised to take no more than 4 courses per semester and to use the tutoring services at ANU's Academic Skills Centre.
