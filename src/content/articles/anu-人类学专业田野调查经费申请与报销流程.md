The fieldwork funding application and reimbursement process for Anthropology at the Australian National University (ANU) is a core administrative step that undergraduate and postgraduate students in the discipline must master before undertaking field research. According to the Fieldwork Funding Guidelines published by the ANU School of Anthropology in 2024, students can apply for up to A$5,000 per project, while 2023 data from the university's Research Office shows that about 72% of approved projects were ultimately reimbursed for less than 90% of their initial budget. The process spans four stages — budget preparation, application submission, mid-term reporting, and final reimbursement — and how well it is handled has a direct bearing on how smoothly a research project runs. With funding tight across the Australian higher education sector, understanding this process is essential for keeping research on track.

## Eligibility and Funding Types

**ANU Anthropology** fieldwork funding is aimed at three groups: Honours students, coursework master's students, and PhD candidates. Under the Student Research Funding Policy updated by ANU Research Services in 2024, Honours students can apply for up to A$3,000 per project, master's students A$4,000, and PhD candidates A$5,000. Funding comes in two types: **Category A standard fieldwork funding**, which covers ordinary transport, accommodation, and living allowances, and **Category B emergency supplementary funding**, which is reserved for unexpected situations in the field (such as itinerary changes caused by natural disasters).

### Application Windows

There are two fixed application rounds each year: **Round 1** runs from March 1 to March 31 and covers fieldwork conducted between July of the current year and February of the following year; **Round 2** runs from September 1 to September 30 and covers projects from January to June of the following year. In 2023 the ANU School of Anthropology received 214 applications, with an approval rate of 67% in Round 1 and 58% in Round 2 (ANU Research Office, 2023 Annual Report).

### Eligibility Restrictions

Applicants must meet three hard requirements: completion of at least one research methods course (such as ANTH3001 Field Methods), written approval from their supervisor, and a research proposal cleared by the Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC). Applications that do not meet the ethics review requirement are returned outright — 12% of applications were rejected on these grounds in 2023 (ANU School of Anthropology, 2024 internal statistics).

## Budget Preparation Guidelines

**Budget preparation** is the most critical stage of the application process and directly influences whether funding is approved. ANU requires all budgets to be completed using the Fieldwork Funding Budget Template (version FWF-2024-01), which divides expenditure into five categories: transport (international and domestic), accommodation, living allowances, equipment rental and consumables, and contingency reserves. Every line item must be backed by a **market rate reference** — accommodation, for instance, must cite the average daily accommodation rate for the Canberra region published by ANU Accommodation Services (A$145 per night in 2024).

### Calculating Transport Costs

International airfares must be benchmarked against real-time quotes from Skyscanner or airline websites, and economy class is the only reimbursable fare. In 2024, ANU Finance specified a budget cap of A$1,800 for a return economy flight from Canberra to Port Moresby, Papua New Guinea. Domestic legs (such as Canberra to Darwin) follow the Australian Government's official travel rate of A$0.85 per kilometre (Australian Taxation Office, 2024 vehicle expense rate).

### Living Allowance Rates

Living allowances are set at three tiers depending on the fieldwork location: A$65 per day within Australia, A$55 per day in Pacific island nations, and A$75 per day in other international locations (ANU Finance Office, 2024 Fieldwork Living Allowance Guide). Applicants must state the number of fieldwork days in the budget — a 30-day project in Papua New Guinea, for example, should budget 30 days × A$55 = A$1,650 for living allowances.

## Application Submission and Review Process

**Applications** are submitted through the ANU research portal (Research Master), with the deadline at midnight Australian Eastern Standard Time (AEST). All materials must be combined into a single PDF containing the funding application form, budget template, supervisor approval letter, ethics approval letter, and a summary of the research proposal. In 2023 the ANU School of Anthropology introduced a **pre-review mechanism** that lets applicants send a draft to the School's administrative office two weeks early and receive feedback on format and content before submitting officially.

### Review Timeline

The review takes 4 to 6 weeks after the submission deadline. Round 1 results are usually announced in mid-May and Round 2 results in mid-November. Each application is assessed by a panel of three reviewers, at least one of whom is a professor in the School of Anthropology and one an external reviewer (from another school or partner institution). In 2023 the review panels spent an average of 45 minutes per application (ANU Research Office, 2024 process audit report).

### Common Reasons for Rejection

According to the ANU School of Anthropology's 2023 data analysis, applications are most often rejected for three reasons: budget items lacking market reference evidence (41% of rejections), ethics approval not obtained before submission (33%), and supervisor approval letters that omit the specific fieldwork dates (26%). Applicants should work through the Application Self-Checklist (version FWF-CL-2024) item by item before submitting.

## Fund Disbursement and Usage Rules

**Funding is disbursed in two instalments**: the first (70% of the total) is transferred to the student's ANU-linked bank account within 10 business days of approval; the second (30%) is released once the mid-term report has been submitted and approved. Since 2024, ANU Finance has required all funds to flow through the student's ANU-designated bank account; cash payments and transfers to third-party accounts are not accepted.

### Time Limits on Use

Approved funds must be spent within 6 months of the start of fieldwork. Any unused balance must be returned to the ANU research account within 30 business days of the fieldwork ending. In 2023, about 15% of approved projects had some unused balance, with an average refund of A$340 (ANU Finance Office, 2024 annual settlement report). When paying tuition fees across borders, some student families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E4%BA%BA%E7%B1%BB%E5%AD%A6%E4%B8%93%E4%B8%9A%E7%94%B0%E9%87%8E%E8%B0%83%E6%9F%A5%E7%BB%8F%E8%B4%B9%E7%94%B3%E8%AF%B7%E4%B8%8E%E6%8A%A5%E9%94%80%E6%B5%81%E7%A8%8B) to convert and remit currency, but fieldwork funding itself is never disbursed through such channels.

### Prohibited Expenditure

Funding may not be used to buy personal electronic devices (such as laptops or cameras), to pay for alcohol, or for any travel unrelated to the fieldwork. Students who breach these rules will be required to repay the full amount and may lose eligibility for future research funding applications.

## Reimbursement Documentation Requirements

The **reimbursement process** begins once fieldwork ends. All documentation must be submitted to ANU Finance within 60 business days of returning to Canberra. Key supporting documents include: original receipts (paper or electronic), transport itineraries, accommodation bills, and a summary of the daily fieldwork log. Receipts must show the supplier's name, transaction date, description of goods, and amount, with amounts stated in Australian dollars or the foreign currency equivalent.

### Receipt Standards

Every single expense over A$50 must be supported by a formal receipt or invoice. Expenses below A$50 can be grouped and claimed via a Statutory Declaration, but the total value of such declarations in a single claim may not exceed A$200. In 2023, ANU Finance rejected about 8% of reimbursement claims, most often because receipts were illegible (52% of rejections) or supplier contact details were missing (31%).

### Foreign Currency Reimbursement

Foreign currency expenses must be converted to Australian dollars at the exchange rate published by the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) on the transaction date. Applicants must attach a screenshot of the exchange rate source (such as the RBA website or XE.com's daily rate) to the claim form. ANU Finance does not accept amounts calculated at estimated or historical average rates.

## Mid-Term and Final Reports

The **mid-term report** is a precondition for release of the second funding instalment and must be submitted halfway through the fieldwork. It should cover: a summary of research activities completed, a breakdown of expenditure to date, and the plan for the next phase. The mid-term report is limited to 1,000 to 1,500 words (excluding appendices) and must be reviewed and signed by the supervisor before being uploaded to the research portal.

### Final Report Requirements

The final report must be submitted within 90 business days of the fieldwork ending and run to 3,000 to 5,000 words, covering the complete research findings, a detailed statement of fund use, and selected excerpts from the fieldwork log. Students who fail to submit the final report on time are placed on the ANU research funding blacklist and cannot apply for any school-level research funding for two years. In 2023, 6 students were added to the list for overdue final reports (ANU School of Anthropology, 2024 Academic Board records).

### Report Assessment Criteria

Final reports are scored independently by two reviewers on: rigour of research methods (40%), reasonableness of fund use (30%), and transferability of outcomes (30%). Scores influence the priority students receive when applying for higher-value research funding in the future.

## Frequently Asked Questions and Appeals

The **appeal mechanism** applies when a funding application or reimbursement claim is rejected. Students have 14 business days from receiving the outcome notice to submit a written appeal to the ANU School of Anthropology Research Committee. The appeal must include the original application number, a point-by-point rebuttal of the rejection reasons, and supporting evidence. In 2023, 12 appeals were received, of which 5 were upheld in full or in part (ANU Research Office, 2024 appeals report).

### Appeal Processing Time

Appeal outcomes are usually announced within 20 business days of submission. Students dissatisfied with the outcome can lodge a final appeal with the ANU School of Graduate Research, whose decision is administratively final.

### Common Questions

Can funding be used to buy field recording equipment? Yes, but it must be listed separately in the budget and returned to the School's equipment store after use. Can additional funding be requested during fieldwork? Yes, but only through the Category B emergency supplementary funding channel, and you must provide evidence of unforeseeable expenditure. Does the funding include travel insurance? No — students must arrange their own insurance that complies with ANU's travel insurance policy.

## FAQ

### Q1: If my ANU anthropology fieldwork funding application is rejected, can I resubmit within the same year?

Yes. Students can resubmit a revised application in the next application round (that is, Round 2 after a Round 1 rejection, and vice versa). Note that the same research project can be applied for at most twice per year. 2023 data shows that the approval rate for resubmitted applications rose to 73% (ANU School of Anthropology, 2024 statistics).

### Q2: Can I still claim reimbursement if I lost a receipt during fieldwork?

Yes, under certain conditions. Lost receipts under A$50 can be replaced with a Statutory Declaration; lost receipts over A$50 require alternative evidence such as bank transaction records, a replacement receipt from the supplier, or a witness statement from a fellow researcher. In 2023, about 4% of claims included alternative evidence for lost receipts, and 89% of these were approved (ANU Finance Office, 2024 reimbursement audit report).

### Q3: Do I need to pay income tax on fieldwork funding?

No. ANU anthropology fieldwork funding is classified as research funding, and under the Australian Taxation Office's (ATO) 2024 Research Grant Tax Guide, funds used to cover direct research-related expenses are not taxable income. However, if funding is used for personal living costs (such as everyday meals), you should consult a tax professional about how it should be treated.
