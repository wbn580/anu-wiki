Course auditing (Audit) is an arrangement at the Australian National University (ANU) that allows students to attend lectures and discussions without participating in formal assessment. According to the compliance guidance for the Education Services for Overseas Students Act (ESOS Act) published by the Australian Department of Education in 2023, international students holding a student visa (Subclass 500) must maintain an enrolment that satisfies full-time study requirements; audited courses do not generate formal credit and therefore do not change a student's visa study load. Clause 7.3 of ANU's official 2024 Coursework Policy and Procedures manual states that audit applications require approval from the Course Convener and the College, and that auditors have no right to submit assignments, sit exams or receive grades. ANU ranked 34th globally in the 2024 QS World University Rankings; its audit system offers international students a flexible path for academic exploration, but because of visa compliance and assessment eligibility constraints, it must follow a specific process.

## Eligibility and visa restrictions

Before applying to audit, international students need to confirm whether their visa status permits it. According to the Department of Home Affairs' 2024 explanation of **student visa conditions 8105 and 8202**, international students must maintain enrolment that meets **full-time study requirements** (usually 24 units per semester). Audited courses earn no credit, so they cannot count towards the 24-unit minimum. If auditing causes a student's formally enrolled credit load to fall below the full-time threshold, they may breach their visa conditions.

### How visa load is calculated

ANU International Student Services clarified in its 2024 enrolment guide that auditing must be done **on top of formally enrolled credit**, and cannot replace any required or elective courses. For example, a student enrolled in 3 formal courses (18 units in total) who audits 1 course (0 units) still has a visa load of 18 units, which meets the full-time requirement. But a student enrolled in only 2 formal courses (12 units) who audits 1 course may be considered part-time for having insufficient credit, affecting visa compliance.

### Academic integrity and copyright restrictions

Auditors must follow ANU's academic integrity policies. Under ANU's 2023 Academic Integrity Rule, auditors must not copy or distribute course materials — including lecture recordings, handouts and reading lists — without written permission from the Course Convener. Violations may trigger an academic misconduct investigation and, in serious cases, termination of audit status.

## Application process and approval timeline

Audit applications are submitted through ANU's online system **ISIS (Interactive Student Information System)** together with the Course Convener's written consent. The whole process usually takes 5 to 10 working days, depending on course capacity and the speed of College approval.

### Step 1: Get the Course Convener's permission

Students should email the convener of the target course, explaining the purpose of auditing (such as building background knowledge or preparing for a future elective) and providing their student ID and current enrolment list. Under ANU's 2024 Course Audit Request Form, conveners may refuse for reasons including: the course is at capacity, the course includes laboratory or practical components (such as a chemistry lab course), or the course is a postgraduate seminar (open to formally enrolled students only).

### Step 2: College approval and registration

Once the convener agrees, students forward the permission email to the **Student Administration Office** of their College. The College verifies the student's visa status and credit load, then marks the course as "audit" mode in ISIS. After approval, the student can see the audited course in the "Course Timetable" section of ISIS, but it will not appear on the official transcript.

### Step 3: Audit confirmation notice

The College sends a confirmation notice to the student's ANU email, stating the valid semester, course code and restrictions. Students must log in to ISIS and confirm acceptance within **14 days** of receiving the notice, otherwise the application automatically lapses.

## Fee structure and exemptions

The fee policy for auditing varies by student status and course type. Under ANU's 2024 Fees and Charges Policy, international students auditing a course usually pay an **administration fee** rather than full tuition.

### Standard audit fees

For undergraduate courses (course codes starting with 1xxx–3xxx), the audit administration fee is **AUD 250** per course (2024 rate). Postgraduate courses (6xxx–8xxx) cost AUD 400. These fees are non-refundable and do not include course materials costs (such as lab coats or software licences). If the audited course includes a field trip, students pay the actual transport and accommodation costs incurred.

### Exemptions

Audit administration fees are waived in the following cases: the student is already enrolled in the course for credit but cannot participate in assessment due to a timetable clash (proof of the clash is required); the student is an ANU alumnus (applying to audit within 2 years of graduating); or the Course Convener proactively invites the student to audit for academic reasons (such as the need to catch up on prerequisite knowledge). Exemption requests must be submitted together with the audit request, with supporting documents.

## Assessment and grade restrictions

The core restriction of auditing is **no participation in any formal assessment**. Under clause 5.2 of ANU's 2024 Assessment Policy, auditors may not submit assignments, sit mid-semester or final exams, take part in graded group projects or receive grades of any kind (including pass/fail marks). Conveners may, at their discretion, allow auditors to join non-graded activities such as class discussion or group collaboration, but such participation produces no academic record.

### Transcript and credit impact

Audited courses do not appear on a student's **Academic Transcript** or the **Australian Higher Education Graduation Statement (AHEGS)**. This means auditing cannot be used to satisfy degree requirements, credit transfer, or prerequisite proof in postgraduate applications. Students who want a formal record of study should choose a credit-bearing course instead of auditing.

### Exception: converting an audit to formal enrolment

Before the end of Week 2 of the semester (before ANU's "Census Date"), students can apply to convert their audit status to formal enrolment, provided the course still has places and the student has met the prerequisite requirements. After conversion, the student pays the full international tuition for the course (averaging AUD 4,800 to AUD 6,200 per course for undergraduate courses in 2024) and must catch up on all outstanding assessment tasks. After the Census Date, audit status cannot be changed.

## Course types and how suitable they are for auditing

Not all ANU courses are open to auditing. According to the audit policies published by ANU Colleges in 2024, the following course types are generally not open to auditors: courses with laboratory or studio practice (such as the physics lab course PHYS1001 or the art studio course ARTV1001); postgraduate research courses (such as the thesis-writing course THES8001); and professionally accredited courses (such as LAWS6101, a core course of the Juris Doctor (JD)). In addition, any course that has reached capacity (with a closed waitlist) will automatically reject audit applications.

### Examples of courses well suited to auditing

Lecture-based courses in the College of Arts and Social Sciences are usually open to auditing, such as **ANTH1002 Introduction to Anthropology** or **POLS1005 Foundations of International Relations**. These courses are lecture-heavy and do not depend on lab equipment or group projects, making it easy for auditors to take part. Undergraduate courses in the College of Business and Economics (such as ECON1101 Microeconomics) also accept auditors, but note that this course includes a 1-hour weekly tutorial — auditors may attend but cannot take part in graded activities.

### Special rules for language courses

Academic English courses offered by the ANU College (such as the ANU Access Program) are not open to auditing, because their design includes intensive assessment and feedback components. International students who need to improve their language skills should enrol formally in an English course rather than audit one.

## How auditing can affect your degree progress

Although auditing earns no credit, it can affect a student's **study plan** and **graduation timeline**. Under ANU's 2024 Degree Rules, students may audit at most 2 courses per semester, and audited courses must not clash with formal courses. If auditing delays enrolment in formal courses, graduation may be postponed.

### Time management advice

International students typically need to spend 40–50 hours per week on formal coursework (including classes and self-study). Auditing 1 course adds 3–4 hours per week (lectures and reading). ANU's Academic Skills Centre, in its 2023 Time Management Guide, recommends that students assess their workload before the semester starts to avoid auditing hurting their GPA in formal courses. If your current GPA is below 4.0 (out of 7.0), auditing is not recommended.

### Things to note when renewing your visa

Audited courses earn no credit, so they cannot be used to demonstrate **academic progress**. When applying for a student visa renewal, the Department of Home Affairs requires official transcripts for the last 2 semesters — audit records are not included. If auditing leaves a student with insufficient formally enrolled credit, they may be found to have "not met course requirements", affecting visa approval.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I ask a professor for a reference letter after auditing their course?

Yes, but with caveats. Under ANU's 2024 Reference Letter Policy, professors may only write academic reference letters for students formally enrolled in a course (that is, those who participated in assessment and received a grade). Because auditors have no grade record, professors can usually only provide a "Letter of Participation" rather than a reference letter. If an auditor wants a reference letter, they should actively join class discussions during the audit and explain their need to the professor after the semester; the professor has full discretion over whether to write one. A 2023 ANU Careers Service survey found that around 15% of auditors successfully obtained informal reference letters from professors.

### Q2: Do I need to buy textbooks separately for an audited course?

Yes. Auditors must obtain the course materials themselves, including textbooks and readings. ANU's bookstore (The Co-op Bookshop) 2024 prices show that the average textbook cost for one undergraduate course is AUD 120 to AUD 200. Auditors do not get priority access to ANU Library's "Course Reserve" loans, but can borrow through the normal channel. If the course uses an online platform (such as e-readings on Wattle), auditors need the Course Convener's permission to access it.

### Q3: What happens if I breach my visa conditions while auditing?

If auditing drops your formally enrolled credit below the full-time standard (24 units per semester), you may breach visa condition 8202. According to Department of Home Affairs data for 2024, around 8% of student visa cancellations in 2023 for insufficient credit involved audited courses. If found in breach, you have 28 days to explain to the Department, or the visa may be cancelled. Before applying to audit, students are advised to check their status using ANU International Student Services' "visa load calculator".
