Summer average maximum temperatures in the Australian Capital Territory (ACT) can reach 28.4°C (Bureau of Meteorology 2023 climate statistics), and Canberra recorded an extreme high of 44.0°C in January 2020. For Australian National University (ANU) student accommodation, especially older buildings without central air conditioning (such as Bruce Hall and Burgmann College), indoor temperatures often exceed 30°C on summer afternoons. According to the ANU Accommodation Services 2023 student satisfaction survey, summer cooling is one of the three most common accommodation complaints. Based on ANU official policies, ACT Government energy-efficiency guidelines and the rental channels available on campus, this article systematically sets out the practical ways to cool a hall room and the information you need for fan rental.

## Hall Building Types and the Summer Thermal Environment

There are 17 residential buildings on the ANU campus, which fall into three categories by construction era: brick-and-concrete halls built in the 1960s-70s (such as Ursula Hall and John XXIII College), concrete slab buildings from the 1990s to the 2000s (such as Davey Lodge and Kinloch Lodge), and modern apartments delivered after 2015 (such as Wright Hall and Yukeembruk Village). **Older brick-and-concrete halls**, lacking insulation layers and double glazing, keep indoor summer temperatures only 2-3°C below outdoor levels, while **modern apartments**, through shading louvres and mechanical ventilation, can maintain a 5-7°C differential (ANU Facilities and Services, 2022, Building Thermal Performance Audit).

### Room Orientation and Sun Exposure

According to ANU Geographic Information System (GIS) data, north-facing hall rooms receive about 40% more solar radiation on summer afternoons than south-facing rooms. **West-facing rooms** (windows oriented 240°-300°) can run 3-5°C warmer than east-facing rooms between 2 pm and 5 pm. The ACT Government's Residential Energy Efficiency Guide (2021 edition) recommends curtains with a shading coefficient of ≥70% or external shading panels on west-facing windows to reduce the cooling load.

### Existing Cooling Equipment

The ANU accommodation contract explicitly **bans self-installed compressor air conditioners** (such as window units and split systems) in hall rooms, for reasons including building electrical load limits and outdoor unit installation requirements. As of 2024, only Wright Hall and Yukeembruk Village have central air conditioning in their common areas; all other hall rooms are limited to ceiling fans or wall-mounted fans. The Summer Cooling FAQ published by ANU Accommodation Services in 2023 states that students may bring or rent desk/tower fans that meet safety standards, with a maximum power rating of 150W.

## Fan Rental Channels and Costs

In Canberra, organisations offering fan rentals include the ANU Op Shop, sharing-economy platforms on and off campus, and seasonal group buys organised by some student clubs. **ANU Op Shop**, in the centre of campus (Building 24), offers fan rentals every year from November to February, with a AUD 20 deposit and weekly rates of AUD 5-8, covering three types: desk fans, tower fans and pedestal fans. In the 2023-2024 summer the shop rented out 187 units, of which tower fans accounted for 52% (ANU Op Shop, 2024, Annual Report).

### Online Rental Platforms

On Gumtree Canberra and Facebook Marketplace, fan rental/second-hand listings grow by about 300% month-on-month between November and January. **ANU Chinese Students and Scholars Association** (CSSA) organised its first fan group buy in December 2023, offering members 60 pedestal fans at AUD 25 each — sold out within 48 hours. For short-term exchange students (semesters of ≤6 months), renting is more cost-effective than buying: over a 12-week period, total rental cost is about AUD 60-96, while buying an equivalent new fan costs AUD 50-120 and you have to dispose of it yourself after moving out.

### Free On-Campus Borrowing Points

The **reception desks** of ANU halls usually keep a small number of spare fans for emergency borrowing, for a 24-hour loan period, with a student ID card held as deposit. According to a field survey in January 2024, the receptions at Burgmann College and John XXIII College each keep 5-8 fans, while Ursula Hall and Bruce Hall keep only 2-3. Students who need a fan long-term should call the reception in advance to check stock, or go straight to the ANU Op Shop to arrange a weekly rental.

## Passive Cooling Strategies and Behavioural Adjustments

Without mechanical refrigeration, the ACT Government's Home Cooling Guide for Summer (2022) recommends three passive cooling strategies: **cross ventilation**, **shading control** and **night purging**. ANU hall rooms usually have openable windows; if a room has two opposite windows (such as corner rooms), opening them creates a through-draught with indoor air speeds of 0.5-1.0 m/s, lowering the perceived temperature by 2-4°C.

### Curtains and Shading Options

**Blackout curtains** can cut radiant heat through windows by 60-80%. ANU Accommodation Services allows students to install removable curtain rods themselves, but bans nails or glue that damage the walls. The ACT Government energy-efficiency program (2023) offers curtain subsidies of up to AUD 200, applicable to students renting private housing — but ANU hall residents are not covered by this subsidy. Students can consider adhesive static-cling window film, which costs about AUD 15 per roll and can lower indoor temperatures by 1-2°C once installed.

### Night Ventilation

Canberra has large summer day-night temperature swings, with a January average minimum of 13.3°C (Bureau of Meteorology). The **night ventilation** strategy requires students to open windows after sunset (around 20:00) and close windows and curtains early the next morning (around 7:00), using the building's thermal mass to reduce daytime indoor peak temperatures by 3-5°C. Simulation research by the ANU College of Engineering (2021) shows that hall rooms that rigorously follow this strategy can keep indoor peak temperatures below 28°C even on 40°C extreme heat days.

## Electronic Cooling Aids

Besides conventional fans, some students use **evaporative coolers** as an alternative. These work by evaporating water to absorb heat, typically draw 50-80W — below the ANU 150W limit. However, average summer relative humidity in the ACT sits between 40-60% (Bureau of Meteorology), and evaporative coolers lose effectiveness significantly when humidity exceeds 50% (cooling by only 1-2°C), whereas in dry weather (humidity <30%) they can cool by 4-6°C.

### Ice Packs and Cold Water Circulation

**Ice packs** and **cold-water towels** are the lowest-cost localised cooling option. Placing an ice pack in front of a fan's air outlet lowers the output air temperature by 3-5°C, with the effect lasting about 30-60 minutes. Every ANU hall has a fridge in the shared kitchen, so students can freeze their own ice packs. Some students use USB-powered mini desk fans (5-10W) combined with ice packs to create a local microclimate over the desk area.

### Smart Plugs and Timers

**Smart plugs** can be programmed to control fan operating hours, avoiding all-night operation that causes noise disturbance. ANU hall power outlets are 240V/10A standard, and smart plugs must carry AS/NZS 3112 safety certification. For cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E5%AE%BF%E8%88%8D%E5%A4%8F%E5%AD%A3%E9%99%8D%E6%B8%A9%E5%BB%BA%E8%AE%AE%E4%B8%8E%E9%A3%8E%E6%89%87%E7%A7%9F%E8%B5%81%E4%BF%A1%E6%81%AF) to complete the settlement. Smart plugs cost about AUD 15-30 (available at Kmart and Bunnings) and can be set to switch the fan off automatically between 2 am and 6 am to cut electricity costs (hall electricity is usually included in the accommodation fee, but very high usage may trigger a reminder from the front desk).

## Safety Rules and the Risks of Non-Compliance

Clause 8.3 of the ANU Terms and Conditions of Residence (2024 edition) explicitly prohibits **daisy-chaining multiple high-power appliances on extension leads** and using **uncertified electrical appliances**. Fans must carry an Australian compliance mark (RCM or C-Tick), otherwise they may be confiscated during hall safety inspections. In the 2023-2024 summer, ANU Accommodation Services carried out 4 hall safety spot checks, finding 23 cases of improper extension lead use, 7 of which required immediate removal because the fan exceeded the power limit (>150W).

### Fire Risk and Insurance

The ACT Fire & Rescue 2023 report notes that summer fan fires account for 12% of residential electrical fires, mainly caused by motor overheating and damaged power cords. **ANU accommodation insurance** (included in the accommodation fee) covers property damage caused by appliance faults, but insurers may refuse claims if the student used uncertified equipment. Students are advised to check fan power cords monthly for wear and plugs for heat, and to keep a 30 cm clearance around fans with no obstructions.

### Noise Complaints and Neighbour Relations

Hall walls offer limited sound insulation, and **fan noise** (typically 40-60 dB) can disturb roommates or adjacent rooms at night. ANU Accommodation Services rules define 22:00 to 7:00 as quiet hours, and fan noise above 50 dB can be complained about. Low-noise fans (≤35 dB), such as Dyson tower fans or the MeacoFan range, cost AUD 150-400 and are rarely seen in the rental market. Students can place fans more than 1.5 metres from the bed, or use rubber pads to reduce vibration transmission.

## Summer Cooling Resources at a Glance

The ANU Accommodation Services website offers a **Summer Cooling Resource Pack**, including the ACT Government energy-efficiency guide PDF, a list of local fan rental providers and DIY shading tutorials. The pack is updated every year on 1 November and can be downloaded from the ANU Accommodation Portal. In addition, **ANU Student Central** (Building 12) sets up a cooling rest area in summer, with air conditioning and drinking water, open Monday to Friday 8:00-18:00.

### Campus Cooling Spaces

Besides the halls, several ANU campus buildings offer air-conditioned public areas: **Chifley Library** (open 24 hours, with air conditioning on levels 1 and 2), **Marie Reay Teaching Centre** (ground floor foyer, open until 22:00) and the **Kambri Precinct** (ANU bar and dining area, with air conditioning at some seats). Students can use these areas to study or rest on extreme heat days (≥38°C). ACT Government rules require all public libraries and community centres to open as cooling centres when temperatures exceed 40°C (ACT Emergency Services Agency, 2023, Heatwave Plan).

### Emergency Procedures

If a hall room stays above 35°C for more than 2 hours, students can contact the hall reception to request a **temporary relocation**. ANU Accommodation Services handled 34 heat-related relocation requests in 2023, of which 22 were approved, moving students to air-conditioned common lounges or vacant apartments. Applications must include room temperature records (using a phone app or a thermometer-hygrometer) and a completed Hall Environmental Issue Report Form (Form E-202).

## FAQ

### Q1: Does ANU allow students to install their own air conditioners in hall rooms?
No. The ANU accommodation terms explicitly prohibit installing compressor-based air conditioners (including window units and split systems); violators may face a AUD 200 fine and be required to restore the room to its original condition. Students may only use desk/tower fans of ≤150W or evaporative coolers.

### Q2: How much does it cost to rent a fan for one summer in Canberra?
Over a 12-week period, renting a tower fan from the ANU Op Shop costs about AUD 60-96 in total (plus a separate AUD 20 deposit). Buying a second-hand fan from Gumtree or Facebook Marketplace costs about AUD 30-60, but you must resell or dispose of it yourself after moving out.

### Q3: Do ANU halls have an emergency plan for extreme heat days (≥40°C)?
Yes. When temperatures exceed 40°C, students can request temporary relocation to air-conditioned common areas, or go to campus cooling spaces such as Chifley Library and Marie Reay Teaching Centre. ANU Accommodation Services activated 3 heat emergency responses in summer 2023, opening 2 reserve air-conditioned lounges.
