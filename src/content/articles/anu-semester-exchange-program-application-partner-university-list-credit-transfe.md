The Semester Exchange Program at the Australian National University (ANU) is one of the key channels through which its students gain international academic experience. According to the *Global Learning Annual Report* published by the ANU Global Programs Office in 2024, the university holds semester exchange agreements with more than 170 partner institutions across over 40 countries and regions, and around 600 to 800 students study overseas through the program each year. At the same time, the Australian Department of Education's 2023 *International Higher Education Mobility Report* found that students who took part in an exchange had full-time employment rates 12.4 percentage points higher within a year of graduating than those who did not. As competition in global higher education intensifies, understanding ANU's exchange application process, partner university selection, credit transfer mechanisms and funding options offers a direct reference point for planning both your academic path and your career.

## Partner University List and Eligibility

ANU's semester exchange partners span the major research universities of North America, Europe, Asia, Oceania and Latin America. According to the database updated by the ANU Global Programs Office in 2024, there are **174 partner institutions** in total: North America accounts for about 38%, Europe 35%, Asia 18% and other regions 9%. Applicants must be current ANU undergraduate or coursework postgraduate students who have completed at least one semester of full-time study.

**Academic requirements** are the core screening criterion. Undergraduates must maintain a weighted average (GPA) of at least 5.0/7.0, and some competitive destinations (such as US Ivy League universities and UK Russell Group members) require a GPA of 5.5 or 6.0 or above. Postgraduate applicants must also meet any additional standards set by their college. For language proficiency, if the host institution's language of instruction is not English, applicants must provide a test score in the relevant language — for example, DELF B2 in French or TestDaF level 4 in German.

**Application windows** run in two rounds. Round 1 (main round) closes on 28 February each year (for departure in Semester 2 of the same year) and 31 August (for departure in Semester 1 of the following year). Round 2 (supplementary round) opens after the first round closes, with limited places. Applications must be submitted through the ANU Global Programs Portal and include academic transcripts, a personal statement, two reference letters and a preliminary list of courses at the host institution.

## Credit Transfer Pre-Approval Process

Credit transfer is the core of the exchange experience, directly affecting your academic progress and graduation timeline. ANU operates a **pre-approval system** that requires students to complete course matching and credit assessment before departure. Under the *Credit Transfer Policy* published by the ANU Academic Board in 2023, each overseas course can be converted into up to 6 ANU units (equivalent to one standard semester course), and a maximum of 24 units can be transferred across the entire exchange.

**The pre-approval process** has three stages. First, students select courses from the host institution's catalogue that match their degree structure and complete the *Overseas Study Course Pre-Approval Form* (Online Study Plan). Second, the form goes to the Academic Advisor in your college for review; the advisor assesses whether the course content, teaching hours and assessment methods are equivalent to the corresponding ANU course. Third, the college records the outcome in the ANU student information system (ISIS), generating a formal credit transfer agreement.

**Common reasons for rejection** include: overseas course content matching the ANU course by less than 70%, the course being vocational rather than academic in nature, or the language of instruction not meeting the college's requirements. According to 2024 statistics from the ANU Global Programs Office, about 15% of pre-approval applications are returned for revision after the first submission, and the average processing time is 14 working days. Students are advised to start the pre-approval process at least 12 weeks before departure to allow plenty of time for adjustments.

## Funding and Scholarship Options

The cost of an exchange covers tuition, living expenses, travel and insurance. ANU uses a **tuition fee waiver model**: during the exchange, students pay their normal tuition to ANU only, with no extra tuition payable to the partner institution. Living expenses, return flights, accommodation and visa costs are borne by the student. According to ANU's 2023 Student Cost of Living Survey, the total living and travel costs for one semester of exchange (about 4 months) in North America or Western Europe come to roughly AUD 15,000 to 22,000.

**Scholarship resources** come mainly from three sources. First, the ANU Global Learning Scholarship is open to all exchange students, worth between AUD 3,000 and 8,000, with about 150 awards made each year; the application deadline coincides with the main round of exchange applications. Second, the Australian Government's Endeavour Leadership Program offers up to AUD 40,000 to high-achieving students, but it is highly competitive — only about 50 awards were made nationwide in 2023. Third, some partner institutions offer their own scholarships; for example, the National University of Singapore provides accommodation subsidies for ANU exchange students.

When paying cross-border tuition, some study-abroad families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-semester-exchange-program-application-partner-university-list-credit-transfe) to complete their remittances and avoid exchange-rate fluctuations and handling fees. In addition, students can apply for an emergency loan through the ANU Financial Aid Office of up to AUD 2,000, repayable within 6 months of the exchange ending.

## Visa and Insurance Requirements

**Student visa** is the legal prerequisite for the exchange to go ahead. Under the *Student Visa Conditions* issued by the Australian Department of Home Affairs in 2024, ANU students taking part in an exchange must hold a valid Australian student visa (Subclass 500) and confirm before departure that the visa's validity covers the entire exchange period. If the destination country requires a separate visa (such as the US J-1 visa or the UK Standard Visitor visa), students must apply on their own, with guidance documents provided by ANU International Student Services.

**Health insurance**: ANU requires all exchange students to purchase the compulsory medical insurance of the destination country. For example, students going to the US must buy insurance that meets J-1 visa requirements (with coverage of at least USD 50,000); students going to EU countries must hold a European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) or equivalent commercial cover. The ANU Global Programs Office provides a list of recommended insurers, with premiums ranging from AUD 300 to 800 per semester. Students without compliant insurance cannot complete pre-departure enrolment.

## Accommodation and Pre-Departure Preparation

**Accommodation options** vary by destination. Partner institutions usually offer on-campus residences or affiliated apartments, with application deadlines about 8 to 12 weeks before the semester starts. For example, the University of Edinburgh reserves a limited number of dormitory places for exchange students, and the 2023 application deadline was 16 weeks before departure. Students can also rent privately through external platforms (such as UniversityRooms or AIRBNB), but should pay attention to contract terms and deposit refund policies.

**The pre-departure checklist** includes: confirming that your credit transfer agreement is active in the ISIS system, booking return flights (flexible, changeable fares are recommended), obtaining an international driving permit translation (if you plan to rent a car), and attending ANU's cross-cultural training workshops. The ANU Global Programs Office runs at least 6 online pre-departure information sessions a year, covering safety briefings, differences in local laws and mental health support resources.

## Academic Experience and Differences in Assessment

**Assessment standards** during the exchange may differ significantly from ANU's. For example, US universities commonly use continuous assessment (with attendance, quizzes and essays each counting toward the final grade), whereas ANU places more weight on final exams. Students should read the course syllabus carefully before enrolling to understand the grading structure and academic integrity policies. The ANU Academic Skills Centre offers online resources to help exchange students adapt to different assessment modes.

**Course load requirements**: ANU requires exchange students to enrol in at least 12 ANU-equivalent units per semester (usually 3 to 4 overseas courses), but no more than 24 units. Some partner institutions (such as the University of Tokyo in Japan) run on a quarter system, so students need to make sure their course timetables do not clash. The ANU Global Programs Office recommends keeping at least 10 hours of self-study time per week to cope with the extra study load that comes with language and cultural adjustment.

## Credit Recognition and Grade Conversion After Return

**Credit recognition** is triggered automatically once the exchange ends. ANU usually converts the credits within 4 to 6 weeks of receiving the host institution's official transcript and records the grades in ISIS. Grades are converted using ANU's 7-point scale: percentage or letter grades from overseas institutions are mapped according to the *International Grade Conversion Table* published by the ANU Academic Board in 2023. For example, a US grade of A maps to 7 on the ANU scale, and B+ maps to 6.

**Transcript notation**: credits and grades earned during the exchange appear on the official ANU transcript, marked with the note "Overseas Study (Exchange)". For students planning postgraduate study or overseas employment, ANU offers a transcript endorsement service that can attach an explanatory letter about differences in overseas grading systems. A 2024 survey by the ANU Careers Centre found that about 68% of exchange students felt the experience counted in their favour in job interviews.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I appeal if my exchange application is rejected?

Yes. ANU allows applicants to submit a written appeal within 10 working days of receiving the rejection letter. Appeals must provide new evidence, such as updated transcripts, additional reference letters or proof that a place has opened up at the host institution. According to 2023 data from the ANU Global Programs Office, the appeal success rate is about 22%. Appeal materials must be submitted through the Global Programs Portal, with a processing time of 15 working days.

### Q2: What happens if I fail a course during the exchange?

If an overseas course is not passed, ANU will not award credit for it, and the grade is not counted toward your ANU GPA. You will need to retake the equivalent course after returning, or apply for credit substitution. ANU allows students to complete the make-up within 12 months of the exchange ending. If the failed course is a compulsory subject, your graduation may be delayed by about one semester. Students are advised to confirm substitution options with their Academic Advisor before enrolling in courses.

### Q3: Does the exchange program affect my graduation timeline?

Under normal circumstances, the exchange does not delay graduation, provided you successfully complete your pre-approved courses and convert at least 12 units of credit. However, if you enrol in fewer than 12 units or some courses are not recognised, you will need extra semesters to make up the shortfall. According to 2023 data from the ANU Academic Board, about 8% of exchange students had their graduation delayed by one semester due to credit transfer issues.
