The Australian National University (ANU) Acton campus, its main campus, covers approximately 145 hectares and is home to more than 15 cafes and light-food outlets. According to the official ANU Campus Services Annual Report 2023, campus cafes serve over 1.2 million customers a year, with about 40% of purchases made during the peak window between 9:00 and 11:00 am. For enrolled students aged 18 to 30, cafes are not merely places to get a caffeine fix — they are important spaces for independent study, group discussion and socialising. Drawing on publicly available campus floor plans, outlet operating data and the 2024 Student Satisfaction Survey (ANU Student Services, 2024), this article systematically reviews the spatial features, power provisions, noise levels and group suitability of each campus cafe, giving students a reference for choosing the right spot for different study scenarios.

## Main Campus Core: Union Court and the Kambri Precinct

As ANU's social and commercial hub, Union Court and Kambri are home to the campus's busiest cafes. Since the area's renovation was completed in 2019, a number of open-plan seating areas and power outlets have been added.

### **Little Brother, Kambri**

Little Brother sits on the ground floor of the Kambri Cultural Centre and is one of the **busiest** cafes on campus. It offers about 60 seats indoors plus roughly 40 outdoor seats in the adjoining plaza. Its **power outlet density** is high: around 70% of indoor seats have an Australian standard three-pin socket nearby. The 2024 student survey found the cafe's average noise level is 65 decibels — moderately high — which suits independent study that benefits from background white noise, but not scenarios that need quiet recording or online meetings. Opening hours are Monday to Friday 7:00 am to 4:00 pm; closed on weekends.

### **The Gods, Union Court**

The Gods is on the second floor of Union Court, next to the ANUSA student association offices. Its layout is dominated by **long tables and high stools**, with about 50 indoor seats and 30 balcony seats outside. Compared with Little Brother, its power outlet coverage is lower, at 55% of indoor seats. Peak hours run from 10:30 am to 1:30 pm, when seats turn over roughly once every 45 minutes. Free Wi-Fi is available, although connection stability occasionally dips during peak hours (ANU IT Services, 2024). It suits quick discussions or solo study sessions of 30 minutes to an hour.

## Hidden Coffee Stations Inside College Buildings

Beyond the main commercial precinct, a number of small coffee stations operate inside ANU's college buildings, usually run by ANU Hospitality or in partnership with local roasters. Seating is limited, but **noise levels** are significantly lower than in the main precinct, and opening hours are often longer.

### **Coffee Lab, CBE Building**

Coffee Lab is on the ground floor of the Crawford Building (CBE) and is the main gathering point for business and public policy students. It has only about 20 seats, but **power provision is comprehensive** — every seat has its own socket and USB port. The average noise level is 50 decibels, firmly in the quiet range. Opening hours are Monday to Friday 7:30 am to 3:30 pm. Because the space is compact, queues are common during exam periods (May and October each year), with an average wait of about 8 minutes (ANU Hospitality, 2024).

### **The Bookplate, Hancock Library**

The Bookplate is at the entrance on the ground floor of Hancock Library and is the only cafe on campus directly connected to library space. It offers about 30 seats, and its **outdoor courtyard** seating is very popular in fine weather. Power outlet coverage is 60% of indoor seats; the outdoor seats have no power. Opening hours are Monday to Friday 8:00 am to 5:00 pm, and Saturday 9:00 am to 2:00 pm. Because it sits next to the library's quiet zones, the cafe asks customers to keep conversations low indoors — a good fit for study sessions that need frequent access to library materials.

## Options in the Residential Precincts and Outlying Buildings

For students living in on-campus residences (such as Bruce Hall or Ursula College) or attending classes in outlying buildings (such as Fenner Hall or the School of Music), the cafes in the residential precincts and outlying buildings offer more convenient choices.

### **Fellows Coffee**

Fellows is on Fellows Road, near University House and Graduate House. Known for its **spacious interior**, it offers about 80 seats, making it one of the campus's largest independent cafes. Power outlet coverage is 65% of indoor seats, and several movable power boards are available. Its average noise level is 60 decibels, suitable for group discussions of 3 to 5 people. Opening hours are Monday to Friday 7:00 am to 3:30 pm; closed on weekends.

### **The Coffee Lab Express, Bruce Hall**

Coffee Lab Express is a satellite outlet of Coffee Lab in the Bruce Hall residential precinct. It has about 15 seats and mainly serves resident students. **Power outlets** are limited — only 8 of the indoor seats have sockets. Opening hours are Monday to Friday 8:00 am to 2:00 pm; closed on weekends. Because of its location close to the residences, students often use it as an informal study room in the evenings (after 6:00 pm) — but note that coffee service stops after 2:00 pm and only the seating remains available.

## Power and Wi-Fi: The Facilities That Decide Study Productivity

According to the ANU 2024 Student Space Usage Survey (ANU Student Services, 2024), power outlet availability and Wi-Fi stability are the two factors students weigh most heavily when choosing a cafe for study, accounting for 38% and 32% of the decision weight respectively.

### **Power Outlet Coverage Comparison**

Among the main campus cafes, **Little Brother** (Kambri) and **Coffee Lab** (CBE) have the highest power coverage, at 70% and 100% of indoor seats respectively. By contrast, **The Gods** (Union Court) and **The Bookplate** (Hancock) have lower coverage, at 55% and 60%. Students who need to use a laptop for long stretches should give priority to outlets with power coverage above 60%.

### **Wi-Fi Network Performance**

ANU campus Wi-Fi (eduroam and ANU-WiFi) provides good coverage across the main cafe areas, but during peak hours (11:00 am to 1:00 pm) latency in the **Kambri precinct** can rise from an average of 15 ms to 40 ms (ANU IT Services, 2024). Cafes inside college buildings (such as Coffee Lab) have more stable networks, with average latency staying below 12 ms. When paying tuition across borders, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E6%A0%A1%E5%9B%AD%E5%92%96%E5%95%A1%E5%BA%97%E8%AF%84%E6%B5%8B%E6%9C%80%E4%BD%B3%E5%AD%A6%E4%B9%A0%E4%B8%8E%E5%B0%8F%E7%BB%84%E8%AE%A8%E8%AE%BA%E5%9C%B0%E7%82%B9%E6%8E%A8%E8%8D%90) to complete the currency conversion.

## Group Discussion Spaces: Noise Levels and Seating Layouts

For students who frequently hold group discussions (such as business group projects or engineering team assignments), a cafe's noise level and seating layout directly affect how productive those discussions are.

### **Noise Level Tiers**

Based on 2024 field measurements (ANU Student Services, 2024), campus cafes fall into three noise tiers:
- **Quiet (≤50 dB)**: Coffee Lab (CBE), The Bookplate (indoor)
- **Moderate (51-65 dB)**: Little Brother (Kambri), Fellows, The Gods (off-peak)
- **Loud (>65 dB)**: Little Brother (peak hours), Union Court outdoor areas

### **Seating Layout Recommendations**

For group discussions of 2 to 4 people, **long tables and booth seating** work best. Both Fellows and The Gods offer this type of seating. For teams of more than 5 people, the outdoor plaza at Kambri is recommended — it can hold about 40 people and the tables and chairs can be rearranged freely. Note that all cafes prohibit the use of amplification equipment or loud discussions in indoor areas.

## Opening Hours and Seasonal Changes

Cafe opening hours differ significantly between the academic year and the holiday periods. According to the annual operations calendar published by ANU Hospitality in 2024, all cafes keep the standard hours above during teaching periods (February to June and July to November), but during the summer break from December to January about 60% of cafes shorten their hours or close.

### **Extended Hours During Exam Periods**

During the exam periods in May and October each year, **Little Brother** and **Fellows** extend their opening hours to 6:00 pm and add temporary seating. During the 2023 exam period, these two outlets served roughly 8,000 student visits in total (ANU Hospitality, 2023). Students are advised to arrive early during exam season to avoid the seating rush between 11:00 am and 2:00 pm.

## FAQ

### Q1: Which cafe on the ANU campus has the most power outlets?

Coffee Lab (CBE Building) has power outlet coverage of 100% of indoor seats, with a dedicated socket and USB port at every seat. Little Brother (Kambri) ranks second at 70% coverage. Students carrying laptops should prioritise these two outlets.

### Q2: What is the average spend at ANU campus cafes?

According to the 2024 ANU student satisfaction survey, a standard latte averages A$4.50 and a sandwich or wrap averages A$8.50. Prices at campus cafes are typically about 10% to 15% lower than comparable outlets in the city.

### Q3: Which cafes extend their hours during exam periods?

Little Brother (Kambri) and Fellows (Fellows Road) extend their opening hours to 6:00 pm during the May and October exam periods, operating 2 to 2.5 hours longer than usual. Other cafes keep their standard hours during exam season.
