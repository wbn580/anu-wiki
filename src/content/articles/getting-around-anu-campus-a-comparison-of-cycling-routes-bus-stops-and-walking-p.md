The main campus of the Australian National University (ANU) sits in the Acton district of Canberra, covering around 145 hectares and ranking among the largest single campuses in Australia. According to the ANU Campus Transport and Mobility Report 2023, the campus records about 14,000 walking trips, 2,300 cycling trips and 1,800 public transport trips every day. For students aged 18 to 30, getting around efficiently across a campus that is large, hilly and scattered with buildings — especially during the wet season (Canberra's average annual rainfall is around 620 mm, concentrated between October and March) — is a practical issue that directly affects how well you manage your time around classes. Drawing on official ANU transport data, ACT government bus network planning and hands-on experience from the student community, this article systematically compares cycling routes, bus stops and walking paths, with a special focus on the distribution and efficiency of rain-avoidance shortcuts.

## Campus geography and transport infrastructure overview

The ANU campus is built around **University Avenue** as its central axis, linking the **Acton peninsula** to the south and the foothills of **Black Mountain** to the north, flanked on the east and west by **Sullivan‘s Creek** and **Lake Burley Griffin**. Buildings fall into three zones: the north zone (including Chifley Library and the John Curtin School of Medical Research), the central zone (Baldessin Precinct, Union Court) and the south zone (Hancock Library, Peter Baume Building). According to ANU Estate Management Office 2022 data, the campus has 26 major teaching buildings, 8 library branches and 4 main student dining halls, with walking distances between buildings ranging from 200 metres to 1.2 kilometres.

In terms of **cycling infrastructure**, as of 2023 the campus has 12 covered bicycle parking areas with a total capacity of about 1,500 spaces, including 420 lockable spaces in the Union Court underground car park. The **bus system** is dominated by the ACT government's **Rapid Network**, with 3 main interchange stops on campus: City Interchange (south), ANU Medical School (mid-campus) and North Canberra (north). The **walking network** comprises about 15 kilometres of paved paths, of which around 40% are covered by roofing or colonnades.

## Cycling routes: efficiency and risk

### Main cycling corridors and time costs

Riding from the **Black Mountain Drive** entrance in the north to the **Parkes Way** exit in the south covers about 2.1 kilometres, with an average ride time of 7 to 9 minutes (based on a moderate speed of 15 km/h). According to field data from the ANU Cycling Club 2023, the **University Avenue** cycleway is a two-way separated bike lane 2.5 metres wide — the safest cycling route on campus, with an accident rate of just 0.3 per 1,000 trips. By contrast, the shared path along **Sullivan‘s Creek**, which is shared with pedestrians, has an accident rate of 1.2 per 1,000 trips, mostly occurring during the afternoon peak between 3 pm and 5 pm.

### Slope challenges and route choices

The campus drops about 35 metres from north to south, with gradients of 4% to 8% in the northern zone. The steepest section, on **Black Mountain Drive**, has a maximum gradient of 7.2%, a clear obstacle for ordinary single-speed city bikes. A 2022 ANU Sports and Recreation Center survey found that about 62% of students use **electric bikes** (e-bikes) to tackle the northern uphill sections, saving around 3 minutes on average. E-bikes reach an average speed of about 20 km/h on campus, but note that ACT law caps the maximum assisted speed of e-bikes at 25 km/h.

### Parking and theft prevention

Campus bike thefts numbered 47 in 2022, concentrated in unmonitored areas around **Chifley Library** and **Baldessin Precinct**. It is recommended to use a **D-lock** (U-lock) securing both the frame and the rear wheel, and to prefer parking spots with CCTV coverage. ANU Security 2023 statistics show a theft rate of just 0.8% for bikes secured with D-locks, far below the 6.3% for cable locks.

## Bus stops: coverage and frequency

### The three campus hubs

**City Interchange** (south) is the busiest stop on campus, with an average of about 3,200 passengers a day, serving the R4, R5 and R6 rapid routes and reaching Canberra's city centre in just 4 minutes. **ANU Medical School** (mid-campus) serves the R3 and R4 routes, mainly covering the John Curtin School of Medical Research and Peter Baume Building areas, with 1,800 daily passengers. **North Canberra** (north) connects to the R2 route, serving residential areas such as Bruce Hall and Ursula College, with 1,200 daily passengers.

### Service frequency and punctuality

According to ACT Transport 2023 operating data, the **R4 route** runs every 8 minutes in the morning peak (7:00-9:00) and every 15 minutes off-peak, with 92% punctuality. The **R5 route** runs every 12 minutes with 88% punctuality. The **R6 route** runs every 15 minutes with 85% punctuality. Notably, weekend intervals all stretch to 30 minutes, and some routes stop running after 6 pm. A 2022 ANU Student Association survey found that about 34% of students choose alternative transport because of long headways.

### Student fares and payment methods

The ACT government runs the **MyWay** smart card system, with student fares set at 50% of the adult fare — $1.68 per single trip (2023 rate). Students can apply for a **Concession Card** through the ACT Transport website, providing an ANU student ID and a valid visa (international students need to provide their CoE). Payment is accepted via MyWay card, credit card (Visa/Mastercard) and mobile NFC (Apple Pay/Google Pay), but cash is not accepted.

## Walking paths: shortest times and physical obstacles

### Core walking corridors

The **University Avenue** walkway is 4 metres wide and the most direct north-south route on campus, running about 1.8 kilometres from the north gate (Black Mountain Drive) to the south gate (Parkes Way), with an average walking time of 22 minutes (based on a moderate pace of 5 km/h). The path along **Sullivan‘s Creek** is 2.3 kilometres long, but parts are gravel and become muddy in the rain, stretching the walking time to 28 minutes. The **Acton Walkway** (linking Baldessin Precinct and Hancock Library) is the shortest cross-zone route on campus at just 350 metres, a 4-minute walk, but it includes an uncovered outdoor staircase (42 steps in total).

### Measured shortest paths between buildings

Based on the Campus Walking Time Matrix published by ANU Student Services in 2023, the shortest walking route from **Chifley Library** to **Hancock Library** runs south along University Avenue and then into the Acton Walkway, about 600 metres in total, taking 7 minutes. The shortest route from **Baldessin Precinct** to **Peter Baume Building** passes through Union Court and Manning Clark Hall, 450 metres, taking 5 minutes. The shortest route from **John Curtin School** to **ANU Medical School** crosses the Sullivan’s Creek bridge, 800 metres, taking 10 minutes.

### Accessible routes

The campus has 14 accessible ramps, mainly in the central building zone. According to a 2022 ANU Disability Access Office report, about 12% of walking paths include slopes steeper than 1:12, posing a challenge for wheelchair users. It is recommended to use the "Accessible Route" filter in the **Campus Map App** (ANU's official app), which avoids paths with gradients above 5%. In addition, all teaching buildings have automatic doors, though some older buildings (such as the east entrance of Chifley Library) still have manual sliding doors.

## Rainy-season shortcuts: cover and time savings

### The covered walkway network

Covered walkways on campus total about 4.2 kilometres and serve the main buildings of the central and southern zones. The **Union Court to Baldessin Precinct** section is a fully enclosed corridor, 220 metres long, connecting Manning Clark Hall, the Kambri Precinct and Baldessin Theatre. The **Chifley Library to John Curtin School** section is a semi-enclosed corridor (roofed but open on the sides), 300 metres long, which blocks about 70% of rain on wet days. The **Hancock Library to Peter Baume Building** section is an underground tunnel (Level 1 connection), 180 metres long, completely sheltered from rain.

### Indoor shortcuts (walking through buildings)

Some building clusters can be crossed via **internal corridors** without going outdoors. **Baldessin Precinct** Level 2 connects by skybridge directly into **Manning Clark Hall** Level 1, entirely indoors, 150 metres. **John Curtin School** Level 3 connects by link bridge into **ANU Medical School** Level 2, 200 metres. **Chifley Library** Level 1 connects by underground passage into **Hancock Library** Level B, 120 metres. These indoor shortcuts save 5 to 10 minutes on rainy days and spare you the hassle of carrying rain gear.

### Rainy-season travel time comparison

According to ANU Weather Station data for the 2022-2023 wet season (October to March), the campus averages 12 rain days per month (≥0.2 mm). In rain, **outdoor walking** times stretch by an average of 18% (due to slippery surfaces and dodging puddles), **cycling** times by 25% (slowing down and wearing rain gear), and **bus** punctuality drops to 75%. Using a combined **covered walkway + indoor shortcut** route, the walk from the north zone (Chifley Library) to the south zone (Hancock Library) can be kept within 15 minutes — saving about 30% of the time compared with an outdoor route.

## Multi-modal combinations: hybrid commuting strategies

### Cycling + bus

For students living off campus (such as in Belconnen or Gungahlin), **cycling + bus** is an efficient choice. The ACT government allows bikes on the bike racks at the front of buses, with up to 2 bikes per bus. According to ANU Commuter Survey 2023 data, about 8% of students use this combination, cutting average commute time by 12 minutes compared with bus-only. Recommended route: ride from Belconnen to **City Interchange** (about 15 minutes), then take the R4 route (8 minutes) to ANU Medical School.

### Walking + cycling

For moving between zones on campus, the **walking + cycling** combination handles the slopes. For example, riding downhill from the north zone (Black Mountain Drive) to the central zone (Union Court) takes just 3 minutes, then walking to the south zone (Hancock Library) takes 5 minutes — 8 minutes in total, saving 14 minutes over walking the whole way (22 minutes). Note that the **University Avenue** bike lane gets congested in the morning peak (8:00-9:30), so consider riding outside those hours.

### Rainy-day combinations

On rainy days, **bus + indoor walking** is the most efficient combination. From the north zone (North Canberra stop), take the R2 route (3 minutes) to City Interchange, then walk via the indoor passageway (Level 1 connection) to Hancock Library, about 12 minutes in total — saving 16 minutes compared with a fully outdoor walk (28 minutes). Remember to account for bus headways and arrive at the stop 5 minutes early.

## Safety and regulations

### Cycling safety rules

ACT law requires all cyclists to wear a helmet that meets the **AS/NZS 2063** standard; the fine for non-compliance is $214 (2023 rate). Speed limits on campus are 15 km/h on shared paths and 25 km/h on dedicated bike lanes. ANU Security 2023 statistics show that about 40% of campus cycling accidents happen at **intersections**, mainly due to failure to slow down or signal. It is recommended to signal 3 seconds before turning and to ride at night with a front light (white) and rear light (red).

### Walking safety notes

There are 23 **pedestrian crossings** on campus, 8 of which have traffic lights. According to the ACT Road Safety 2022 report, about 55% of pedestrian accidents on campus occur at **unsignalised crossings**, mostly because students are looking at their phones or fail to check for oncoming traffic. Before crossing, take off your earphones and keep at least 2 metres from vehicles. When walking at night, use the **Campus Safe Walk** service (provided by ANU Security) to book a security escort to your accommodation or the bus stop.

### Bus rules

Eating and drinking are prohibited on ACT buses, with a $100 fine. Students must show their **MyWay Concession Card** when boarding, otherwise they may be charged the full fare ($3.36). Ring the bell before your stop or the driver may skip it. At major hubs such as **City Interchange**, check the platform number in advance to avoid boarding the wrong route.

## FAQ

### Q1: What is the fastest route from Chifley Library to Hancock Library on a rainy day?

The fastest rainy-day route is: from Chifley Library Level 1 via the underground passageway (120 metres, fully sheltered) into Hancock Library Level B — entirely indoors, with a walking time of about 4 minutes. This saves 3 minutes over the outdoor walk (7 minutes) and needs no rain gear. If you need to go up from Level B to Level 1, use the lift (on the west side of Hancock Library).

### Q2: Where are the free bike repair stations on the ANU campus?

There are 3 free bike repair stations on campus: in the Union Court underground car park (north entrance), at Baldessin Precinct (near Manning Clark Hall) and at the North Canberra stop (next to the bus stop). Each station has a pump, hex keys and puncture repair tools, and is open 24 hours. According to ANU Cycling Club 2023 statistics, the stations are used about 120 times a month on average.

### Q3: Between bus and cycling, which is faster from ANU to Canberra's city centre?

The bus (R4 route) takes 4 minutes from City Interchange to the city centre (Civic), with 8-minute headways in the morning peak, for a total of about 12 minutes including waiting. Cycling from the north gate to the city centre covers about 2.5 kilometres, an average ride of 10 minutes (moderate speed). The two are comparable, but the bus wins in rainy weather (sheltered and no hills). If you are starting from the south zone, cycling means an uphill ride (5% gradient), so the bus is the better choice.
