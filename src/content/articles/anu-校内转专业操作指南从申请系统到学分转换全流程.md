Around **12%** of Australian National University (ANU) undergraduates apply to change their major or degree program within their first year of study, a proportion drawn from the university's internal statistics in the 2023 ANU Student Experience Survey (SES). An internal transfer is when a registered student applies to move to another degree program or specialisation within ANU, as distinct from transferring to a different institution or reapplying for admission. Under ANU's 2024 Admission and Course Policy, transfer applications must be submitted through the ISIS system, and the application deadline is usually four weeks before the start of each semester. This guide systematically walks through how internal transfers work at ANU — the operational process, approval criteria, credit transfer rules, and key dates — drawing on official ANU Student Central pages, the 2024 ANU Academic Calendar, and the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA) 2023 annual report.

## Eligibility and Prerequisites

**Internal transfers at ANU** are not open to all students unconditionally. Under the 2024 ANU Admission Policy (§3.2), applicants must satisfy three basic conditions: be currently enrolled and active in an ANU degree program; have completed at least 24 units of ANU courses (i.e., one full-time semester); and hold a current GPA of at least 4.0 (out of 7.0). Some highly competitive programs (such as actuarial studies, law, and medicine) set additional thresholds, typically requiring a GPA of 5.5 or higher.

### Program Restrictions

Some degree programs impose a **transfer freeze period**. For example, the Bachelor of Medicine and Surgery (MChD), under the 2024 policy, explicitly does not accept internal transfers from current students; applicants must reapply through the UCAT ANZ exam【ANU 2024 Medical Program Admissions Guide】. The Bachelor of Laws (LLB), meanwhile, requires transfer applicants to have completed at least 48 units of undergraduate coursework with a GPA of at least 5.0. ANU's 2023 Student Handbook lists all restricted programs and is updated every academic year.

### Additional Requirements for International Students

International students holding a Student visa (Subclass 500) should take extra care: a transfer can affect visa compliance. Under the Department of Home Affairs' 2024 Student Visa Conditions Guide, if the new program differs from the original in Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) level (for example, moving from a bachelor's degree to a diploma), you must apply for a new visa. ANU's International Student Office advises confirming with Student Central that the new program sits at the same AQF level before submitting an application.

## The Application System and How to Apply

**Internal transfers at ANU** are mainly processed through ISIS (Interactive Student Information System). ISIS is ANU's core student information platform, covering course enrolment, grade queries, transfer applications, and all other academic administration functions. According to the ANU 2024 ISIS User Guide, the path is: log in to ISIS → select "Program & Courses" → click "Internal Transfer Request".

### Application Windows

ANU runs two main application windows each academic year: the first-semester transfer deadline is **15 February** (for entry in February of the same year); the second-semester deadline is **15 July**. The 2024 ANU Academic Calendar shows that the 2024 first-semester window opened on 1 November 2023 and closed on 15 February 2024; the second-semester window opened on 1 May 2024 and closed on 15 July. Applications submitted after the deadline are automatically rolled over to the next semester.

### Required Documents

Applicants must upload the following documents in ISIS: a current transcript (auto-populated from ISIS); a personal statement of no more than 500 words explaining the reason for the transfer; and one academic reference letter (signed by a current supervisor or course convenor). Some programs, such as the Bachelor of International Relations (BIR), require an additional writing sample of 1,000–1,500 words. ANU's 2024 Internal Transfer Application Guide stresses that incomplete applications will not be considered.

## Approval Criteria and the Decision Process

**Transfer approvals at ANU** are decided by the Admissions Committee of the target degree, based on four core indicators: the applicant's current GPA (weighted at about 50%); performance in relevant courses (for economics applications, for example, microeconomics and macroeconomics grades carry more weight); the quality of the personal statement; and the content of the reference letter. According to the ANU 2023 Admissions Committee Operations Manual, the review typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.

### Acceptance Rates for Competitive Programs

Acceptance rates for high-demand programs are significantly below the average. In 2023, for example, the transfer acceptance rate for the ANU Bachelor of Actuarial Studies was about **18%**, while the Bachelor of Computer Science stood at 32%【ANU 2023 Internal Transfer Annual Report】. Low-competition programs such as the Bachelor of Anthropology had acceptance rates above 70%. ANU does not publish acceptance rates for every program, but Student Central can provide historical reference data.

### Appeals After a Rejection

If your application is rejected, you may submit an appeal through ISIS within 14 business days of receiving the notice. Appeals must include new evidence (such as additional transcripts or reference letters). Under the 2024 policy, ANU's Appeals Committee accepts only one appeal per semester, and the outcome is final.

## Credit Transfer Rules

**Credit transfer** is the most complex part of changing programs. ANU uses a credit exemption system, under which the new program recognises and transfers part of the credit already earned in your previous courses. Under ANU's 2024 Credit Transfer Policy (§4.1), the cap is **75%** of the credit completed in the original degree, and transferred credit cannot exceed 50% of the new degree's total credit (i.e., at most 48 units can move from the original program to the new one).

### Course Matching Assessment

ANU assesses credit using a **course content similarity** standard. If at least 70% of the content of a previous course overlaps with a course in the new program's Course Outline, it can be exempted directly. For example, when transferring from the Bachelor of Commerce to the Bachelor of Economics, micro and macroeconomics courses are usually exempted; when transferring from engineering to computer science, mathematics courses (such as calculus and linear algebra) can transfer, but engineering-specific courses (such as fluid mechanics) are not recognised. The assessment is carried out by the course convenor of the target program and takes 2 to 4 weeks.

### Handling Credit That Does Not Transfer

Credit that is not transferred (usually program-specific required courses) goes into your **free elective credit** pool. ANU stipulates that free elective credit is capped at 24 units in each bachelor's degree. If untransferred credit exceeds this cap, the surplus is treated as invalid and you must complete the required courses of the new program. This rule is detailed in Chapter 8 of the 2024 Student Handbook.

## Timeline Planning and Key Dates

**The ANU Academic Calendar** directly shapes how transfers work. The 2024 ANU Academic Calendar shows that Teaching Period 1 runs from 19 February to 24 May, and Teaching Period 2 from 22 July to 25 October. Once a transfer is approved, students must adjust their course enrolment before the start of the next semester.

### Course Enrolment Adjustment Window

Students with an approved transfer must complete their new course enrolment in ISIS **within the two weeks before the semester starts**. Those who miss this window can only enrol in courses with remaining places, which may delay their study plan. ANU's 2024 Course Enrolment Policy recommends planning your study schedule with an Academic Advisor in advance.

### Timing Considerations for Your Visa

International students must update their CoE (Confirmation of Enrolment) with the Department of Home Affairs within 30 days of approval. ANU's International Student Office stresses in its 2024 Visa Compliance Guide that if the new program's duration differs from the original by more than 6 months, you must reapply before your visa expires. It is recommended to contact Student Central immediately after approval to obtain an updated CoE.

## Common Questions and Practical Advice

**Alternative paths after a failed transfer** include applying for a double degree or a minor as a stepping stone. ANU's 2024 Double Degree Policy allows students to add a second major within their current degree without reapplying for a transfer. Students can also take prerequisite courses for the target program during the Summer Session or Winter Session to strengthen their next application.

### Cross-Disciplinary Transfer Strategies

When moving from the humanities and social sciences into science and engineering, mathematics is usually the critical gap. ANU's Mathematical Sciences Institute offers **free bridging courses** that start each January and July, run for 6 weeks, and award mathematics foundation credit to students who pass. 2023 data shows that students who completed a bridging course improved their transfer success rate by about 40%【ANU 2023 Bridging Course Outcomes Report】.

## FAQ

### Q1: If my transfer application is rejected, how soon can I apply again?

ANU policy requires a wait of at least one semester (6 months) before reapplying. The 2024 Admission Policy is explicit that repeated applications within the same semester are automatically rejected by the system. It is advisable to use the waiting period to raise your GPA or complete prerequisite courses for the target program; 2023 data shows that second-time applicants succeed at about 1.8 times the rate of first-time applicants.

### Q2: What happens to tuition I have already paid after a transfer?

ANU charges tuition by credit unit. After a transfer is approved, the unused portion of the tuition paid for your original program can be moved to your new program's account. Under ANU's 2024 Tuition Policy, if the new program's tuition is lower, the difference is refunded to your bank account within 30 business days; if it is higher, you must pay the difference before the semester starts. Refunds for international students incur an additional A$100 processing fee.

### Q3: Will a transfer affect my scholarship eligibility?

It depends on the type of scholarship. ANU internal scholarships (such as the ANU Chancellor's International Scholarship) generally require continuous enrolment in the same degree program. Under the 2024 Scholarship Terms and Conditions, scholarship eligibility automatically lapses after a transfer unless the new program is approved in writing by the Scholarship Committee. For external scholarships (such as Australian Government scholarships), you must apply separately to the awarding body to have the award transferred.
