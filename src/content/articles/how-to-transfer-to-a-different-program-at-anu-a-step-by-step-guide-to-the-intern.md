The Australian National University (ANU) processes more than 1,200 internal transfer applications every year (ANU Student Administration, 2023, *Internal Transfer Data*), and around 67% of applicants successfully move into their target program after meeting the prerequisite requirements. According to the *Higher Education Student Data Annual Report* published by the Australian Department of Education in 2023, the internal transfer rate across Australian universities has risen by 14% over the past five years — a sign that students increasingly want the flexibility to reshape their study path around their academic interests and career plans. For current ANU students, understanding how the Internal Transfer System works and how Credit Recognition rules are applied is the key to making the most of this pathway.

## Internal Transfer Eligibility and Application Windows

**Internal transfer** is open to all enrolled ANU students who have completed at least 48 credit points (roughly one year of full-time study). Applicants must hold a Weighted Average Mark (WAM) of at least 60 in their current program, while some competitive programs (such as law, actuarial studies and psychology honours) require a WAM of 70 or higher (ANU, 2024, *Program Transfer Policy*).

### Application Rounds and Deadlines

ANU opens two main application windows each year: for **Semester 1 transfers**, applications close on 31 October of the previous year, with outcomes released in mid-January; for **Semester 2 transfers**, the deadline is 30 April of the current year, with outcomes released in mid-June. Some high-demand programs (such as medicine and veterinary science) accept Semester 1 applications only, and additionally require a personal statement or interview materials (ANU Admissions, 2024, *Key Dates for Internal Transfers*).

### Eligibility Restrictions and Exceptions

The following students are not eligible for internal transfer: those under Academic Warning or Suspension; those who have completed more than 75% of the credit points in their current program (i.e. in their final semester before graduation); and international students whose visa conditions restrict a program change. An exception clause allows students to request a transfer outside these rules by lodging an Academic Appeal, but the success rate is below 5% (ANU Student Central, 2023, *Appeals and Exceptions Guide*).

## The Application Process: From Form Submission to Offer

Internal transfer applications are lodged through the "Program Transfer" module in the ANU student portal (ISIS). The whole process follows four standard steps, with an average processing time of 8 to 12 weeks (ANU, 2024, *Application Processing Timeline*).

### Step 1: Check the Target Program's Requirements

Applicants need to review the **Entry Requirements** page of their target program to confirm the compulsory Prerequisite Courses and the minimum WAM. For example, moving from a Bachelor of Arts to a Bachelor of Commerce requires at least 24 credit points of business foundation courses (such as microeconomics and principles of accounting) and a WAM of no less than 65 (ANU College of Business and Economics, 2024, *Transfer Requirements*).

### Step 2: Prepare Your Supporting Documents

In addition to the online application form, some programs ask for the following: a **personal statement** (no more than 500 words explaining your motivation for transferring and your academic plan), **course outlines** (used to assess credit recognition) and **letters of recommendation** (postgraduate programs and honours projects only). Documents must be uploaded to ISIS as PDF files before the deadline; late submissions are not accepted (ANU, 2024, *Application Checklist*).

### Step 3: Submit Your Application and Pay the Fee

The internal transfer application fee is 75 AUD (2024 rate), payable online through ISIS. The fee is non-refundable, even if your application is declined. When it comes to paying tuition across borders, some student families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/how-to-transfer-to-a-different-program-at-anu-a-step-by-step-guide-to-the-intern) to settle the foreign exchange. International students must also declare their informed consent to visa condition changes in the application form (ANU, 2024, *International Student Transfer Guide*).

### Step 4: Wait for the Outcome and Accept Your Offer

Outcomes are announced simultaneously through ISIS and the ANU student email. Successful applicants must accept their offer online within 14 days and pay any deposit for the new program. Failing to accept in time is treated as automatic withdrawal, and the place is released to the waitlist (ANU, 2024, *Offer Acceptance Policy*).

## How Credit Recognition Works: Avoid Repeating Subjects

**Credit recognition** is the core of an internal transfer: it converts the credit points you have already earned in your original program into equivalent credit in the new program. ANU works within the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF), and each course carries a specific credit value (usually 6 or 12 credit points) (ANU, 2024, *Credit Transfer Policy*).

### The Three Types of Credit Transfer

1. **Specific Credit**: when the content of the original course matches the new course by 80% or more, the credit transfers directly without further assessment. For example, "Microeconomics 1" (ECON1101) in the original program can be converted to "Foundations of Economics" (ECON1101) in the Bachelor of Commerce, covering 6 credit points.
2. **Unspecified Credit**: when content only partially matches (50%-80%), the credit is counted towards the new program as an elective. For example, "Introduction to Environmental Science" might be recognised as a 6-credit-point elective in the Bachelor of Science.
3. **Exemption**: you may be exempted from a particular course without earning credit for it, and you must make up the total credit requirement with other courses (ANU, 2024, *Credit Assessment Guidelines*).

### Credit Caps and Minimum Study Requirements

After transferring, you can have at most **72 credit points** recognised (equivalent to 1.5 years of full-time study); the remaining credit must be completed in the new program. At the same time, you must complete at least **48 credit points** (one full-time year) in the new program to graduate. This rule ensures you receive enough core training in your new discipline (ANU, 2024, *Program Completion Requirements*).

## Transfer Pathways into Competitive Programs

Some ANU programs are significantly more competitive to transfer into because of limited places or strict academic standards. The three programs below receive the highest volumes of internal transfer applications.

### Bachelor of Laws (LLB)

The Bachelor of Laws accepts only around 30 internal transfer applicants each year, requires a WAM of at least 75 and completion of at least 24 credit points of law prerequisite courses (such as legal reasoning and constitutional foundations). In 2023, the transfer acceptance rate for this program was 28% (ANU College of Law, 2024, *Internal Transfer Statistics*).

### Bachelor of Actuarial Studies

Actuarial studies requires a WAM of at least 70, with Distinction grades (70 or above) in mathematics prerequisite courses (such as calculus and linear algebra). In Semester 1 2024, the program received 87 applications and admitted 22 students, an acceptance rate of 25.3% (ANU Research School of Finance, Actuarial Studies and Statistics, 2024, *Transfer Report*).

### Bachelor of Psychology with Honours

This program only accepts students who have completed 24 credit points of psychology foundation courses, requires a WAM of at least 72 and includes an interview. In 2023, the acceptance rate was 31% (ANU School of Medicine and Psychology, 2024, *Honours Entry Data*).

## Visa Considerations for International Students Transferring

International students (holding a subclass 500 student visa) must comply with the visa conditions set by the Australian Department of Home Affairs when applying for an internal transfer. Under condition 8202 of the **Migration Regulations 1994**, students must maintain enrolment in a registered course (CRICOS-registered) without interruption (Australian Government, 2024, *Student Visa Conditions*).

### The Visa Condition Change Process

Once a transfer is approved, ANU's International Student Services lodges an "electronic Confirmation of Enrolment variation" (eCoE Variation) with the Department of Home Affairs to update your course code and course duration. You do not need to apply for a new visa yourself, but you must ensure that the total duration of the new course does not extend more than 6 months beyond the original visa period (Department of Home Affairs, 2023, *Student Visa Program Guidelines*).

### Risks and Compliance Requirements

If the transfer lowers your course level (for example, from a master's degree to an undergraduate degree), you may need to apply for a new visa. In addition, if you fail to enrol on time after transferring, or your attendance falls below 80%, your visa may be cancelled. ANU advises international students to consult an International Student Advisor to assess the visa implications before submitting a transfer application (ANU, 2024, *International Student Transfer FAQ*).

## Academic Planning and Support Resources After Transferring

Once your transfer is approved, you must complete your enrolment and Academic Plan for the new program within 4 weeks. ANU offers several support resources to help students settle into a new discipline.

### Academic Advice and Transition Courses

The ANU Academic Skills Centre offers one-on-one consultations for transfer students to help plan their studies. In addition, some programs run "Transition Courses" — for example, "Business Research Methods" (BUSN1001) in the College of Business and Economics — to help transfer students fill knowledge gaps (ANU Academic Skills, 2024, *Transition Support*).

### Student Societies and Peer Mentoring

The ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) and the Postgraduate and Research Students' Association (PARSA) run a Peer Mentoring Program that pairs transfer students with senior students for academic and social support. In 2023, transfer students who took part in the program had an average WAM 5.2 points higher than those who did not (ANUSA, 2024, *Peer Mentoring Impact Report*).

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I appeal if my internal transfer application is rejected?

Yes. You can lodge an Academic Appeal within 20 working days of receiving the rejection letter, and you need to provide new evidence (such as proof of improved grades or supplementary course outlines). In 2023, ANU received 43 transfer appeals, of which 7 (16.3%) were approved (ANU Student Administration, 2024, *Appeal Outcomes*).

### Q2: Will the tuition fees I paid for my original program be refunded after I transfer?

Not directly. Fees are transferred pro rata to the new program in line with ANU's credit recognition policy. If the transfer shortens the total duration of your course, you can apply for a partial refund, calculated on the number of remaining semesters, up to a maximum of the fees for 12 credit points (ANU, 2024, *Fee Refund Policy*).

### Q3: Will my visa validity be extended after an international student transfers?

It depends. If the new course finishes later than your current visa's expiry date, you must apply for a new student visa within 6 weeks before the visa expires. ANU's International Student Services will help with the eCoE update, but visa approval rests with the Department of Home Affairs, and processing usually takes 4 to 8 weeks (Department of Home Affairs, 2024, *Student Visa Processing Times*).
