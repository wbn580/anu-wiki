Getting an Australian driver's licence is an important step for many international students at the Australian National University (ANU) to settle into local life and get around more easily. As of 2024, road rules and the licence application process in the Australian Capital Territory (ACT) differ significantly from other states, and international students need to pay particular attention. According to the report Australian Vehicle Registration and Driver Licensing National Framework by the Australian Department of Infrastructure, Transport, Regional Development, Communications and the Arts (2023), international students holding a valid overseas licence can drive legally in the ACT for up to 6 months, after which they must apply for an ACT licence. In addition, data from the ACT road and transport authority (Access Canberra) shows that in the 2022-2023 financial year, more than 15,000 new driver licences were issued in the ACT, with around 12% of applicants holding an overseas background. This article systematically walks ANU international students through the complete process — from the knowledge test and the driving test to complying with ACT road rules — providing a practical guide for students planning to drive in Canberra long-term.

## Eligibility and overseas licence conversion rules

Before applying for an ACT licence, international students first need to confirm whether their overseas licence is eligible for conversion. The ACT road and transport authority divides overseas licence holders into two categories: **recognised countries** and **non-recognised countries**. Under the ACT Government's (2024) Overseas Driver Licence Conversion Guide, holders of licences from recognised countries — including the United Kingdom, Japan and Singapore — can exchange them directly for an ACT licence without taking a driving test, while holders from non-recognised countries (including China) must pass both the knowledge test and the driving test.

**Students holding a valid overseas licence** can drive legally for the first 6 months after arriving in the ACT using the original licence plus an official English translation (provided by a NAATI-accredited translator). Once the 6 months are up, driving without converting to an ACT licence counts as unlicensed driving, with a maximum fine of $2,200 (ACT Road Transport Act 1999, section 31). For holders of Chinese licences, note that a mainland China licence cannot be exchanged directly for an ACT licence — you must start from scratch and sit all the tests.

**Identity and residency documents** are prerequisites for applying. Students need their passport, visa (usually the Subclass 500 student visa), proof of ACT address (such as a bank statement or lease) and a birth certificate or passport-sized photo. Access Canberra requires original documents or certified copies, and non-English documents must be accompanied by a NAATI-accredited translation.

## The knowledge test: theory preparation and exam process

The knowledge test is the first hurdle in the ACT licence process, assessing applicants' understanding of **ACT road rules**, traffic signs and driving safety. The exam content is based on the Road Rules Handbook and covers topics such as speed limits, give-way rules, alcohol limits and parking rules.

**Exam format**: a computerised multiple-choice test of 45 questions; you need 35 correct answers (i.e. 78%) to pass. The test is available in Simplified Chinese, but the translation may contain subtle inaccuracies, so it is worth consulting the English handbook as well. Access Canberra (2024) data shows a first-attempt pass rate of about 62% for international students on the knowledge test, below the 78% for local applicants, with most marks lost on ACT-specific roundabout rules and speed limit changes.

**Study resources** include free online practice tests on the Access Canberra website (with a question bank of 200+ questions) and the official printed Road Rules Handbook (available free from Access Canberra service centres or libraries). ANU's on-campus transport clubs (such as the ANU Cycling and Driving Society) occasionally organise preparation workshops — keep an eye on their semester activity calendar. The knowledge test costs $46.70 (2024 rate) and can be booked at any Access Canberra service centre or designated post office.

## The driving test: booking and the practical assessment

After passing the knowledge test, applicants can book the driving test. ACT driving tests are conducted by Access Canberra-accredited examiners and last about 35-45 minutes, assessing **vehicle control**, **observation skills**, **decision-making** and **compliance with road rules**. Test routes typically cover residential areas, main roads, roundabouts and school zones to simulate real driving conditions.

**Booking and fees**: book through the Access Canberra online system or by phone (13 22 81); the test costs $61.10 (2024 rate). Because Canberra test slots are in high demand, book at least 4-6 weeks in advance, especially during end-of-semester peaks (June and November). If you fail, you must wait at least 7 days before rebooking, and each retake costs the full fee.

**Vehicle requirements**: you must supply the test vehicle, which has to pass Access Canberra's vehicle safety inspection (lights, brakes, tyres, number plates, etc.) and display L plates — even if you hold an overseas licence, this rule applies during the test. ANU students can hire instructor cars from on-campus or nearby driving schools (such as Canberra Driving School, roughly $80-120 per hour), and some schools offer test-car packages.

**Common reasons for failing**: according to Access Canberra's (2023) Driving Test Annual Statistics, the most common failure reasons for international students are failing to give way correctly at roundabouts (32%), speeding or driving too slowly (28%) and failing to check blind spots (22%). Before the test, aim for at least 5-10 hours of local driving practice to get used to Canberra-specific road designs (such as multi-lane roundabouts and give-way rules at T-junctions).

## Key ACT road rules

ACT road rules are based on the Australian Road Rules but have several local differences. ANU international students should master the following rules to avoid fines and accidents.

**Speed limits**: ACT school zones have a 40 km/h limit, in force only during the posted hours (usually 8:00-9:30 am and 2:30-4:00 pm); breaching it carries a fine of up to $353 and 3 demerit points (ACT Road Transport Act 1999, section 20). Main roads in Canberra's city area are usually 60 km/h, while freeways (such as the Tuggeranong Parkway) are 90 km/h.

**Roundabout rules**: the ACT has more roundabouts than anywhere else in Australia. The rule is that vehicles entering a roundabout must **give way** to all traffic already in it (including cyclists). This differs from the "give way to the left" rule in some countries (such as the United States), so pay extra attention. Failing to give way is the most common infringement type among international students.

**Alcohol and drug limits**: drivers on a learner licence (L plates) or provisional licence (P plates) must have a zero blood alcohol concentration (BAC). Fully licensed drivers have a BAC limit of 0.05%. ACT Police conduct regular random breath tests; offenders face heavy fines and licence suspension.

**Mobile phone use**: the ACT bans holding a mobile phone while driving (including when stopped at a red light); offenders face a $487 fine and 3 demerit points. Hands-free calls via Bluetooth or the car's built-in system are allowed, but only for drivers who already hold a full licence.

## L plates and P plates: the graduated licensing system

The ACT uses a graduated licensing system. International students first get a **learner licence (L plates)** after passing the knowledge test, then upgrade to a **provisional licence (P plates)** after passing the driving test, and finally earn a full licence.

**The L-plate stage**: L-plate drivers must be supervised by someone holding a full licence and must display L-plate signs on the front and rear of the vehicle. This stage lasts at least 6 months (reducible to 3 months if you are 25 or older), during which you must complete at least 50 hours of logged driving (including 10 hours of night driving). ANU students can record their hours with in-car logbook apps such as Logbook Buddy.

**The P-plate stage**: after passing the driving test you receive a provisional licence (P1 or P2). The P1 licence (red P plates) applies to drivers under 25, lasts 12 months, and prohibits driving high-performance vehicles (such as V8-engined models); the BAC must be zero. The P2 licence (green P plates) applies to drivers aged 25 or over or those who have completed the P1 stage, lasts 24 months, after which you can apply for a full licence. P-plate drivers are limited to 90 km/h across the ACT and may not use phones (including hands-free).

**Special exemption for international students**: if you hold a full overseas licence with more than 3 years of driving experience, you can apply directly for an ACT full licence after passing the knowledge test and the driving test, without going through the L-plate and P-plate stages. You will need to provide the original licence and proof of driving history (a Chinese licence requires a NAATI translation and notarised proof of driving experience).

## Fee summary and timeline planning

The ACT licence process involves several fees, so international students should budget in advance. The following are the official Access Canberra rates for 2024:

- Knowledge test: $46.70
- Learner licence (L plates) issue fee: $53.00
- Driving test: $61.10 (per attempt)
- Provisional licence (P plates) issue fee: $62.50
- Full licence (5-year): $79.00

**Additional costs**: NAATI translations cost around $30-50 per document, and driving instructor lessons run about $60-120 per hour (if you need extra practice). Set aside a total budget of at least $400-600 (covering one knowledge test and one driving test).

**Timeline planning**: going from scratch to a full licence typically takes about 3-6 months (including the learning stage). Aim to sit the knowledge test within the first month of arriving in Canberra and the driving test in months 2-3. If you need the L-plate stage, the total stretches to 6-9 months. When paying cross-border tuition, some student families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/how-to-get-an-australian-drivers-licence-as-an-anu-international-student-knowled) to complete the remittance, freeing up funds for local expenses such as the licence application.

## Common challenges and how to handle them

International students often face the following challenges during the licence process, so prepare in advance.

**Language barriers**: both the knowledge test and the driving test can be taken in English, and Access Canberra offers the theory test in Chinese. If your English is not strong, take the Chinese version, but memorise English traffic signs (such as "Give Way" and "Stop"), because the examiner gives instructions entirely in English during the driving test. You can also join ANU English for Driving (a free workshop run by the ANU Academic Skills centre) to build relevant vocabulary.

**Different driving habits**: holders of Chinese licences need to adapt to driving on the left with right-hand drive. Before the actual test, get at least 10 hours of local driving practice, focusing on roundabouts, right turns (the mirror image of left turns back home) and lane changes. Some driving schools (such as Canberra Driving School) offer courses tailored to international students for about $70-90 per hour.

**Paperwork**: make sure all documents (passport, visa, proof of address, NAATI translation) are ready before applying. Access Canberra accepts electronic visas (VEVO online check), but print a backup anyway. Proof of address must be a bill dated within the last 3 months; ANU students can use the residence certificate issued by the university (such as an On-Campus Accommodation Letter).

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I use my Chinese licence in the ACT directly, and how long is it valid?

Holders of a Chinese licence can drive legally for the first 6 months after arriving in the ACT, using the original licence plus a NAATI-accredited translation. After 6 months, you must convert to an ACT licence, or you will be considered unlicensed. The conversion requires passing the knowledge test and the driving test — a direct exchange is not possible.

### Q2: Is the ACT knowledge test available in Chinese, and what is the pass rate?

Yes, the ACT knowledge test is available in Simplified Chinese. According to Access Canberra (2024) data, the first-attempt pass rate for international students is about 62%, below the 78% for local applicants. Prepare with the English handbook as well, because the Chinese translation may contain terminology inaccuracies.

### Q3: If I fail the driving test, how long must I wait to retake it?

After failing the driving test, you must wait at least 7 days before rebooking. Each retake costs $61.10. Before retaking, do at least 5 hours of extra practice and run targeted drills on the reasons for your failure (such as giving way at roundabouts and checking blind spots).
