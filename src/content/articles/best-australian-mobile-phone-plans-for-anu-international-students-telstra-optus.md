As of 2024, the Australian National University (ANU) has more than 10,400 international students on campus — around 38% of the total student population [Australian Government Department of Education, 2024, 'International Student Data']. For new ANU students arriving in Canberra, picking a SIM card with solid campus coverage and good value is the first step to settling into local life. Australia's three big carriers — Telstra, Optus and Vodafone — differ significantly in network coverage and pricing around the ANU campus and its surroundings, which directly affects daily communication, navigation and study efficiency. Drawing on the Australian Communications and Media Authority's (ACMA) annual reports and the carriers' published coverage maps, this article provides international students with an objective, data-driven guide to choosing a plan.

## Campus Coverage Comparison of the Big Three

ANU's main Acton Campus lies west of Canberra's city centre, covering around 145 hectares of dense teaching buildings, libraries and student accommodation. The three carriers' signal quality in this area is by no means equal.

**Telstra** claims 4G/5G coverage of more than 99.5% across the ANU campus according to its official coverage map, with signal stability superior to competitors in underground or steel-heavy areas such as the Coombs Building and Hancock Library [Telstra, 2024, 'Mobile Coverage Map']. Its low-band 700 MHz network penetrates buildings better, which helps inside older teaching blocks.

**Optus** covers roughly 98% of open campus areas, but signal can drop indoors in the Chifley Library basement levels and some student residences (such as Wright Hall). Optus has deployed more mid-band 5G sites around Canberra's city centre, and its outdoor download speeds often beat Telstra's.

**Vodafone** covers the campus core (Union Court, Kambri Precinct) well, but has dead zones at the campus edges (such as the sports fields along Sullivans Creek) and in some basements. With lower user density, its network is noticeably less congested than the other two during peak times (e.g. 12 noon to 2 pm).

## Prepaid Plans for International Students

All three carriers offer Prepaid plans aimed at short-term visitors and new arrivals, with no long-term contract — a good fit for semester-based students.

**Telstra Prepaid** starts at $30 per month and includes 30 GB of data plus unlimited domestic calls and texts. Its biggest draw is the nationwide Telstra Air Wi-Fi hotspot network, which connects automatically at places like the ANU library and Canberra Airport, saving mobile data. Plans run on 28-day cycles and support auto-renewal.

**Optus Prepaid**'s equivalent is $25 per month (25 GB data) — cheaper, but with a smaller data allowance. Optus offers a 'Data Bank' feature that rolls unused data into the next cycle, up to a 200 GB cap. For students whose monthly usage fluctuates, this avoids waste.

**Vodafone Prepaid**'s entry plan costs $20 per month (20 GB data), the lowest starting price of the three. Vodafone also offers 'Family & Friends', which allows unlimited international calls to 5 selected numbers, covering major source countries such as China, India and Indonesia. Median measured download speeds on campus are about 35 Mbps, slightly below Telstra's 45 Mbps [OpenSignal, 2024, 'Australia Mobile Network Experience Report'].

## Long-Term Contracts and Number Porting

For students planning to stay in Australia for more than 12 months, Postpaid plans generally offer a lower cost per GB.

**Telstra** postpaid plans require your passport, visa and an Australian bank account, with the entry plan at $49 per month (80 GB data) on 12- or 24-month contracts. Early termination attracts a break fee of 50% of the remaining monthly charges.

**Optus** postpaid plans start at $45 per month (60 GB data) and include a free 'Optus Sport' subscription with live Premier League coverage. Its mobile number porting (MNP) process can be completed online and takes about 15 minutes on average.

**Vodafone** postpaid plans start at $40 per month (40 GB data), and the 'Red Plus' add-on lets you add 10 GB of overseas roaming data for $5 per month — handy for students heading home for holidays or travelling to New Zealand. Number porting requires no outstanding balance on the old account and your service password.

For cross-border tuition payments, some international student families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/best-australian-mobile-phone-plans-for-anu-international-students-telstra-optus) to complete their currency exchange, avoiding extra costs from exchange rate fluctuations or bank fees.

## Mobile Virtual Network Operators (MVNOs) as an Alternative

Beyond the big three, Australia has more than 20 mobile virtual network operators (MVNOs) that lease network infrastructure from Telstra or Optus and resell it at lower prices.

**Boost Mobile** is the only MVNO with full access to Telstra's network, including the low-band 700 MHz coverage. Its prepaid plans start at $30 per month (30 GB data), and real-world speeds are identical to Telstra's own brand — the right choice for students who demand the best coverage.

**Amaysim** rides on the Optus network, offering plans from $20 per month (25 GB data) with eSIM activation, so there's no waiting for a physical SIM to arrive by post. Its customer service includes a Chinese-language phone line, which is friendly to newcomers who aren't confident in English.

**Aldi Mobile** also uses the Telstra network but throttles speeds to a maximum of 100 Mbps (Telstra's own brand can exceed 200 Mbps). Its $25-per-month plan (22 GB data) includes unlimited international SMS, suiting students who rely mainly on instant messaging apps like WeChat.

## Using Campus Wi-Fi Alongside Your Phone Plan

ANU provides eduroam, the global education roaming Wi-Fi service, to all enrolled students, covering every teaching building, library and most public areas. Once connected to eduroam, students can use campus resources for free without consuming mobile data.

**Data-saving strategy**: on campus, set your phone to prefer eduroam Wi-Fi and use mobile data only while on the move. Assuming 8 hours on campus per day, this strategy saves roughly 10-15 GB of mobile data per month, letting you pick a cheaper plan tier.

**Residential broadband**: ANU on-campus residences (such as Bruce Hall and Lena Karmel Lodge) typically provide wired Ethernet ports and their own Wi-Fi, though speeds vary by building. A 2023 report from ANU IT Services shows average download speeds of 50 Mbps and uploads of 20 Mbps in residential areas [ANU IT Services, 2023, 'Residential Network Performance Report']. There's no need to buy a separate mobile broadband router for your room.

## International Roaming and Managing Time Back Home

During semester breaks (June-July and November-February), international students often travel home or to third countries, and the roaming policy of your plan directly affects costs.

**Telstra** prepaid plans don't support automatic international roaming: students must buy a 'Travel Pass' before departure, at $5 per day, to use the data in their existing plan across 40 countries and regions including New Zealand and Southeast Asia. Postpaid customers can use the 'Telstra Plus' roaming pack at $10 per day, covering mainland China and Hong Kong.

**Optus** prepaid plans support roaming in selected countries (including China, Japan and South Korea) at $5 per day, throttled to 3G speeds. Postpaid customers can roam at 4G speeds for $10 per day through 'Optus Roaming'.

**Vodafone**'s 'Red Plus' roaming add-on ($5 per month) works in 15 countries including New Zealand, Indonesia and India, with a 10 GB allowance that drops to 256 kbps once exceeded. For students who travel frequently between China and Australia, Vodafone's roaming package offers the best value.

## A Step-by-Step Plan-Choosing Guide

Based on the analysis above, ANU international students can follow a three-step process when choosing a plan:

**Step 1: Check coverage at your address.** Use each carrier's coverage map and enter the postcode of your residence (Acton is 2601, City is 2600) to see indoor signal predictions. Telstra's map offers 'Street Level' and 'Indoor' modes; the latter is more accurate.

**Step 2: Estimate your monthly data usage.** Calculate what you need based on eduroam habits, social media frequency and video watching. ANU student surveys put the average monthly usage at 18 GB [ANU Student Survey, 2023, 'Technology Usage Report']. If you use less than 20 GB, Vodafone or an MVNO is more economical; above 30 GB, a Telstra or Optus postpaid plan is better value.

**Step 3: Choose eSIM or physical SIM.** Since 2024, all three major carriers and mainstream MVNOs support eSIM activation, so students can set up their account via an app before arriving in Australia and have the network activate automatically on landing. Physical SIMs are bought at airports, supermarkets or carrier stores and take about 24 hours to activate.

## FAQ

### Q1: Which carrier has the best signal on the ANU campus?

Telstra's indoor coverage on the ANU campus exceeds 99.5%, performing best in library basements and residential halls [Telstra, 2024, 'Mobile Coverage Map']. Optus is faster outdoors but occasionally drops signal indoors. Vodafone works well in the core areas but has dead zones at the edges.

### Q2: Can international students skip contracts and just use prepaid?

Yes. Australian law allows student visa holders to use prepaid plans without a credit history or long-term contract. 2024 data show that about 65% of ANU international students choose prepaid [Unilink Education, 2024, 'International Student Telecom Survey']. Prepaid cards can be topped up via BPAY or credit card without affecting your visa status.

### Q3: How can I keep my Australian number while back home?

Switch to Vodafone's 'Red Plus' roaming pack ($5 per month), or use Optus's 'Keep Your Number' service ($3 per month, keeping your number without data). Telstra doesn't offer a parking service, so you'd need to keep paying the minimum monthly fee. Keeping your number matters for receiving bank verification codes and tax notices.
