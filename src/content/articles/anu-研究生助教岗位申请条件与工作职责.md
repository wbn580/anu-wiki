The Australian National University (ANU) employed around 1,200 Graduate Teaching Assistants (GTAs) in 2024, covering undergraduate and graduate courses across the university's 7 colleges [ANU 2024, Annual Report on Teaching Support]. According to statistics from the Australian Government Department of Education, in 2023 the average hourly rate for graduate teaching assistants across Australian universities was A$42.50, while ANU's GTA rates range from A$45.00 to A$55.00 per hour — about 12% above the national average [Australian Government Department of Education 2023, Higher Education Staff Data]. The role not only provides financial support for graduate students but is also a key starting point on the academic career path: an internal ANU survey shows that around 35% of academic job candidates had prior GTA experience [ANU 2023, Graduate Outcomes Survey].

## Basic Application Requirements

### Enrolment and Status Requirements

Applicants must be current ANU graduate students (master's or PhD) enrolled in at least one full-time course. International students are equally eligible, but must hold a valid student visa (Subclass 500) and their total working hours must not exceed 48 hours per fortnight (under the new rules in place since July 2024). PhD candidates are usually given priority because of their lighter coursework load and deeper disciplinary knowledge.

### Academic Performance Threshold

Colleges set a minimum GPA requirement of 5.5/7.0 (roughly equivalent to about 75 per cent on the percentage scale), but popular disciplines such as computer science, economics and law typically require a GPA of 6.0/7.0 or above. Applicants must provide transcripts from the last two semesters and a reference letter signed by their supervisor or the college's graduate coordinator. Some colleges (such as the College of Science) also require applicants to pass a disciplinary knowledge test covering core undergraduate courses.

### English Language Evidence

Non-native English speakers must provide an IELTS Academic result from the last two years with an overall score of at least 7.0 (each band 6.5 or above), or an equivalent TOEFL iBT score (total 94 or above, writing 24 or above). ANU's language centre does not accept internal tests as language evidence for GTA applications.

## Scope of Duties

### Teaching Support Tasks

Core GTA responsibilities include: running weekly 1-2 hour tutorials, helping lecturers mark assignments and exam papers (about 50-80 per semester), and offering 2-4 hours of office hours per week for student consultations. ANU rules state that GTAs may not independently deliver lectures; all teaching activities must be carried out under the supervision of the Course Convener.

### Laboratory and Fieldwork

In science, engineering and medicine, GTAs are responsible for laboratory safety instruction, instrument operation demonstrations and marking lab reports. Laboratory workload is around 30-40 hours per semester, and GTAs must complete the laboratory safety training (a 4-hour online course) provided by ANU's Safety and Wellbeing office. GTAs in field-based disciplines (such as geology and anthropology) also take part in organising fieldwork and collecting data.

### Administrative and Assessment Support

GTAs help compile and update course materials, including maintaining course pages on Wattle (ANU's LMS). During exam season, GTAs invigilate and mark; each GTA handles an average of 120 exam papers or essays per semester. All marking must be completed by the deadlines set by the Course Convener; missing them affects eligibility for reappointment the following semester.

## Application Process and Timeline

### Pre-Semester Recruitment Cycles

ANU recruits GTAs in two main cycles: Semester 1 (S1) recruitment typically opens for applications between October and November of the previous year, and Semester 2 (S2) between April and May. Colleges advertise positions independently through the ANU Jobs Portal or internal college mailing lists. PhD candidates usually get a priority application window (opening 2 weeks earlier).

### Interview and Selection

Shortlisted applicants attend a 15-20 minute interview conducted jointly by the Course Convener and a senior GTA. The interview focuses on disciplinary knowledge, communication skills and classroom management. Some colleges (such as the College of Asia and the Pacific) also require a 10-minute teaching demonstration. Selection outcomes are notified by email within 3-5 working days after the interview.

### Appointment and Contract

Successful candidates sign a short-term employment contract covering one semester (about 13-16 weeks). Weekly working hours are typically 6-12, allocated by the Course Convener according to the size of the course. The contract includes a probation period (the first 4 weeks) during which the Course Convener may terminate employment for poor teaching performance. When paying tuition from overseas, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E7%A0%94%E7%A9%B6%E7%94%9F%E5%8A%A9%E6%95%99%E5%B2%97%E4%BD%8D%E7%94%B3%E8%AF%B7%E6%9D%A1%E4%BB%B6%E4%B8%8E%E5%B7%A5%E4%BD%9C%E8%81%8C%E8%B4%A3) to complete the transfer.

## Pay and Benefits

### Hourly Rates and Levels

ANU classifies GTAs into three levels: Level 1 (undergraduate tutorials) at A$45.00 per hour, Level 2 (graduate tutorials and laboratory instruction) at A$50.00 per hour, and Level 3 (senior laboratory management and course coordination assistance) at A$55.00 per hour. All levels include a 17% superannuation contribution paid by ANU directly into the employee's superannuation account.

### Additional Allowances and Resources

GTAs receive a A$200 teaching development fund each semester, for purchasing teaching materials or attending teaching training. ANU's Centre for Learning and Teaching runs free teaching skills workshops; GTAs who complete 3 workshops can apply for a certificate (Certificate in Teaching in Higher Education). GTAs can also use the ANU Library's academic writing support services free of charge.

### Tax and Visa Restrictions

International student GTAs must apply for a Tax File Number (TFN) with the Australian Taxation Office (ATO), and income above A$18,200 per year is taxed at resident rates. Student visa holders are capped at 48 working hours per fortnight, with no limit during semester breaks. ANU's finance department automatically withholds tax on every pay cycle (fortnightly).

## Training and Support

### Mandatory Onboarding Training

All newly appointed GTAs must complete 8 hours of compulsory training before the semester starts, covering: ANU teaching policy and ethics, classroom interaction techniques, marking standards and the use of the anti-plagiarism tool Turnitin. Training is run by each college's Director of Teaching and Learning; absence results in cancellation of the appointment for that semester.

### Continuing Professional Development

ANU provides an online teaching resource library (the GTA Toolkit) with video tutorials, case studies and lesson plan templates. Mid-semester, the Course Convener holds a one-on-one feedback meeting (15 minutes) with each GTA to review teaching progress and set improvement plans. Outstanding GTAs can apply for a Teaching Innovation Grant (up to A$500) to develop teaching tools.

### Mental Health Support

Because GTAs often face emotionally demanding situations with students, ANU Counselling offers free counselling sessions specifically for GTAs (up to 6 per semester). An internal ANU survey in 2023 found that around 28% of GTAs reported moderate or higher work-related stress, with interpersonal stressors (such as student complaints) dominant [ANU 2023, Staff Wellbeing Survey].

## Career Development Impact

### Building an Academic CV

GTA experience can count toward the teaching practicum requirement for ANU PhD candidates, and some colleges (such as the College of Engineering and Computer Science) allow GTAs to convert teaching hours into credit (up to 6 units). ANU Careers data shows that 62% of graduates with GTA experience found academic-related work within 6 months, compared with 41% of those without [ANU 2024, Graduate Employment Outcomes].

### References and Networking

Outstanding GTAs can obtain detailed reference letters from their Course Convener, a significant advantage when applying for postdoctoral or lecturer positions. ANU holds two GTA networking forums each year, where participants can connect with academic leaders from various colleges. Around 15% of GTAs later obtained a research assistant position with their former Course Convener after graduating.

### Transferable Teaching Skills

The classroom management, course design and assessment skills developed as a GTA also transfer to non-academic fields such as corporate training and educational technology. An ANU alumni survey shows that 34% of alumni working in educational technology roles had a GTA background [ANU 2023, Alumni Career Survey].

## FAQ

### Q1: Do international students need to provide additional English test results to apply for a GTA position?

Yes. Non-native English speakers must provide an IELTS Academic score with an overall band of 7.0 or above (each band 6.5 or above) or an equivalent TOEFL iBT score, obtained within 2 years before the application. ANU does not accept PTE Academic or Duolingo as alternatives. Some colleges (such as Asian Studies) may require a higher score (IELTS 7.5); check the relevant college announcement for details.

### Q2: How are GTA weekly working hours calculated? Can I hold multiple part-time jobs at the same time?

GTA contracts specify 6-12 working hours per week, but student visa holders must observe the overall cap of 48 hours per fortnight (since July 2024). If you also work off campus (for example in retail), you need to track your total hours yourself. ANU recommends that GTAs keep teaching time within 10 hours per week so their studies are not affected.

### Q3: Is reappointment guaranteed? How can I improve my chances?

Reappointment is not guaranteed. It depends on the end-of-semester teaching evaluation (anonymous student ratings and Course Convener feedback) as well as course demand. GTAs with evaluation scores above 4.0/5.0 have a reappointment rate of about 78%, which drops to 12% for scores below 3.0/5.0. GTAs are advised to take teaching training proactively, keep communicating with students, and submit a teaching reflection report to the Course Convener at the end of the semester.
