The field trial system for the Australian National University (ANU) agricultural science program is part of the core teaching facilities of the School of Agriculture, Food and Wine, covering a total of about **50 hectares** across two sites: the Acton main campus and the Ginninderra Experiment Station. According to the ANU 2023 official course handbook, Bachelor of Agricultural Science students must, from the second semester of their first year, complete at least **8** field-based practical courses related to the trial fields, totalling no fewer than 96 hours. This arrangement responds directly to the warning about a "practical skills gap" in the *Agricultural Workforce Assessment Report* published by the Australian Bureau of Agricultural and Resource Economics (ABARES) in 2022 — the report noted that Australia's agricultural industry will need an additional 17,000 graduates with field operation experience by 2030. Field trial visits and practical activities are not just a credit requirement; they are also a key teaching component in ANU's response to the National Agricultural Productivity Strategy 2022-2030.

## Field trial locations and facility configuration

ANU's agricultural science trial fields are divided into two core areas: the **Acton campus teaching farm** and the **Ginninderra Experiment Station**. The Acton campus farm covers about **12 hectares** in the south-west corner of the campus, within walking distance of the main teaching buildings, and mainly hosts first- and second-year foundation courses in crop science, soil science and plant pathology. The site is equipped with 4 automatic weather stations, 2 drip irrigation systems and an insect-proof net house covering 0.5 hectares, with data collected every 15 minutes and uploaded in real time to the ANU agricultural data platform (AgData@ANU).

The Ginninderra Experiment Station, about 10 km north of Canberra, covers **38 hectares** and is a long-term agro-ecological research base operated jointly by ANU and the Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation (CSIRO). The station has been part of ANU's teaching system since 2018, hosting field trial design, precision agriculture technology applications and climate change adaptation research for advanced courses (third year to honours year). The station includes a **3-hectare** no-till trial area, a drone-based multispectral remote sensing system (collecting data every two weeks), and a mobile rain shelter facility for simulating drought stress.

### Soil types and trial zones

The Acton campus farm's soils are predominantly **Red Ferrosol**, with a pH range of 5.8 to 6.4, well suited to rotation trials of wheat, barley and legume crops. The Ginninderra Experiment Station includes comparison zones across **three soil types**: shallow calcareous soil (pH 7.2–7.8), deep alluvial soil (pH 6.0–6.5) and sandy luvisol (pH 5.2–5.6). This diversity lets students run soil–crop interaction comparison experiments in a single location.

### Irrigation and drainage systems

Both trial sites use **precision irrigation systems**. The Acton campus uses a subsurface drip irrigation (SDI) system with one flow sensor buried at the root zone of each plant, achieving 92% irrigation efficiency. Ginninderra is equipped with a centre-pivot sprinkler (covering 8 hectares) and a surface drip system (covering 5 hectares); students can adjust irrigation parameters in real time via the "IrriApp" mobile application developed by ANU. System records show total water use of 423,000 litres for 2022.

## Field practical activities in the undergraduate program

The ANU Bachelor of Agricultural Science curriculum requires students to complete **24 courses** within **three years**, of which **8** include mandatory field trial practical modules. According to the 2023 course outlines, these practical courses total 192 hours, about 11% of the degree's total 144 credit points. Each practical course typically includes **2 to 3** field sessions, each lasting 3 to 4 hours, scheduled between weeks 3 and 12 of the semester.

### List of compulsory practical courses

The following **compulsory courses with field trial modules** are explicitly listed in the 2023 course handbook:

- AGRD1001 Introduction to Agricultural Systems (Semester 1): includes 2 field trial visits, focusing on crop phenology identification and soil profile observation
- AGRD2002 Crop Physiology (Semester 2): includes 3 field measurements, including leaf area index (LAI) determination and photosynthetic rate recording
- AGRD2005 Soil Science and Management (Semester 1): includes 2 soil sampling and physicochemical analysis sessions, requiring a 2,000-word field report
- AGRD3001 Field Crop Production (Semester 1): includes 3 full sowing-to-harvest cycles; students independently manage the planting of a 0.1-hectare plot
- AGRD3004 Agroecology (Semester 2): includes 2 insect population surveys and natural enemy release experiments

### Assessment criteria for practical courses

Field trial practical modules usually account for **20% to 35%** of the final course grade. Using AGRD3001 as an example, the weighting is: field operation logbook (10%), crop growth data analysis report (10%), final yield measurement (5%) and group oral presentation (5%). All practical courses require students to submit an electronic observation log within **48 hours** of each field activity; late submissions incur a 10% mark deduction.

### Safety and equipment requirements

All students taking part in field trial activities must complete **ANU field safety training** (an online module of about 2 hours) and pass a closed-book test (pass mark 80%). The training covers sun protection and heat stress prevention, farm machinery safety, pesticide exposure protection and emergency evacuation procedures. The university requires students to wear long trousers, closed-toe shoes and a wide-brimmed hat in the field, and to carry at least **1 litre** of drinking water. First-aid kits and emergency communication equipment are provided at the trial field entrances, and each activity is staffed by at least 1 instructor holding a "wilderness first aid certificate".

## Field trial use in advanced courses and research projects

For third-year students and above, field trial use moves from "visits and operations" to **independent design and management**. In honours and master's research projects, students complete a full field trial cycle under supervisor guidance, including experimental design, plot layout, data collection, statistical analysis and thesis writing. According to ANU research ethics committee records for 2022, the agricultural science discipline approves an average of **15 to 20** undergraduate research projects involving the trial fields each year.

### Example honours field project

In 2022, an honours student completed a field trial titled "Effects of different irrigation strategies on chickpea yield and water use efficiency in the Canberra region". The trial was conducted at the Ginninderra Experiment Station using **12 plots of 5 m x 5 m**, with 3 irrigation treatments (full irrigation, deficit irrigation and a rainfed control), each treatment replicated 4 times. The trial ran from sowing in May 2022 to harvest in November, spanning 190 days, and collected **more than 20,000 data points**, including soil water content (recorded every 30 minutes), biomass (sampled every 3 weeks) and final yield (weighed per plot). The study was published in the March 2023 issue of the *Australian Journal of Agricultural Research*.

### Practical modules in postgraduate programs

Taught master's programs such as the **Master of Agricultural Science** (2 years) require students to complete at least **2 courses** with field trial modules. AGRD8001 Advanced Crop Production Systems, offered in 2023, includes **4** full-day field internships covering variable-rate fertilisation technology, yield mapping and precision seeder calibration. **30%** of the final exam mark for this course comes from a field operations assessment.

## Visit booking and external visitor policy

ANU trial fields offer limited visiting opportunities for **people outside the university**, mainly aimed at high school students, partner research institutions and industry partners. According to the *Visitor Guide* published by the ANU Farm Management Office in 2023, individual visitors can apply through the **online booking system** (farm-visits.anu.edu.au) and must submit applications at least **14 working days** in advance. The university runs **2** guided group tours per month, each limited to **20 people** and lasting about 2 hours. The tour route is fixed, covering the main trial blocks of the Acton campus farm but not the Ginninderra Experiment Station.

### Group visit process

Group visits (10 or more people) require a written application from the group leader stating the purpose of the visit, group size, expected duration and any special needs (such as accessible pathways). The university replies with confirmation within **5 working days** of receiving the application. On the day of the visit, all visitors must sign a **liability waiver** (ANU Legal Office template) and comply with safe dress requirements. Without approval, visitors may not enter fenced areas or touch trial materials.

### Industry partnerships and open day

Every **October**, ANU holds the "Ag Science Open Day" to showcase the latest trial field research projects to the public. The 2023 open day attracted **about 300 visitors** and featured **6** live exhibits, including drought-tolerant wheat variety comparisons, soil carbon sequestration trials and drone crop protection demonstrations. The trial fields also serve as a field visit site for the "Australia–China Agricultural Cooperation Research Network" (AACARN), hosting a **12-person** academic delegation from China Agricultural University in 2022.

## Joint operating arrangement with CSIRO

The Ginninderra Experiment Station is the product of long-term cooperation between ANU and the **Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation (CSIRO)**. Under the *Collaborative Research and Teaching Agreement* signed in 2017, both parties share the station's operating costs — ANU covers **40%** of the annual budget (about AUD 120,000) and CSIRO covers the remainder. The station serves both CSIRO's "Sustainable Agriculture Flagship" and ANU's undergraduate teaching needs.

### Data sharing and teaching resources

All sensor data at the station (weather, soil moisture, crop growth indicators) is stored on the **ANU cloud platform** (AWS Sydney node), where students and researchers can access it in real time using personal accounts. In 2022, the platform recorded **about 1.5 million data records**, of which about 20% were used in undergraduate teaching cases. ANU has also developed **6** interactive teaching modules based on this data, covering topics such as irrigation scheduling, pest and disease early warning and yield prediction.

### Joint training opportunities

At the Ginninderra Experiment Station, ANU students have the chance to **work alongside** CSIRO scientists. In 2023, the station offered **4** undergraduate summer internship positions (6 weeks, 35 hours per week) on topics including soil microbiome analysis and drone remote sensing data calibration. At the end of the internship, students submit a research report, and outstanding participants may receive a reference letter from CSIRO.

## Seasonal scheduling of practical activities

Field trial activities strictly follow the **southern hemisphere temperate agricultural calendar**, which falls into three seasonal cycles: autumn (March–May) sowing, winter (June–August) field management, spring (September–November) growth monitoring and summer (December–February) harvest and fallow. The practical content for each semester is adjusted according to the crop stage of the current season. ### Autumn sowing practice

Practical courses in Semester 1 (February to June) focus on **sowing and soil preparation**. Students operate small seeders (such as the Wintersteiger Plotseed), setting sowing depth (usually 2–5 cm) and row spacing (15–25 cm). In autumn 2023, the Acton campus farm sowed **6** wheat varieties and **3** barley varieties, each with 3 replicate plots.

### Spring monitoring practice

Practical courses in Semester 2 (July to November) focus on **crop growth monitoring**. Students use SPAD-502 chlorophyll meters to measure leaf nitrogen content and LI-6400 photosynthesis systems to record net photosynthetic rates (unit: µmol CO₂/m²/s). In spring 2022, AGRD3001 students completed 4 consecutive measurements within **8 weeks**, and the data were used to analyse yield formation patterns under different irrigation levels.

### Summer harvest practice

Summer (December to February) practical courses mainly target honours and postgraduate students, covering **plot yield determination** (using the Haldrup plot combine harvester), grain moisture measurement (using the Dickey-john moisture meter) and straw biomass weighing. In summer 2022, the Ginninderra Experiment Station harvested **48** trial plots, and the yield data were entered into the ANU agricultural database for long-term trend analysis.

## FAQ

### Q1: Are the ANU agricultural science trial fields open to international students?
Yes. International students can apply through the **online booking system** to visit the Acton campus farm, submitting applications **14 working days** in advance. 2023 data shows that about **15%** of visit applications came from international students, with Chinese students the largest group. Visits are free, but visitors should bring their own sunscreen and drinking water. The Ginninderra Experiment Station is open only to current ANU students and staff of partner institutions.

### Q2: Do the trial field practical courses cost extra?
No. The cost of the field trial practical modules is already included in **course tuition**. In 2023, international tuition for the ANU Bachelor of Agricultural Science was **AUD 48,000 per year**, covering all seeds, fertiliser, irrigation and equipment use required for field activities. Students only need to buy their own personal protective equipment (such as sunscreen and gloves), costing about **AUD 50 to 80** per year.

### Q3: How are trial field activities adjusted in bad weather?
The ANU Farm Management Office decides whether to cancel or postpone activities based on real-time warnings from the **Australian Bureau of Meteorology (BOM)**. When temperatures exceed **38°C**, wind speeds exceed **40 km/h**, or a thunderstorm warning is issued, all field activities are automatically cancelled and replaced with indoor alternative teaching (such as virtual field inspections or data analysis workshops). In 2022, **3** field activities were cancelled due to weather, accounting for **7.5%** of the annual total.
