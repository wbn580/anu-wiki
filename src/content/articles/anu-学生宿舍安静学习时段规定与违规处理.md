Since 2024, the Australian National University's (ANU) on-campus accommodation service (ANU Accommodation) has formally enforced the revised Residential Code of Conduct, which standardises the rules for **Quiet Study Hours** and the penalties for breaching them. According to the Accommodation Handbook published by ANU Accommodation in 2024, all 17 student halls enforce a unified quiet hours framework: 22:00 to 7:00 from Monday to Thursday, and 23:00 to 9:00 from Friday to Sunday. The Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) Higher Education Accommodation Status Report 2023 notes that around 68% of accommodation complaints at Australian universities relate to noise, and this revision aims to reduce that figure. The rules currently cover around 4,200 accommodation beds across the university, involving mixed communities of undergraduate and postgraduate residents.

## Definition and Scope of Quiet Study Hours

ANU Accommodation defines **Quiet Study Hours** as the periods during which noise in all public areas and rooms of a hall must be kept at a level that is "inaudible from adjacent rooms or corridors". According to clause 4.2 of the 2024 Accommodation Handbook, prohibited behaviour during these hours includes: playing audio equipment without headphones, holding social gatherings, talking loudly in corridors, using high-volume appliances such as vacuum cleaners, and knocking on walls or floors.

**The specific time periods** are as follows: during teaching weeks (Monday to Thursday), quiet hours run from 22:00 to 07:00 the next day; on weekends and public holidays (Friday to Sunday and ANU official holidays), quiet hours run from 23:00 to 09:00 the next day. During exam periods (Swot Vac and exam weeks as marked in the ANU academic calendar), quiet hours are extended to 20:00 to 10:00 daily across all halls.

The rules apply to **all accommodation types**, including single rooms, studio apartments, common rooms within halls, laundry rooms and corridors. In its Annual Accommodation Report published in June 2024, ANU Accommodation confirmed that all 17 halls have posted quiet hours signage and require residents to sign the accommodation agreement acknowledging the rules.

### Differences Between Public Areas and Private Rooms

In public areas (such as common rooms, kitchens and laundry rooms), all non-essential social activity is prohibited during quiet hours. ANU Accommodation requires these areas to maintain "library-level" quiet during quiet hours, meaning noise levels no higher than 40 decibels. In private rooms, residents may study, read and carry out other low-noise activities, but if the noise is complained about by neighbours or confirmed by patrol staff, it is still treated as a violation.

### Extended Rules for Exam Periods

During the exam weeks defined in the ANU academic calendar (typically early to mid-June and early to mid-November each year), quiet hours start at 20:00. Clause 4.3 of the 2024 Accommodation Handbook specifically notes that during exam periods, social gatherings of any kind are prohibited in the halls (regardless of size), and all music playback devices must use headphones. Residents who breach this extended rule go **straight into the formal warning process**, skipping the verbal reminder stage.

## The Tiered Penalty System for Breaches

ANU Accommodation operates a **three-tier penalty system** for quiet hours violations, first set out in writing in the 2024 revision. Under Chapter 7 "Disciplinary Procedures" of the Accommodation Handbook, violations are graded from minor to serious into three levels, each with its own process and consequences.

**Level 1: Verbal reminder and written record.** For a first offence (such as a single noise exceedance or brief disturbance), Residential Advisors (RAs) give a verbal reminder and record it in the resident's file. Internal ANU Accommodation statistics for 2023 show that about 72% of first offences are resolved at this level without proceeding further. After receiving a verbal reminder, residents must sign a Noise Acknowledgment Form within 24 hours confirming they understand the rules.

**Level 2: Formal written warning.** If a resident commits a second offence within 30 days, or the first offence is serious (such as a late-night gathering or noise lasting more than 30 minutes), they receive a formal written warning. The warning is issued by the Hall Manager, with a copy filed in the resident's accommodation record and copied to the ANU Accommodation central office. The written warning remains valid for 12 months; any further violation during this period escalates directly to Level 3.

**Level 3: Termination of the accommodation agreement.** For a third violation or a serious violation (such as deliberately damaging quiet facilities or threatening staff), ANU Accommodation has the right to terminate the accommodation agreement immediately. Under clause 7.5 of the ANU Accommodation 2024 Disciplinary Procedures, termination requires the joint sign-off of the Hall Manager, the Director of Accommodation and the office of the Deputy Vice-Chancellor (Student and University Experience). Residents whose agreements are terminated must vacate the hall within 48 hours and may face a 12-month campus accommodation ban.

### How Long Violation Records Last

All violation records are retained for 24 months after the resident moves out. ANU Accommodation's 2024 Privacy Statement notes that these records are used only for internal disciplinary management and do not automatically affect academic results or visa status. However, if a violation involves criminal conduct (such as serious disturbance requiring police involvement), ANU Security reports the matter to the Australian Federal Police (AFP).

## Appeals and Review Process

Residents who disagree with a penalty decision may submit an appeal within **5 working days** of receiving the written notice. ANU Accommodation has an independent Accommodation Appeals Committee, made up of the Director of Accommodation, one academic representative and one student representative. Under the 2024 Appeals Procedure, appeals must be submitted in writing to the Accommodation Services office, accompanied by relevant evidence.

The Appeals Committee issues its decision within 10 working days of receiving the materials. There are three possible outcomes: uphold the original decision, reduce the penalty level, or overturn the decision. ANU Accommodation's annual statistics for 2023 show that of the 47 appeals received that year, about 31% were partially or fully upheld. Residents who remain dissatisfied with the committee's decision may seek further review from the ANU Deputy Vice-Chancellor (Student and University Experience) office, but that review only examines procedural compliance and does not re-evaluate the facts.

### Temporary Measures in Emergencies

In extreme situations (such as a large late-night gathering or serious noise lasting more than an hour), hall staff may take **temporary measures**, including requiring residents to stop the activity immediately, clearing people from public areas, or even calling ANU Security. The ANU Security 24-hour phone line is (02) 6125 2249. Temporary measures do not replace the formal disciplinary process, but they can serve as the basis for subsequent action.

## Noise Monitoring Methods in the Halls

ANU Accommodation has deployed a range of **noise monitoring methods** across the halls to help staff enforce the rules. According to Appendix C of the 2024 Accommodation Handbook, the main methods include:

**Manual patrols:** every hall schedules at least 2 Residential Advisors (RAs) to patrol each night, covering the full quiet hours period. If RAs detect excessive noise during patrols, they are authorised to enter public areas directly and (on reasonable suspicion) ask residents to open their doors for inspection. The ANU Accommodation 2023 training manual requires RAs to wear identification and to note the noise source, duration and number of people involved in their records.

**Noise monitoring equipment:** some halls (such as Wright Hall and Bruce Hall) have **noise sensors** installed in corridors and public areas. Maintained by ANU Facilities Management, these sensors monitor decibel levels in real time. When noise exceeds a preset threshold (usually 45 decibels) for more than 10 seconds, the system automatically sends an alert to the Hall Manager. ANU Accommodation's 2024 statement stresses that the sensors do not record speech content — they only capture noise intensity data.

**Resident reporting system:** residents can report noise anonymously through the official ANU Accommodation app or the 24-hour service hotline (02) 6125 5555. Reports automatically generate a work order assigned to the duty RA. 2023 data shows that about 43% of noise work orders come from resident reports rather than patrol findings.

### Privacy Protection and Data Use

In its 2024 Data Use Policy, ANU Accommodation states that noise monitoring data is used only for disciplinary management and facility optimisation, and is not shared with ANU academic departments or external organisations. Data is retained for 12 months and automatically deleted after that period. Residents with questions about monitoring methods can contact the Accommodation Privacy Officer for details.

## How Enforcement Differs Across Halls

Although ANU Accommodation sets a unified framework, there are **specific differences** in how individual halls enforce it. According to the hall-specific rules published by each hall in 2024, the main differences are as follows:

**Heritage buildings:** older colleges such as Burgmann College and John XXIII College enforce stricter standards because their buildings have poor sound insulation. Their 2024 rules specify that during quiet hours, noise from a room must not be audible 3 metres away in the corridor. Burgmann College also adds a "midday quiet period" (13:00-14:00) to accommodate residents who nap at lunchtime.

**Newer apartment-style halls:** apartment buildings built after 2010, such as Kinsella and Fenner Hall, use modern sound-insulating materials and enforce relatively more relaxed standards. These halls allow residents to use televisions at low volume (with headphones required) during quiet hours, and have "social lounges" in public areas for use outside quiet hours. During exam periods, however, all halls enforce the same standard.

**Postgraduate-only halls:** at Graduate House, where most residents are research students, quiet hours start at 21:00 — one hour earlier than undergraduate halls. The Graduate House 2024 Resident Handbook also requires public areas to maintain "study-room-level" quiet during weekday daytime hours (09:00-17:00), with social activity prohibited.

### Rules for Transferring Between Halls

Residents who want to change halls because of noise issues must submit a written application to Accommodation Services. Under ANU Accommodation's 2024 Transfer Policy, transfer applications must state the reason and include the signature of the current Hall Manager. After a successful transfer, previous violation records do not follow the resident, but if a similar violation occurs at the new hall, the penalty level starts from Level 2.

## Special Notes on Quiet Hours for International Students

ANU Accommodation provides **special notes** for international students in its 2024 International Student Accommodation Guide. According to the Australian Department of Education's 2023 International Student Accommodation Survey, international students make up about 38% of ANU accommodation residents, with the largest groups coming from China, India and Southeast Asia.

**Language and cultural differences:** ANU Accommodation advises international students to attend the hall's "Quiet Hours Orientation Session" within 24 hours of check-in, which offers materials in Chinese, Korean, Vietnamese and other languages. Clause 3.2 of the 2024 International Student Accommodation Guide states that for a first violation caused by cultural differences (such as unfamiliarity with local daily routines), staff may use discretion to give one additional verbal reminder, but must note a "cultural adjustment period" in the file.

**Visa and accommodation links:** the Australian Department of Home Affairs' 2024 Student Visa Conditions (Subclass 500) clause 8105 does not list accommodation violations as a direct ground for visa cancellation. However, ANU Accommodation notes in its 2024 Accommodation Agreement that if a resident's agreement is terminated for a serious violation and they cannot find alternative accommodation, this may affect the visa condition requiring them to "maintain adequate accommodation arrangements". The ANU International Student Support centre can help affected students find off-campus housing.

**Cross-border tuition and accommodation fee payments:** when paying accommodation fees, some international students' families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E5%AE%BF%E8%88%8D%E5%AE%89%E9%9D%99%E5%AD%A6%E4%B9%A0%E6%97%B6%E6%AE%B5%E8%A7%84%E5%AE%9A%E4%B8%8E%E8%BF%9D%E8%A7%84%E5%A4%84%E7%90%86) to complete the settlement, ensuring funds arrive on time and avoiding exchange rate fluctuation risk. ANU Accommodation requires all accommodation fees to be paid in full 14 working days before the start of each semester; late payments incur a daily late fee of 0.5%.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I make phone calls in my own room during quiet hours?

Yes, but you must make sure the call cannot be heard from the corridor or adjacent rooms. ANU Accommodation recommends using earphones or lowering the volume. If a neighbour complains or an RA patrol confirms sound escaping, it counts as a Level 1 violation. The 2024 Accommodation Handbook states that a call is a violation if the conversation can be clearly identified 3 metres away in the corridor.

### Q2: If my roommate makes noise during quiet hours, will I be penalised too?

No. ANU Accommodation applies the **principle of individual responsibility** — only the resident who makes the noise is recorded and penalised. However, if you share the room and fail to cooperate with an RA investigation (such as refusing to open the door), that may be treated as obstructing staff and result in a separate verbal warning. ANU Accommodation's 2023 statistics show that about 12% of noise complaints involve multi-occupancy rooms, and in those cases only the resident who was the noise source was recorded.

### Q3: Do the quiet hours rules apply to visitors in the halls?

Yes. All visitors must observe the same rules as residents during quiet hours. Residents are responsible for ensuring their visitors follow the rules. If a visitor makes noise, the violation is recorded against the resident's name. ANU Accommodation's 2024 Visitor Policy states that visitors may not stay overnight during quiet hours and must leave the hall by 23:00.
