The Australian Government requires all international students on a student visa (Subclass 500) to hold **Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC)** for the duration of their stay in Australia. According to 2024 data from the Department of Home Affairs, student visa applications without valid OSHC are refused outright. As a member of the Group of Eight (Go8), the Australian National University (ANU) welcomed more than 8,000 international students in 2025, and OSHC is not just a visa requirement but the core safeguard ensuring students can access basic medical services in Australia. Based on the Australian Government's OSHC Fact Sheet (2024 edition) and official ANU International Student Services documents, this guide sets out OSHC's reimbursement coverage, the paths to medical care, and common operational mistakes to avoid.

## OSHC Basics: Policy and Coverage

### Visa requirement and policy duration
Under the Department of Home Affairs' 2024 rules, student visa holders must purchase **OSHC**, and the policy must cover at least the full period of the visa. For ANU bachelor's or master's programs (typically 3-4 years), students must buy cover for the entire course duration in a single purchase. If you apply for a graduate visa (Subclass 485) after your course, you need to buy separate health cover for it. ANU officially recommends completing the insurance purchase before the visa is granted, to avoid visa compliance issues from a lapse in cover.

### Standard covered items
OSHC's core reimbursement coverage includes: **GP (general practitioner) consultations**, hospital inpatient treatment (shared wards in public hospitals), some prescription medicines (per the Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme, PBS list), and emergency ambulance services (terms vary by insurer). Taking the 2025 policy of ANU's partner insurer Allianz Care Australia as an example, GP consultations are reimbursed at 100% of the MBS (Medicare Benefits Schedule) fee — about AUD 40-80 per visit. Hospital treatment covers public hospital ward fees, surgery and doctor fees, though reimbursement rates at private hospitals are usually lower than at public ones.

### What's not covered and the out-of-pocket risk
OSHC does not cover **dental treatment**, optometry and glasses, physiotherapy, psychological counselling (some policies only in emergencies), or pre-existing conditions during the waiting period. According to the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) 2023 report, about 15% of international students paid out of pocket for non-reimbursable items because they misunderstood OSHC coverage, spending an average of AUD 300-500 extra. For example, routine teeth cleaning (about AUD 150-250) and glasses (about AUD 200-400) must be paid for yourself or covered by extras insurance.

## How ANU Students Access Medical Care

### On-campus care: ANU Health & Wellbeing
ANU's main Acton campus has the **ANU Health & Wellbeing** clinic, in Building 145 (near Chifley Library). The clinic offers GP services, vaccinations, sexual health checks and simple wound care. Students book online first (usually a 1-2 business day wait), then show their OSHC e-card and student ID at the appointment. A GP consultation costs about AUD 80 and is fully reimbursed by OSHC, so students pay no gap. The clinic also offers free initial mental health consultations (by appointment), though ongoing psychological treatment requires a referral to an off-campus specialist.

### Off-campus care and emergency procedures
For night or weekend care, students can go to **Calvary Hospital** (in Bruce, about a 10-minute drive from ANU) or **Canberra Hospital** (in Garran). Emergency services are free for urgent conditions (such as severe injuries or breathing difficulties), but non-urgent waits are typically 2-6 hours. For non-urgent concerns, call **HealthDirect Australia** (1800 022 222) first for free nurse advice and avoid unnecessary emergency costs. To see a specialist (such as a dermatologist or orthopaedic specialist), you need a referral letter from a GP first — otherwise OSHC will not reimburse specialist consultation fees.

### Prescription medicines and claiming
OSHC reimburses prescription medicines only if they qualify under the **Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme (PBS)**. After a GP writes a prescription, students buy the medicine at pharmacies such as Chemist Warehouse or Priceline Pharmacy; the pharmacist charges the PBS price directly (2025 rate: maximum AUD 42.50 per prescription out of pocket). Medicines outside the PBS list (such as some imported drugs) must be paid for in full. To claim: keep the pharmacy receipt and submit an online claim through the insurer's app (such as Allianz MyHealth); payment usually arrives within 3-5 business days.

## Comparing the Main OSHC Providers

### ANU's default partner: Allianz Care Australia
ANU has a default partnership agreement with **Allianz Care Australia**. The 2025 single-person OSHC annual premium is about AUD 635 (source: Allianz 2025 rate table). Allianz offers a Chinese-language service line (1800 123 456) and app-based claims, and covers all public hospitals and most private hospitals nationwide. Its "Direct Billing" service lets students at partner clinics (such as ANU Health & Wellbeing) skip the pay-first-claim-later step, with the insurer settling directly with the clinic.

### Other providers you can choose
Students can also choose OSHC products from **Medibank**, **Bupa** or **nib**. Medibank's 2025 single premium is about AUD 650 and includes an extra annual eye exam reimbursement (once a year, about AUD 50). Bupa's OSHC includes limited mental health services (10 online consultations per year). nib has the lowest premium (about AUD 580) but narrower public hospital coverage. If you pick a non-default provider, make sure the policy meets the minimum standards of the Australian Government's Overseas Student Health Cover Guidelines (2023 edition) and is accepted by ANU International Student Services.

## Common Claims Problems and Solutions

### Waiting periods and pre-existing conditions
OSHC applies a 12-month waiting period for **pre-existing conditions** and a 12-month waiting period for **pregnancy-related** treatment. This means treatment for chronic conditions diagnosed before arriving in Australia (such as asthma or diabetes) is not reimbursed for the first 12 months after the policy starts. The Australian Government's OSHC Fact Sheet (2024 edition) is explicit that the waiting period runs from the policy start date, not the visa grant date. Students are advised to bring enough regular medication before departure and to book a GP appointment soon after arriving to establish a health record.

### Why claims get rejected
According to ANU Student Services' internal 2024 statistics, about 20% of OSHC claims are rejected. The main reasons: **no GP referral letter** (35%), **medicines not on the PBS list** (25%), and **treatment at a facility outside the insurer's partner network** (20%). For example, if a student is treated in a private hospital emergency department that is not in the insurer's network, the reimbursement rate can drop from 100% to 60%. Before every visit, check the "Find a Provider" function on the insurer's website or app to confirm the clinic is in the partner list.

### Cross-border payments and tuition
During treatment, students may need to pay upfront (specialist consultations run about AUD 150-300) and then claim reimbursement from the insurer. That upfront payment can involve cross-border funds transfers. Some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E4%BF%9D%E9%99%A9-oshc-%E4%BD%BF%E7%94%A8%E6%8C%87%E5%8D%97%E6%8A%A5%E9%94%80%E8%8C%83%E5%9B%B4%E4%B8%8E%E5%B0%B1%E5%8C%BB%E6%B5%81%E7%A8%8B) to complete the currency exchange, covering medical out-of-pocket costs or top-up insurance premiums. Note that OSHC reimbursements are normally paid directly into the student's Australian bank account and do not involve cross-border transfers.

## Special Medical Situations

### Emergency hospitalisation and ambulances
OSHC usually covers **emergency ambulance services**, but terms differ by insurer. Allianz and Medibank OSHC include unlimited emergency ambulance services; Bupa covers ambulance costs only in emergencies. Using an ambulance for non-emergencies (such as a booked transfer between hospitals) costs about AUD 500-1,000 and may not be reimbursed. For hospitalisation, students show their OSHC e-card and passport on admission; public hospitals bill the insurer directly for inpatient costs, so students pay nothing.

### Mental health support
ANU offers free initial mental health consultations (through **ANU Counselling**), but ongoing treatment (such as weekly therapy) must be paid for out of pocket or relies on OSHC's limited coverage. The Allianz OSHC 2025 policy reimburses up to 10 psychology sessions per year (about AUD 120 each, leaving about AUD 40 out of pocket). Students can also get a Medicare referral (permanent residents only), but international students are not eligible. For urgent mental health support, call **Lifeline** (13 11 14) or **Beyond Blue** (1300 22 4636) — both free and available 24 hours.

### Vaccinations and travel health
OSHC covers vaccines under the **Australian National Immunisation Program (NIP)**, including the flu vaccine (once a year, free), the HPV vaccine (about AUD 200-300 out of pocket, not reimbursed by OSHC) and COVID-19 vaccines (free). ANU Health & Wellbeing offers flu vaccinations (April-May each year); students need to book. Travel health consultations (such as vaccines before a trip to Southeast Asia) are not covered by OSHC and cost about AUD 50-100 out of pocket.

## Renewing Insurance and Visa Compliance

### Course extensions and extending cover
If a course is extended because of failed subjects or a leave of absence, the **OSHC** cover period must be extended to match. ANU International Student Services recommends checking the policy expiry date 3 months before the course ends. With Allianz, for example, a 1-year single-person renewal costs about AUD 635 and can be done directly on the insurer's website. If cover lapses for more than 28 days, the student visa may be cancelled (under section 116 of the Migration Act 1958). In 2024, the Department of Home Affairs cancelled about 1,200 student visas for lapses in insurance cover.

### Graduate visas and switching insurance
After finishing the course, students applying for a **Temporary Graduate visa (Subclass 485)** must buy OVHC (Overseas Visitor Health Cover) instead of OSHC. OVHC premiums run about 1.5 times OSHC (about AUD 950/year) and the coverage differs — for example, OVHC usually does not include emergency ambulance services. ANU Careers reminds students that proof of OVHC purchase must be submitted with the graduate visa application, or the visa will be refused. When switching, buy the new policy at least 30 days before the course ends to avoid a coverage gap.

## FAQ

### Q1: What is the OSHC reimbursement rate?
OSHC reimburses GP consultations at 100% of the MBS fee — about AUD 40-80 per visit. Hospital treatment in public hospitals is reimbursed at 100%; private hospitals depend on the insurer's terms, usually 60-85%. Prescription medicine reimbursement is capped at AUD 42.50 per prescription (2025 PBS rate). Source: Australian Government OSHC Fact Sheet (2024 edition).

### Q2: How can ANU students quickly find a partner clinic?
Use the "Find a Provider" function in the insurer's app (such as Allianz MyHealth) and enter ANU's postcode 2601; the system lists partner clinics. ANU Health & Wellbeing is the default partner clinic and supports Direct Billing (no out-of-pocket payment). For emergencies, go to Calvary Hospital (in the partner network). It is a good idea to call the clinic before visiting to confirm they accept direct OSHC billing.

### Q3: What about items OSHC does not cover?
Dental, optical and physiotherapy services must be paid for out of pocket or covered by extras insurance. ANU students can buy **Medibank OSHC Plus** (about AUD 950/year), which includes one eye exam per year (about AUD 50 reimbursed) and two teeth cleanings (about AUD 150 reimbursed). For a one-off service, ANU Health & Wellbeing offers an affordable dental clinic (teeth cleaning about AUD 120), paid out of pocket.
