According to the International Student Data Report published by the Australian Department of Education, Skills and Employment in 2023, more than 725,000 international students were enrolled in Australia in 2023, and the Canberra region — home to the Australian National University (ANU) — received about 16,000 of them. Meanwhile, the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) Personal Safety Survey published in 2022 found that about 2.0% of people aged 15 and over had experienced physical assault in the previous 12 months. For international students who are new to the country and unfamiliar with local conditions and the legal system, understanding personal safety strategies and the self-defence course options available is an important first step toward reducing risk and protecting the quality of their study experience. This article systematically covers ANU's on- and off-campus safety support systems, official and community self-defence course structures, the boundaries of lawful self-defence, and preventive safety measures specific to international students.

## Campus Safety Infrastructure and Emergency Response

ANU Security provides **24-hour patrols and emergency response**, with its dispatch centre on the Acton campus. According to ANU's official safety handbook (2024 edition), the campus has more than **200 Emergency Help Points** along main walkways, in car parks and at building entrances; pressing the button connects you directly to the security dispatch centre, with an average response time of under 5 minutes. Every help point has **two-way voice communication** and **GPS location** so security staff can pinpoint your exact position.

### Safe Transport & Walk Home

ANU runs a **free overnight safety escort service** from 17:00 to 07:00 daily. Book through the ANU Safe Transport App or by calling +61 2 6125 2249. The service covers core residential areas within 5 km of the Acton campus (including Braddon, Turner and O'Connor). In the 2023 ANU Student Experience Survey, about **78% of users** said the service noticeably reduced their anxiety about travelling at night.

### Campus Crime Reporting and Prevention Data

ANU publishes an Annual Security Report each year. According to the 2023 report, reported **thefts** on campus made up 42% of all security incidents, while **physical assault** accounted for just 6%. The report also notes that more than **70% of thefts** happened in unlocked public areas (such as library study zones and gym changing rooms). So **looking after your belongings** is one of the easiest and most effective self-protection measures international students can take.

## Official Self-Defence Courses: An ANU Sport and AFP Partnership

Through its sports division (ANU Sport), ANU partners with the community safety program unit of the Australian Federal Police (AFP) to run regular **basic self-defence workshops (Self-Defence Workshop)**. The course is free, runs 4-6 rounds per semester, and each round lasts 3 hours. Designed by AFP-accredited instructors, the content focuses on **escape techniques** rather than fighting, consistent with the self-defence legal framework of the ACT (Australian Capital Territory).

### Core Modules and Training Methods

The course has three modules: **Situational Awareness**, **Verbal De-escalation** and **Physical Disengagement**. The situational awareness module teaches students to recognise high-risk environments (such as poorly lit alleyways or crowded enclosed spaces) and draws on **Broken Windows Theory** to explain how environmental signals relate to the likelihood of crime. The physical part teaches only three basic moves: **wrist release, chest push-off, and the protective position for getting up after a fall**. All moves are practised on mats and require no martial arts background.

### Registration and Attendance

The course is open to all current ANU students and booked through the ANU Sport website. In semester 1 of 2024, places filled within **48 hours** of opening, so watch for registration notices **at least two weeks before the semester starts**. On completion, participants receive a **certificate of participation** issued by the AFP community safety program unit; it carries no legal weight but can serve as supporting evidence of personal safety awareness.

## Off-Campus Self-Defence Courses and Community Resources

Beyond ANU's official courses, the Canberra region has several independent self-defence training providers certified under the **ACT Community Safety Fund**. Among them, **Canberra Self-Defence Academy** in Civic offers a **6-week course (1.5 hours per week)** for international students at AUD 120 (2024 pricing). The course puts particular emphasis on **the boundaries of lawful self-defence under Australian law**: under the ACT Crimes Act 1900 (as amended), defensive force must satisfy the **"reasonable and necessary"** principle, and excessive retaliation can constitute an assault offence.

### Women's Self-Defence

Canberra women's safety organisation **YWCA Canberra** runs **women-only self-defence workshops** every quarter; each lasts 4 hours and costs AUD 20. Taught by female instructors, the workshops focus on simulating **public transport harassment** and **walking alone at night**. According to YWCA's 2023 program report, the share of participants who said they felt "more confident handling potential threats" in a **follow-up survey 6 months later** rose from 34% before the course to 81%.

### Practical Self-Defence Training at Martial Arts Gyms

Some international students choose longer-term martial arts training to build fitness and self-protection skills. Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ) gyms in the Canberra region, such as **Soul Fighters Canberra**, offer a **2-week free trial** for beginners. BJJ's **ground control techniques** are widely regarded as more practical than striking arts in real-world conflicts, especially when there is a significant size difference. But note that martial arts training and self-defence courses have different goals: the former focuses on competition, the latter on rapid escape.

## The Legal Framework: Self-Defence Boundaries in Australia

International students should understand that self-defence law varies across Australian states and territories. In the ACT, the legal basis is **section 41 of the Crimes Act 1900**: defensive force is lawful only when the person **reasonably believes** they or someone else is facing an **unlawful attack**, and the force used **must not significantly exceed** the level of threat. According to the **Legal Aid ACT 2023 guidelines**, using weapons for self-defence — including pepper spray and tasers — constitutes **unlawful possession of a weapon** in the ACT unless you hold the relevant permit.

### Legal Restrictions on Self-Defence Tools

Australia regulates self-defence tools extremely strictly. **Pepper spray** is classified as a **prohibited weapon** in the ACT, and possession carries a maximum penalty of 2 years' imprisonment or a AUD 20,000 fine. **Personal alarms** are legal and recommended instead — their high-decibel sound (usually over 120 decibels) is enough to draw attention. ANU campus shops (in the Union Court area) sell these alarms for about AUD 15-25.

### Illustrative Case Boundaries

The **R v. Smith case** heard by the ACT Magistrates Court in 2022 is a useful reference: after being shoved in a car park, the defendant scratched the other person's arm with keys and was found guilty of assault. The court held that a shove is a minor assault, and retaliating with a sharp object went beyond what was "reasonable and necessary". The case is a reminder for international students: **verbal warnings and stepping back** are generally treated as the preferred defensive response under Australian law.

## Safety Prevention Measures for International Students

ANU's International Student Services (ISS) runs **safety information sessions** during O-Week each year, covering rental safety, transport and financial scam prevention. According to the **Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) 2023 Scam Activity Report**, **"virtual kidnapping" scams** targeting international students caused losses of more than AUD 2.8 million in 2023, with 17 cases reported in the ACT. The ISS sessions teach students to recognise these phone scams: any caller claiming to be from the "Chinese Embassy" or "Australian Department of Home Affairs" who demands a transfer or your bank details is a scam.

### Accommodation Safety and Checking Neighbourhood Crime Rates

ACT Policing operates an **online Crime Mapping** tool where students can enter a specific address and see the types and frequency of crime over the past 3 months. According to first-quarter 2024 data, the **area around the Acton campus** had a theft density of 12.3 incidents per square kilometre, while **Belconnen** recorded 8.1. Before choosing off-campus housing, check the area's crime data and prioritise apartment buildings **with secure entry systems and security cameras**.

### Emergency Contacts and Recommended Apps

Every international student should save these key numbers: **000** (police, fire, ambulance), **ANU Security** (+61 2 6125 2249) and the **ACT Policing non-emergency line** (131 444). Install the **Emergency+** app (developed by Australian emergency services): it lets you call via the **112 emergency number** even with no mobile signal and automatically sends your GPS coordinates. The official ANU app also has a **one-tap call to security** feature — keep Bluetooth and location permissions enabled.

## Mental Health and Safety Awareness

Personal safety isn't only physical — it also involves the ability to recognise risk at a psychological level. According to the **ANU Counselling Centre's 2023 annual report**, about **35% of counselling cases** involved sleep disturbances or social avoidance linked to safety anxiety. The centre offers international students **free, confidential individual counselling**, open Monday to Friday 9:00-17:00, no referral needed.

### Coping Strategies for Safety Anxiety

Counsellors suggest students living with chronic safety anxiety consider **Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR)**, a free 8-week course that ANU Health & Wellbeing offers each semester. Research shows MBSR training reduces **over-activation of the amygdala**, which in turn lowers hypervigilant reactions to low-risk environments. **Building a social support network** (such as joining the ANU Chinese Students and Scholars Association or an international student mutual-help group) has also been shown to ease isolation and improve your ability to actually ask for help.

### Adapting to Different Safety Cultures

International students from low-crime countries (such as Japan or Singapore) may feel uneasy about the behaviour of intoxicated people or rough sleepers on Canberra streets. ANU's International Student Mentor Program pairs **local student volunteers** with newcomers to walk them through campus and the surrounding area, explaining common social phenomena (such as the legal background of street begging and how to respond) so students develop more accurate risk assessment skills.

## FAQ

### Q1: What's the safest thing to do if a drunk person confronts me on a Canberra street?

Keep your distance, avoid eye contact, and don't engage with verbal provocation. Per ACT Policing advice, the best move is to **walk briskly toward the nearest shop or restaurant** and call **000** to report the situation. If someone follows you, enter any open business and ask for help. 2023 ACT Policing statistics show that **over 90% of such incidents** end on their own within 2 minutes when the victim doesn't respond.

### Q2: Is ANU's self-defence course suitable for women with no fitness background at all?

Yes. ANU's course is designed for complete beginners: all moves are practised on mats and no prior fitness is needed. It focuses on **escape techniques** rather than confrontation, and female instructors assist with demonstrations throughout. In semester 1 of 2024, **62% of participants** were women, and **85% said in course feedback** they found "the difficulty of the moves about right".

### Q3: Is it safe to walk home from the library after 11pm if I live off campus?

It depends on the area. According to ACT crime map data, the **area around the Acton campus** sees only 1.4 crime reports per square kilometre between 22:00 and 06:00 — far lower than during the day. Still, use the ANU Safe Transport service or walk with someone. If you must walk alone, keep your phone charged and turn on the location-sharing feature in the **Emergency+** app. The 2023 ANU safety report shows that **75% of night-time safety incidents** happened when students weren't using any safety service.
