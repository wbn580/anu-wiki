## Breaking Down GMAT Critical Reasoning

The GMAT Critical Reasoning section tests your ability to evaluate arguments. Three common question types are **weaken**, **strengthen**, and **assumption**. A powerful technique for these is **logic chain analysis**, which involves mapping out the premises and the conclusion to see the logical connection between them.

## The Logic Chain Method

To apply logic chain analysis, follow these steps:

1. **Identify the conclusion**: What is the author trying to prove?
2. **Identify the premises**: What evidence or reasons are given to support the conclusion?
3. **Trace the logical link**: How do the premises connect to the conclusion? What unstated assumption bridges the gap?

Once you have this chain, you can see where the argument might be vulnerable (for weaken questions) or where it needs support (for strengthen and assumption questions).

## Applying the Method to Question Types

### Weaken Questions

For weaken questions, your goal is to find the answer choice that attacks the logical link between premises and conclusion. By mapping the logic chain, you can spot the weakest point. For example, if an argument claims that a new training program improves test scores because test scores rose after the program was introduced, the logic chain would be: premise (scores rose after program) -> conclusion (program caused the improvement). To weaken this, you might point out that a different factor, such as a change in curriculum, also occurred during that time—breaking the causal link.

### Strengthen Questions

Strengthen questions ask you to choose an answer that supports the argument. Using the logic chain, you look for a choice that reinforces the connection or adds a premise that makes the conclusion more likely. For instance, if the argument relies on a survey, a strengthener might be that the survey sample was representative of the entire population.

### Assumption Questions

Assumption questions require you to identify an unstated premise that must be true for the argument to hold. Logic chain analysis helps you see the gap: the missing link between premises and conclusion. The correct answer is the statement that fills that gap. For example, if the argument says "All students who attend the review session pass the exam," and the conclusion is "You will pass the exam if you attend the session," the assumption is that you are a student or that the statement applies to you.
