As an international student at the Australian National University (ANU), part-time work is not only an important source of financial support but also a key way to gain local experience. Under the student visa conditions updated by the Australian Department of Home Affairs in July 2024, international students holding a Student Visa (Subclass 500) may legally work up to 48 hours per fortnight, an increase from the pre-pandemic limit of 40 hours per fortnight that took effect on 1 July 2023 (Department of Home Affairs, 2024, Student Visa Work Conditions). Meanwhile, ANU's 2023 graduate employment survey shows that about 68% of current international students had worked part-time during their studies, with on-campus roles and Canberra's local hospitality sector the two main choices (ANU Careers & Employability, 2023, ANU Student Employment Report). With Australia's national minimum wage set at AUD 23.23 per hour (effective July 2024) (Fair Work Commission, 2024, Annual Wage Review Decision), understanding the types of work available, the legal restrictions, and time management strategies is crucial to maintaining a healthy balance between study and work.

## Student Visa Work Hour Limits and Compliance Requirements

Australian migration law strictly defines how many hours international students may work. Under clause 8104 of the Migration Regulations 1994, the **48 hours per fortnight** limit means that within any continuous 14-day period starting from a Monday, total work hours must not exceed 48. This limit applies during the teaching period; during **scheduled academic breaks** (such as ANU's winter and summer vacations), students may work full-time with no hourly limit (Department of Home Affairs, 2024, Student Visa Work Conditions).

### Calculating Work Hours and the Consequences of Breaches

**Work hours** include all paid work, but not volunteer work (unpaid and not required by your role). Breaching your work conditions can lead to your visa being cancelled and a three-year ban on applying for other visas. ANU Global Programs (the university's international student services) recommends that students track their weekly hours with a time-tracking app (such as Toggl or Harvest) to avoid accidentally breaching the rules.

### Course Requirements and Work Restrictions

Student visa holders must maintain **full-time enrolment** (usually 24 units per semester). If work causes your attendance or academic performance to slip, ANU has the right to report you to the Department of Home Affairs, which may affect your visa renewal. In 2023, the Department of Home Affairs cancelled approximately 1,200 student visas for breaching work conditions, including some ANU students (Department of Home Affairs, 2023, Annual Student Visa Compliance Report).

## On-Campus Job Opportunities

ANU offers a wide range of **on-campus jobs**. These positions usually give priority to students, are based right on campus, and involve minimal commuting. According to ANU Human Resources, there were about 1,500 student roles on campus in 2024, with average hourly pay of AUD 28.50 to 35.00 (above the legal minimum wage) (ANU Human Resources, 2024, Student Employment Statistics).

### Academic Support Roles

**Academic support roles** include Tutor, Research Assistant, and Demonstrator. These positions require strong academic results (usually a GPA above 5.0/7.0) and passing a departmental interview. Pay ranges from AUD 35 to 45 per hour, with 5 to 15 hours of work per week. Applicants need to submit their résumé and academic transcript via ANU's CareerHub platform at the start of each semester.

### Administrative and Service Roles

**Administrative and service roles** cover library assistants, student centre front-desk staff, IT support, and similar positions. These roles have lower academic requirements but value communication skills and customer service ability. Pay is about AUD 28 to 33 per hour, with 8 to 12 hours of work per week. ANU Library hired 80 library assistants in 2024, with application deadlines usually two weeks before the start of semester (ANU Library, 2024, Student Employment Opportunities).

### Campus Commercial Roles

**Campus commercial roles** include part-time positions at on-campus cafés, bookshops (such as Co-op Bookshop), and the sports centre (ANU Sport). These jobs pay AUD 25 to 30 per hour and offer flexible hours that suit study schedules. ANU Sport offered 45 gym front-desk and class assistant positions in 2024; applicants must hold a first aid certificate (HLTAID011).

## Hospitality Jobs in Canberra

As Australia's capital, Canberra has a stable services job market. In 2024, the vacancy rate in Canberra's services sector was about 6.2%, above the national average of 4.8% (Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), 2024, Labour Force Survey). For ANU students, **local hospitality and retail** are the most direct employment options outside campus.

### Food and Retail Roles

**Food and retail roles** include part-time staff at cafés, restaurants, bars, supermarkets, and clothing stores. Hourly pay ranges from AUD 23.23 to 30.00, depending on the size of the business and whether an industry award rate applies. Canberra's city centre (Civic) and the Belconnen district are the main job clusters, 15 to 25 minutes from the ANU main campus by foot or bus. In 2024, the Canberra Hospitality Association reported that about 35% of part-time positions were filled by international students (Canberra Hospitality Association, 2024, Industry Workforce Report).

### Internships and Professionally Relevant Roles

**Internships and professionally relevant roles** are usually tied to ANU internship programs (such as Internships Australia) or industry partnership projects. These positions may be paid or credit-bearing, with hourly rates of AUD 30 to 50, but the hours count towards your visa limit. For example, ANU's College of Engineering, Computing and Cybernetics works with Canberra tech companies (such as Seeing Machines) to offer around 60 paid internships a year, with 10 to 20 hours of work per week (ANU College of Engineering, Computing and Cybernetics, 2024, Industry Partnerships Report).

### Job Search Channels and Preparation

**Job search channels** include ANU's CareerHub platform, Seek.com.au, and local career fairs. ANU Careers & Employability runs two career fairs per semester, and the spring 2024 event attracted more than 80 local employers. Applicants should prepare an Australian-format résumé (usually no more than 2 pages) and a tailored cover letter. When it comes to paying cross-border tuition fees, some student families use specialised channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/part-time-work-options-for-anu-students-on-campus-jobs-hospitality-roles-and-leg) to complete the remittance and ensure funds arrive on time.

## Managing Your Time Between Study and Work

Balancing study and work is one of the main challenges international students face. ANU's Academic Skills Centre recommends keeping **weekly work to no more than 15 hours** (below the visa limit of 24 hours) to significantly reduce academic stress (ANU Academic Skills Centre, 2023, Time Management for Working Students).

### Allocating Time During Semester

**Time allocation during semester** should account for your course load: full-time students need about 40 hours a week for classes, preparation, and assignments. If you work 12 hours a week, around 28 hours remain for study, rest, and socialising. ANU runs time management workshops (6 per semester) that teach methods such as time blocking and priority matrices.

### Adjusting Around Exam Periods

**Work schedules during exams** need particular care. ANU's academic calendar sets aside weeks 12 to 14 of each semester as the exam period, and it is advisable to reduce or pause work during this time. In 2023, an ANU student wellbeing survey found that 62% of students who kept working more than 10 hours during exam week reported moderate to severe stress (ANU Student Wellbeing, 2023, Student Stress Survey Report).

## Tax and Financial Planning

International students working in Australia must comply with tax laws. Under Australian Taxation Office (ATO) rules, **international students** need to apply for a Tax File Number (TFN); otherwise employers must withhold tax at the top rate (47%) (Australian Taxation Office, 2024, Taxation for International Students).

### Tax Rates and Refunds

**Tax rates and refunds**: in the 2024–2025 financial year, income below AUD 18,200 is tax-free; the portion between AUD 18,201 and 45,000 is taxed at 19%. International students usually hold non-resident tax status, but you may be treated as a resident if you live in Australia for more than 183 days with the intention of staying long term. The tax return window runs from 1 July to 31 October each year, and returns can be lodged free of charge through myGov. ANU's College of Business and Economics offers free tax advice workshops (each August).

### Superannuation

**Superannuation**: employers must pay superannuation for employees earning more than AUD 450 per month (the rate in 2024 is 11.5%). International students can claim their super when leaving Australia through the Departing Australia Superannuation Payment (DASP), which is taxed at 35% (2024 rate). ANU's Financial Assistance Office recommends choosing a low-fee super fund (such as AustralianSuper or Hostplus) and keeping records of your TFN and employer details.

## Rights and Protections

International students have the same workplace rights as local employees. The **Fair Work Ombudsman** enforces minimum wages, working hours, and working conditions (Fair Work Ombudsman, 2024, International Students and Workplace Rights).

### Minimum Wage and Penalty Rates

The **minimum wage** is AUD 23.23 per hour (from July 2024), and some industries (such as hospitality and retail) have higher award rates. Penalty rates (for weekends and public holidays, for example) are usually calculated at 1.5 to 2.5 times the base rate. In 2023, the Fair Work Ombudsman conducted 120 compliance inspections in the Canberra region, recovering about AUD 450,000 in unpaid wages, including cases involving 7 ANU students (Fair Work Ombudsman, 2023, Compliance Activity Report – ACT).

### Complaints and Support

**Complaints and support**: if you experience underpayment or discrimination, you can lodge an anonymous complaint with the Fair Work Ombudsman (phone 13 13 94). The ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) offers free legal advice, with a stall on campus every Wednesday afternoon. In 2024, ANUSA handled 34 work-related enquiries, 15 of which involved working hour breaches.

## Long-Term Career Value

Part-time work is not just a source of income — it is also a springboard for your career. **ANU alumni surveys** show that students with part-time work experience during their studies have an employment rate 15% higher within 6 months of graduation (ANU Careers & Employability, 2023, Graduate Outcomes Survey).

### Building Skills and Strengthening Your Résumé

**Skill building** covers customer service, teamwork, time management, and industry-specific skills. ANU's Employer Relations Office notes that local Canberra employers (such as the Australian Public Service) prioritise graduates with local work experience. For example, the Australian Taxation Office recruits about 50 interns a year, 30% of whom are ANU graduates, and requires at least 6 months of part-time work experience (Australian Taxation Office, 2024, Graduate Recruitment Report).

### Industry Networks and Reference Letters

**Industry networks** built through part-time work can turn into formal job opportunities. ANU's Career Mentoring Program matches 300 students with industry mentors each year, and 40% of those matches come from colleagues or clients students met through part-time jobs. A reference letter is an important document when applying for full-time positions — it is a good idea to ask your employer for a written evaluation once every six months.

## FAQ

### Q1: How many hours per week can ANU students work at most?

ANU international students on a student visa may work up to 48 hours per fortnight during term time, which averages 24 hours per week. During academic breaks (such as the summer break from December to February), you can work full-time with no hourly cap. Breaches can lead to visa cancellation. In 2024, penalties imposed by the Department of Home Affairs for breaching working hour rules included a three-year re-entry ban (Department of Home Affairs, 2024, Student Visa Work Conditions).

### Q2: What part-time roles on the ANU campus suit international students?

ANU offers about 1,500 student roles on campus, including tutors (AUD 35–45 per hour), library assistants (AUD 28–33 per hour), and café staff (AUD 25–30 per hour). These positions are usually advertised through the ANU CareerHub platform, and applicants need to submit a résumé. In 2024, ANU Library hired 80 assistants, with applications due two weeks before the start of semester (ANU Human Resources, 2024, Student Employment Statistics).

### Q3: What do international students need to prepare when looking for part-time work in Canberra?

You will need an Australian-format résumé (no more than 2 pages), a Tax File Number (TFN), and a tailored cover letter. Job search channels include ANU CareerHub, Seek.com.au, and local career fairs. In 2024, Canberra's services sector vacancy rate was about 6.2%, and food and retail roles paid AUD 23.23 to 30.00 per hour (Australian Bureau of Statistics, 2024, Labour Force Survey).
