The Australian National University (ANU) International Student Orientation Week (O-Week) is a mandatory transition program run for new students before the start of each academic year, typically held once in February (Semester 1) and once in July (Semester 2), spanning five to seven days. According to official ANU data, the Semester 1 2024 O-Week drew around 4,500 new international students from more than 100 countries [ANU 2024, International Student Orientation Program Report]. Coordinated by ANU Student Experience and Recruitment, the program is designed to carry new students through the full transition — from visa compliance checks and getting to know campus facilities to adapting to the academic culture. According to the Department of Education's 2023 International Student Annual Statistics, the first-year attrition rate for international students at Australian universities is around 8.2%, but participation in a structured orientation program can bring that figure below 4.1% [Australian Department of Education 2023, International Student Data 2022-2023]. Knowing the core elements of O-Week is therefore essential to starting your studies on the right foot.

## Registration and Visa Compliance Checks

The first item on the O-Week agenda is the **mandatory enrolment and visa compliance check**, which every new international student must complete on day one. ANU asks new students to bring their passport, visa grant letter and CoE (Confirmation of Enrolment) to **Student Central** at their allotted time for in-person verification. Under Department of Home Affairs rules, international students must keep their enrolment consistent with their visa conditions, and anyone who fails to enrol during O-Week risks having their visa cancelled [Australian Department of Home Affairs 2024, Student Visa Conditions 8104/8202].

### The Visa Verification Process

New students first upload their visa documents to the **ISIS online system** (Integrated Student Information System), then head to the **Kambri Cultural Centre** on day one of O-Week for manual verification. ANU processed 4,532 visa files during the 2024 O-Week, with an average processing time of 12 minutes per student [ANU 2024, Student Central Annual Report]. Students who do not complete verification cannot activate their student account or campus card (ANU ID Card).

### Activating Your Student Card

The **ANU ID Card** is the key credential for moving around campus, borrowing library books and using printing services. After visa verification, new students have their photo taken on the spot at the **Card Office in Union Court** and collect their card. Within 24 hours of activation, the card works for entry to teaching buildings, libraries and some residential halls. ANU introduced a digital student card in 2023 — students can generate a virtual version through the **ANU Safe+ App** to reduce the risk of losing the physical card.

## Academic and Course Navigation

The core academic component of O-Week is **course enrolment and academic navigation**, designed to help new students understand ANU's credit system and enrolment rules. ANU runs on the **semester system**: each semester normally comprises 4 to 6 courses (each worth 6 units of credit), and undergraduates must complete 144 units within three years. During O-Week, each college hosts **Course Information Sessions** where academic advisors answer enrolment questions on the spot.

### The Course Enrolment Window

ANU requires new students to complete course enrolment by the Friday of week 1, after O-Week ends. In Semester 1 2024, ANU offered more than 2,100 undergraduate courses with an average capacity of 120 students per course [ANU 2024, Timetable and Enrolment Data]. Popular courses such as **Financial Mathematics** (FINM1001) and **Introduction to International Relations** (POLS1002) often fill up during O-Week, so new students should build their timetable on **Timetable@ANU** in advance to avoid clashes.

### Academic Integrity Training

All new international students must complete the **Academic Integrity Module**, an online training unit of about 90 minutes covering the definition of plagiarism, citation conventions and the consequences of exam misconduct. ANU's 2023 academic integrity report notes that new students who skip the module are 3.2 times more likely to face an academic misconduct allegation in their first semester than those who complete it [ANU 2023, Academic Integrity Annual Report]. On finishing the training, students receive a digital certificate that must be submitted to their college office before O-Week ends.

## Campus Facilities and Transport Tours

The **Campus Tour** is one of the highest-attended O-Week activities, with 3,870 new students signing up in February 2024 [ANU 2024, O-Week Participation Statistics]. The route covers core facilities including the **Chifley Library**, **Jacaranda Hall**, the **Kambri Precinct** and the **ANU Sport centre**, and runs for about 90 minutes. Tours are led by student volunteers (O-Week Ambassadors) in groups of no more than 20 to keep them interactive.

### Library Resources

The **Chifley Library** is ANU's largest academic library, holding more than 2.5 million physical volumes and over 80,000 electronic journal titles. During O-Week, the library runs **Information Literacy Workshops** on using databases such as JSTOR and Scopus and citation management tools such as EndNote. In 2024, 32 workshops were held with an average capacity of 40 places each, and spots routinely filled within 48 hours of registration opening [ANU 2024, Library Services Report].

### Public Transport and Commuting to Campus

ANU's main campus sits in Canberra's **Acton** district, about 3 km from the city centre. During O-Week, **Transport Canberra** sets up temporary information desks on campus to help students buy a **MyWay bus card** and plan routes. Students get discounted fares with their student ID: a single trip costs about $2.50, versus $4.20 for the adult fare. ANU also runs the **ANU Shuttle**, which operates free of charge during O-Week and links residential halls (such as Ursula Hall) with the main teaching buildings.

## Accommodation and Settling In

**Securing accommodation** is a priority for new international students during O-Week. ANU offers both on-campus accommodation and off-campus housing. On-campus halls include **Bruce Hall**, **Ursula Hall** and **Fenner Hall**; in Semester 1 2024 they provided 2,800 beds in total, with applications typically closing 4 weeks before O-Week begins [ANU 2024, Accommodation Services Annual Data].

### Moving Into an On-Campus Hall

Students moving into an on-campus hall collect their keys and sign their residence agreement at the **Residential Front Desk** on day one of O-Week. Halls typically offer single or twin rooms furnished with a bed, desk and wardrobe, plus shared kitchens. Bruce Hall hosted a **Community Dinner** during O-Week 2024 that drew 420 people — the first formal social-networking event for many new students.

### Opening a Bank Account and Health Cover

During O-Week, **Commonwealth Bank** and **ANZ** set up temporary counters on campus to help international students open Australian bank accounts. You will need your passport, visa and CoE, and the process takes about 30 minutes. Insurers such as **Medibank** and **Allianz** also staff booths at O-Week to help students activate their **Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC)**. Under Department of Home Affairs rules, international students must hold OSHC for the entire duration of their visa; in 2024, annual OSHC premiums ranged from roughly $530 to $650 [Australian Department of Home Affairs 2024, OSHC Requirements].

For cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%9B%BD%E9%99%85%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E8%BF%8E%E6%96%B0%E5%91%A8%E6%B4%BB%E5%8A%A8%E5%AE%89%E6%8E%92%E4%B8%8E%E5%BF%85%E5%8F%82%E5%8A%A0%E7%8E%AF%E8%8A%82) to settle their fees, ensuring transparent exchange rates and trackable delivery times.

## Social Events and Cultural Integration

**Social events** are the key to helping international students build their networks during O-Week. The ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) organises more than 50 events, including the **International Student Welcome Party**, a **cultural food festival** and **sports challenges**. The February 2024 welcome party was held in **Kambri Plaza**, drew around 2,100 new students and featured a buffet and live music [ANU 2024, ANUSA O-Week Report].

### Club Carnival

The **Club Carnival** usually takes place on day 3 of O-Week, when more than 200 registered ANU student clubs (covering academic, cultural, sporting, volunteering and other categories) set up stalls in **Union Court**. New students can sign up on the spot, with annual membership fees typically between $5 and $20. The 2024 carnival handed out around 8,500 club brochures, and the most popular clubs included the **ANUSA International Students' Department** and the **ANU Debating Society**.

### Canberra City Tour

On day 5 of O-Week, ANU runs a **Canberra City Bus Tour** that takes new students past landmarks including **Parliament House**, the **National Museum** and **Lake Burley Griffin**. The tour is free for new students; in 2024, 1,450 people signed up and departed in 6 buses [ANU 2024, O-Week City Tour Data]. The tour lasts about 4 hours with commentary in English and Chinese, helping new students get their bearings in the city.

## Health and Safety Essentials

**Health and safety training** is a non-negotiable part of O-Week. ANU requires all new international students to attend the **Campus Safety Briefing**, which covers emergency evacuation procedures, the night-time ANU Security Escort service and mental health support resources. The ANU Security Office handled more than 1,200 safety-related calls in 2023, including 840 night escort requests [ANU 2023, Security Services Annual Report].

### Mental Health Resources

ANU offers **free counselling services** through the ANU Counselling Centre, located in the **Health and Wellbeing Centre** (Building 156). New students can book a 15-minute introductory session during O-Week to learn how the service works. The centre saw 320 new students during O-Week 2024, about 65% of them international [ANU 2024, Counselling Centre O-Week Data]. ANU also runs a **24-hour wellbeing helpline** on 1800 808 374.

### Emergency Contacts

During O-Week, every new student receives an **emergency contact card** listing key numbers: campus security (6125 2249), Canberra Police (131 444) and **ACT Ambulance** (000). Around campus there are **72 Blue Light Emergency Phones** located in teaching buildings, residential areas and car parks. New students must also complete the online **Safety Quiz** during O-Week — passing it unlocks campus network access.

## Academic Support and Skills Workshops

The **Academic Skills Workshops** are O-Week training sessions designed specifically for international students and run by the ANU **Academic Skills Centre**. They cover three modules — **academic writing basics**, **time management strategies** and **exam preparation techniques** — with each session lasting about 60 minutes. During O-Week 2024, 24 workshops were held with a combined attendance of 1,800 [ANU 2024, Academic Skills Centre Workshop Data].

### Language Support Programs

The **English Language Support** program offers free tutoring for international students whose first language is not English, including **one-on-one writing consultations** and a **Conversation Club**. During O-Week, new students can register at the **Marie Reay Teaching Centre** (MRTC); 600 students completed registration in 2024. Tutors are ANU postgraduate linguistics students, and each student receives an average of four free sessions.

### Library Research Skills Training

**Research Skills Training** is provided by the Chifley Library and covers **literature search strategies**, **data management tools** and **reference formats** (APA/MLA/Harvard). During O-Week 2024, the library ran 8 **EndNote introductory workshops** capped at 30 places each; every place was booked within 3 days of registration opening [ANU 2024, Library Training Data]. Students who complete the training earn a **Digital Badge** they can add to their LinkedIn profile.

## FAQ

### Q1: Is O-Week compulsory? What happens if I miss it?

ANU requires international students to attend the **visa verification and enrolment session** on day one of O-Week; missing it can lead to your student account being frozen. For non-compulsory sessions (such as social events), absence does not affect your enrolment status, but attending at least 2–3 academic workshops is recommended. Around 98% of new international students completed the mandatory enrolment process at O-Week 2024 [ANU 2024, O-Week Participation Statistics].

### Q2: How do I enrol in courses during O-Week? Can I swap courses later?

New students enrol through the **ISIS system** during O-Week and must finalise their selection by the Friday of week 1. If a course is full or clashes with your timetable, you can request a **course swap** free of charge up to **Sunday of week 2**. ANU processed more than 1,200 course swap requests in Semester 1 2024, with an average turnaround of 24 hours [ANU 2024, Timetable and Enrolment Data].

### Q3: Should I buy textbooks in advance during O-Week?

The ANU **Co-op bookshop** (Union Court) offers a **textbook reservation service** during O-Week, but new students are advised to buy after classes start in week 1, since some lecturers provide electronic copies or assign free online resources. In Semester 1 2024, around 35% of courses used open educational resources (OER) and required no physical textbooks [ANU 2024, Library Resources Report]. Second-hand textbooks can be bought through **ANU Student Facebook groups**, usually at 40% to 60% of the new-book price.
