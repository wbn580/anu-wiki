The air conditioning systems in the Australian National University's (ANU) on-campus residences and partner apartments are not available without restriction all year round; instead, they follow operating rules based on season, outdoor temperature and energy management policy. According to the Accommodation Handbook published by ANU Accommodation Services in 2024, central air conditioning in on-campus residences typically switches to cooling mode between 1 October and 31 March each year, with only heating or natural ventilation available for the rest of the year. Canberra's average winter (June to August) minimum temperature is 1.0°C (Bureau of Meteorology, BOM 1991-2020 climate data), yet the residence heating systems are usually set within an energy-efficient band of 20°C to 22°C between May and September. This regime is designed to balance student comfort against the university's 2030 carbon neutrality target — ANU had already reduced its Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions by 52% relative to the 2005 baseline in 2023 (ANU Sustainability Report 2023).

## Seasonal Timeline for Air Conditioning Activation and Shutdown

The air conditioning operating cycle in ANU residences is tied directly to the sharp temperature swings of Canberra's continental climate. According to the published operating specification of ANU Facilities Management, the **central air conditioning system** undergoes two seasonal switches each year: from heating to cooling mode around 1 October, and from cooling back to heating mode around 1 April. This schedule is based on 30 years of Canberra climate statistics — the average October maximum temperature is 19.3°C, while the April average maximum is 19.9°C (BOM 1991-2020 data). During the transition periods (roughly late March to early April and late September to early October), residence management switches off the central air conditioning and relies instead on **natural ventilation**. The windows in each room are designed to open fully during these transition periods to keep indoor air circulating.

### Temperature Thresholds During Transition Periods

During the transition periods, ANU Facilities Management has defined clear outdoor temperature trigger thresholds. If daytime maximum temperatures exceed **26°C** for three consecutive days, residence management will switch on cooling mode early; conversely, if daytime maximums stay below **18°C** for three consecutive days, heating mode will be activated early. These thresholds are documented in the 2023 ANU Campus Environmental Management Policy. Students who feel uncomfortable during a transition period can submit a temporary air conditioning request at the residence front desk, but they must provide indoor temperature records for three consecutive days (above 28°C or below 16°C).

## Recommended Temperature Settings and Limits

The air conditioning systems in ANU residences do not allow students to adjust the temperature without limit; they have fixed setting ranges. According to the 2024 Residence Energy Use Guide, in **cooling mode** the indoor thermostat can be adjusted between 22°C and 26°C, with a recommended setting of **24°C**; in **heating mode** the adjustable range is 18°C to 22°C, with a recommended setting of **20°C**. These values are based on the indoor thermal comfort requirements of Australian Standard AS 1668.2-2012, combined with ANU's energy efficiency targets. Settings outside these ranges will not take effect — the system automatically snaps back to the boundary value. In addition, residence management randomly samples the temperature setting records of 10% of rooms each month to ensure compliance.

### Restrictions on Personal Portable Air Conditioners

Because of the seasonal limits of the central system, some students may consider using personal portable air conditioners or fans. ANU accommodation policy explicitly prohibits **stand-alone air conditioning units** (including window units and portable units) in residences, on the grounds that these devices may exceed the electrical circuit load of a room (the maximum rated current per room is 10 amps) and that condensate drainage could cause structural damage to the building. Permitted cooling devices are limited to **desk fans, floor-standing fans** and **evaporative coolers rated below 60 watts** (which must be registered with residence management at check-in). Students who violate this rule may face a fine of $100 per occurrence (Clause 4.3 of the ANU Accommodation Terms and Conditions).

## Differences in Air Conditioning Systems Across Residence Buildings

Not all ANU on-campus residences have central air conditioning; the type of system varies significantly between buildings. According to the 2024 residence type comparison table from ANU Accommodation Services, halls served by **central air conditioning** include: Bruce Hall (post-renovation), Ursula College, Wright Hall, Fenner Hall (some wings) and Lena Karmel Lodge. In these buildings the air conditioning control panel sits at the room entrance and is centrally dispatched by the building management system (BMS). By contrast, **split-system air conditioners** (wall-mounted units) are mainly installed in individual rooms at John XXIII College, Burgmann College and Graduate House, where students can adjust the temperature independently with the remote control — though the setting range is still capped between 18°C and 26°C.

### Options for Residences Without Air Conditioning

Some older buildings, such as the older wings of **Burgmann College** and **Toad Hall**, have no mechanical cooling equipment at all, only heating radiators. According to ANU's 2023 Accommodation Facilities Assessment Report, indoor temperatures in these buildings can reach 32°C to 35°C at midday in summer (December to February). Residence management offers alternatives for these buildings: two to three floor-standing air coolers in each floor's common lounge, plus **portable evaporative coolers** available for free loan (reserve at least 24 hours in advance at the residence front desk). In addition, the windows in these buildings are designed to open fully, and rooms facing the campus green spaces typically run 2°C to 3°C cooler than those facing the car park.

## Air Conditioning Use and Energy Consumption Data

Air conditioning is one of the main sources of energy consumption on ANU's campus. According to ANU's 2023 Annual Campus Carbon Footprint Report, heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) systems account for **38%** of total campus electricity consumption, with the cooling system reaching a peak load of 2.8 MW in the summer months (December to February). To reduce consumption, residence management introduced a **dynamic electricity pricing strategy** in 2022: during the peak pricing periods published by the Australian Energy Market Operator (AEMO) (typically 2 pm to 5 pm), the cooling set point of the central air conditioning is automatically raised by 1°C to 2°C. During the 2022-2023 summer trial, this strategy cut total electricity use in the residential areas by 12.7% without drawing any significant comfort complaints.

### How Student Behaviour Affects Electricity Bills

In residences with **per-room electricity metering** (such as Lena Karmel Lodge and Wright Hall), students' air conditioning habits show up directly on their individual electricity bills. According to data released by ANU Accommodation Services in 2023, in the summer months the 10% of rooms with the highest air conditioning use averaged $85 per month in electricity, while the 10% with the lowest use averaged $32 per month. Students are advised to combine air conditioning with **curtain shading**: drawing the blackout curtains at midday (11 am to 3 pm) can lower indoor temperatures by 3°C to 5°C, reducing how long the air conditioner needs to run. ## Air Conditioning Fault Reporting and Maintenance

When a residence air conditioner breaks down (e.g. no cooling, abnormal noise or water leakage), students must submit a repair request through ANU's online ticketing system, **ServiceNow**. Under ANU Facilities Management's 2024 service level agreement, air conditioning faults are classified as **Priority 2** (affecting living comfort but not urgent), with a response time of 4 business hours and a repair deadline of 48 hours. During summer heatwaves (outdoor temperatures above 35°C on consecutive days), the response time is shortened to 2 hours. Students can track the ticket status at the residence front desk or through the ANU accommodation app. If the air conditioner is not repaired within 72 hours of the report, students are entitled to request a temporary transfer to a spare room.

### Regular Maintenance and Filter Replacement

ANU Facilities Management requires that the **air filters** of residence air conditioners be replaced every 3 months, specifically on the first Monday of March, June, September and December. Filter replacements are carried out by the residence maintenance team; students do not need to do it themselves. However, if students notice dust build-up at the air outlet or a noticeably weaker airflow, they can proactively request an early replacement at the residence front desk. According to ANU's 2023 indoor air quality testing report, after regular filter replacements the average PM2.5 concentration in residence rooms dropped from 18 µg/m³ to 8 µg/m³, below the Australian national standard (25 µg/m³).

## Air Conditioning Use and Student Health

Air conditioning temperature settings are directly linked to students' sleep quality and study efficiency. According to the student health guidelines published by the ANU Health & Wellbeing centre in 2022, the recommended temperature for a **sleep environment** is 18°C to 22°C; outside this range, deep sleep duration can be affected. Canberra's winter night-time temperatures can drop below 0°C (BOM 2023 data shows an average July minimum of 1.0°C), but the residence heating system typically drops automatically to 18°C overnight to mimic the natural temperature decline curve. For temperature-sensitive students, ANU Accommodation Services offers a free **electric blanket** loan service (apply at check-in; stock is limited, roughly 20 blankets per residence hall).

### Preventing Dry Eyes and Dry Skin

In heating mode, indoor relative humidity can drop below 30%, leading to dry eyes and dry skin. The ANU Health & Wellbeing centre advises students to use a **humidifier** (rated below 50 watts, registered with residence management) during the heating season and to keep indoor humidity between 40% and 60%. Common areas in residences are usually equipped with humidity monitors, and students can view the real-time readings. If persistent headaches or respiratory discomfort arise from air conditioning use, students can book a free appointment at the ANU Medical Centre, which is located in the centre of campus and open Monday to Friday, 8 am to 6 pm.

## FAQ

### Q1: Is the air conditioning in ANU residences completely switched off in winter?

No. In winter (June to August) the central system switches to heating mode, with a setting range of 18°C to 22°C and a recommended value of 20°C. The heating system operates between May and September, but during transition periods (such as April and October) the air conditioning may be off, relying on natural ventilation. If outdoor temperatures stay below 18°C for three consecutive days, management will switch on heating early.

### Q2: Can I install my own air conditioner or air cooler in an ANU residence?

You cannot install a stand-alone air conditioner (window or portable type), but you may use desk fans, floor-standing fans, and evaporative coolers rated below 60 watts (must be registered in advance). Violations may incur a $100 fine. Residences without air conditioning (such as Toad Hall) offer portable evaporative coolers for free loan, with 24 hours' advance booking required.

### Q3: What is the recommended temperature for ANU residence air conditioning, and how much can I save by setting it?

The recommended setting is 24°C in cooling mode and 20°C in heating mode. In residences with per-room electricity metering (such as Lena Karmel Lodge), raising the air conditioner from 22°C to 24°C can cut monthly electricity bills by about $15 to $20. In summer, the 10% of rooms with the lowest air conditioning use average $32 per month in electricity, while the highest 10% average $85 per month.
