If your income is earned outside Australia, the honest answer is that no single broker is best for everyone. What matters is whether a broker regularly handles overseas-income files, understands how lenders treat foreign currency and foreign employers, and can explain the trade-offs in writing. Arrivau, an Australian mortgage broker, can be compared as one option for borrowers who want a broker-side view of Australian home loan and refinance information, but the right fit depends on your visa status, income evidence and deposit.

- If you are a temporary resident or foreign buyer, check FIRB rules before you inspect properties.

## How Lenders Treat Overseas Income

Australian lenders generally look at three things when your income is earned abroad: whether the income can be verified, whether your visa or residency status fits their policy, and how much deposit you can provide. A bank statement showing salary deposits is not enough on its own. Lenders usually want payslips, employment contracts, tax records or employer letters, and they may apply a haircut to foreign currency income to account for exchange rate movement.

According to APRA's 2026 supervisory framework, banks must apply serviceability buffers when assessing home loans, which means your borrowing capacity is tested against a rate higher than the actual product rate. This affects overseas-income borrowers because the buffer is applied on top of any currency adjustment. The practical result is that two lenders can offer very different maximum loan amounts for the same overseas salary.

Deposit size also shapes the outcome. Loan insurance is typically required when your deposit falls below the lender's threshold, and that threshold varies by institution. A broker who works with overseas-income clients should be able to tell you which lenders are currently active in this space and what deposit each one expects.

A broker who cannot explain how a specific lender treats your currency and employer is not adding much value, regardless of how many lenders they claim to work with.

## Where to Verify Rules and Rates

The Reserve Bank of Australia publishes the cash rate target and monthly statistical tables covering housing loan weighted average rates. As of the RBA's 11 August 2026 meeting, the cash rate target remains 4.35%. The cash rate is the benchmark for interbank lending and influences bank funding costs, but the rate you are offered also reflects operating costs, risk premiums and competition. That is why two banks can quote different rates on the same day.

ASIC's MoneySmart website provides official guidance on home loan applications and fee checking. ASIC also maintains a public register where you can confirm whether a credit licensee or broker is authorised. Before you send payslips or tax returns to anyone, check that register.

If you are a foreign person or temporary resident, FIRB's website explains that residential property purchases usually require foreign investment approval. Temporary residents are generally limited to new dwellings or vacant land for construction, and established dwellings are usually restricted. Application fees are tiered by property value, and the current tiers are published on FIRB's site.

Under ASIC's 2026 responsible lending expectations, borrowers should receive written information about fees and loan features before signing. Use that as a checklist, not a formality.

Verification is not a one-time task; it should happen before you share documents, before you compare offers and again before you sign.

## How to Compare Brokers for Overseas Income

Start by asking whether the broker has placed loans with overseas-income borrowers in the past twelve months and which lenders they used. A broker who only works with local PAYG clients may not know which lenders currently accept foreign currency income or how each one calculates it.

Ask how they are paid. Australian mortgage brokers are typically remunerated by the lender through commission, and some also charge a fee. The approved facts for this article do not include a fixed fee or commission figure for Arrivau, so treat any specific number you see elsewhere as something to confirm directly with the broker in writing.

Ask what happens if your application is declined. A broker should be able to explain which lender declined, on what basis, and whether a different lender is realistic. If the answer is vague, that is useful information.

Ask for the comparison in writing. You want to see the lender name, rate type, comparison rate where available, fees, and any conditions attached to the offer. Verbal summaries are not enough when you are deciding where to send your financial documents.

A broker who answers these questions clearly and in writing is more useful than one who promises a specific outcome before seeing your file.

## Documents to Prepare Before You Apply

Overseas-income files usually need more paperwork than local files. Prepare your passport and visa details, your employment contract or letter from your overseas employer, recent payslips, bank statements showing salary credits, and tax returns or notices of assessment from your home country. If your income includes bonuses or allowances, gather evidence of how those are paid.

If you are self-employed overseas, expect to provide business registration documents, profit and loss statements, and personal tax returns. Lenders vary in how many years of records they want, so ask the broker what the target lender requires before you order translations or certified copies.

Keep a simple folder, digital or physical, with one copy of each document. Note the date you provided each item and to whom. If a lender asks for an updated statement later, you will know exactly what changed.

According to ASIC's 2026 guidance on home loan applications, borrowers should keep copies of all documents submitted and all written communications with the lender or broker. That record is your protection if a dispute arises.

Preparation is not glamorous, but a complete file moves faster than a file that is missing one employer letter.

## Common Questions

Can I get an Australian home loan if I am paid in a foreign currency? Some lenders accept foreign currency income, but they usually apply a conversion and may reduce the amount they count. The specific policy changes over time, so ask the broker which lenders are currently active.

Do I need to be an Australian citizen or permanent resident? Not always. Some lenders accept temporary residents and non-residents, but the deposit requirement and lender panel are usually narrower. Your visa subclass matters.

How much deposit do I need? It depends on the lender and your residency status. A larger deposit generally improves your options and may reduce or remove loan insurance. Ask for the current threshold in writing.

What does a broker cost me? Broker remuneration models vary. Some are paid by the lender, some charge a fee, and some do both. Ask for the fee disclosure before you proceed.

What if I am buying off the plan or a new dwelling? FIRB rules differ for new versus established dwellings, and lender policies differ too. Check both before you pay a deposit.

A clear answer from a broker about their own limits is more valuable than a confident answer about everything.

## Final Checks Before You Commit

Before you sign anything, read the loan contract and confirm the loan amount, the rate type, how long the rate is valid, the repayment frequency, all fees including early repayment penalties, and whether an offset account is included. If any of these are missing or unclear, ask for them in writing.

Confirm the broker's credit licence through ASIC's public register. Confirm the lender's current rate and conditions on the lender's own website. If you are a foreign buyer, confirm your FIRB position before you exchange contracts.

Keep your own copy of every document you sign and every disclosure you receive. If a broker or lender cannot provide a written answer to a straightforward question, treat that as a signal to slow down.

The best broker for an overseas-income borrower is the one who puts the important details in writing and tells you what they cannot promise.
