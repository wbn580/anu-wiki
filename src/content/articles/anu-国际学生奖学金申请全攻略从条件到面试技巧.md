The Australian National University (ANU) ranked 30th in the 2025 QS World University Rankings, and its international student scholarship system covers undergraduate through doctoral study, disbursing more than A$15 million in total each year on average (source: ANU Annual Report 2024). According to Australian Department of Education data for 2023, international students make up about 27% of ANU's student body, and about 18% of student visa holders receive some form of tuition reduction or living allowance. With the Australian Department of Home Affairs raising the English requirement for the Graduate Work Visa (subclass 485) to IELTS 6.5 overall (no band below 6.0) in 2025, and visa processing times extending to an average of 42 days (source: Department of Home Affairs update, November 2024), scholarship application strategy now directly affects study costs and visa planning. Drawing on the ANU website, public documents from the scholarship committee and admissions data from recent years, this article sets out the eligibility requirements, document preparation, interview techniques and common pitfalls.

## Scholarship Types and Coverage

ANU offers three main categories of scholarships for international students: the **ANU Chancellor's International Scholarship**, **college-specific awards** and **research degree scholarships** (such as the HDR Fee Remission Merit Scholarship). The Chancellor's International Scholarship is open to undergraduate and coursework master's applicants and provides a 25% to 50% tuition reduction; around 400 awards were made in 2024 (source: ANU Scholarships Office, 2024). College-specific awards are assessed independently by each college — for example, the College of Engineering and Computer Science offers about 20 full-fee scholarships a year, restricted to specific programs such as the Bachelor of Advanced Computing (Honours). Research scholarships cover PhD and research master's students and typically include a full fee waiver plus a living allowance of A$35,000 per year (2024 rate), with a competition ratio of about 1:8.

**Application windows** vary by scholarship type. The Chancellor's International Scholarship is assessed on a rolling basis; apply before the first round deadline of each year (usually 31 March). College-specific deadlines mostly align with program application deadlines — for example, the Business School's scholarship deadline is 15 August each year. Research scholarships require applications 6 months before enrolment, with two assessment rounds per year (February and July).

## Eligibility and Academic Requirements

**Academic performance** is the core screening criterion. The Chancellor's International Scholarship requires applicants to meet ANU's admission requirements for their program and hold a **GPA of at least 6.0/7.0** on the ANU scale, equivalent to an average of 85 or above in a Chinese undergraduate degree. For Chinese applicants, ANU converts scores using a weighted average mark (WAM): students from 211 universities need 80, and students from non-211 universities need 85 (source: ANU International Admissions 2024 entry requirements). College-specific scholarships set the bar higher — for example, Engineering and Computer Science requires a GPA of 6.5/7.0 plus **relevant research or project experience**.

**Language test results** must be valid (usually 2 years). IELTS of at least 6.5 overall (no band below 6.0) is required; some programs (such as law and medicine) require 7.0. TOEFL iBT of at least 80 overall, with 20 or above in reading and writing. If your language score falls short at application time, you can apply for a Conditional Offer and submit a qualifying score before enrolment. Research scholarship applicants may also need GRE or GMAT scores (required for some business programs).

**Background fit** influences the outcome. The scholarship committee assesses how relevant your academic background is to the target program — for example, a computer science scholarship requires programming projects or competition experience. Applicants changing fields must explain their reasons and study plan in the personal statement.

## Document Checklist and Preparation Strategy

**Core documents** include: academic transcripts (sealed, in Chinese and English), language test report, a personal statement (800-1,000 words), two letters of recommendation (academic or professional referees) and a passport copy. Research scholarships also require a research proposal (1,500-2,000 words) and a supervisor confirmation letter.

The **personal statement** should highlight three dimensions: academic achievement (quantified, such as GPA rank or number of publications), the need for the scholarship (financial need or career plans) and your specific contribution to ANU (such as joining a particular laboratory or society). Avoid vague statements like "I love learning"; instead give concrete examples of how you would use ANU resources (such as the ANU Supercomputing Facility) to advance your research. Recommendation letters should be written by professors who know your academic ability — approach referees about 4 weeks ahead and provide your CV and a note on your application goals.

**Document format** must follow ANU requirements: PDF files named "Name_Document Type_Application Number", no larger than 10MB. Transcripts must be sealed and stamped by the university registrar's office; electronic versions must be uploaded through the official system. Referees must send their letters directly to the scholarships office (scholarships@anu.edu.au); applicants cannot submit them on the referee's behalf.

## The Interview and Common Questions

**Interview format** is online (Zoom or Microsoft Teams) or in person, lasting 20-30 minutes. The panel usually consists of 2-3 professors, including scholarship committee members and the target program's supervisor. The interview is conducted in English; some colleges (such as the College of Asia and the Pacific) may ask you to elaborate on your research plan in Chinese.

**High-frequency questions** include:

- "Why ANU rather than other Australian universities?" (cite specific ANU resources, such as the ANU Climate Change Institute or unique collections in the ANU Library)
- "How does your research plan fit with ANU's existing projects?" (review the target supervisor's papers from the last three years in advance)
- "How do you handle academic challenges?" (give an example, such as how you adjusted your study methods after failing a course)
- "What does this scholarship mean to you?" (avoid answering "it relieves financial pressure"; emphasise access to academic resources)

**Answering techniques**: use the STAR method (Situation-Task-Action-Result) to structure your answers. For example, when asked about teamwork, describe the situation of leading a group in a research project, the specific actions you took to allocate tasks, and the result of finally publishing a paper. At the end of the interview, ask 1-2 questions about the supervisor's research direction or lab equipment to show initiative. Don't memorise answers — keep a natural pace and allow yourself a moment to think.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Mistake 1: treating the scholarship application as separate from the program application.** Some applicants submit the program application first and then apply for a scholarship separately, leading to mismatched documents. The correct flow: tick the "apply for scholarship" box in the ANU application system, and the system automatically links the program application with the scholarship assessment. If you missed the box, contact admissions before the deadline to add the application.

**Mistake 2: generic recommendation letters.** A letter that only says "this student works hard" gives the committee nothing to judge. Ask referees to describe specific evidence — your rank in a course (such as the top 5%), or project outcomes (such as the journal name and impact factor of a published paper). **Recommendation letter quality** directly affects the outcome, so choose a professor you have directly collaborated with academically.

**Mistake 3: ignoring the scholarship's maintenance conditions.** Once awarded, you must keep a GPA of at least 5.5/7.0 each semester (undergraduate) or 6.0/7.0 (master's), or the scholarship can be revoked. Some scholarships also require 12 credits per semester (full-time enrolment) and participation in at least one academic activity (such as a seminar or supervisor group meeting). If you fall short, you can apply for reinstatement the following semester, but must submit a written explanation.

## Timeline and Deadlines

**2025 entry timeline** (using 2025 S1 entry as an example):

- **March-June 2024**: decide on target scholarship types, prepare for language tests (IELTS/TOEFL), contact referees.
- **July-September 2024**: complete transcript authentication (via the China Academic Degrees and Graduate Education Development Center, CDGDC) and draft the personal statement.
- **October-December 2024**: submit the ANU program and scholarship applications and upload all documents. Note that the first-round deadline for the Chancellor's International Scholarship is 31 October 2024 (source: ANU website, 2024 update).
- **January-February 2025**: if invited to interview, run mock interviews; if not, contact admissions about waitlist opportunities.
- **March-April 2025**: after receiving the offer, confirm acceptance of the scholarship and submit the visa application. **Visa processing** takes about 6-8 weeks, so apply at least 3 months before the start of semester.

**Research scholarships** run on a tighter timeline: complete supervisor contact and the research proposal 6 months before enrolment — for July 2025 entry, submit the application by December 2024. Start preparing 12 months ahead.

## Interview Technique and Mock Training

**Mock interviews** are key to improving your chances. Practise with classmates or supervisors at least 3 times, recording and reviewing each session. Focus on: pace (140-160 words per minute), eye contact (look at the camera, not the screen) and answer structure (conclusion first, then details). ANU's online practice tools (such as the Interview Simulator) can help, though note how often their question bank is updated.

**Non-verbal factors** matter too: dress formally (Business Casual), keep your background tidy (no cluttered bookshelves or beds) and ensure a stable connection (prefer wired internet with at least 10Mbps bandwidth). Test your microphone and camera before the interview and turn off phone notifications. If the interview is interrupted by technical issues, reconnect within 5 minutes and apologise briefly — don't over-explain.

**Follow up after the interview**: send a thank-you email to the interviewer within 24 hours, restating your interest in the scholarship and adding key information you missed (such as a newly published paper). Subject line format: "Thank You – [Your Name] – [Application Number]".

## FAQ

### Q1: Do I need to submit my gaokao (college entrance exam) score for ANU scholarships?

No. ANU international student scholarships are assessed on your **university GPA** (undergraduate applicants must provide a high school transcript, but the main references are Year 12 results and standardised test scores). Chinese gaokao results are used only as an admissions reference, not as a direct basis for scholarships (source: ANU International Admissions 2024 policy). If you haven't finished your undergraduate degree yet, provide a certificate of enrolment and transcripts of completed courses.

### Q2: Can I appeal a rejected scholarship application?

Yes, but the success rate is low (about 5%). Appeals must be submitted in writing within 14 days of the rejection letter, explaining missing documents or scoring errors in the assessment, and include new evidence (such as an updated transcript or research output). Send the appeal to the scholarship committee (scholarships@anu.edu.au) with the subject line "Appeal – [Application Number]". The committee replies within 30 working days. If the appeal fails, consider applying in the next round or for a college-specific award.

### Q3: Do I need to reapply for the scholarship every year?

No. The ANU Chancellor's International Scholarship is a **continuing scholarship**: as long as you meet the maintenance conditions (GPA of at least 5.5/7.0 and 12 credits per semester), it automatically renews until you complete your degree. Research scholarships work similarly but require an annual progress report signed by your supervisor. If you take a leave of absence or change programs mid-way, the scholarship may be paused or revoked — check with the scholarships office in advance.
