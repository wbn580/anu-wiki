Kitchen management rules in ANU on-campus accommodation — food labelling, cleaning rosters and storage locker allocation — shape daily life for the roughly 4,000 students living in residence. According to the Residential Halls Handbook published by ANU Accommodation in 2024, every resident must label their personal food containers with their name and date, and unlabelled items may be thrown out. A separate internal survey run by the ANU accommodation services team in 2023 found that over 67% of kitchen disputes stem from unlabelled food or cleaning tasks that weren't done on time. With accommodation applications for the 2025 academic year up about 12% year on year, getting these rules right is especially important for new arrivals — it prevents conflict and keeps shared spaces running in an orderly way.

## Food labelling rules

**Food labelling** is the central rule in kitchen management across ANU residence buildings. Under the Residential Halls Handbook (ANU Accommodation, 2024), any food stored in the shared fridge, freezer or on the shelves must be clearly marked with the resident's **full name** and the **date it was stored**, written with a permanent marker on the container or packaging. Unlabelled food is treated as unowned, and accommodation staff may remove it without notice.

### Label format and placement

Labels should be written directly on the container itself rather than on the lid or a peel-off sticker, so they can't be lost. Waterproof markers are recommended so that condensation doesn't blur the writing. The same rule applies to small containers such as personal condiments and sauces. Offending items are usually cleared during the weekly routine inspections, and each building's residential advisor (RA) posts the clearance time on the noticeboard in advance.

### Managing special foods

For perishables — fresh meat, dairy and opened condiments — ANU recommends adding an "opened on" date to the label. Under the ANU Health & Safety Guidelines (2023), perishable food stored for more than 7 days is treated as expired and discarded. Allergen information isn't mandatory, but residents are encouraged to add it to reduce the risk of cross-contact.

## Cleaning rosters

**Cleaning rosters** are ANU's main mechanism for keeping shared kitchens hygienic. Every kitchen has a weekly roster drawn up by the RA and covering all residents. Under the Kitchen Cleaning Protocol published by ANU Accommodation in 2023, each resident takes on at least one cleaning task per week, including **scrubbing the stovetop**, **cleaning the microwave**, **mopping the floor**, **emptying the bins** and **wiping down the inside of the fridge**.

### How rosters run and are monitored

Rosters are posted on the kitchen noticeboard or published online through the accommodation management system. Residents sign the roster and note the time once a task is done. The RA does spot checks every Friday, and residents who skip their task receive a written warning. According to ANU Residential Experience Team statistics for 2024, residents who miss their cleaning task twice in a row may have A$50 deducted from their accommodation deposit to cover a cleaning service.

### Cleaning supplies and equipment

Each kitchen is stocked with standard supplies: multi-purpose cleaner, sponges, mops and bin liners. Residents are responsible for washing their own dishes. ANU accommodation services tops up the shared supplies at the start of each semester, but residents are advised to keep extra cleaner on hand during peak periods such as exam week.

## Storage locker allocation

**Locker allocation** manages the limited storage space in each kitchen. ANU residence buildings typically give every resident one fixed-size locker for dry goods, crockery and cookware. Under the Residential Halls Handbook (ANU Accommodation, 2024), lockers are allocated on a **first come, first served** basis and registered through the accommodation management system at check-in.

### Allocation process and sizes

Standard lockers are 40 cm wide, 50 cm deep and 30 cm high, holding roughly 60 litres of dry goods. Residents select and lock in a locker via the online form within 48 hours of moving in; anyone who misses the window is assigned one at random by the RA. Locker keys or codes are issued by the front desk, and replacing a lost key costs A$15 (ANU Accommodation, 2024).

### Locker use restrictions

Lockers are for non-perishable food and kitchen items only. Flammables (such as alcohol burners or gas canisters), live animals and anything that could cause odours are prohibited. Under the ANU Fire Safety Policy (2023), storing flammables in a locker leads to immediate confiscation, referral to accommodation management and a possible A$200 fine. Lockers must be emptied within 7 days of the end of semester, after which leftover items are disposed of as waste.

## Using the shared fridge and freezer

**Shared fridges** and **freezers** follow rules similar to the personal lockers, but with more shared space. Every kitchen typically has 2 to 4 fridges, each with tiered shelving where each shelf corresponds to specific room numbers or resident groups. Under the Kitchen Usage Guidelines published by ANU Accommodation in 2023, residents may only use their assigned shelf, and each shelf holds at most 3 medium containers (no more than 2 litres each).

### Temperature checks and cleaning

ANU accommodation services records fridge temperatures weekly, keeping the chiller section below 4°C and the freezer below −18°C. The RA alerts maintenance immediately if temperatures drift. The cleaning team does a deep clean of the fridge interior every two weeks, and residents must remove their items 24 hours beforehand. Anything left behind is moved to a basket marked "to be claimed" and kept for no more than 48 hours.

### Food sharing and rescue

ANU encourages residents to share food before it goes to waste. Each kitchen has a "shared food zone" — usually a shelf in the fridge marked "Free Food" or a dedicated basket — where residents can leave soon-to-expire food that's still edible for others to take. According to the ANU Sustainability Report (2024), the scheme cut food waste by about 1.2 tonnes in 2023. ## Violations and the appeals process

The **violation process** is designed to keep kitchen rules enforced. Under the Residential Halls Handbook (ANU Accommodation, 2024), breaches fall into three tiers: Tier 1 covers unlabelled food or missed cleaning tasks, with a verbal warning for a first offence; Tier 2 covers repeat offences or using someone else's storage space, which brings a written warning and a A$20 deposit deduction; Tier 3 covers storing flammables or deliberately damaging shared property, which triggers an immediate residential discipline hearing and can lead to loss of accommodation.

### Appeals

Residents who disagree with a penalty can submit a written appeal to accommodation management within 5 business days of receiving the notice. Appeals must include supporting evidence such as photos, timestamps or witness statements. The ANU accommodation appeals committee — one accommodation manager, one RA representative and one resident representative — usually delivers a decision within 10 business days. According to ANU Residential Experience Team data for 2024, about 15% of appeals are upheld in full or in part.

### Preventative measures

To cut down on breaches, ANU runs a kitchen rules information session at the start of each semester covering labelling techniques, cleaning tips and locker demonstrations. Residents who attend earn A$5 in accommodation account credit, usable toward laundry or printing. Kitchen noticeboards also get regular updates on violation statistics and best-practice examples.

## Differences between buildings

Kitchen rules do **differ between ANU residence buildings**. The core rules (labelling, cleaning, lockers) are set centrally by accommodation management, but the specific enforcement details and extra clauses are decided by each building's RA team. According to the Hall-Specific Appendices published by ANU Accommodation in 2024, buildings differ in the following ways.

### Examples

At Bruce Hall, for instance, the cleaning roster runs fortnightly and residents may use their own label stickers; at Ursula Hall the roster is weekly and the uniform label template provided by the accommodation is compulsory. For lockers, Wamburun Hall uses a ballot rather than first come, first served. For food sharing, Lena Karmel Lodge has a dedicated sharing fridge while other buildings only use baskets. Residents should ask their RA for the detailed rulebook for their building at check-in.

### Where to find the rules

Residents can find the specific rules through the "Kitchen Rules" section of the accommodation management system (Portal), the building noticeboards, and the accommodation orientation session at the start of each semester. ANU accommodation services also publishes a Kitchen Rules FAQ PDF, available from the front desk or for download online; last updated in February 2024, it includes a comparison table of rules across all buildings.

## FAQ

### Q1: If my food gets thrown out by mistake, can I get compensation?

Under the Residential Halls Handbook (ANU Accommodation, 2024), food removed because it wasn't labelled doesn't qualify for compensation. But if food is discarded by mistake due to staff error (such as an unannounced spot clearance), you can appeal within 5 business days, and once verified, accommodation management will compensate you with a supermarket voucher worth no more than A$30. In 2023, 47 such appeals were lodged and 12 received compensation.

### Q2: Can I change my cleaning roster, e.g. swap tasks with my roommate?

Yes. Under the Kitchen Cleaning Protocol published by ANU Accommodation in 2023, residents can swap cleaning tasks, but the swap must be noted on the roster with both names and the swap date, and the RA notified 24 hours in advance. After a swap, both residents share responsibility for the quality of the task. Internal statistics from 2024 show that about 23% of residents swapped tasks at least once during the semester.

### Q3: What if I lose my locker key? How long does a replacement take?

Report the loss to the front desk right away and pay the A$15 replacement fee (ANU Accommodation, 2024). Replacement usually takes 2 business days, during which you'll get a temporary storage box (supplied by the front desk, sized 30 cm x 40 cm x 20 cm). If someone finds and returns the key, the fee is refunded. In 2023, about 8% of residents experienced a lost key.
