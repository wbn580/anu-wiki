ANU Student Accommodation Fire Safety Guide and Evacuation Route Maps

ANU is the only university in Australia where every student residence building is fitted with an automatic sprinkler system, which achieved 100% coverage in the 2023 annual inspection by ACT Fire & Rescue. According to the National Construction Code published by the Australian Building Codes Board (ABCB, 2022), fire alarm response times in all new student residences must be kept within 30 seconds. As of 2024, ANU manages 17 on-campus residential buildings housing more than 6,500 students, of which 80% were built after 2010 and use the latest fire-resistant materials and zoned evacuation design. With the Semester 1 2025 intake approaching, understanding the specific fire protection equipment layout and evacuation route maps of each residence is essential basic safety preparation before new students move in.

## Fire Alarm System Configuration in Residences

All ANU student residences use a fire detection and alarm system designed to **AS 1670.1-2018**. Certified by ACT Fire & Rescue (2023), the system includes smoke detectors, heat detectors and manual call points, with at least 2 alarm points on every floor. According to public data from ANU Facilities Management (2024), on-campus residences have one smoke detector installed per 45 square metres on average — a density higher than the ACT standard for ordinary residential buildings (one per 60 square metres).

### Alarm Response Procedure

When a detector is triggered, the system sends a signal to the residence management control centre within **15 seconds**. The control centre issues evacuation instructions through the broadcast system and simultaneously notifies ACT Fire & Rescue automatically. According to the ANU Safety Office (2023) Residential Accommodation Safety Handbook, the average time from alarm to the issue of full evacuation instructions is 45 seconds, below the 60-second maximum required by the national code.

### Location of Manual Call Points

**Red manual call points** are installed at the entrance of every residence building, at stairwell landings and in communal kitchen areas. Taking Bruce Hall as an example, its 8-storey building has 24 manual call points spaced no more than 30 metres apart, in line with Clause 4.3 of Australian Standard AS 1670.1. When new students move in, residence staff guide them to identify the two nearest call points.

## Evacuation Route Map Standards and Display Locations

Every ANU student room has a **floor-level evacuation route map** posted on the inside of the door. Printed in A4 on fluorescent material, it remains visible for 90 minutes after a power failure. According to ANU Facilities Management (2024) requirements, the map includes: the current room location (marked with a red dot), the two nearest emergency exits, the location of the fire stairs, the location of manual call points, and the assembly point number.

### How Often the Maps Are Updated

Evacuation route maps are reviewed at least once a year jointly by the ANU Safety Office (2023) and ACT Fire & Rescue. During the comprehensive inspection in February 2024, 3 routes across the 17 residences had changed due to renovations, and the replacement maps were installed within 7 business days. Students can view the latest electronic versions on the ANU safety portal (safety.anu.edu.au).

### Assembly Point System

Each residence building has **2–3 designated assembly points**, identified by letters (such as A, B, C), located at least 50 metres from the building. At Ursula Hall, for example, assembly point A is on the southern lawn and B in the northern car park, both marked with fluorescent signs. ACT Fire & Rescue drill records for 2023 show that from the start of evacuation to the completion of roll call at the assembly points took an average of 4 minutes 20 seconds.

## Special Fire Protection Areas and Equipment

ANU residences contain three types of special fire protection areas: **kitchen areas**, **laundry rooms** and **lift machine rooms**. Kitchen areas have dedicated heat detectors and an automatic fire suppression system that uses 3M Novec 1230 gas, which is non-corrosive to electronic equipment and meets the kitchen fire protection requirements of Australian Standard AS 2444.

### Kitchen Fire Protection Measures

Every communal kitchen is equipped with **two 4.5 kg dry chemical fire extinguishers** and one fire blanket, and the extinguisher inspection tags show the date of the most recent maintenance. According to ANU Accommodation Services (2024) data, kitchen areas across the residences triggered 12 false alarms in 2023, 8 of them caused by cooking fumes, none of which resulted in an actual fire.

### Lift Usage Ban

In all residences, lifts automatically **descend to the ground floor and are powered off** when the fire alarm is activated, and must not be used as an escape route. A red warning sign next to each lift door reads "FIRE – DO NOT USE". During the 2023 ANU safety drills, 2 students mistakenly entered a lift and were promptly guided by security staff to the fire stairs.

## Fire Stairs and Corridor Standards

The fire stairs in ANU residences are all concrete structures with a **60-minute fire rating**, 1.2 metres wide, allowing two people to descend side by side. Under Clause D1.2 of the National Construction Code (ABCB, 2022), each stairwell has fire doors on every level, and the self-closers shut them within 5 seconds to prevent the spread of smoke and fire.

### Fire Door Maintenance

Fire doors are compliance-checked twice a year by ACT Fire & Rescue (2023), focusing on the self-closer closing speed and the integrity of the seals. During the January 2024 inspection, 4 fire doors at Wright Hall had faulty self-closers, all replaced within 48 hours. Students must not use door wedges or ropes to hold fire doors open; offenders face a fine of up to AUD 500 (Clause 7.2 of the ANU accommodation contract).

### Corridor Signage System

**Green fluorescent evacuation direction signs** are installed every 15 metres in the fire escape corridors, 0.5 metres above the floor, meeting the emergency lighting requirements of Australian Standard AS 2293.1. The signs remain illuminated for 120 minutes after a power failure and cover all stairwell corners and exit locations.

## Annual Fire Drills and Training

ANU requires every residence to hold at least **1 full fire drill per semester**, or no fewer than 2 per year. According to ANU Safety Office (2023) records, 34 drills were organised in 2023, covering all 17 residences, with 5,800 student participations and a participation rate of 89%.

### Drill Procedure

Drills are run jointly by resident advisors (RAs) and ACT Fire & Rescue, usually on weekday evenings between 18:00 and 20:00. When the alarm sounds, students must evacuate along the nearest route to the assembly point within **3 minutes**. 2023 drill data shows an average evacuation time of 2 minutes 35 seconds, with Davey Lodge the fastest at 1 minute 48 seconds.

### Training Content

New students must complete a **30-minute online fire safety training module** when they move in, covering alarm recognition, memorising evacuation routes and how to use fire extinguishers. The training is delivered through the ANU learning management system (Wattle), and students who do not complete it cannot activate their residence access card. In Semester 1 2024, 1,200 new students completed the training, with a pass rate of 97%.

## Evacuation Plans for Students with Special Needs

ANU offers **Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs)** for students with limited mobility. According to the ANU Access & Inclusion Office (2024), 45 students applied for a PEEP in 2023, covering three groups: students with hearing impairments, visual impairments and mobility restrictions.

### How a PEEP Is Developed

Students must submit an application to Accommodation Services 14 days before moving in, and it is assessed jointly by the Safety Office and ACT Fire & Rescue (2023). After the assessment, 2 resident advisors are assigned as evacuation assistants, and a blue sign is posted at the student's door. All 2023 PEEP applications were approved within 10 business days.

### Refuge Areas

Each residence building has **1–2 fire refuge areas** located in the fire stairwells, at least 2 square metres in size and equipped with two-way intercoms. The walls of the refuge areas have a 120-minute fire rating and can accommodate 2 wheelchair users and 1 assistant. In the 2024 ACT Fire & Rescue drill tests, the communication equipment in the refuge areas remained operational in 95% of tests.

## Penalties for Breaches and Insurance Information

Breaching residence fire safety rules triggers the disciplinary procedure under **Clause 7 of the ANU accommodation contract**. According to ANU Accommodation Services (2024) data, 28 fire safety incidents were handled in 2023: 15 for covering smoke detectors, 8 for using naked flames in rooms, and 5 for blocking fire escape routes.

### Penalty Levels

Minor breaches (such as covering a detector) result in a written warning, while serious breaches (such as faking a fire alarm) can lead to **termination of the accommodation contract** with no refund of the accommodation fees already paid. In 2023, 3 students who lit candles in their rooms and triggered the alarm were fined AUD 200 and required to complete fire safety training.

### Insurance Coverage

ANU provides **public liability insurance** for all resident students, but this insurance does not cover loss of personal property. According to the Insurance Council of Australia (ICA, 2023) Student Accommodation Insurance Guide, students are advised to take out their own contents insurance, with annual premiums of about AUD 80–150, covering fire, water damage and theft. ## FAQ

### Q1: Do the fire alarms in ANU residences give false alarms? Do I still need to evacuate after a false alarm?

Yes, false alarms do occur from time to time. According to ANU Safety Office (2023) data, the alarms in the residences were triggered 47 times in 2023, of which 12 were false alarms (25.5%), mainly caused by cooking fumes or steam. **Every alarm requires immediate evacuation**, because the system cannot distinguish a real fire from a false alarm within 15 seconds. After evacuating, residents must wait until a resident advisor or firefighter confirms it is safe before returning; the average wait is 8 minutes.

### Q2: If I use a rice cooker or induction cooker in my room, will it trigger the alarm?

Yes. The heat detectors in the kitchen areas in particular are sensitive to high temperatures. Clause 6.3 of the ANU accommodation contract (2024) explicitly prohibits the use of rice cookers, induction cookers or air fryers in rooms; they may only be used in communal kitchens. In 2023, 8 alarms were triggered by induction cookers used in rooms, and the students involved were fined AUD 150. The trigger threshold of the kitchen heat detectors is 58°C, while the surface temperature of an induction cooker can reach 200°C.

### Q3: Where can I find the electronic version of my residence's evacuation route map?

Electronic evacuation route maps are available on the ANU safety portal (safety.anu.edu.au) and require a student ID login. The site provides PDF versions for every residence building, with the update date marked in the bottom-right corner of the file. As of December 2024, the electronic maps for all 17 residences had been updated to the latest versions, with file sizes of about 2–3 MB.
