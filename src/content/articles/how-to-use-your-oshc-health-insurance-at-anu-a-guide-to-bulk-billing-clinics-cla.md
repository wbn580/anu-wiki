The Australian Government requires all international students on a student visa (Subclass 500) to hold Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) to cover basic medical costs during their studies. According to 2024 data from the Department of Home Affairs, OSHC is a mandatory condition for visa grant [Department of Home Affairs, 2024, *Student Visa Application Requirements*]. For students at the Australian National University (ANU), OSHC is usually arranged through the university's partner insurers (such as Medibank, Allianz Care and Bupa), at an annual cost of between 477 and 632 AUD for a single policy [Australian Government Department of Health and Aged Care, 2024, *OSHC Rate Guide*]. This guide explains how to use OSHC effectively at ANU and across Canberra, covering how to identify **bulk billing clinics**, how to lodge claims online, and the full pathway for getting a specialist referral through a general practitioner (GP) — so international students can avoid the financial risk of paying for healthcare out of pocket.

## What OSHC Covers and Your Core Benefits

OSHC does not cover every medical item; its core coverage is defined by the Australian Government's *Health Insurance Act* (1973). A standard OSHC policy covers **Medicare Benefits Schedule (MBS)** listed in-hospital services (such as surgery and public hospital ward fees), out-of-hospital services (such as GP consultations and pathology tests), and emergency ambulance transport. According to a 2023 report by the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC), OSHC does not cover dental, optical (except optometry), physiotherapy or non-urgent psychological treatment — these extras must be paid for out of pocket or covered by add-on insurance [ACCC, 2023, *Private Health Insurance Report*].

### Waiting Periods and Pre-Existing Conditions
All OSHC policies apply a 2-month waiting period for **pre-existing conditions**, a 2-month waiting period for treatment of mental illness, and a 12-month waiting period for pregnancy-related services. Data from the Private Health Insurance Ombudsman (PHIO) for 2023 shows that around 8% of OSHC claims are rejected because the waiting period had not been served [PHIO, 2023, *Annual Complaints Report*]. Students should activate their policy before arriving in Australia so that cover is in effect from day one.

### Public vs Private Hospitals
In the Australian Capital Territory (ACT), public hospitals (such as Canberra Hospital) offer **fully covered** (100% MBS fee) hospital treatment, but the average wait for non-urgent surgery is 42 days (Australian Institute of Health and Welfare data, 2024) [AIHW, 2024, *Public Hospital Waiting Times Report*]. Private hospitals can shorten the wait, but you need to confirm whether your policy covers private hospital costs (OSHC typically covers only the MBS portion, with the gap paid out of pocket).

## Bulk Billing Clinics: The Zero Out-of-Pocket Path

Bulk billing is the most economical way for OSHC members to see a doctor. At a bulk billing clinic, the doctor bills your OSHC insurer directly and the patient pays nothing. According to 2024 data from the Australian Department of Health, the bulk billing rate in the ACT is 68.3%, below the national average of 76.1%, so students need to actively screen clinics [Australian Department of Health, 2024, *Medicare Bulk Billing Statistics*].

### Bulk Billing Clinics in Canberra
Three clinics on or near the ANU campus are explicitly bulk billing:
- **ANU Health Centre** (on campus, 10 Eggleston Road): bulk bills all OSHC students with no upfront payment required. Opening hours are Monday to Friday, 8:30-17:00.
- **Canberra After Hours Locum Medical Service** (Braddon): offers bulk billed consultations outside working hours (18:00-22:00), suitable for urgent but non-critical conditions.
- **YourGP@Civic** (Civic): accepts direct billing with Medibank OSHC; standard consultations last 15 minutes.

### Verifying Bulk Billing Status
Before booking, call the clinic and confirm: "Do you bulk bill international students with OSHC?" Some clinics operate on a "Mixed Billing" model — they bulk bill regular patients but charge OSHC patients the gap. The Australian Medical Association (AMA) 2023 guidelines suggest asking the clinic for a **Fee Estimate** that sets out clearly how much OSHC will cover and what you would pay out of pocket [AMA, 2023, *Medical Fees Transparency Guide*].

## The Claims Process: Online Submission and Manual Applications

OSHC claims come in two modes: direct billing and rebate claims. Direct billing applies at bulk billing clinics or partner hospitals, where the insurer processes everything automatically. When a clinic does not offer direct billing, you need to claim manually.

### How to Claim Online (Medibank OSHC Example)
1. Log in to the Medibank OSHC member portal (app or website).
2. Select "Make a Claim" and upload the **original invoice** (which must include: clinic name, date, MBS item number, the fee charged, and the patient's name).
3. Enter your bank account details (an Australian bank account); claim payments usually arrive within 5 working days. Medibank's 2023 claims data shows that 92% of online claims are processed within 72 hours [Medibank, 2023, *OSHC Claims Processing Times Report*].

### Common Reasons Claims Are Rejected
A 2024 report by the Australian Health Ombudsman found the top three reasons OSHC claims are rejected are: **incomplete invoices** (31%), **services not covered by OSHC** (27%) and **waiting period not yet served** (19%) [Australian Health Ombudsman, 2024, *Private Health Insurance Complaints Report*]. Students should keep all medical receipts for at least 2 years in case they need to appeal.

When it comes to paying tuition across borders, some student families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/how-to-use-your-oshc-health-insurance-at-anu-a-guide-to-bulk-billing-clinics-cla) to settle the foreign exchange, ensuring funds arrive on time and in line with foreign exchange controls.

## Specialist Referrals: The GP as Gatekeeper

The Australian health system operates on a **tiered care model**: you can only get Medicare (or OSHC) rebates for specialist care with a referral from a general practitioner (GP). If you book a specialist directly without a referral, OSHC will not cover any of the cost, and the specialist may refuse to see you.

### Getting a Referral Letter
After booking a GP appointment at the ANU Health Centre, the doctor will decide based on your condition whether a referral is needed. A referral letter is usually valid for **12 months** (for chronic conditions) or **3 months** (for a single consultation). Ask the GP to state "OSHC covered" explicitly in the referral letter and to include the MBS item code. The Royal Australian College of General Practitioners (RACGP) 2023 standards recommend that a referral letter include the type of specialist required, the clinical reasons and the name of the recommended specialist [RACGP, 2023, *Referral Letter Writing Guide*].

### Common Specialists in Canberra and Booking
- **Dermatology**: Canberra Dermatology Centre (Braddon), first consultation around 280-350 AUD, with OSHC covering roughly 75% (MBS item 104).
- **Optometry**: Optometry ACT (Civic), requires a GP referral before booking; a standard eye examination costs around 200 AUD, with OSHC covering about 85%.
- **Orthopaedics**: ACT Orthopaedic Clinic (Deakin), average wait of 14 days after referral (ACT Health data, 2024) [ACT Health, 2024, *Specialist Waiting Times Report*].

### Emergency and Hospital Referrals
If the GP decides you need hospital treatment, they will issue a **Hospital Admission Letter**. You must contact your OSHC insurer for **pre-approval** of the hospital stay; otherwise you may be left with out-of-pocket gap costs. Data from the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare for 2023 shows that around 12% of OSHC hospital claims are partially rejected because pre-approval was not obtained [AIHW, 2023, *Private Health Insurance Hospital Claims Report*].

## Prescription Medicines and the Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme

OSHC does not directly cover over-the-counter medicines from regular pharmacies (such as Chemist Warehouse). However, for prescription medicines listed on the **Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme (PBS)**, OSHC can cover part of the cost. According to the 2024 PBS price list from the Australian Department of Health, the OSHC patient co-payment cap for general medicines (such as amoxicillin) is **42.50 AUD** (general patients) or **7.30 AUD** (concession card holders) [Australian Department of Health, 2024, *PBS Patient Co-payment Schedule*].

### Claiming for Prescription Medicines
After the GP writes the prescription, take it to a pharmacy to buy the medicine. Keep the pharmacy receipt (which must show the PBS code), then submit a "Pharmacy Claim" through your OSHC portal. Note: OSHC only covers medicines on the PBS list — non-PBS medicines (such as some topical dermatology products) must be paid for in full. Data from the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) for 2023 shows that around 34% of OSHC medicine claims are rejected because the medicine is not on the PBS list [TGA, 2023, *Annual Medicines Reimbursement Data Report*].

### Getting Urgent Medicines
If you need a medicine urgently (such as an antibiotic), the ANU Health Centre can issue an **Emergency Prescription**, valid for 7 days. You can fill it at the Chemist Warehouse on campus (City West), which offers 24-hour dispensing.

## Emergencies and Ambulance Services

OSHC policies usually cover **emergency ambulance transport** (including ground ambulances and helicopter rescue), but non-emergency transport (such as transport to a booked appointment) is not covered. ACT Emergency Services Agency (ESA) 2024 data shows that a single ambulance call-out in Canberra costs **1,200 AUD** (including basic life support); with advanced life support (ALS) the cost rises to 2,000 AUD [ACT Emergency Services Agency, 2024, *Ambulance Service Fees Schedule*].

### When to Call an Ambulance
The Australian Resuscitation Council (ARC) 2023 guidelines recommend calling 000 only in these situations: **breathing has stopped**, **severe bleeding**, **chest pain**, **loss of consciousness** and **severe allergic reaction**. For non-emergency situations (such as a minor sprain or a cold), go to a GP or an after-hours clinic instead, to avoid unnecessary ambulance costs. ARC data shows that around 22% of ambulance call-outs are classified as non-urgent [ARC, 2023, *Ambulance Use Guidelines*].

### Insurer Pre-Authorisation Requirements
Some OSHC policies require students to notify the insurer within **48 hours** of calling an ambulance, otherwise reimbursement may be affected. Keep the ambulance Incident Number, which you will need for the later claim.

## Renewing OSHC and Switching Insurers

OSHC must cover the entire validity period of your student visa. Under the Department of Home Affairs 2024 rules, a gap in OSHC cover of more than 28 days before your visa expires can affect visa compliance [Department of Home Affairs, 2024, *Student Visa Compliance Guide*].

### How to Renew
Students can renew online through ANU's OSHC partner insurer (usually Medibank). The 2024 Medibank OSHC single renewal cost is **632 AUD per year** (including a 6.2% annual premium increase). If you switch insurers, you must provide the new policy's "Cover Start Date" and "Cover End Date" to the ANU International Student Support Office.

### Cancelling a Policy and Refunds
If you graduate early or withdraw from your studies, you can apply for an OSHC refund. The refund formula is: remaining days × (annual premium / 365), minus an administration fee (usually 50 AUD). Data from the Australian Department of Health and Aged Care for 2023 shows that OSHC refunds take an average of 21 working days to process [Department of Health and Aged Care, 2023, *OSHC Refund Process Report*].

## FAQ

### Q1: Do I need to pay upfront to see a doctor at the ANU Health Centre?
No. The ANU Health Centre offers **bulk billing** to students with valid OSHC, so you pay nothing at the time of the consultation. The clinic bills the insurer directly. You will need to show your OSHC membership card and passport. 2024 data shows that around 89% of OSHC students at ANU have used the centre's services (ANU Health Centre internal statistics).

### Q2: If my GP recommends a CT or MRI scan, how much will OSHC reimburse?
OSHC covers imaging tests on the MBS list. Taking a CT scan as an example: MBS item 56001, standard cost around 480 AUD, with OSHC reimbursing **85%** (about 408 AUD) and a gap of around 72 AUD out of pocket. However, you must obtain pre-approval from your insurer before the scan; otherwise the reimbursement can drop to 75% (about 360 AUD). Data from the Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Radiologists for 2023 shows a 14% rejection rate for imaging claims without pre-approval [RANZCR, 2023, *Imaging Claims Statistics*].

### Q3: Does OSHC cover the HPV vaccine or the flu vaccine?
No. The HPV vaccine (Gardasil 9) is a **preventive vaccine** and is not covered by OSHC. Getting the HPV vaccine at a Canberra pharmacy costs around 180 AUD per dose (3 doses in total). The seasonal flu vaccine can be received at the ANU Health Centre at your own cost, around 25 AUD. However, some OSHC insurers (such as Allianz Care) may offer one free flu vaccine per year — check your policy terms. Data from the Australian Immunisation Register (AIR) for 2024 shows that only around 3% of OSHC policies include vaccine benefits.
