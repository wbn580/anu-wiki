Each semester, the Australian National University (ANU) issues a course outline (Course Outline / Syllabus) to around 25,000 students (ANU Annual Report 2023). The document is the key governing instrument defining the assessment structure, academic integrity requirements and required readings for a course. Under the standards of the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA 2023), a course outline must set out the weighting of at least 60% of continuous assessment (such as essays and quizzes) alongside the final exam. For ANU students, correctly reading the assessment weightings and required readings in the Syllabus is the first step in planning a semester strategy and avoiding academic misconduct.

## Reading the Assessment Weightings: Weighting Systems and Pass Conditions

ANU course outlines typically list the **weighting percentage** of each assessment task in a table. A common structure is a mid-term essay (30%), class participation (10%), a final essay or exam (40%) and short quizzes (20%). The key is to spot the **hurdle requirement**: some courses stipulate that even if the overall mark exceeds 50%, a fail still results if the final exam score does not reach 40% or 50%. Students should check this clause in week one to avoid an unexpected fail at the end of semester.

### How the Weighted Average Is Calculated

ANU uses a 7-point GPA scale, but assessment marks are usually reported out of 100. Each task's score is multiplied by its weight and the results summed to give the final percentage mark. For example, a mid-term essay scored 75 (weighting 30%) and a final essay scored 65 (weighting 70%) gives a final mark of 75×0.30 + 65×0.70 = 68.0. That mark is then mapped to the 4.0–7.0 GPA range using ANU's official conversion table. The **weighting system** determines which task matters most to the final grade, so students should allocate time to the highest-weighted tasks first.

### The Weighting Trap in Group Work and Peer Assessment

Some course outlines note that group assignments include a **peer assessment** component, typically worth 10% to 20% of that task's mark. If a group assignment carries a total weighting of 30% and peer assessment makes up 20% of it, the peer assessment effectively influences 6% of the overall mark (30% × 20%). Students should read the assessment criteria descriptions in the Syllabus carefully to see whether deductions can be made for indicators such as "teamwork" or "contribution".

## Required Readings: Essential vs Recommended

The "required readings" section of an ANU course outline is usually split into **Essential Readings** and **Recommended Readings**. According to the ANU Library's 2024 Course Resource Guide, essential readings typically include 3 to 8 journal articles or book chapters that are directly linked to assessment tasks such as essay or exam questions. Recommended readings serve to extend your knowledge but are not directly assessed. Students should prioritise the Essential list and use ANU Library electronic resources (e.g., JSTOR, Scopus) to obtain full texts.

### Electronic Resources and Open Educational Resources (OER)

Since 2023, ANU has encouraged teaching staff to use **Open Educational Resources** (OER) to lower the cost of course materials. Outlines may include "Open Access" or "ANU eReserve" links. Note that some links are only accessible on the ANU campus network or after logging in via VPN. If a link is broken, search again through the ANU Library's course reading list system (Leganto) rather than buying a third-party version.

### Editions and Chapter Requirements for Required Readings

Outlines often cite a specific edition (e.g., 3rd edition) or chapters (e.g., chapters 4-7). Using an older edition can mean page numbers no longer match, so key content may be missed. Students should check the **ISBN of the prescribed edition** provided by the ANU bookstore or library. For journal articles, outlines usually provide a DOI or stable URL — use these identifiers directly instead of searching via a general search engine, which can land you on unauthorised sites.

## The Assessment Calendar: Deadlines and Late Penalties

The "assessment calendar" section lists the **submission deadline** for every task and the time (usually 23:59 Australian Eastern Time). ANU enforces a strict late penalty policy: for every 24 hours late, 5% of the task's total mark is deducted, up to a maximum of 5 days (i.e., 25%). From day 6 onwards, the task is not accepted and is recorded as 0. Students should mark every deadline in their calendar and build in at least a 24-hour buffer to allow for technical failures.

### Extensions: Process and Time Limits

If you cannot submit on time for medical or force-majeure reasons, apply through ANU's **Special Consideration** system before the deadline, attaching a medical certificate or other evidence. Under ANU student policy (2024 revision), applications must be lodged within 3 business days of the deadline; late applications are not accepted. Approved extensions typically add 7 to 14 days, with the exact length decided by the course convener.

### Eligibility for a Supplementary Exam

The outline will state whether a **supplementary exam** is available. Normally, only students with an overall mark between 45% and 49% who have submitted all assessment tasks are eligible to apply. The supplementary exam is capped at 50% (the pass mark) and does not count towards the GPA. Applications must be lodged with the college within 5 business days of results being released.

## The Academic Integrity Statement: Turnitin and AI Use Policies

Every ANU course outline includes an **Academic Integrity Statement** requiring all submitted work to go through the Turnitin plagiarism-checking system. Since 2024, ANU has piloted **AI detection tools** (such as the Turnitin AI detection module) in some courses, with a rule that unacknowledged AI-generated content exceeding 20% of an assignment will be treated as academic misconduct. Students should read the "permitted use of AI" paragraph in the outline carefully — for example, whether AI may be used for brainstorming or grammar checking.

### Citation Format Requirements

Outlines usually specify a **citation style**, such as APA 7th edition, Harvard or Chicago. Requirements can differ between colleges: ANU's law school, for example, mostly uses the Australian Guide to Legal Citation (AGLC), while business school courses prefer APA. Students should use the specified style consistently all semester and check citation completeness with tools such as Zotero or EndNote before submitting. For cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E8%AA%B2%E7%A8%8B%E5%A4%A7%E7%B6%B1-syllabus-%E8%A7%A3%E8%AE%80%E6%96%B9%E6%B3%95%E8%80%83%E6%A0%B8%E6%AF%94%E9%87%8D%E8%88%87%E5%BF%85%E8%AE%80%E6%9D%90%E6%96%99) to settle foreign exchange.

### Resubmission and Self-Plagiarism

Outlines may explicitly prohibit **self-plagiarism** — submitting the same piece of work to two different courses. If you want to build on research from a previous course, you must obtain written permission from both course conveners before submitting and clearly cite the original work in the assignment.

## Course Objectives and Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)

Every ANU course outline includes **Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)** — usually 3 to 6 specific capability goals, such as "be able to analyse the structure and function of Australia's political system". These ILOs are directly linked to assessment tasks: outlines often show in a table which ILOs each task addresses. Using this mapping, students can tell which tasks reward knowledge recall and which reward critical analysis, and adjust their revision priorities accordingly.

### Where to Find the Marking Rubric

Some course outlines include the **marking rubric**, or state that it will be published on Wattle (ANU's LMS). The rubric details the marking dimensions for each task (e.g., argument structure, use of evidence, language) and the performance level corresponding to each mark band. Students should download the rubric before starting to write and check their work against it point by point to make sure every criterion is covered.

### Links to Graduate Attributes

The end of an outline often lists how the course contributes to ANU's **graduate attributes**, such as "critical thinking" and "global perspective". This section does not directly affect your mark, but it helps you understand where the course sits in the bigger picture — and it is useful for articulating your capability development when applying for jobs or further study.

## Teaching Team and Contact Details

The first page of a course outline usually lists the **course convener** and **tutor(s)** with names, email addresses and office hours. In week one, email to confirm office hours and book ahead. For questions about marks or academic policy, contact the course convener directly rather than the tutor. All formal communication must use your ANU email address (@anu.edu.au); personal email addresses may be ignored.

### Tutorial Attendance Policy

Some course outlines require **tutorial attendance** of at least 80%, otherwise your final mark may be affected or a "not completed" (NW) record may result. Check whether attendance is assessed (e.g., as a participation mark) and whether attending remotely via Zoom is permitted. If you cannot attend because of timetable clashes, ask the course convener to switch tutorial groups early in the semester.

### Student Support Services

The end of an outline often lists the **academic support services** ANU provides, such as writing support at the Academic Skills Centre, library research support and counselling services. Save this list and use it proactively when you run into difficulties, rather than waiting until just before a deadline.

## FAQ

### Q1: What should I do if I spot an error in the Syllabus (e.g., weightings that do not add up to 100%)?

Email the course convener immediately from your ANU account to point out the error. Under ANU's course outline amendment policy (2024), the convener must issue a corrigendum notice within 5 business days and announce it via Wattle. Until the official correction, students should follow the weightings listed in the Syllabus, but may ask the convener for written confirmation.

### Q2: What if an article in the required readings is not available in the library?

First check whether the article is accessible through the ANU Library's Leganto system. If it is still unavailable, request it via the **ANZSRC interlibrary loan service**, which normally takes 3 to 7 business days. You can also ask the course convener to substitute an accessible resource. According to ANU Library data for 2023, around 92% of required readings can be obtained instantly through electronic resources.

### Q3: Is AI-assisted writing allowed in ANU courses?

It depends on the specific rules in the course outline. Since 2024, ANU has required every course to state its AI use policy explicitly in the Syllabus. Some courses allow AI for grammar checking or brainstorming but prohibit generating answers directly. Misuse can trigger an academic misconduct investigation, with failure of the course as the maximum penalty. Read that section carefully before using AI, or ask the course convener directly.
