## Understanding Deposit Protection Schemes

In both Australia and the UK, residential tenancy deposits (often called "bonds") are held under government-backed protection schemes. The main purpose is to safeguard your money until the end of the tenancy and to provide a clear mechanism for resolving disputes over deductions.

These schemes usually require the landlord or agent to lodge the deposit with an authorised body within a set timeframe. If they fail to do so, you may be entitled to take action. Always check that your deposit is registered and that you receive the prescribed information, including the scheme's contact details and how to raise a dispute.

## Move-In Inspection Checklist: Your First Line of Defence

Before you move in, do a thorough inspection with the landlord or agent and record the condition of everything. This creates a baseline that protects you when you move out. Our checklist covers the key areas:

- **Walls and floors**: note any marks, cracks, stains, or damaged floorboards.
- **Windows and doors**: check locks, handles, and that all windows open and close properly.
- **Kitchen**: test appliances (oven, stovetop, fridge, dishwasher), check cupboards, and look for leaks.
- **Bathroom**: run the taps, flush the toilet, check for mould or cracked tiles, and test the shower pressure.
- **Furniture and fixtures**: if furnished, photograph the condition of each item, including any existing stains or tears.
- **Utilities and smoke alarms**: verify they are in working order and note their locations.

Take dated photos and videos, and keep a copy of the signed inspection report. If anything is marked as "unclean" or "damaged" at the start, you can avoid being blamed later.

## End-of-Tenancy Cleaning Standards: What Landlords Expect

Most deposit disputes involve cleaning. The general standard is that the property should be returned in the same condition as when you moved in, allowing for fair wear and tear. That means:

- **Kitchen** – degrease the oven, hob and extractor fan, wipe down all surfaces, and clean inside cupboards.
- **Bathroom** – remove limescale and mould, clean the shower screen, and polish taps and sinks.
- **Floors and carpets** – vacuum and, if needed, shampoo carpets that have stained. Check the original condition notes.
- **Windows** – clean interior sills and glass, especially if they were clean at check-in.
- **General dusting** – wipe skirting boards, light switches, and door frames.

If you are unsure about the required level, review the check-in report and any cleaning clauses in your tenancy agreement. When in doubt, hire professional cleaners and keep the receipts – this can be a good investment compared to a small deduction.

## Avoiding Bond Deductions: Practical Tips

- **Do a final inspection yourself** – walk through with the original check-in list and fix any issues before the landlord inspects.
- **Follow the dispute process** – if your landlord proposes deductions, ask for itemised invoices. In many jurisdictions you can challenge unreasonable claims through the deposit protection scheme.
- **Keep all receipts** for professional cleaning or repairs you pay for.
- **Communicate in writing** – an email saying "I will return the keys on Friday" creates a paper trail.

If your landlord or agent refuses to refund the bond without justification, you can apply to the relevant scheme for a free dispute resolution. The scheme will assess the evidence, including your check-in report and photos, to decide a fair outcome.

## FAQ: Quick Answers

**Q: What should I do if I forget to check something on the move-in day?**
A: Immediately notify the landlord or agent in writing and add it to the inspection report. Most schemes allow you to update the report within a short period.

**Q: Do I need to pay for professional cleaning?**
A: Only if the property was professionally cleaned at the start and your tenancy agreement requires it. Otherwise, you just need to return it in the same condition.

**Q: Can the landlord keep the whole bond for cleaning?**
A: No. Deductions must reflect the actual cost of cleaning or damage beyond fair wear and tear. They must provide receipts or quotes.

**Q: How long does the bond have to be returned?**
A: The time frame varies by state or territory in Australia and by the tenancy scheme in the UK. Usually it is within a few weeks after you vacate, but check the specific rules for your location.
