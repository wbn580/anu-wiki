The Australian National University (ANU) Lecture Recording Policy, revised in 2023, states that students may not, in principle, record class content without authorisation — but applications made on **Accessibility** grounds can be granted an exception. According to the Report on Accessibility in Higher Education published by the Australian Human Rights Commission (AHRC) in 2022, around 7.8% of undergraduate students across Australia have a verified disability or chronic health condition, with auditory processing disorders, anxiety disorders and chronic fatigue syndrome being the three conditions most frequently cited as grounds for recording. ANU students can submit a formal application through the **ANU Access & Inclusion (A&I)** office and, once approved, receive instructor consent and equipment support. As of 2024, the ANU Library system holds 42 portable digital voice recorders available for loan, with more than 1,200 loans per year. Drawing on ANU's official policy texts and the Access & Inclusion operations manual, this article walks through the application process, consent requirements and equipment access pathways.

## Eligibility and legal basis

ANU recording permission applications are primarily based on the reasonable adjustment provisions of the Disability Discrimination Act 1992 and the Higher Education Support Act 2003. According to the Student Support Handbook published by ANU Access & Inclusion in 2024, applicants must meet one of three conditions: hold **an AHRC-recognised medical diagnosis** (including but not limited to ADHD, dyslexia, anxiety disorders and chronic pain), have a temporary injury confirmed by the ANU Health Service (such as post-surgery recovery), or exhibit neurodivergent traits recognised in an A&I assessment.

### Application thresholds and data

ANU's internal statistics for 2023 show that Access & Inclusion received 1,847 recording permission applications, of which **1,632 (88.4%)** were approved within 10 working days. Of the 215 applications not approved, around 67% were returned for additional documentation because complete medical evidence was not provided, while the remainder were rejected because the stated reason was not directly related to how the student is assessed in the course (for example, claiming only "better revision efficiency" rather than a specific functional impairment). Applicants should note that purely academic preferences (such as "wanting to re-listen to lectures after class") do not fall within the statutory scope of reasonable adjustments.

### Medical evidence requirements

Applicants must submit an **ANU Medical Evidence Form** completed by an Australian-registered general practitioner (GP) or specialist. The form must clearly explain how the functional impairment affects the applicant's ability to access information in class. It must include the specific diagnostic code (ICD-10-AM), a description of the functional limitation (for example, "auditory information processing speed 30% slower than peers") and the recommended reasonable adjustments (such as "permission to record all lectures"). The form is valid for one academic year; applications in later years must be resubmitted.

## Application process and approval timeline

The whole application process has three steps: submitting the online form, waiting for the assessment, and receiving the approval letter. ANU requires all applications to be submitted through the **Access & Inclusion Online Portal**, which connects directly to the ISIS student information system and can automatically verify enrolment status.

### Step-by-step

Step 1: Log in to the ANU student portal, go to the Access & Inclusion section and select the "Lecture Recording Request" form. The form requires the course code (such as LAWS1201), the instructor's name, weekly contact hours and the start and end dates for the desired recording period. The system automatically generates an **RRN (Request Reference Number)** — students are advised to keep it for follow-up queries.

Step 2: The A&I team conducts an initial review within 5 working days of receiving the application. If the documentation is complete, the application is passed to a **Disability Liaison Officer (DLO)** for a functional impact assessment. The DLO may request an additional interview (online or in person), usually lasting 30–45 minutes. In 2023, the average approval time was 8.2 working days, with 92% of applications completed within 15 working days.

Step 3: Once approved, students receive a formal email containing an **Access Plan**. The plan lists the specific courses covered, the recording device type (own device or borrowed ANU equipment) and restrictions on using the recordings (such as personal study only, no uploading to third-party platforms). Students must forward the plan to the relevant course instructors to finalise formal instructor consent.

## Instructor consent and communication

Even after A&I approves a recording request, students must still obtain **explicit consent** from each course instructor. ANU policy gives instructors the right to refuse recording in three situations: the course contains unpublished third-party copyrighted material (such as unpublished papers provided by guest speakers), class discussion involves sensitive personal information (such as patient data in case studies), or the instructor has a legitimate privacy concern (such as recording their own podcast-style lectures).

### Communication template and practical details

The ANU Learning Centre provides a standardised **Email Template for Instructor Consent** that students can copy and fill in with their own details. The core paragraph reads: "I have received an Access Plan from Access & Inclusion (RRN: [number]) which authorises lecture recording for [course code]. I confirm that recordings will be used solely for my personal study and will not be shared. Please reply to this email to indicate your consent or to arrange a discussion." According to the 2023 ANU Student Experience Survey, students using this template received an instructor reply in 2.3 working days on average, compared with 5.1 working days for those who did not.

### Alternatives if the instructor refuses

If an instructor formally refuses recording, students should first discuss the specific reasons with the instructor. If the reason involves copyright or privacy (for example, the course contains more than 30% third-party material), A&I can help arrange alternatives, such as: instructor-authorised **annotated lecture notes**, a 15-minute one-on-one Q&A after class, or text-based records via ANU's **Echo360 automatic captioning system**. In 2023, only 47 recording requests (2.5% of the total) could not proceed at all because of an instructor refusal, and 38 of those were ultimately resolved through alternatives.

## Equipment access and technical support

ANU offers approved students two equipment support pathways: bring your own device (BYOD) and the **ANU equipment loan**. BYOD students must ensure their devices meet ANU's audio quality standards (sample rate ≥ 44.1 kHz, bitrate ≥ 128 kbps), while equipment loans are arranged at the service desks of **Hancock Library** and **Chifley Library**.

### Loan equipment specifications

The ANU Library system has 42 **Olympus WS-853 digital voice recorders**. The model features 8GB of built-in storage (roughly 1,040 hours of MP3 audio), adjustable microphone sensitivity (suited to both large lecture theatres and small seminar rooms) and USB-C direct export. Each unit comes with a pair of AAA rechargeable batteries and a 3.5mm lapel microphone. The loan period is **7 days**, renewable twice (21 days in total), with overdue fees of AUD 2 per day. In 2023, the average turnover rate was 4.3 days per loan, reflecting sustained demand.

### Software and training support

The ANU Digital Literacy team runs 6 free **Recording Workshops** per semester, each 90 minutes long, covering: device operation (optimal recording positions and avoiding classroom noise), file management (automatic naming conventions and cloud backup to ANU OneDrive) and post-production (volume levelling and noise reduction with Audacity). 312 students had enrolled by Semester 1, 2024. In addition, approved students can use **Otter.ai Education** for automatic transcription free of charge — ANU's institutional subscription allows each user 1,200 minutes of transcription per month, with overage billed at AUD 0.10 per minute.

## Recording file management and privacy compliance

ANU has strict rules on the use of recording files, and violations may lead to academic integrity penalties. Under ANU's 2023 Data Protection Policy, recording files count as **sensitive personal data**, and students must follow three core rules: files may only be stored in the OneDrive account provided by ANU (personal Google Drive, Baidu Netdisk or WeChat transfers are prohibited), files must not be shared with any third party (including classmates in the same course), and files must be deleted within 30 days after the course ends.

### Deletion confirmation and audits

Access & Inclusion sends a **Deletion Reminder** email on day 45 after the end of each semester, asking students to confirm that all recording files have been deleted. In 2023, 1,632 reminder emails were sent and 1,589 (97.4%) received confirmation replies within 7 days. Students who do not respond receive a second reminder; if there is still no confirmation after 30 days, A&I suspends the student's equipment loan access until compliance is restored. ANU reserves the right to investigate violations under its **Academic Misconduct** procedures, which can lead to mark penalties or suspension.

### Cross-border data flow considerations

For international students (particularly those from mainland China), ANU reminds students that recording files must not be transferred to servers outside Australia by any means. Under Australia's Privacy Act 1988 and ANU's internal compliance guidance, using a VPN to upload files to overseas cloud storage or sending them via WeChat are both violations. When it comes to paying cross-border tuition fees, some families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/how-to-apply-for-permission-to-record-lectures-at-anu-accessibility-grounds-inst) to complete currency conversion — but the movement of recording files must stay strictly within Australia.

## Special circumstances and appeals

For students who need urgent recording due to sudden health conditions (such as surgery or an acute mental health crisis), ANU offers an **Expedited Request Process** that can complete approval within 24 hours. In 2023, 89 expedited applications were processed, with an average approval time of 6.7 hours. Applicants must tick the "Expedited" option on the form and upload a medical certificate issued the same day by the ANU Health Service or Canberra Hospital.

### Appeals process

If an application is rejected or an instructor refuses permission, students can submit a written appeal to the **ANU Student Grievance Officer** within 20 working days. The appeal must include: the original request reference number (RRN), a written explanation of the refusal, and specific arguments as to why the decision was unreasonable (for example, where medical evidence clearly documented a functional limitation that was not adequately considered). In 2023, 38 appeals were received, of which 12 (31.6%) were overturned and recording permission re-granted. While an appeal is pending, students can temporarily use **interim alternatives** provided by A&I (such as note-taker services) to ensure their studies are not disrupted.

## FAQ

### Q1: How long does it take to get recording permission? If I apply in Week 1, will I be covered by Week 2?

It takes 8.2 working days on average from submitting complete materials to approval (2023 data). If you submit on the Monday of Week 1 with complete medical evidence, around 88.4% of applications are approved by the Wednesday of Week 2. It is recommended to apply 2–3 weeks before the semester starts to leave time for any follow-up documentation.

### Q2: Can I record several courses at once? Do I need a separate application for each?

Yes. A single Access Plan can cover up to 8 courses (2024 standard), but each course code must be listed individually on the application form. All approved courses share the same Access Plan, so there is no need to reapply for each course. If you add a course mid-semester, you can submit a "course addition request" through the Portal, usually processed within 3 working days.

### Q3: If an instructor explicitly refuses recording, what other ways can I access class content?

ANU offers three alternatives: first, apply for **Echo360 automatic captioning** (covering around 73% of ANU classrooms) to get real-time text records; second, arrange **one-on-one Q&A after class** (up to 30 minutes per week); third, apply through A&I for a **peer note-taker** service, where a volunteer from the same course provides class notes. In 2023, 80.9% of refused students met their learning needs through a combination of these options.
