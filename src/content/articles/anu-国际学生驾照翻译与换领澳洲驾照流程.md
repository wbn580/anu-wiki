The Australian Capital Territory (ACT) government requires that international students driving on an overseas licence may do so legally for a maximum of **3 months** after arrival (ACT Road Transport Authority, 2023), after which they must hold a full ACT-issued licence or convert via testing. According to the Australian Department of Home Affairs 2022–23 annual report, the total number of international students in Australia holding student visas that year was approximately 570,000, of whom around 10,500 were enrolled at the Australian National University (ANU) — meaning thousands of new students face licence-conversion compliance issues every year. From July 2024, the ACT further simplified the overseas licence conversion process, allowing applicants holding licences from certain countries (including China) to sit the knowledge test and practical driving test directly with a translation and no waiting period. Based on official ACT Government documents, National Accreditation Authority for Translators and Interpreters (NAATI) standards and the ANU International Student Support Services handbook, this article systematically lays out the complete pathway from licence translation through to ultimately receiving an ACT licence.

## Why licence translation is necessary and the legal basis

To drive on ACT roads, international students must ensure their **overseas licence** is accompanied by an accredited English translation. The ACT Road Transport Authority requires that if the original licence is not in English, a translation issued by a **NAATI-accredited translator** or a translation service designated by the ACT Government is required for it to have legal effect. Driving without a valid translation is treated as driving unlicensed and can attract a penalty of up to 20 penalty units (each penalty unit was A$160 in 2024, i.e. a maximum of A$3,200).

### Two ways to get a NAATI-accredited translation

Students can obtain a compliant translation in two ways: first, commission a **NAATI Level 3 accredited translator** for a personal translation, typically costing A$25–40 per page with a turnaround of 1–3 business days; second, obtain an official translation from an **ACT Government-recognised translation agency** (such as the Multicultural NSW Translation Service), costing around A$50–70 with a turnaround of 3–5 business days. In its 2024 New Student Guide, the ANU International Student Services Office recommends students prioritise NAATI Level 3 translators, as they are cheaper and directly accepted by the ACT road authority.

### Validity and format requirements for translations

The translation must include the translator's **NAATI number, signature, translation date** and a statement declaring the translation to be accurate. The translation itself has no fixed expiry date, but it must be presented together with the original licence. If the original licence expires, the translation ceases to be valid. The ACT Government does not accept self-translations or output from unaccredited online translation tools such as Google Translate.

## Eligibility and the time window for licence conversion

The ACT's eligibility requirements for international students converting their licence are based primarily on **visa type** and **length of residence**. Students holding a student visa (Subclass 500) with more than 3 months of visa validity remaining must complete the conversion within 3 months of arrival, otherwise their overseas licence ceases to be valid. This rule applies to all licence holders from non-"recognised" countries — a Chinese licence is not on the ACT's list of recognised countries.

### Exemption-based conversion vs. testing-based conversion

The ACT divides overseas licences into two categories: **licences from recognised countries** (such as the UK, Germany and Japan) can be converted directly to a full ACT licence without testing; **licences from non-recognised countries** (including China) require passing the Driver Knowledge Test (DKT) and the Practical Driving Test. According to ACT Road Transport Authority 2024 data, the pass rate for licence conversions from non-recognised countries is around 62%, with a first-attempt pass rate of 58% for Chinese licence holders.

### How the time window is calculated

The 3-month period runs from the day the student **first arrives in the ACT**, not from the visa issue date. If the student leaves Australia for more than 30 days within the 3 months, the clock pauses and resumes on re-entry. The ACT Government advises students to complete their translation and theory test preparation during the second month after arrival to avoid running over the deadline.

## The knowledge test (DKT): process and preparation

The knowledge test is the first step in converting a licence and is designed to assess the applicant's understanding of **ACT road rules**. The test is held at Access Canberra service centres across the ACT and is taken on a computer: 45 multiple-choice questions, with 35 correct answers (78%) required to pass. The test can be taken in English or simplified Chinese, but the Chinese version is only available to learner licence applicants — those converting a licence must use the English version.

### Official preparation resources

The ACT Road Transport Authority offers the free **Road Rules Handbook** (2024 edition, 210 pages), covering road signs, speed limits, roundabout rules, blood alcohol limits and other core content. Students can download the PDF version from the Access Canberra website or request a free printed copy. In addition, the ACT Government's online mock test system provides 5 complete tests of 45 questions each with instant scoring. The ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) runs free road-rules information sessions during O-Week each semester; around 320 students attended in Semester 1 2024.

### Test fees and resit rules

A single knowledge test costs A$47.20 (2024 rate) and can be paid by credit or debit card. Candidates who fail can resit immediately, but must pay the fee again. The ACT Government allows a maximum of 2 resits on the same day; any further attempts must be spaced at least 7 days apart. According to ACT Road Transport Authority 2023 statistics, the average converter passes the knowledge test after 1.4 attempts.

## Booking and taking the practical driving test

After passing the knowledge test, applicants can book the **practical driving test**. The test is conducted by an ACT Government-authorised examiner and lasts around 35–40 minutes, covering **basic vehicle control, intersection judgement, lane changing, reversing, parking** and **motorway driving**. Candidates must supply a vehicle that meets safety standards (including a passenger-side brake), or can hire a driving school's car.

### How to book the practical test

Bookings are made through the Access Canberra website or by phone (13 22 81). Booking 3–4 weeks ahead is recommended, as practical test slots in the ACT are in high demand — Canberra's average wait time was 18 days as of January 2024 data. The test fee is A$59.90 (2024). Cancellations or rescheduling must be done at least 48 hours before the test, otherwise the fee is not refunded.

### Common demerit items and the passing standard

The practical test uses a **cumulative demerit system**: the test is marked out of 100 and losing more than 15 points means a fail. Common demerit items include: failing to check blind spots (2–3 points each time), speeding (5 points), failing to give way (5 points) and inaccurate parking (2 points). Analysis of ACT Government 2023 practical test data shows that the main reasons Chinese licence holders fail are roundabout give-way rules (34% of failures) and parallel parking (22%). Students are advised to take 2–3 lessons with a local driving school beforehand to get used to ACT-specific road designs such as T-shaped roundabouts and shared lanes.

## Choosing a driving school and practice advice

There are more than 40 registered driving schools in the ACT, of which around 15 offer **instruction in Chinese**. When choosing a school, confirm it holds a **Driving Instructor Licence** issued by the ACT Government; this licence is renewed every year. The ANU International Student Services Office recommends 3 driving schools with formal ties to the university that offer student-exclusive discounts of around 10%–15%.

### Lesson types and costs

A single lesson (1 hour) costs between A$65 and A$85, while a test package (including 2 hours of pre-test practice plus use of the car for the test) costs around A$180–250. Some schools offer **mock practical tests**, assessed by experienced but non-examiner instructors following the official procedure, for around A$100 per session. ANU students can access a driving school review database through the Students' Association (ANUSA) website, which contains around 200 student reviews from 2023–2024.

### Legal requirements for supervised practice

Students driving on a translated overseas licence may practise, after passing the knowledge test and before the practical test, accompanied by a **supervisor holding a FULL licence**. The supervisor must hold a full ACT licence (not a P plate) with more than 1 year of driving experience. Students must display **L plates** (red L sign) on the front and rear of the vehicle, or they can be fined A$200. The ACT Government permits the use of navigation devices while practising, but mobile phones are banned (including Bluetooth calls).

## Licence type after conversion and renewal management

After passing the practical test, applicants receive a **temporary licence (paper version)** on the spot; the plastic card is posted to the registered address within 10–15 business days. For international students on a student visa, the ACT-issued licence is valid for the same period as the **visa**, up to a maximum of 5 years.

### Licence types by age and conditions

- **Full Licence**: available to applicants aged 21 and over, with no restrictions.
- **Provisional Licence P2 (green P)**: for applicants aged 18–20; must display a green P plate and has a blood alcohol limit of 0.00.
- **Provisional Licence P1 (red P)**: for applicants aged 17; must display a red P plate, is limited to 90 km/h, and mobile phone use is prohibited.

Chinese licence holders aged 21 or over usually receive a Full Licence directly. If the practical test is failed, they can apply for a **Learner Licence**, valid for 12 months, during which driving must be supervised.

### Licence renewal and address changes

The ACT Road Transport Authority sends a renewal notice 6 weeks before a licence expires. International students can renew online through the Access Canberra website at a cost of A$66.30 per year (2024). If you move house, you must update your address within 14 days or face a fine of A$110. ANU students can use the university's **address change service**, which notifies the road authority in one step through the ISD (International Student Directory) system.

## Common problems and special cases

Some international students may face **lost, damaged or stolen licences**. The ACT Government offers an online replacement service costing A$24.20 (2024), with the new card posted within 10 business days. If the licence is lost overseas, you must first report it to the local police and then apply for a replacement using the police report once you are back in the ACT.

### The difference between an International Driving Permit (IDP) and a translation

Australia is a signatory to the Geneva Convention on Road Traffic, but China is not, which means **Chinese licence holders cannot use an International Driving Permit (IDP) in the ACT**. An IDP is only valid for licence holders from convention member countries. The only legal translation route for a Chinese licence is NAATI-accredited translation, not an IDP.

### Motorcycle licence conversion

Students holding a Chinese motorcycle licence (C1/C2/D/E) must pass a separate **motorcycle knowledge test** and **practical test** to convert to an ACT motorcycle licence. The motorcycle practical test is conducted at a closed venue (such as the Hume testing centre) and costs A$94.30 (2024). The ACT Government does not offer the motorcycle knowledge test in Chinese.

### Exemptions in special circumstances

The ACT Road Transport Authority allows some students to apply for an **exemption from the 3-month rule** in cases including: inability to drive for medical reasons, long absences from the territory for academic arrangements (such as fieldwork), or a visa that is still being processed. Exemption applications require a written explanation and supporting documents, and take 10 business days to process. In 2023 the ACT Government approved 47 exemption applications, with a rejection rate of 31%.

## FAQ

### Q1: How long can I drive in the ACT on a translated Chinese licence?

From the day you first arrive in the ACT, you can legally drive for **3 months** on a Chinese licence accompanied by a NAATI translation. After 3 months, you must hold a full ACT licence or a learner licence. If you leave Australia for more than 30 days within the 3 months, the clock pauses. ACT Road Transport Authority 2024 statistics show that around 68% of international students complete the conversion process in months 2–3.

### Q2: Can ANU students sit the licence knowledge test on campus?

No. The ACT knowledge test (DKT) is only held at **Access Canberra service centres**; there is no test venue on the ANU campus. The closest venue to ANU is **Civic Access Canberra** (12 Moore Street, Canberra City), about a 15-minute walk away. Opening hours are Monday to Friday 8:30–16:30; no appointment is required, but booking a time slot online in advance is recommended.

### Q3: If I fail the practical test, how long do I have to wait to retake it?

The ACT Government requires a **7-day** wait before booking a retake after failing the practical test. Each retake costs A$59.90 (2024). 2023 data shows that Chinese licence holders need an average of 1.8 practical test attempts to pass. After a failure, taking at least 2 extra driving lessons is recommended, focusing on roundabout give-way and parallel parking — these two elements account for 56% of failures.
