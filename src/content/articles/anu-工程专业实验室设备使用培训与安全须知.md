The Australian National University's (ANU) College of Engineering and Computer Science (CECS) operates more than 15 specialised teaching and research laboratories spanning mechanical, electronic, materials, chemical and biological engineering. According to the Laboratory Safety Manual published by the ANU Safety and Health Office (2023), all engineering students must complete the compulsory online safety training module provided by the College before using laboratory equipment; the module takes an average of 4.2 hours and ends with a final assessment (pass mark of 80%). At the same time, Australia's Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (WHS Act 2011) requires educational institutions to formally authorise and record operators of high-risk equipment. This article sets out ANU engineering laboratories' equipment training process, safety access standards and how common violations are handled, providing a practical reference guide for current and prospective students.

## Laboratory Categories and the Equipment Access System

ANU's engineering laboratories are divided by risk level into **general teaching laboratories** (such as the mechanical workshop and electronics benches) and **specialist research laboratories** (such as the nanofabrication facility and high-voltage electrical test rooms). Under the ANU College of Engineering and Computer Science (2024) Laboratory Access Policy, students must complete **specific equipment operation training** for the laboratories relevant to their courses or research projects — a single general induction does not cover every laboratory. Each laboratory displays a green (low risk), yellow (medium risk) or red (high risk) sign at its entrance, and students must check that their training authorisation matches the zone's level before entering.

### Tiered Equipment Authorisation

The College uses a **three-tier equipment authorisation system**: Level 1 (observation) allows students to observe operations under the supervision of a qualified technician; Level 2 (operation) allows students to use designated equipment independently, but requires passing a written exam and a practical assessment; Level 3 (training) is granted to students who complete the full training course and can provide basic instruction to others. For example, in the mechanical workshop, the **CNC milling machine** is a Level 2 device: students must complete 6 hours of dedicated training (including safety videos, a written test and hands-on practice) before they can be issued an authorisation card.

### Lab Access Control and Booking Systems

All engineering laboratories have **smart access control systems**: students tap in with their student ID card (ANU ID Card) and the system automatically checks their individual training records. Students who have not completed the safety training for a given laboratory are denied entry. In addition, equipment must be booked in advance through the College's online booking platform (LabBooking), which shows how many people are currently authorised for each machine and which time slots are available. According to ANU Facilities Management (2024) data, laboratory booking rates exceed 85% during peak periods (weeks 4 to 10 of semester), so it is advisable to book at least 48 hours ahead.

## The Mandatory Safety Training Module

All ANU engineering students (including first-year undergraduates and postgraduate students) must complete the **General Safety Induction** within the first week after enrolment. Developed centrally by the ANU Safety and Health Office (2023), the module has 8 sections covering chemical safety, electrical safety, machine guarding, emergency evacuation procedures and other core topics. The training is delivered online through ANU's learning management system (Wattle), and students must finish all sections and sit the online test within 14 days.

### Course Structure and Assessment Standards

The General Safety Induction contains **28 interactive videos** totalling 4.2 hours, each followed by 3–5 multiple-choice questions that must be answered with 100% accuracy before you can move on. The final test draws 40 questions at random from all sections, with a **pass mark of 80%** (at least 32 correct answers). If you fail on the first attempt, you can retake it after 24 hours, up to a maximum of 3 retakes. Anyone who fails more than 3 times must contact the College Safety Officer to arrange in-person remedial training.

### Dedicated Training for Specialist Equipment

In addition to the general induction, each category of high-risk equipment (such as **high-voltage power systems**, **laser cutters** and **chemical reactors**) has its own dedicated training module. For example, students using Class 4 lasers (common in photonics laboratories) must complete an 8-hour training course based on the standards of the Australian Radiation Protection and Nuclear Safety Agency (ARPANSA, 2022) and pass a practical assessment. Training records are entered into the ANU Personnel Safety Database (PSD) and are usually valid for 12 months, after which you must take refresher training.

## Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Requirements

ANU engineering laboratories strictly enforce **minimum personal protective equipment (PPE) standards**: everyone entering a laboratory — students, staff and visitors alike — must wear compliant equipment. Under the ANU Safety and Health Office (2023) PPE Policy, basic PPE includes: closed-toe shoes (sandals and thongs are not allowed), long trousers (reaching the ankles), a lab coat (cotton or flame-resistant material), safety goggles (meeting the AS/NZS 1337 standard) and gloves (nitrile or chemical-resistant, depending on what you are handling).

### Lab-Specific PPE Upgrades

Different laboratories have additional PPE requirements. In **chemical engineering laboratories**, students must wear a face shield and a chemical-resistant apron; in the **mechanics of materials laboratory**, steel-toe safety boots (meeting the AS/NZS 2210.3 standard) are required for tensile or compression testing. The College provides a **PPE checkpoint** at the entrance to every laboratory, stocked with spare goggles and gloves, but students should bring their own properly fitted equipment. According to ANU's incident reporting system (2023), around 34% of minor laboratory incidents involve improperly worn PPE.

### PPE Maintenance and Replacement

Students must check their PPE regularly for damage. Safety goggles with scratches or fogging that affect visibility should be replaced immediately; nitrile gloves that are punctured or show signs of chemical degradation must not be reused. The College places a **PPE disposal bin** in every laboratory: discarded protective equipment should go into the designated container and must never be mixed with general rubbish. For reusable lab coats, students are advised to wash them at least once a week at a temperature no lower than 60°C.

## Emergency Procedures

ANU engineering laboratories operate a **three-tier emergency response system** covering minor incidents (such as chemical spills), moderate incidents (such as fires or injuries) and major incidents (such as explosions or toxic substance releases). After completing safety training, all students must memorise their laboratory's **emergency evacuation route map** and **emergency contact details**, which are usually posted inside the entrance or near the workbenches.

### Chemical Spills and Fire Response

For **chemical spills**, students should first notify a laboratory technician or the Safety Officer, then choose a clean-up method based on the Safety Data Sheet (SDS) for the substance involved. Small spills (under 1 litre) can be cleaned up yourself using the lab's spill kit; for large spills (over 1 litre), or those involving flammable or toxic substances, you must evacuate immediately and call ANU Security (6125 2249). For **electrical fires**, water-based extinguishers are strictly prohibited; every laboratory is equipped with carbon dioxide (CO₂) or dry powder extinguishers, and students should confirm their locations and how to use them during training.

### First Aid and Incident Reporting

If someone is injured (for example, a cut, a burn or a chemical splash in the eye), the laboratory has **first aid kits** and **eyewash stations**: provide initial first aid right away (such as rinsing the eyes for at least 15 minutes), then go to the ANU campus medical centre (Building 4, open 24 hours) or call 000 for an ambulance. Under ANU's incident reporting policy (2024), any event that causes injury or equipment damage must be reported in writing within 24 hours through the online Incident Reporting Tool; failing to report can result in suspension of laboratory access.

## Equipment Operation Rules and Penalties for Violations

ANU's College of Engineering has clear **written standard operating procedures (SOPs)** for equipment use, and every major piece of equipment comes with an operations manual and a safety checklist. Before each use, students must complete a **pre-use equipment check**, including inspecting the power cord for damage, confirming that guards are in place, and verifying that the emergency stop button works. The results of the pre-check must be recorded in the paper or electronic log provided in the laboratory.

### Common Violations and Their Consequences

Under the ANU College of Engineering and Computer Science (2024) Laboratory Code of Conduct, the following are classified as **serious violations**: operating Level 2 or higher equipment without authorisation, removing safety guards from equipment, eating or smoking in the laboratory, and failing to dispose of hazardous waste correctly. A first offence results in a written warning and a 2-week suspension of laboratory access; a second offence brings a one-semester suspension; a third offence can lead to a permanent ban from the laboratories and referral to the College's academic board. ### Liability for Equipment Damage

If equipment is damaged through improper operation, the student is liable for the **repair costs**, with the exact amount determined by an equipment assessment report. ANU engineering laboratory insurance covers accidental damage up to an excess of AUD 500; anything beyond that is borne by the student or their research group. The College advises students to take out additional insurance for their own valuable equipment (such as laptops and cameras), as the laboratory accepts no responsibility for loss or damage to such items.

## Annual Audits and Continuing Education

The ANU engineering laboratory safety system includes an **annual audit mechanism**: in February and July each year, the ANU Safety and Health Office (2023), together with external auditors (such as Standards Australia), conducts compliance inspections of the laboratories. The audits cover the completeness of training records, equipment maintenance logs, PPE availability and emergency drill records. The results are published as reports on the ANU safety portal, where students can see their laboratory's score and the recommended improvements.

### Refresher Training and Skill Development

Every student's **safety training record** is valid for 12 months, and the system automatically sends a reminder email 30 days before it expires. Refresher training is usually a 1-hour online module covering new equipment procedures and recent incident case studies. In addition, the College runs 2–3 **laboratory safety seminars** each semester on topics such as "high-voltage electrical safety" or "nanomaterials handling guidelines". Students can volunteer to attend, and those who complete a seminar receive an additional authorisation level upgrade.

### The Student Safety Representative System

ANU engineering runs a **Student Safety Representative** scheme: each semester the College selects 3–5 representatives from senior students to collect feedback on laboratory hazards and take part in monthly safety meetings. Representatives must complete an additional 16-hour training course (covering Australia's WHS legal framework and incident investigation methods), and their work counts toward volunteering hours. According to a survey by the ANU Students' Association (2024), about 72% of students believe the representative scheme has effectively improved safety communication in the laboratories.

## FAQ

### Q1: How long does it take for ANU engineering students to complete laboratory safety training?

The General Safety Induction takes an average of 4.2 hours (including 28 videos and the final test) and must be completed within 14 days of enrolment. Dedicated equipment training (for lasers or high-voltage systems, for example) takes an additional 6–8 hours. Total training time is usually no more than 12.2 hours, but it is recommended that you spread it over at least 2 days to avoid failing the test through one long, tiring session.

### Q2: Who should I contact if I have an accident in the laboratory?

Provide initial first aid or evacuate first, then call ANU Security immediately at 6125 2249 (24 hours). For medical emergencies, call 000 for an ambulance. All incidents must be reported in writing within 24 hours through the ANU Incident Reporting Tool. The Safety Officer's and technicians' contact details are posted at the laboratory entrance — save them to your phone in advance.

### Q3: Do I have to buy my own lab PPE? Does the university provide free equipment?

You can collect one free set of basic PPE (goggles, lab coat, gloves) at the College's PPE checkpoint, but replacements must be purchased yourself. Students are advised to bring their own properly fitted equipment; goggles cost about AUD 15–30 a pair and lab coats about AUD 25–50 each. Advanced PPE such as steel-toe safety boots must be bought yourself — the College does not provide free replacements. Note: sharing PPE with others carries a cross-contamination risk and is not recommended.
