The Australian National University (ANU) handled approximately 420 formal student complaints in 2024, of which about 35% related to grade assessment and 15% involved allegations of discrimination or unfair treatment (ANU Student Administration, 2024 Annual Report). For students aged 18 to 30 at ANU, understanding the full formal academic grievance process — from informal communication to independent review — is essential to protecting your academic rights. Drawing on ANU's official policies and the framework of the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA), this article sets out the complaint procedures for grade disputes, discrimination, and unfair treatment, with actionable steps and timelines.

## The Informal Stage Before Lodging a Complaint

ANU policy requires students to first attempt informal communication with the directly involved parties before starting a formal grievance. This step is designed to resolve misunderstandings or procedural errors at the lowest cost, and it succeeds in about 60% of cases (ANU Student Complaint Policy, revised 2023).

**Step 1: Speak to the course convenor.** Within **20 business days** of receiving a grade or experiencing an incident, raise your concerns in writing with the course lecturer or convenor, by email or a booked meeting. Be specific about the disputed points, such as particular clauses of the marking rubric or records of classroom interaction. ANU requires the academic staff member to respond in writing within **10 business days**.

**Step 2: Escalate to the College level.** If the course convenor cannot resolve the issue, submit a written request to the Deputy Dean (Education) of your College. This stage requires attaching all records of prior communication. The College must organise a mediation meeting within **15 business days** and issue minutes of the mediation. According to ANU 2023 data, about 70% of informal complaints are resolved at this stage without entering the formal process.

## Starting a Formal Complaint: Eligibility and Acceptance Criteria

If informal communication fails, students have the right to submit a formal complaint to ANU Student Administration. Formal complaints must meet strict acceptance criteria, or they may be dismissed outright.

**Acceptance criteria include**: the complaint must be based on specific facts (such as a marking calculation error, a procedural breach, or evidence of discrimination), not subjective dissatisfaction with academic judgment; the complaint must be lodged within **20 business days** of the end of informal communication; and it must use ANU's designated Form 1, with all supporting documents attached (such as email correspondence, marking criteria, and witness statements). After revising its policy in 2022, ANU explicitly listed "pure disappointment with a grade" as a ground for non-acceptance (ANU Academic Appeals Policy, 2022).

**How to submit**: send the complete package to student.complaints@anu.edu.au, or hand it in at the Student Central counter in person. ANU commits to sending an acknowledgement of receipt within **5 business days** and assigning a Complaints Officer to manage the case.

## The Dedicated Appeal Path for Grade Disputes

For grade disputes, ANU operates a two-track mechanism: "Grade Review" and "Academic Appeal". Students must first apply for a Grade Review, then decide whether to proceed to an academic appeal.

**Grade review process**: within **15 business days** of the release of results, students can submit a grade review application to the College offering the course. The review is conducted by an academic staff member who was not involved in the original marking, focusing on whether the marking strictly followed the published criteria and whether there were calculation errors or administrative oversights. ANU's 2024 internal audit shows that after grade review, grades were raised in about 12% of cases and lowered in 3%. The review outcome must be communicated to the student in writing within **20 business days**.

**Academic Appeals Committee**: if dissatisfied with the grade review outcome, students can appeal to the ANU Academic Appeals Committee (AAC). The AAC comprises three senior academic staff members, at least one of whom must come from a different College. Appeals must be lodged within **10 business days** of receiving the grade review outcome. The AAC may uphold the original grade, order a re-mark, or recommend repeating the course. Its decision is final within ANU.

## Complaint Procedures for Discrimination and Unfair Treatment

For discrimination based on race, gender, disability, age, or sexual orientation, as well as unfair treatment by staff or fellow students, ANU has a dedicated "Discrimination and Harassment Complaint" channel, separate from the grade appeal system.

**Initial complaint**: students should first contact the ANU Equity & Diversity Centre, which offers confidential counselling. Complaints must be submitted in writing with a detailed account of the incident: time, place, people involved, and witnesses. ANU commits to commencing a preliminary investigation within **10 business days** and assessing whether there is "immediate risk" (such as continuing to attend class causing further harm). If immediate risk exists, ANU may temporarily adjust the student's course schedule or contact arrangements during the investigation.

**Formal investigation**: if the initial complaint cannot be mediated, the case is referred to the ANU Conduct & Complaints Office. The investigation is conducted by an External Independent Investigator, usually within **30 business days**. The investigator has the power to summon witnesses and obtain surveillance or communication records. Under the Australian Human Rights Commission (AHRC) 2023 guidelines, such investigations must follow the "principles of natural justice", meaning both parties have the right to know the allegations and respond to them.

**Remedies**: if discrimination or unfair treatment is substantiated, ANU's possible measures include: a formal apology, training for the staff member involved, re-assessment of grades, compensation in the form of psychological counselling, or — in extreme cases — dismissal of the staff member. If dissatisfied with ANU's internal investigation outcome, students can take an external complaint to the Australian Human Rights Commission (AHRC) or a state-level anti-discrimination body.

## Timeline and Key Milestones During the Complaint Process

Strict time management is critical to a successful complaint. ANU policy sets hard deadlines at each stage; missing them usually results in automatic dismissal.

**Core timeline**: after the incident or grade release, the informal communication window is **20 business days**; after informal communication fails, the formal complaint window is **20 business days**; the grade review application window is **15 business days**; and after the grade review outcome, the AAC appeal window is **10 business days**. From start to final decision, the whole process theoretically lasts no more than **120 business days**, although in practice about 80% of cases conclude within 60 business days (ANU Student Administration, 2024 data).

**Expedited procedure**: for urgent cases involving "immediate safety risks" or "graduation delays", students can request expedited processing. ANU must decide whether to expedite within **5 business days** and complete the investigation within **15 business days**. For example, if a discrimination incident prevents a student from sitting the final exam normally, the expedited procedure can arrange an alternative assessment as a priority.

## External Appeal Channels and Legal Remedies

If ANU's internal processes fail to resolve the dispute, students have the right to seek external remedies from Australian federal or state regulators. These bodies hold decision-making powers independent of the university.

**TEQSA complaints**: the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA) regulates university compliance. Students can complain to TEQSA about whether ANU has breached the Higher Education Standards Framework (2021). TEQSA does not handle individual cases directly, but if it finds systemic breaches (such as missing anti-discrimination policies), it can fine ANU or restrict its ability to enrol students. In 2023, TEQSA received about 150 complaints against Australian universities, 8 of which related to ANU.

**AHRC complaints**: if the complaint involves discrimination, students can lodge a complaint with the Australian Human Rights Commission (AHRC). The AHRC offers free conciliation services, with a success rate of 65% (AHRC Annual Report 2022-2023). If conciliation fails, students can take the case to the Federal Court. AHRC complaints carry no application fee, but must be lodged within **24 months** of the incident.

**State-level anti-discrimination bodies**: the ACT Anti-Discrimination Commission also accepts related complaints. Its process is more streamlined than the AHRC's, and mediation is usually completed within **3 months**.

## Preparing Your Complaint: Documentation and Evidence Management

A complete complaint package significantly improves your chances of success. ANU recommends following the "FACT" principle: Facts, Actions, Communication, Timeline.

**Core evidence checklist**: all emails related to the incident, marking rubrics, marking feedback, the course outline, class recordings (with consent), written witness statements, and medical or psychological counselling records (if health impacts are involved). ANU explicitly requires evidence to be in English or accompanied by a NAATI-certified translation.

**Structure of the written statement**: the statement should have three parts: an objective account of the incident (time, place, people, and a summary of conversations); specific references to ANU policy or legal provisions (e.g., "under clause 4.2 of the ANU Student Conduct Rules, staff must not..."); and the remedy sought (e.g., "request a re-mark" or "request a written apology"). Avoid emotional language such as "unfair" or "discrimination"; instead use specific allegations such as "breach of clause 3 of the marking criteria".

## FAQ

### Q1: How soon after grades are released must I lodge a formal complaint?

After grades are released, students must apply for a grade review within **15 business days**, or lodge a formal complaint within **20 business days** of informal communication failing. ANU is entitled to reject complaints lodged after these deadlines. According to ANU 2024 data, about 18% of complaints are dismissed outright for being out of time.

### Q2: Can I keep attending classes while my complaint is pending?

While a complaint is pending, students generally retain the right to attend classes and sit exams normally. However, in cases involving discrimination or harassment, ANU may temporarily adjust course arrangements based on an "immediate risk assessment", such as changing classes or permitting remote participation. Under ANU's 2023 policy, such adjustments do not affect the student's academic record.

### Q3: If ANU's internal complaint fails, where else can I go?

If ANU's internal complaint fails, students can lodge external complaints with TEQSA (for university compliance), the AHRC (for discrimination), or the ACT Anti-Discrimination Commission (for local discrimination). None of these bodies charge application fees, and the AHRC's conciliation success rate is 65% (AHRC Annual Report 2022-2023).
