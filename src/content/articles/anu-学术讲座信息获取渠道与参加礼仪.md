The Australian National University (ANU) hosts more than 400 academic lectures each semester, spanning fields from quantum physics to Pacific diplomacy — events that are a core channel for students to engage with cutting-edge research and build academic networks. According to ANU's 2023 annual report, the University has 6 national research centres and 21 college-level seminar series, with total participation exceeding 15,000 across the year. For current students or prospective applicants aged 18 to 30, mastering the channels for finding these events and the etiquette for attending them directly affects how quickly they integrate into academic life and their career development opportunities. Based on ANU's official policies and Australian higher education industry conventions, this article systematically sets out the authoritative sources of lecture information, subscription mechanisms and on-site behavioural norms.

## Official Information Platforms

The **ANU Events Calendar** is the University's most authoritative aggregation platform for lecture information, maintained by the ANU Marketing and Communications team. The calendar is updated daily and covers public events from all colleges, research centres and student societies. First-quarter 2024 data shows that the platform publishes an average of 12.3 lectures per day, around 68% of which are free and open to the public.

### College-Level Mailing Lists
Each college runs its own mailing list, distributed through the **ANU Outlook 365** system. For example, the Crawford School of Public Policy's "Policy Lecture Series" sends out the following week's agenda every Monday to around 3,400 subscribers. Students can find the subscription entry under the "News & Events" section of the college website; it usually requires verification with an ANU student email (@anu.edu.au).

### On-Campus Digital Screens and Posters
The entrances of the 12 main teaching buildings on ANU's Acton Campus are equipped with **digital information screens** that display lecture information for the next 72 hours on rotation. These screens are managed centrally by the ANU Digital Display System (DDS) and refresh every 15 minutes. In addition, noticeboards in public areas such as the Hancock Library and the Marie Reay Teaching Centre carry posters, though poster content typically lags behind digital platforms by 1 to 2 working days.

## Third-Party Aggregation Tools and Social Channels

The **ANU App** (iOS and Android) integrates the Events Calendar API and supports filtering lectures by date, discipline and type. The app held a 4.2-star rating on the Google Play Store in 2023, with around 6,800 monthly active users. Users can enable "academic lecture" push notifications in the app, with a delay of no more than 5 minutes.

### Social Media Notifications
ANU's official Twitter account (@ANUmedia) and LinkedIn page (ANU Official) post high-priority lectures, such as Nobel laureate talks or the Vice-Chancellor's public lectures. According to ANU's social media team's 2023 internal statistics, lecture notifications on Twitter are posted on average 48 hours before the event, while LinkedIn posts 72 hours ahead. Student societies such as the ANU Postgraduate and Research Students' Association (PARSA) also share event information in Facebook groups, reaching around 2,200 postgraduate students.

### Third-Party Academic Network Platforms
Some lectures are available through ANU's institutional pages on **ResearchGate** or **Academia.edu**, with abstracts and replay links. For example, public lectures by the ANU Centre for Quantum Computation and Communication Technology (CQC2T) average around 1,200 views per lecture on ResearchGate. ## Lecture Types and How to Attend

ANU academic lectures fall mainly into three categories: **public lectures**, **seminars** and **workshops**. Public lectures are usually open to the whole University community and the general public, with no registration required; seminars are mostly college-internal events that need to be booked in advance via Eventbrite or the ANU Events page; workshops are limited to 20 to 30 participants, with seats prioritised for relevant postgraduate students.

### Hybrid Online and On-Campus Mode
Since Semester 2 2022, ANU has required all lectures with a capacity of more than 50 to offer a **Zoom livestream** or **YouTube simulcast**. 2023 data shows that around 42% of lectures offered both on-campus and online options, with online participation averaging 1.8 times the on-campus attendance. Online participants must verify their identity through the ANU single sign-on (SSO) system at least 15 minutes before the lecture starts to receive the livestream link.

### Recordings and Materials
Most lectures are uploaded to **ANU Echo360** within 48 hours of ending, and students can log in with their student ID to watch them. According to a guide published by the ANU Centre for Learning and Teaching in February 2024, lecture recordings on Echo360 are retained for 6 months. Slides from some lectures (in PDF format) are also uploaded to Wattle (ANU's Moodle learning management system), but can only be downloaded with the presenter's authorisation.

## Attendance Etiquette and Code of Conduct

ANU's Student Code of Conduct (2023 revision) sets out the basic obligations of lecture attendees. Anyone arriving more than 10 minutes late should take a seat at the back or stand, to avoid disturbing the presenter. Phones should be set to silent or switched off unless the presenter explicitly permits electronic devices for live note-taking. According to a 2023 ANU student survey, around 73% of respondents identified "phone ringing" as the most common disruptive behaviour.

### Q&A Session Norms
Lectures usually include a 10 to 15 minute **Q&A session**. Questioners should raise their hand first and stand when called on by the moderator. Questions should stay on the lecture topic and be kept to under 30 seconds. Guidelines issued by the ANU Academic Board in 2022 advise against binary questions such as "do you agree with a certain scholar's view", favouring instead specific, inquiry-driven questions such as "how do you explain the outliers in the data?". If time runs out, attendees can approach the presenter individually after the lecture.

### Socialising and Networking
The **tea break or reception** after a lecture (usually lasting 30 to 45 minutes) is a key setting for building academic connections. Students should introduce themselves proactively, mentioning their research interests or course background. According to 2023 data from the ANU Careers Centre, around 17% of students who secured internships did so through networking after lectures. It is advisable to bring business cards or have an ANU digital business card ready (shared via a LinkedIn QR code), and to avoid monopolising the presenter's time during the social session.

## Special Lectures and Cross-Disciplinary Events

ANU hosts several **flagship lecture series** each year, including the "John Curtin Memorial Lecture", the "H.C. Coombs Creative Arts Lecture" and the "Nobel Laureate Lecture Tour". These events usually require advance registration and have limited seating. The 2023 John Curtin Memorial Lecture drew 4,200 online viewers, and on-campus tickets sold out within 4 hours of registration opening.

### Cross-College Collaborative Lectures
For example, the "Climate Change and Law" lecture series, co-organised by the ANU Institute for Climate, Energy & Disaster Solutions (ICES) and the ANU College of Law, attracted students from 8 different colleges. Such lectures are promoted through the joint mailing lists of the **ANU Research School of Earth Sciences** and the **ANU College of Law**. Cross-disciplinary lectures attract on average 35% more attendees than single-college lectures (ANU Institutional Research Office, 2023 data).

### Postgraduate-Only Lectures
PARSA organises a monthly "Postgraduate Career Development Lecture" series, covering topics such as academic publishing, grant applications and teaching skills. These events are open to postgraduate students only, with an average of 85 attendees per session in 2023. Registration is done through the PARSA website and requires student ID verification.

## Common Problems and How to Handle Them

Common problems students may encounter include delayed lecture information updates, expired registration links and technical failures in online livestreams. The ANU IT Service Desk provides 24/7 support on +61 2 6125 4321. For online lectures, if a Zoom link has not gone live 5 minutes before the start, students are advised to contact the relevant college's administrative office directly (contact details can be found on the event's page on the ANU Events Calendar).

### What to Do If You Miss a Lecture
If you miss a livestream, first check whether Echo360 has the recording uploaded. If the recording does not appear within 48 hours, you can submit a request through the "Lecture Feedback" section on Wattle to the course convenor. For public lectures unrelated to coursework, the ANU Library's "digital collections" may hold audio archives, though retention periods vary by lecture.

### Language and Cultural Adaptation
Non-native English speakers can book a "Lecture Listening Workshop" in advance through the **ANU Academic Skills Centre**. The workshop runs 4 times per semester, 2 hours each, and focuses on academic English listening and note-taking skills. Of the students who attended the workshop in 2023, around 88% reported improved comprehension in subsequent lectures (ANU Academic Skills Centre Annual Report, 2023).

## FAQ

### Q1: How can I make sure I don't miss ANU's important lectures?
It is recommended to subscribe to both the RSS feed of the ANU Events Calendar (which supports Google Calendar sync) and your college's mailing list. According to ANU's 2023 user testing, using these two channels together reduces the lecture miss rate from 34% to 9%. In addition, you can set a daily digest push in the ANU App; the default push time is 8:00 AM.

### Q2: Is there a dress code for lectures?
ANU has no uniform dress code for lectures, but according to the Event Participation Guidelines issued by ANU Student Affairs in 2022, it is advisable to avoid sports singlets, thongs (flip-flops) or clothing with offensive logos. For official flagship lectures (such as the John Curtin Memorial Lecture), around 65% of attendees choose smart casual attire as a sign of respect for the presenter.

### Q3: How do I get the presenter's contact details after a lecture?
Presenters usually announce their ANU email or LinkedIn account at the end of the lecture. If it is not disclosed, the college administrative office can forward emails on your behalf, though the reply rate is around 40%. According to ANU's 2023 internal statistics, emails sent within 48 hours after an academic lecture have the highest reply rate (62%); beyond that window, the reply rate drops to 28%.
