Data from ANU's 2023 *Student Appeals and Complaints Policy* shows that the university handled 1,247 formal academic appeals in 2022, of which 68% involved grading disputes and 22% involved academic integrity allegations. According to the Australian Department of Education and Training's 2023 *International Student Annual Report*, the average appeal success rate for international students at Australian universities is 41%, but ANU's success rate through formal channels comes in slightly above the national average at 47%. Part of this gap comes down to the university's strict insistence on complete documentary evidence — for every key piece of evidence missing from an appeal, the success rate drops by about 12 percentage points. For ANU international students, mastering the official appeal channels and a systematic approach to evidence collection is an essential skill for protecting your academic rights.

## The Three-Tier Structure of Formal Appeal Channels

ANU's academic appeal system is structured in three tiers, each handled by a different body with its own time limits. Tier one is **informal resolution**: within 20 working days of receiving your result or penalty notice, you request a review through the course lecturer or the college student centre. Tier two is the **formal appeal**: if informal resolution fails, you have 10 working days to submit a written appeal to the Associate Dean (Education) of your college. Tier three is the **University Appeals Committee (UAC)**, which hears appeals against college decisions; the deadline is 15 working days from when the college decision is delivered.

### Tier One: Practical Notes on Informal Resolution

Informal resolution is not a compulsory first step, but ANU officially recommends it as the preferred route. Students need to fill in the *Course Result Review Request Form* and attach the marking rubric and a copy of their own submitted work. According to ANU's 2023 *Annual Academic Quality Assurance Report*, about 34% of informal resolution requests get a result within 10 working days, and 62% of grade-increase requests are partially or fully approved.

### Tier Two: Written Requirements for a Formal Appeal

A formal appeal must use ANU's *Formal Appeal Form* and include three core elements: the **legal basis** for the appeal (such as procedural unfairness, marking error, or special/compassionate circumstances), an indexed list of supporting evidence, and the remedy sought (such as re-marking, a supplementary exam, or a grade change). Citing clause 9 of the *ANU Student Conduct Rules* (grading procedures) or clause 12 (academic integrity handling) in your grounds for appeal increases the chance of it being accepted.

### Tier Three: Independent Review by the University Appeals Committee

The UAC has three members: a professor from another college (chair), a student representative, and an administrative staff member. The committee may call for the original marking records, invigilation footage (if any), and all communication records. In 2022 the UAC heard 89 cases and overturned or modified the college decision in 27 of them (30.3%). The committee usually issues its written decision within 30 working days.

## The Four Core Categories of Evidence

Evidence is the foundation of a successful appeal. ANU divides acceptable evidence into four categories: **academic records** (transcripts, marking rubrics, assignment feedback), **communication records** (emails and meeting minutes with lecturers or the college), **medical and compassionate circumstances** (medical certificates, counselling records, death certificates), and **procedural documents** (course outlines, exam arrangement notices, policy texts). Every category must meet the format and currency requirements set out in ANU's *Evidence Admissibility Guidelines*.

### How to Obtain Academic Record Evidence

Students can download electronic transcripts from the ANU ISIS system, but appeals require a timestamped PDF version. Marking rubrics are normally provided by the course lecturer; if the lecturer refuses, students can request them under clause 5 of ANU's *Freedom of Information Policy*. Assignment feedback (including Turnitin originality reports) should be kept in the original submitted version — do not submit cropped screenshots.

### How to Keep Communication Records

All formal correspondence with the university should be sent from your official ANU email address (ending in @anu.edu.au) and copied to your personal email as a backup. After a phone conversation, send a confirmation email within 24 hours summarising what was discussed and asking the other party to confirm. Meeting minutes should record the date, attendees, items discussed and conclusions, and be confirmed by signature or email from both sides.

## The Specific Process for Grade Dispute Appeals

Grade disputes are the most common appeal type among ANU international students, making up 68% of appeals in 2022. The process has three steps. First, within 20 working days of receiving your grade, submit a *Grade Review Request* to the course lecturer, attaching your submitted work and the marking rubric. Second, if the lecturer upholds the original grade, you can ask the Associate Dean (Education) for an **independent marker** — another professor who was not involved in the original marking — to re-mark the work. Third, if you are still not satisfied, you can appeal to the UAC.

### How the Independent Marker Mechanism Works

The independent marker is appointed by the Associate Dean (Education) from another college or an external body, and must complete the re-marking within 15 working days. The new mark and the original mark are combined as a weighted average (original mark 60%, new mark 40%) to produce the final grade. According to ANU internal statistics for 2022, under the independent marker mechanism about 28% of cases had their grades raised, 12% lowered, and 60% unchanged.

### How to Argue a Dispute About the Marking Criteria

If you believe the marking criteria themselves are flawed (for example, unreasonable weightings or vague marking dimensions), you need to provide marking rubrics from comparable courses as comparative evidence. ANU policy requires rubrics to be published before the course starts; if you can prove the rubric was changed after the exam, your appeal success rate can rise to 68%.

## The Appeal Path for Academic Integrity Allegations

Allegations of academic misconduct (plagiarism, contract cheating, collusion) follow a process separate from grade disputes. ANU's Academic Integrity Office handled 412 cases in 2022, 35% of which involved international students. The process has an investigation phase and a hearing phase. In the investigation phase, students have 10 working days to submit a written explanation and evidence. The hearing is run by the Academic Integrity Committee, and students may bring a support person.

### Evidence Checklist for the Investigation Phase

Students need to submit: the original assignment file, the Turnitin originality report, drafting history (such as Google Docs version history), the list of cited sources, and any evidence that the work was completed independently (such as study notes or discussion records). If the allegation involves contract cheating, students must also provide login records, IP address information, and device usage logs.

### Your Procedural Rights at the Hearing

At the hearing, students have the right to question witnesses for the accusing party, view all evidence against them, and submit supplementary evidence. The committee issues its decision within 15 working days after the hearing. Possible outcomes include: dismissing the allegation, reducing the penalty (for example, from zero marks to a deduction), or upholding the original decision. In 2022, about 23% of allegations were dismissed or downgraded at the hearing stage.

## Evidence Standards for Special Circumstances

Special Consideration applies to students whose academic performance was affected by uncontrollable events such as sudden illness, family emergencies, or natural disasters. ANU requires students to submit the *Special Consideration Application Form* within 3 working days of the event, together with supporting documents issued by a third party. Medical evidence must be issued by a registered doctor or psychologist and must state the period during which the illness affected your ability to study.

### Specific Requirements for Medical Evidence

A medical certificate must include: the date of diagnosis, a description of symptoms, the recommended rest period, and the doctor's registration number (AHPRA registration number). ANU does not accept brief certificates that only say "attended a consultation." In 2022, appeals rejected for incomplete medical evidence made up 41% of all appeals of this type. Psychological assessment reports must be issued by a clinical psychologist and include a DSM-5 diagnostic code.

### Certification Requirements for Family Emergency Evidence

Family emergencies (such as the death or serious illness of an immediate family member) require a death certificate, hospital diagnosis, or court documents. Documents not in English must be translated by a NAATI-accredited translator, with the translator's credentials attached. ANU does not accept written statements from relatives as standalone evidence.

## Appeal Deadlines and Extension Requests

Every tier has strict time limits: 20 working days for informal resolution, 10 working days for a formal appeal, and 15 working days for a UAC appeal. If you cannot meet a deadline due to force majeure, you can apply for an **extension of time**, but the written application — with supporting evidence — must be submitted before the original deadline. In 2022, ANU approved 63% of extension applications, with an average approval time of 5 working days.

### How to Apply for an Extension

Your extension request must clearly state: the specific reason you cannot submit on time, your expected completion date, and the steps you have already taken. Submit the application directly to the body currently handling your case (the college office or the UAC secretariat). If the extension is refused, you can appeal to the next-higher body within 5 working days. ### What to Do If You Miss All Deadlines

If you miss every appeal deadline, you can make a special request to the ANU Student Ombudsman. The Ombudsman has the power to restore appeal rights in "extremely exceptional circumstances" (such as hospitalisation, detention, or natural disasters). In 2022, the Ombudsman received 34 such requests and approved 9 of them (26.5%).

## External Appeal Channels as a Backstop

If you have exhausted all internal ANU channels and remain unsatisfied, you can lodge a complaint with the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA). TEQSA oversees universities' compliance with the *Higher Education Standards Framework*, but it does not hear individual grade disputes — it only deals with procedural breaches (such as an appeal not being handled according to policy). In 2023, TEQSA received 127 complaints against ANU, 11 of which proceeded to formal investigation.

### When TEQSA Will Accept a Complaint

You must provide a written record of the entire internal appeal process, including all decision letters and correspondence. TEQSA only intervenes when it finds "systemic procedural deficiencies" at ANU; isolated marking errors are generally outside its jurisdiction. Complaints must be lodged within 12 months of ANU's final decision.

### Legal Aid and Free Advice

The ANU Student Association (ANUSA) offers free legal advice, open Tuesdays and Thursdays from 2-4 pm. Legal Aid ACT provides limited representation services for students on low incomes. In 2022, ANUSA's legal aid department handled 89 consultations related to academic appeals, 52% of which involved international students.

## FAQ

### Q1: Do I have to keep attending classes while my appeal is pending?

Yes. Under ANU policy, the appeal process does not suspend your academic obligations. You must keep attending classes, submitting assignments and sitting exams until the appeal outcome is announced. In 2022, 7 appeals were declared "inadmissible" because the student had missed classes during the process.

### Q2: Can I resubmit an appeal if it fails?

The same grounds can only be appealed once. However, if you discover new evidence (such as marking records or witness testimony you did not previously have), you can submit a "second appeal based on new evidence" within 30 working days of the original decision. In 2022, 18% of such second appeals were accepted, and 33% of those changed the original decision.

### Q3: Do international students have to pay anything to appeal?

Tier one informal resolution and tier two formal appeals are both free. A tier three UAC appeal requires a AU$100 deposit, which is fully refunded if the appeal succeeds. In 2023, the UAC deposit refund rate was 30.3% (matching the appeal success rate). TEQSA complaints are free.
