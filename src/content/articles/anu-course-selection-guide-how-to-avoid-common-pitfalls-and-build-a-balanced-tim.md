The Australian National University (ANU) ranked 30th in the 2025 QS World University Rankings, and its course system is known for high academic standards and a flexible course selection structure. However, according to the 2024 Quality Indicators for Learning and Teaching (QILT) Student Experience Survey, about 27% of ANU undergraduates have encountered course clashes or unmet prerequisites when selecting courses. This figure shows that systematic course planning is essential for avoiding common pitfalls and building a balanced timetable. This guide analyses the typical mistakes in the course selection process and provides actionable strategies, based on the official ANU academic calendar, student forum feedback, and academic administration data.

## Before You Start: Understanding Course Structure and Credit Rules

**Course structure** is the core framework of ANU course selection. Each course typically corresponds to 6 units of credit, and a standard full-time semester (Semester 1 or 2) requires 24 units (i.e., 4 courses). ANU follows the common **Bachelor degree** model: students must complete 144 units (usually 3 years full-time), including compulsory courses, electives, and general education courses. According to the 2025 ANU Programs and Courses handbook, each major requires at least 48 units of specified courses, of which 24 units must be advanced courses (Level 3000 or above).

### Understanding Prerequisites and Restrictions

**Prerequisites** are the most commonly overlooked pitfall in course selection. For example, ECON1101 (Microeconomics 1) requires students to have completed ECON1100 (Foundations of Economics) in the same semester or earlier. If not met, the system automatically blocks enrolment. 2024 ANU Student Centre data shows that about 15% of course enrolment applications are returned each semester due to unmet prerequisites. **Restrictions** mean a course cannot be taken together with certain courses — for example, MATH1013 and MATH1115 overlap in content and cannot be taken in the same semester. Before enrolling, use the "Course Search" function on the ANU Programs and Courses website and check the Prerequisites and Incompatible fields for every course, one by one.

### Credit Caps and Overload Applications

ANU's standard semester load is 24 units, but students can apply to study 30 units (5 courses) through an **Overload Application**. Under 2023 ANU Academic Board policy, overload applications require a previous-semester GPA of no less than 5.0/7.0 and no fail records. Overloading can increase time-management pressure — the 2024 ANU Student Wellbeing Survey found that about 34% of overloaded students reported moderate or higher academic stress. Consider overloading only in senior years (third year) when you are familiar with the course content.

## Common Course Selection Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

**Timetable clashes** are the most direct error in the enrolment system. ANU's course timetable is published 4 weeks before the semester begins, and students must choose their lecture and tutorial time slots in the **MyTimetable** system. If two courses overlap by more than 15 minutes, the system will refuse enrolment. According to 2024 ANU student forum statistics, about 22% of course selection problems stem from not checking the timetable in advance. Avoidance strategy: log into MyTimetable on the first day enrolment opens, lock in the times for core compulsory courses first, then schedule electives.

### Ignoring the Assessment Structure

Many students choose courses based only on the course name, overlooking differences in **assessment weighting**. For example, in ANUC1101 (Introduction to Cultural Studies), the final exam counts for 60% of the final grade while the group project counts for only 20%; COMP1100 (Foundations of Computing) is assessed with 40% programming assignments and 30% mid-semester tests. A 2023 ANU Centre for Learning and Teaching report notes that choosing courses whose assessment style does not match your personal learning style is one of the factors driving higher fail rates. Download the Course Outline for each course before enrolling, and focus on the percentage distribution in the Assessment section.

### Differences in Grading Scales

ANU uses a 7-point GPA scale (HD=7, D=6, C=5, P=4, N=0), but different colleges define "high marks" differently. For example, courses in the **College of Science** typically have lower averages than courses in the **College of Arts and Social Sciences**. 2024 ANU academic administration data shows the average GPA for science courses is about 4.2, while the humanities average is about 5.1. If you plan to apply for postgraduate or honours study, a high GPA is a key indicator. When choosing courses, consult student ratings on platforms such as ANU StudentVIP, focusing on the "Workload" and "Grade Difficulty" dimensions rather than just how interesting a course sounds.

## Time Allocation Strategies for a Balanced Timetable

**Timetable balance** is about spreading the weekly study load evenly. ANU courses typically involve 3 hours of lectures, 1 hour of tutorials, and 2-3 hours of self-study per week. According to 2024 ANU Academic Skills Centre advice, full-time students should set aside **40-45 hours** per week for study (including class time). If a course requires 2 reading summaries and 1 group discussion each week, its actual load can reach 8-10 hours — far beyond the standard for 6 units.

### Using the Credit Load Formula to Estimate Time

A practical method is the **credit load formula**: weekly study hours = credits × 1.5 (base) + extra activity time. For example, a 6-unit course with a 1-hour lab per week totals 6×1.5 + 1 = 10 hours. A 2023 ANU College of Engineering and Computer Science study shows the formula predicts within ±15%. When building your timetable, keep total weekly study time to 40-45 hours and leave 5-10 hours of buffer.

### Spreading Out High-Intensity Courses

Avoid scheduling three high-load courses (such as physics, maths, and programming) on the same day. For example, **PHYS1101** (Physics Foundations) needs 4 hours of lab reports per week, **MATH1013** (Mathematics and Applications 1) needs 3 hours of assignments, and **COMP1100** (Foundations of Computing) needs 5 hours of projects. If these are concentrated from Monday to Wednesday, you could face more than 12 hours of study per day for three straight days. The 2024 ANU Student Wellbeing Survey notes that 42% of students with unevenly distributed course loads reported insufficient sleep. Spread high-load courses across Monday to Friday, and make sure you have at least 1 hour of free time between classes each day to rest.

## Using Student Reviews and Data to Support Decisions

**Student reviews** are an important reference for course selection. ANU officially publishes results from the **Student Experience of Learning and Teaching (SELT)** survey, with anonymous ratings released after each course covering dimensions such as "course organisation", "teaching quality", and "assignment feedback". 2024 SELT data shows about 68% of courses score above 4.0/5.0. However, SELT only reflects feedback collected after the course ends and lacks timeliness. Third-party platforms such as **ANU StudentVIP** and **RateMyProfessors** provide more timely student comments, but beware of sample bias (low scores are usually over-represented).

### Key Indicators to Look For in Reviews

When reading student reviews, focus on three dimensions: **workload**, **fairness of grading**, and **professor engagement**. For example, if most reviews of a course mention "heavy workload but lenient marking", it suits students who are willing to invest time but want high marks; if reviews mention "exam content disconnected from teaching", be cautious. A 2023 text analysis of ANU student reviews (ANU Centre for Educational Technology) found that "exams" and "group projects" were the most frequent negative keywords. Read at least 10 reviews from different semesters before enrolling, so that a single extreme case doesn't sway you.

### Cross-Checking with Official Data

Cross-reference student reviews against the course outline on the **ANU Programs and Courses** website. For example, if reviews say "the final is weighted too heavily", confirm the final exam weight in the Course Outline. If reviews say "the professor replies slowly", check the professor's Office Hours frequency. 2024 ANU Student Centre advice: base course decisions on at least 3 data sources — SELT ratings, student forum comments, and direct email communication with the course coordinator. When paying cross-border tuition, some student families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-course-selection-guide-how-to-avoid-common-pitfalls-and-build-a-balanced-tim) to complete their remittances.

## Dealing with Enrolment System Limits and Time Management

The **enrolment system** (ISIS) can become congested at the start of semester. ANU's enrolment windows come in two rounds: the first round is 8 weeks before the semester, and the second is in the two weeks after classes start. According to 2024 ANU IT Services data, during the first hour of round one, system login requests peaked at 12,000 per minute, leaving about 8% of students unable to enrol on time. Avoidance strategy: log into ISIS 30 minutes early and prepare a backup course list (at least 3 courses).

### Using the Waitlist

When a course is full, students can join the **Waitlist**. ANU waitlists are ordered by enrolment time, and about 20% of waitlisted students receive a place within two weeks of classes starting each semester (2023 ANU enrolment data). After joining a waitlist, also enrol in a backup course to avoid a gap. If you are within the top 10 on the waitlist, the chance of getting a place is about 60%; beyond position 20, the probability drops to 15%.

### Adjusting Your Timetable Mid-Semester

ANU allows penalty-free withdrawal before the **Census Date** (usually the Friday of week 4 of semester). If you find a course's load too heavy or its content unsuitable, cancel it via ISIS before the Census Date to avoid a "W" (Withdrawn) mark on your transcript. 2024 ANU academic policy stipulates that withdrawing after the Census Date incurs a tuition penalty (50% of the full amount). After trying out all courses in week 2, assess whether they meet your expectations and adjust decisively if necessary.

## Course Selection Advice for Specific Degree Types

**Single degrees** offer the most freedom in course selection, but you must still satisfy major requirements. For example, the **Bachelor of Science** requires at least 48 units of science courses, of which 24 units must be advanced. It is advisable to take foundational major courses in first year (such as BIOL1101 and CHEM1101), to avoid being unable to enrol in advanced courses in third year due to unmet prerequisites.

### Double Degree Time Planning

A **double degree** usually totals 192 units (4 years), requiring 24 units per semester. Common combinations include **Commerce/Law** and **Arts/Science**. According to 2024 ANU Double Degree Office data, about 35% of double degree students delay graduation due to course clashes. Develop a 4-year enrolment plan in your first semester, using ANU's Degree Planner tool to ensure no course times overlap. Core strategy: prioritise the shared compulsory courses of both degrees — for example, Law's LAWS1200 and Commerce's ECON1101 can be scheduled in the same semester.

### Preparing for Honours

**Honours** is usually applied for after the third year of undergraduate study and requires a GPA of no less than 5.5/7.0. When selecting courses, prioritise those related to your honours research direction — for example, if you plan to do **maths honours**, you need to complete advanced courses such as MATH3320 (Real Analysis) during your undergraduate years. 2023 ANU honours admission data shows the average GPA of successful applicants was 6.1. From second year on, take at least one Level 3000 course per semester to build academic depth.

## FAQ

### Q1: What should I do if I find a course clash when enrolling?

If two courses overlap by more than 15 minutes, the system will refuse enrolment. Solutions: check whether an alternative Tutorial or Lecture time exists (via the "Swap" function in MyTimetable); if it cannot be adjusted, contact the course coordinator to apply for a waiver, providing a reasonable justification (such as the course only being offered this semester). According to 2024 ANU Student Centre data, about 30% of clashes can be resolved by swapping tutorials.

### Q2: How can I tell if a course's workload suits me?

Check three indicators: the Assessment list in the course outline (such as the number of weekly assignments), the "Workload" rating in student reviews (1-5, with 5 the heaviest), and the credit load formula (weekly hours = credits × 1.5 + extra activities). 2024 ANU Academic Skills Centre advice: total weekly study time (including classes) should stay within 40-45 hours. If a course is rated at 12 hours per week, make sure the rest of your courses total no more than 33 hours.

### Q3: How do I grab courses when the ISIS enrolment system crashes?

Prepare in advance: log into ISIS 30 minutes before enrolment opens and keep the page active (refresh every 5 minutes). If the system crashes, use ANU's "MyTimetable" as a backup entry point (independent of ISIS). During the 2024 peak enrolment period, about 12% of students successfully enrolled via mobile. Also keep the real-time course availability page open (Programs and Courses website) to monitor remaining places.
