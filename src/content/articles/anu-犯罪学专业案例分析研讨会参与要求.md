The Case Analysis and Workshop module, offered by the ANU Criminology program since 2017, is a core compulsory component of the university's undergraduate and postgraduate criminology degrees. According to the Criminology Course Handbook published by the ANU College of Law in 2024, the workshop requires students to complete at least 3 in-depth case dissections with oral presentations of no less than 90 minutes each per semester, with participation scores accounting for 35% to 40% of the final grade. The Australian Institute of Criminology (2023) noted in its *National Crime Data Annual Report* that ANU's case workshop model has been adopted by the Australian Federal Police (AFP) as one of the reference standards for recruit training, making this course a practical model worth attention in criminology education today.

## Course Positioning and Academic Background

The **ANU Criminology case workshop** is not a conventional classroom discussion; it is a simulated exercise based on real judicial case files and published court judgments. The course is designed by the Centre for Criminology, part of the ANU College of Humanities and Social Sciences (CHL), and has had data-sharing agreements with the Australian Federal Police (AFP) and the ACT Justice Directorate since 2019, allowing students to work with de-identified real case materials.

Each semester the workshop centres on 4 to 6 themes spanning **white-collar crime, cybercrime, violent crime and miscarriages of justice**. Students receive a case summary package (averaging 25 to 40 pages) 48 hours before class and take part in role-play during the workshop — as defence counsel, prosecutor, victims' representative or sentencing judge. ANU Centre for Criminology data from the 2024 spring semester shows that about 78% of participants felt the model improved their understanding of judicial processes.

## Participation Eligibility and Prerequisites

### Undergraduate Participation Requirements

Undergraduates applying for the case workshop must meet the following conditions: have completed both **CRIM1001 (Introduction to Criminology)** and **CRIM2002 (Criminal Justice System)**, with a grade of at least 65 (Credit) in each. Under the ANU 2024 admissions handbook, each semester's workshop is capped at 40 places allocated on a first-come, first-served basis, with priority given to students with a GPA above 5.5/7.0.

### Postgraduate Participation Requirements

Postgraduates (including Master of Criminology students and PhD candidates) must complete **CRIM6001 (Criminological Theory and Methods)** and submit a 500-word case analysis statement of intent. PhD candidates may apply directly to the course coordinator for a waiver of prerequisites, but must provide evidence of relevant research experience. ANU Graduate School statistics for 2023 show that about 15 postgraduates participate each semester, 30% of whom are international students.

### Language and Skills Threshold

Because the workshop involves extensive legal terminology and impromptu debate, the **English language requirement** is explicitly written into the course policy. Non-native English speakers must provide an IELTS overall score of 7.0 (with no band below 6.5) or equivalent. In addition, students must complete a 2-hour "case dissection workshop" at the start of the semester to learn how to identify key chains of evidence and legal loopholes in a case.

## Workshop Format and Schedule

### Semester Timeline

Case workshops are normally held **between Weeks 3 and 12**, once a week, 90 minutes per session. The ANU academic calendar (2024 revised edition) requires students to confirm their groups in Week 1, with 4 to 5 students per group. Each workshop session has three segments: opening statements (20 minutes), adversarial debate (50 minutes) and tutor feedback (20 minutes).

### Pre-Class Preparation Requirements

Students must submit a **personal case analysis report** 48 hours before each workshop, with a word limit of 800 to 1000 words (undergraduates) or 1200 to 1500 words (postgraduates). The report must include a summary of the case facts, the legal issues in dispute, at least 3 citations of academic literature, and a statement of personal position. Late submissions incur a 20% deduction from that workshop's participation score.

### Absence and Make-Up Policy

Under the ANU 2024 *Student Assessment Policy*, students may miss 1 workshop per semester (with a medical certificate or explanation of an emergency). More than 2 absences result in automatic loss of eligibility for participation marks, requiring the module to be retaken the following semester. A make-up opportunity is allowed only once and requires an additional administration fee of A$300 (ANU Student Services 2024 fee schedule).

## Assessment Criteria and Grade Composition

**Participation score** is the core of the workshop grade, accounting for 35% to 40% of the total. The assessment rubric released by the ANU Centre for Criminology in 2024 shows three dimensions: quality of case analysis (40%), debate performance (35%) and teamwork (25%). Specifically, analysis quality requires citation of at least 3 peer-reviewed studies; debate performance is judged on logical clarity and the effectiveness of rebuttals; teamwork is determined by intra-group peer assessment.

In addition, students must submit a **comprehensive reflective report** at the end of the semester (2,000 words for undergraduates, 3,000 words for postgraduates), worth 25% to 30% of the total grade. The report must compare cases from at least two workshops and analyse systemic biases in judicial decision-making. An internal ANU College of Law evaluation report from 2023 found that 85% of participants believed the reflective report helped them apply theory to practice.

## Case Material Sources and Confidentiality Agreements

All cases used in the workshop come from the **ANU Criminology Database**, established in 2020 by ANU in partnership with the National Archives of Australia. As of 2024, the database holds more than 1,200 de-identified judicial case files covering cases from 1990 to 2023. Students must sign a confidentiality agreement prohibiting the sharing of case details outside the classroom; violations lead to an academic integrity investigation.

## Common Challenges and Coping Strategies

### Time Management Pressure

Workshop preparation requires an average of 8 to 10 hours per week, and the ANU academic calendar shows that students typically have 3 to 4 other courses running at the same time. A 2023 ANU Student Support Centre survey found that 42% of participants had applied for extensions due to time conflicts. Recommended strategies include using ANU's "weekly planning template" and booking appointments with academic skills advisers.

### Debate Anxiety and Language Barriers

Non-native English speakers often report stress during the debate segments. Since 2022, the ANU Language Centre has run a "Legal English and Debate" workshop offering 6 free tutoring sessions per semester. In addition, students can request one-on-one mock debates with their tutors, 30 minutes per session.

### Managing Team Conflict

Peer assessment within groups can lead to disagreements. Under the ANU Centre for Criminology's 2024 policy, if irreconcilable conflict arises within a group, students may apply to the course coordinator for regrouping, but must provide a written explanation. Data shows that about 8% of teams experience restructuring during the semester.

## FAQ

### Q1: Are workshop places open to all majors?

No. Places are limited to ANU Criminology students (including majors, minors and postgraduates). 2024 data shows that about 120 undergraduates and 40 postgraduates compete for 40 places each year, an acceptance rate of around 27%.

### Q2: Do international students need to submit additional visa documents?

No. As a course module, the workshop does not involve off-campus internships, so no additional visa is required. However, international students must ensure their student visa (Subclass 500) covers the entire semester and that their weekly study hours comply with the 20-hour work limit.

### Q3: How does the workshop grade affect the final degree classification?

The workshop grade counts directly towards the GPA. ANU 2023 degree classification rules show that a workshop grade of 75 (Distinction) or above can raise the overall GPA by about 0.3 to 0.5 points, which significantly helps applications for honours degrees or PhD programs.
