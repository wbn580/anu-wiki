Australian Department of Home Affairs data shows that around 194,000 Temporary Graduate visas (Subclass 485) were granted in the 2023-24 financial year, with Chinese applicants ranking among the top three nationalities for approvals [Australian Department of Home Affairs, 2024, Annual Migration Report]. For international graduates of the Australian National University (ANU), the 485 visa is a critical transitional pathway from study to long-term career development: it allows holders to work full-time in Australia for 2 to 4 years after graduation (depending on the level of qualification) while accumulating local work experience and points for a later skilled migration application. In July 2024, the Australian immigration department introduced several new policies for the 485 visa, including lowering the age cap from 50 to 35, shortening visa durations, and adding a new "skills in demand" stream. Based on ANU's official international student support documents and Australian migration regulations, this article systematically covers eligibility requirements, timelines, document checklists and frequently asked questions, providing a practical process reference for current ANU students and recent graduates.

## Eligibility and Qualification Requirements

One of the core eligibility requirements for the **485 visa** is that applicants must have completed a degree awarded by an Australian education institution within 6 months before lodging the application. As a member of the Group of Eight, ANU's bachelor's, master's and doctoral programs all satisfy the "Australian Study Requirement" — at least 2 academic years (92 weeks of registered study) with no fewer than 16 months of actual study in Australia.

**Age and language thresholds** were adjusted from 1 July 2024. Applicants must be under 36 years of age at the time of lodgement (holders of Hong Kong or British National (Overseas) passports still qualify under the 50-year upper limit). For English language, an overall IELTS score of 6.5 (no band below 5.5) or the equivalent PTE Academic score of 57 (no component below 43) is required. Language test results must be dated within 12 months before the visa application is lodged.

**Qualification level and visa duration**
- Bachelor's degree (including Honours): up to 2 years
- Master by Coursework: up to 2 years
- Master by Research and PhD: up to 3 years
- Holding a degree in a designated "skills in demand" field (such as nursing, engineering or IT): an additional 1-2 years [Australian immigration department, 2024, 485 visa legislative instrument LIN 24/048]

## Application Timeline and Key Milestones

The **optimal lodgement window** is within 6 months of the date the Completion Letter is issued. ANU usually generates the Completion Letter automatically 2-4 weeks after final results are released; it can be downloaded from ISIS (the student information system). Note: the graduation ceremony date is not the same as the completion date — the immigration department uses the "course completion date" stated on the Completion Letter.

**Health examinations and police checks** should be arranged in advance. The health examination must be booked through Bupa Medical Visa Services, the immigration department's designated provider, and is usually completed 1-2 weeks before lodgement. The AFP National Police Check should be applied for within 3 months before lodgement; processing takes 10-15 business days.

**Visa processing times**: according to Department of Home Affairs data from October 2024, 75% of 485 visa applications are finalised within 47 days and 90% within 5 months. Allow a buffer of at least 8 weeks to avoid delays from requests for additional documents or peak-season backlogs affecting your subsequent plans. For cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%9B%BD%E9%99%85%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E6%AF%95%E4%B8%9A%E5%90%8E-485-%E5%B7%A5%E4%BD%9C%E7%AD%BE%E8%AF%81%E7%94%B3%E8%AF%B7%E5%85%A8%E6%B5%81%E7%A8%8B) to settle foreign exchange, but the 485 visa itself does not involve any tuition payment.

## Document Checklist and Preparation Tips

**Core documents**
- Passport (scans of all pages containing visa labels and entry/exit stamps)
- ANU Completion Letter and official Academic Transcript (an electronic copy can be requested from ISIS)
- Proof of Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC): cover must extend from graduation until the visa is granted, or purchase bridging cover
- English language test results (IELTS/PTE/TOEFL iBT, valid within 12 months)
- Notarised birth certificate or notarised passport (for identity verification)

**Supporting documents**
- AFP police check (application code 33, for migration purposes)
- Health examination receipt (HAP ID)
- Passport-size photos (45mm x 35mm, white background, taken within the last 6 months)
- Proof of marriage/de facto relationship (if applicable; at least 12 months of relationship evidence required)

**Document format requirements**: all non-English documents must be accompanied by translations from a NAATI-accredited translator. Scans must be in colour, in PDF format, with each file no larger than 5MB. ANU's free document scanning service (on level 2 of Chifley Library) is recommended.

## Online Application System Procedures

**ImmiAccount registration and login**: all 485 visa applications must be lodged through the Department of Home Affairs' online system, ImmiAccount. First-time users need to create an account with an email address, password and security verification. Use a personal email rather than your university email to avoid losing access after graduation.

**Completing the application form (Form 485)**: the system guides you to select a visa stream — the Post-Study Work stream (for applicants who first obtained a student visa after 5 November 2011) or the Graduate Work stream (for courses on a specific occupation list). The Post-Study Work stream applies to the vast majority of ANU students.

**Uploading documents and paying the fee**: upload files one by one as prompted. As of October 2024, the visa application fee is $1,960 (excluding surcharges). Payment can be made by credit card (Visa/Mastercard), PayPal or BPAY. Once payment succeeds, the system generates a TRN (Transaction Reference Number), which should be kept safe.

**Biometrics collection**: after lodgement, the system sends a notice asking you to attend a designated location (such as Australia Post or an immigration office in Canberra) to provide fingerprints and a photo. The appointment must be booked within 14 days of receiving the notice.

## Rights and Obligations After the Visa Is Granted

**Work rights**: 485 visa holders can work full-time in Australia without industry or employer restrictions. During the visa's validity, holders can freely change employers, start their own business or hold multiple jobs. Note: you must apply for another visa (such as the 482 employer-sponsored visa or the 189 Skilled Independent visa) before this one expires to remain lawfully in Australia.

**Study restrictions**: the 485 visa allows holders to undertake short courses (such as vocational training or language programs), but enrolling in a full-time degree program (such as a second master's) requires a student visa. Under immigration rules, study time on a 485 visa does not count towards the "Australian Study Requirement" for a subsequent student visa.

**Tax and superannuation**: working on a 485 visa requires applying for a Tax File Number (TFN) and lodging tax returns on time. Employers must pay superannuation at 11.5% of wages (the Superannuation Guarantee rate for the 2024-25 financial year). On leaving Australia, you can apply for a Departing Australia Superannuation Payment, taxed at about 35%-45%.

**Health insurance**: 485 visa holders no longer have OSHC cover and must purchase private health insurance (Overseas Visitors Health Cover, OVHC) themselves. It is recommended to buy it within 30 days of the visa being granted to avoid breaching visa condition 8501.

## Common Refusal Reasons and How to Avoid Them

**Incomplete documents or formatting errors** are the most common reason for refusal, accounting for about 37% of all 485 visa refusals in the 2023-24 financial year [Australian Administrative Appeals Tribunal, 2024, Annual Report]. Common mistakes include: failing to provide the original Completion Letter, language test results older than 12 months, and unclear passport scans. It is recommended to use the free document pre-check service offered by the ANU International Student Centre.

**Errors in calculating course completion**: some ANU students in double degrees or with credit exemptions may not meet the 92-week registration requirement. The immigration department uses the CRICOS-registered course length, not the actual time spent studying. For example, a 2-year master's with 4 courses waived (equivalent to 0.5 years) may not satisfy the "2 academic years" requirement. Consult ANU Student Central before enrolling to confirm whether a course qualifies for the 485.

**Age limit exceeded**: under the new policy from July 2024, applicants over 36 (except Hong Kong/BNO passport holders) are no longer eligible for the Post-Study Work stream. If you are close to the age limit, lodge your application before your 36th birthday. The department uses the lodgement date, not the processing date.

## Pathways from the 485 Visa to Permanent Migration

**Skilled migration points**: work experience accumulated in Australia during the 485 visa (paid, at least 20 hours per week) can earn points for the 189, 190 and 491 visas. For example, 1 year of relevant Australian work experience adds 5 points, and 3 years adds 10 points. Common point combinations for ANU graduates include: age (30 points for ages 25-32) + qualification (15 points for a bachelor's, 15 for a master's) + Australian study (5 points) + NAATI community language accreditation (5 points).

**Employer sponsorship transition**: while holding the 485, if you find a qualifying employer (an Australian business with annual turnover of at least $2.5 million), you can apply for the 482 Temporary Skill Shortage visa and later transition to the 186 permanent residence visa. ANU's careers centre (ANU CareerHub) offers employer-matching services; 2023 data shows that about 22% of ANU international graduates obtained employer sponsorship during their 485 visa.

**Regional extension**: 485 holders who completed their degree in a designated regional area of Australia (Canberra is classified as a "regional centre city") can apply for an extra 1 year of visa (Subclass 485 Designated Area Stream). Graduates in the Canberra region can use this to extend their total visa duration to 3-4 years (bachelor's/master's) or 4-5 years (PhD).

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I lodge a 485 visa application from outside Australia?
No. The 485 visa requires applicants to be in Australia both when lodging the application and when it is granted. If you need to leave Australia after graduating, you must lodge the application before departing, or return to Australia and lodge it then. The COVID-19 concession that previously allowed offshore lodgement was terminated on 25 November 2023.

### Q2: Can I work while my 485 visa is being processed?
Yes. After lodging the 485, applicants automatically receive a Bridging Visa A, which allows full-time work until the 485 is granted or refused. Bridging Visa A automatically ceases when you leave Australia; to travel overseas you need a Bridging Visa B, which costs $175.

### Q3: Can I renew my 485 visa after it expires?
In general, each person can only hold one 485 visa as the primary applicant in their lifetime. Exceptions include: Hong Kong/BNO passport holders can apply for a second 485 visa (up to 2 years); completing a degree in a regional area qualifies for an additional 1 year; or an extension via the "skills in demand" stream. From July 2024, ordinary applicants no longer have the "second 485" option.
