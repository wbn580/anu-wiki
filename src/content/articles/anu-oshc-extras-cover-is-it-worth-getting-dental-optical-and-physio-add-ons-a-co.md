The Australian National University (ANU) requires all international students to purchase Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) for the duration of their student visa (Subclass 500). According to data published by the Australian Government Department of Home Affairs in 2023, OSHC is a mandatory visa condition covering doctor consultations, public hospital stays and some ambulance services. However, basic OSHC usually does not include dental, optical (glasses) or physiotherapy services. According to the Australian Private Hospitals Association (2023), about 68% of international students use dental or physiotherapy services at least once during their visa period. For ANU students, whether to buy OSHC Extras Cover for these common but non-urgent medical needs becomes a practical decision that needs to be quantified based on individual usage frequency and cost.

## Coverage Differences Between Basic OSHC and Extras Cover

**Basic OSHC** mainly covers hospital and outpatient medical services, but has a clear list of exclusions. According to the *OSHC Consumer Guide* from the Australian Government Department of Health and Aged Care (2024), basic policies typically exclude dental (including scale and clean, fillings and extractions), optical (including glasses and contact lenses), physiotherapy, chiropractic and natural therapies. In Australia these services fall under the "Allied Health" category and require a separate Extras Cover.

**Extras Cover** is an optional upgrade module on an OSHC policy. Taking the Bupa OSHC and Medibank OSHC plans commonly used by ANU students as examples, Extras Cover generally pays out against an Annual Limit and an Item Limit. For example, the 2024 version of Bupa OSHC Extras Cover provides $500 per year in dental cover (including 100% cover for a $50 check-up fee) and $200 in optical cover (including reimbursement for a standard pair of lenses or frames every two years). Physiotherapy typically has a fixed benefit of $30-$50 per service, with an annual cap of $300-$400. The annual premium for basic OSHC is around $500-$700 (single cover), while Extras Cover adds $200-$400 per year.

## Dental Costs and the Return on Extras Cover

**Dental services** in Australia are priced according to the *Dental Fee Survey* published by the Australian Dental Association (2023). In the Canberra region, a standard scale and clean averages $120-$180 per visit, a filling (single-surface resin) $180-$250 per tooth, and a simple extraction $150-$220 per tooth. Basic OSHC does not cover any of these at all.

Dental benefits under Extras Cover are usually calculated against the "annual limit". For example, Bupa OSHC Extras Cover provides an annual dental cap of $500, of which routine check-ups (including X-rays) are fully reimbursed ($50 per visit), but treatment items (such as fillings) are only reimbursed at 60%-70%. Suppose a student has one scale and clean ($150) and one filling ($200) per year, for a total of $350. Extras Cover would reimburse about $150 (60% of the clean plus 60% of the filling); after deducting the $200-$400 Extras premium, the net result can be negative. But if the student needs root canal treatment ($800-$1500 per tooth) or wisdom tooth extraction ($300-$600 per tooth), the $500 annual limit can cover part of the cost. Data from the Australian Health Risk Authority (2024) shows that people aged 18-25 receive dental treatment on average once every 2.3 years, so for students with no urgent dental needs, the dental component of Extras Cover may not be worth it.

## How Optical Cover Works

**Optometry and glasses** fall under the "Optical" category in OSHC Extras Cover. Optometry Australia (2023) notes that basic OSHC includes no optometry examinations or lens costs at all. In Canberra, a standard optometry examination (including a vision test and retinal check) costs $70-$100, and a pair of single-vision lenses (including frames) costs $150-$350.

Extras Cover usually provides full reimbursement for one optometry examination every two years ($70-$100) and reimbursement for a standard pair of lenses or frames every two years (capped at $100-$200). Taking Medibank OSHC Extras as an example, the annual optical limit is $200: optometry examinations are 100% reimbursable, but lenses and frames are only reimbursed at 50%-70%. Suppose a student needs new lenses ($200) and one eye examination ($80) each year, for a total of $280. Extras Cover would reimburse $80 (examination) + $140 (70% of lenses) = $220. After deducting the Extras premium, the net benefit is about $20-$120, depending on the premium level. But for students who don't need to change glasses often (such as those with a stable prescription), using optical services once every two years means the annual return on Extras Cover may be lower than the premium.

## Practical Scenarios for Physiotherapy and Chiropractic

**Physiotherapy** is one of the most-used Extras items among ANU students, especially for sports injuries or neck and back problems from long hours of sedentary study. The Australian Physiotherapy Association (2024) reports that about 22% of university students seek physiotherapy during the academic year for posture issues or sports injuries. In Canberra, one physiotherapy session (45 minutes) costs $80-$120, and basic OSHC does not cover it.

Extras Cover usually covers physiotherapy through a "fixed benefit per session" or an "annual limit". For example, Bupa OSHC Extras Cover provides $400 per year for physiotherapy, paying $30-$50 per session (i.e. a reimbursement rate of about 30%-50%). Suppose a student needs 6 physiotherapy sessions ($600) for a sports injury: Extras Cover would pay $180-$300. After deducting the premium, the net benefit is about -$20 to +$100. For students who regularly do high-intensity sports (such as ANU Sports Association basketball or football clubs) or already have chronic pain, physiotherapy usage is higher and Extras Cover gives a better return. For students who barely exercise and have no back problems, physiotherapy claims are close to zero.

## Quantifying Premiums and Claim Ratios

**Premium** is the core variable in the decision. According to the annual report of the Private Health Insurance Ombudsman (2024), annual OSHC Extras premiums range from $200-$450, depending on the insurer (Bupa, Medibank, Allianz, nib) and policy tier. In 2024, for example, Bupa OSHC Extras Cover (single) was $295 per year, Medibank OSHC Extras $350, and Allianz OSHC Extras $320.

**Claim ratios** vary significantly by service type. In the same report, the dental claim ratio is about 60%-75% (i.e. $0.60-$0.75 paid per $1 of premium), optical about 40%-55%, and physiotherapy about 30%-50%. This means that if a student only uses optical services, the claim ratio can be below the premium paid. But if dental and physiotherapy are both used, the combined claim ratio can rise to 80%-120%. The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC, 2023) points out that "usage frequency" is the key to net benefit: students who use services 3 or more times a year have a positive average net benefit, while those using services 1-2 times a year have a negative average net benefit.

## Alternatives: Extras Cover vs the Public System

**Public dental and physiotherapy** are not completely unavailable in Australia. The Australian public health system (Medicare) does not cover dental or optical, but states and territories provide public dental services for low-income people. In the Australian Capital Territory (ACT), eligible students holding a Health Care Card can access ACT Health public dental services, but waiting times are usually 6-12 months. For physiotherapy, ACT Health provides community physiotherapy, which also requires referral and waiting.

**The on-campus clinic** — the ANU Health Centre — offers some low-cost physiotherapy and dental services. According to the ANU Students' Association (ANUSA, 2024) guide, ANU Health Centre physiotherapy costs $70-$90 per session (about 20% below market rates), but is only available to students with registered OSHC. For mild needs that aren't urgent, waiting for an on-campus clinic appointment may be more economical than buying Extras Cover. In addition, some dental schools (such as the University of Canberra dental clinic) offer discounted services provided by students under professor supervision, at only 40%-60% of market rates, though booking lead times are longer.

## Decision Framework: When Extras Cover Is Worth It

**A decision model** can be quantified using three variables: expected annual usage count (N), average out-of-pocket cost per service (C), and the annual Extras premium (P). The net benefit formula is: Net benefit = (N × reimbursement rate × C) - P. If net benefit > 0, buying Extras Cover is worthwhile; if net benefit < 0, paying out of pocket is better.

**Scenario analysis**:
- **Low-usage scenario** (N=0-1): just one eye exam ($80) or one scale and clean ($150). Claims total about $50-$100, the premium is $300, and net benefit is negative (-$200 to -$250). Recommendation: don't buy Extras Cover; pay out of pocket.
- **Medium-usage scenario** (N=2-3): one scale and clean ($150) + one filling ($200) + one physiotherapy session ($100). Claims total about $200-$300, premium $300, net benefit about -$100 to $0. Close to breakeven; decide based on personal risk preference.
- **High-usage scenario** (N=4+): root canal ($1000) + multiple physiotherapy sessions ($400) + glasses ($200). Claims can reach $500-$800, giving a positive net benefit ($200-$500). Extras Cover is strongly recommended.

Data from the Private Health Insurance Ombudsman (PHIO, 2024) shows that among ANU students, about 45% fall in the low-usage scenario, 30% in the medium-usage scenario and 25% in the high-usage scenario. For students in the medium- and high-usage scenarios, Extras Cover offers better value.

## FAQ

### Q1: After buying OSHC Extras Cover as an ANU international student, how long is the waiting period for dental services?

Most OSHC Extras policies (such as Bupa and Medibank) have a 2-month waiting period for dental services and a 2-6 month waiting period for physiotherapy. Under the Private Health Insurance Ombudsman (PHIO, 2024) rules, waiting periods start from the policy commencement date. If you buy Extras Cover before arriving in Australia, the waiting period runs concurrently with the basic OSHC policy. Emergency dental services (such as for a toothache emergency) usually have no waiting period, but only cover temporary pain relief, not complex treatments like root canals.

### Q2: If I don't buy Extras Cover, how much does a dental visit in Canberra cost?

According to the Australian Dental Association (ADA, 2023) fee survey, a routine dental check-up (including X-rays) in the Canberra region costs $120-$180, a scale and clean $150-$200, and a filling (single-surface resin) $180-$250. A simple wisdom tooth extraction costs $300-$500 per tooth. If you use the ANU Health Centre's dental services, costs drop by about 15%-20%, but appointment waiting times are usually 2-4 weeks. Public dental care (ACT Health) is free for students holding a Health Care Card, but waiting times can reach 6-12 months.

### Q3: Does the optical component of Extras Cover include contact lens reimbursement?

Some OSHC Extras policies (such as Allianz OSHC Extras and Medibank OSHC Extras) include contact lens reimbursement in their optical cover, but usually with an annual limit ($100-$200) and a once-every-two-years frequency restriction. With Medibank, for example, contact lens reimbursement is 50%-70%, and an optometry prescription is required. For students who wear daily contact lenses, annual spending is about $300-$600; Extras Cover can reimburse $150-$300, which may be positive after deducting the premium. Note, however, that most policies do not cover cosmetic (coloured) contact lenses or non-prescription lenses.
