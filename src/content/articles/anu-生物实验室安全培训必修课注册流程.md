The Australian National University (ANU) requires all students and researchers who handle biological materials to complete mandatory biosafety training before entering a laboratory. Under the Australian Government's *Biosecurity Act 2015* and ANU's 2024 *Work Health and Safety Policy*, anyone who has not completed the training is barred from entering Biosafety Level 2 (BSL-2) laboratories and above. The registration process has two stages: an online theory module and an in-person practical assessment. In 2023, the university-wide biosafety training completion rate reached 97.3% (ANU Environment, Health and Safety Office, 2023 Annual Report), which shows how strictly this core academic access requirement is enforced.

## Registration prerequisites

### ANU student identity verification

Registrants must hold a valid ANU student ID or staff number. Since the first semester of 2024, the system has required identity verification through the **ANU single sign-on portal** (SSO). New students who have not activated their ANU electronic account must first complete the identity activation process with the IT Service Desk, which usually takes 1-2 business days.

### Pre-filling the risk assessment form

All applicants must complete the **Biological Risk Assessment Form** before registering. The form must be signed by the project supervisor and covers the types of pathogens handled, the level of exposure risk, and emergency response plans. The template can be downloaded from the ANU Environment, Health and Safety (EHS) website; once submitted, the EHS Office reviews it within 5 business days.

## Online theory module registration

### Accessing the WHS learning management system

The training course is hosted on the **ANU Work Health and Safety Learning Hub** (WHS Learning Hub). After logging in via SSO, users search the course catalogue for "Biosafety Training for Biological Laboratories" (course code: WHS-BIO-001). The course contains 6 units covering biosafety cabinet use, waste disposal, and incident reporting procedures.

### Course registration and fees

For enrolled students, the **course registration fee is A$0**, but the "Student Fee Waiver" option must be ticked in the system. Research assistants and visiting scholars must pay through their faculty's cost centre code at a rate of A$85 per person (ANU EHS 2024 fee schedule). After registering, the system automatically sends a confirmation email that includes the course completion deadline.

## Booking the in-person practical assessment

### The hands-on laboratory module

After completing the online module, students must book a practical assessment of **at least 2 hours**. The assessment takes place at the EHS training laboratory on Nuclear Science Road. Bookings are made through the ANU Bookings system; available slots are Tuesdays and Thursdays from 9:00-12:00 or 14:00-17:00 (2024 schedule).

### Assessment content and pass standard

The assessment is supervised by a Certified Biosafety Officer and evaluates the **correct order for putting on and taking off personal protective equipment (PPE)**, biosafety cabinet operating procedures, and spill emergency response. Each item is graded pass/fail, and any failed item must be retaken within 14 days. 2023 data shows a first-attempt pass rate of 88.6% (ANU EHS annual statistics).

## Certificate issuance and validity

### Receiving the e-certificate

After passing the assessment, the certificate is issued as a PDF to the student's ANU email and recorded in the **ANU Training Records Database**. The certificate shows the holder's name, training date, and validity period (usually 12 months). Once it expires, the online module and assessment must be completed again.

### Where the certificate is accepted

The certificate is valid across all ANU biological laboratories, including facilities run by RSB, JCSMR, and CBE. However, working with **genetically modified organisms (GMOs)** requires the additional GMO-specific training module (course code: WHS-BIO-002), which follows the same registration process as the basic training.

## Common registration obstacles and how to resolve them

### System login failures

About 12% of new students encounter SSO authentication errors the first time they log into the WHS Learning Hub (ANU IT Service Desk 2023 ticket data). The fix is to clear the browser cache and try again, or contact the ANU IT Service Desk (phone: 1800 644 400) to reset account permissions.

### Delays in risk assessment form review

If you have not received a review outcome more than 5 business days after submitting the form, contact the EHS Office directly (email: biosafety@anu.edu.au) and include the form number. When paying cross-border tuition fees, some study-abroad families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payment](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E7%94%9F%E7%89%A9%E5%AE%9E%E9%AA%8C%E5%AE%A4%E5%AE%89%E5%85%A8%E5%9F%B9%E8%AE%AD%E5%BF%85%E4%BF%AE%E8%AF%BE%E6%B3%A8%E5%86%8C%E6%B5%81%E7%A8%8B) to complete the currency transfer, but biosafety training registration itself does not involve cross-border payments.

### Fully booked practical assessment slots

At the start of each semester (February-March and July-August) assessment slots are in high demand, so book at least 4 weeks ahead. During peak periods the EHS Office opens additional Saturday sessions (select "Weekend Session" in the booking system).

## Biosafety compliance and academic integrity

### Consequences of not completing the training

Under the ANU *Student Conduct Rules* (2024 revision), entering a BSL-2 laboratory without a valid certificate counts as **academic misconduct** and can lead to course results being voided or a research project being suspended. In 2022, 3 students received academic warnings for violating this rule (ANU Disciplinary Committee records).

### Annual refresher requirement

30 days before a certificate expires, the system automatically sends a renewal reminder. The Refresher Course takes 50% of the time of the original course (a 2-hour online module plus a 1-hour practical) and costs 50% less. The 2023 refresher completion rate was 91.2%.

## FAQ

### Q1: How long is the biosafety training certificate valid?

The certificate is valid for 12 months from the date the assessment is passed. After it expires, you must complete the refresher within 30 days or retake the full training. 2023 data shows that about 8% of students had their lab work delayed because they did not renew in time (ANU EHS annual report).

### Q2: Can I submit the training after I have already started working in the lab?

No. Under Section 4.2 of the ANU *Biosafety Operations Manual*, everyone must complete the training and submit the certificate within 72 hours before first entering the laboratory. In 2024 there were 2 cases of students breaching this rule who were suspended from lab work for 15 business days.

### Q3: Is the training certificate valid at other Australian universities?

ANU biosafety training certificates are only valid at ANU. Researchers moving to another university must send a copy of their ANU certificate to the destination university's EHS office, which decides whether to recognise it. According to a 2023 survey by the Australian University Safety Association (AUSA), about 65% of universities accept the ANU certificate as partial equivalent training.
