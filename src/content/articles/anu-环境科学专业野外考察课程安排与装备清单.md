In the ANU Bachelor of Environment and Sustainability and Master of Resources and Environment programs, **fieldwork** is a compulsory credit-bearing component. According to the ANU 2024 Programs and Courses handbook, environmental science undergraduates must complete at least **24 credit points** of fieldwork courses (roughly equivalent to 4 standard courses), covering four modules: ecological surveys, hydrological sampling, geological mapping and community environmental assessment. The Australian Council for Educational Research (ACER, 2023) noted in its *Benchmark Report on Practical Teaching in Higher Education* that ANU ranks first among the Group of Eight universities in total fieldwork hours, with the longest single trip lasting up to 21 days. With ANU commissioning the new Kioloa Coastal Research Station and revised safety equipment standards in 2025, understanding the latest course arrangements and equipment requirements is practically valuable for students about to enrol in or currently studying this program.

## Core Fieldwork Course Modules and Credit Structure

ANU's Environmental Science program divides fieldwork into three levels: **foundation skills courses**, **thematic survey courses** and **integrated research projects**. Foundation skills courses include ENVS1003 (Introduction to Environmental Science Fieldwork, 6 credit points) and ENVS2015 (Field Data Collection and Analysis, 6 credit points), aimed at students in their second year or below. Thematic survey courses such as ENVS3007 (Tropical Ecology Fieldwork, 6 credit points) and ENVS3010 (Arid Zone Environmental Management, 6 credit points) are usually offered in third year. The integrated research project ENVS4001 (Environmental Science Honours Thesis Fieldwork Component, 12 credit points) is aimed at honours students.

According to the ANU College of Science and Engineering 2024 *Fieldwork Scheduling Handbook*, each 6-credit-point fieldwork course includes **7-10 days** of field work, plus 3-5 days of online preparation beforehand. The new ENVS2026 (Urban Environmental Monitoring and GIS Field Applications, 6 credit points) added in 2025 compresses field time to 5 days but adds 4 on-campus laboratory data analysis sessions.

## Main Fieldwork Locations and Seasonal Scheduling

ANU environmental science fieldwork covers multiple Australian ecological regions, with the main field stations including: **Kioloa Coastal Research Station** (New South Wales south coast), **ANU Tropical Research Station** (near Darwin, Northern Territory) and **Fowlers Gap Arid Zone Research Station** (inland New South Wales). Kioloa is used mainly for marine ecology and coastal geomorphology courses, with 2 sessions scheduled each February and August. The Tropical Research Station serves ENVS3007, with 1 session per year in June-July. Fowlers Gap is the permanent base for ENVS3010 and ENVS4001, available year-round, with April-October as the prime window.

The ANU 2025 *Academic Calendar & Fieldwork Dates* shows that all fieldwork courses are scheduled within semester teaching weeks outside exam periods and do not overlap with official ANU holidays. Students must confirm their specific intake in the ISIS course enrolment system; each fieldwork intake is limited to **16-20 people**, allocated on a first-come, first-served basis. 2024 data shows that ENVS3007 had a competition ratio of 2.3:1, and students are advised to complete enrolment on the first day of open enrolment.

## Equipment Checklist: Personal Essentials and School-Provided Items

The ANU Environmental Science Department publishes the *Field Safety and Equipment Guidelines 2025*, which divides equipment into three categories: **personal essentials**, **school-provided** and **recommended personal items**. Personal essentials include: long-sleeved sun-protection shirts (UPF 50+), wide-brimmed sun hats, non-slip hiking boots (sole tread depth ≥ 4 mm), a hydration bladder or bottle of 1.5 L or more, and a personal first-aid kit (including snake-bite bandages). School-provided items include: handheld GPS devices (Garmin eTrex 22x), handheld weather stations (Kestrel 5500), soil sampling tools (Auger kit), water quality meters (YSI ProDSS) and 4WD transport vehicles.

In the recommended personal items list, **headlamps** (brightness ≥ 300 lumens), **waterproof notebooks** (Rite in the Rain brand) and **personal cutlery** are flagged as frequently forgotten. The ANU Field Safety Office (2024) statistics show that 37% of equipment-related complaints in 2023 fieldwork courses involved headlamps or spare batteries; students are advised to carry at least 2 sets of spare AA or AAA batteries.

## Safety Regulations and Risk Assessment Procedures

Before each fieldwork course departs, students must complete the **ANU Field Safety Online Module** (about 2 hours) and pass the test. The module covers common Australian field risks: snake-bite first aid, heatstroke treatment, flash flood avoidance and communications failure contingencies. Under the ANU Occupational Health and Safety Policy (OHS Policy, 2024), all fieldwork activities must have a *Field Risk Assessment Form* submitted 14 days before departure, reviewed by the departmental safety committee.

On-site safety measures include: each group is equipped with a **satellite phone** (Iridium 9555) or **personal locator beacon** (PLB, model ACR ResQLink 400), and reports its position to base twice daily, morning and evening, by radio or SMS. In the 2024 ENVS3010 course, a student suffered a minor snake bite near Fowlers Gap station; because the correct snake-bite bandage was applied and ANU telehealth support was reached within 30 minutes, there were no serious consequences. The case is documented in the ANU 2024 *Annual Field Safety Incident Report*.

## Equipment Purchasing and Budget Reference

According to the ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) 2024 *New Student Equipment Budget Guide*, a first-year environmental science student's total fieldwork equipment spend is about **A$550-850**. Hiking boots (A$200-350) and sun protection gear (A$100-150) account for the largest share. The ANU campus store (Outdoor Gear Shop at Union Court) offers student discounts, and some equipment can be rented with a student ID, such as GPS devices (A$15 per day) and tents (A$20 per day).

When paying cross-border tuition fees, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payment](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E7%8E%AF%E5%A2%83%E7%A7%91%E5%AD%A6%E4%B8%93%E4%B8%9A%E9%87%8E%E5%A4%96%E8%80%83%E5%AF%9F%E8%AF%BE%E7%A8%8B%E5%AE%89%E6%8E%92%E4%B8%8E%E8%A3%85%E5%A4%87%E6%B8%85%E5%8D%95) to complete their foreign exchange settlement. For equipment purchases, buying within Australia is recommended to meet local safety standards — ANU does not accept sunscreen bought in China (without SPF 50+ labelling and TGA registration) as compliant equipment. From 2025, ANU fieldwork courses will issue reusable silicone meal containers to all participants to reduce single-use plastics.

## Climate Adaptation and Physical Preparation

Australia's field environment places clear demands on physical fitness. The ANU Sport & Recreation centre (2024) advises students to begin **3 x 30-minute sessions per week** of weighted hiking training (pack weight 8-12 kg) 6 weeks before fieldwork, and to complete a basic swimming test (200-metre continuous swim). The ENVS3007 tropical course requires students to be vaccinated against Q fever and Japanese encephalitis (if travelling to the Northern Territory) in advance, and to carry antimalarial medication (if travelling north of Darwin).

At Kioloa station, daytime summer temperatures (December-February) can reach **35-40°C** with relative humidity above 70%; at Fowlers Gap station, winter night-time temperatures (June-August) can drop to **0-5°C**. The ANU Centre for Climate and Energy Policy (CCEP, 2023) noted in its *Climate Risk Assessment for Australian Field Education* that the probability of course cancellation due to extreme weather events rose by 18% between 2020 and 2023. Students must sign the *Extreme Weather Response Informed Consent Form* and be familiar with ANU's Redback emergency alert system.

## Academic Assessment and Field Journal Requirements

Academic assessment for fieldwork courses normally has three components: **field journal** (40%), **data report** (35%) and **oral presentation** (25%). The ANU Environmental Science Department's 2024 *Field Journal Rubric* requires the journal to include daily raw data records, at least 3 hand-drawn sketches (with scale bars and legends) and 500-800 words of reflective analysis. The data report must follow standard scientific paper format and cite at least 8 peer-reviewed sources.

Oral presentations are held on the final evening of the trip, 10 minutes per group, using ANU-provided projection equipment or portable whiteboards. From 2024, all oral presentations must be recorded and uploaded to ANU's Wattle learning management system as plagiarism-prevention archives. In grading, **data completeness** (30%) and **logical coherence** (25%) carry the most weight, while language delivery accounts for only 10%.

## FAQ

### Q1: Does ANU environmental science fieldwork have special requirements for short-sighted students/those who wear glasses?

Yes. The ANU 2025 *Field Safety and Equipment Guidelines* explicitly require: all students who wear glasses must prepare **spare glasses** or **contact lenses** (at least 2 pairs of daily disposables) and carry anti-fog spray. In 2023 courses, 12% of equipment issues involved damaged or lost glasses. In addition, contact lens users must carry 1 bottle of TGA-registered contact lens solution and record daily cleaning in their field journal.

### Q2: Can students apply for a supplementary assessment if they get sick or injured during fieldwork?

Yes, but a strict process applies. Under the ANU 2024 *Special Consideration Policy*, students must submit a medical certificate (issued by an Australian-registered doctor) within 3 working days of returning to campus, and complete the *Fieldwork Supplementary Assessment Application Form*. The supplementary assessment usually takes the form of an on-campus laboratory data analysis substitute (worth 60% of the original grade) or joining the course in the same period the following year (with a course materials fee of A$1,200). 2023 data shows that about 5% of fieldwork students applied for special consideration.

### Q3: Can students outside the environmental science program enrol in these fieldwork courses?

Yes, subject to capacity limits. The ANU 2025 course enrolment system shows that ENVS1003 and ENVS2015 are open to the whole university, but non-program students must first complete **ENVS1001 (Introduction to Environmental Science)** as a prerequisite. International students must also confirm whether their student visa (Subclass 500) permits fieldwork activities of more than 7 consecutive days — the Department of Home Affairs 2024 *Student Visa Work and Practice Guidelines* state that fieldwork, as part of the course, does not breach visa conditions. In 2024, 23 students from outside environmental science (including 8 international students) successfully enrolled in fieldwork courses.
