ANU Official Transcript Translation and Notarization for International Students

The Australian National University (ANU) issues more than 8,000 academic transcripts to international students each year, and about 65% of recipients need their documents translated into Chinese and notarised, whether for job applications back home, qualification recognition or further study (Australian Department of Education, 2023, *International Student Post-Graduation Outcomes Report*). According to 2023 data from the Chinese Service Center for Scholarly Exchange (CSCSE), about 12% of qualification-recognition applications are rejected because the translated documents don't meet the requirements. As mutual recognition of qualifications between China and Australia keeps tightening, official translation and notarisation of transcripts has become one of the most critical administrative steps for ANU international students around graduation. Drawing on ANU's official policies and the public guidance of Australia's Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) and the CSCSE, this article lays out the complete path from transcript translation to notarisation — covering on-campus processing, NAATI-certified translation, the levels of notarisation and authentication by Chinese consulates in Australia.

## Getting Your Transcript: Channels and Formats

ANU international students can obtain an official transcript through two official channels: the **Academic Transcript** and the **Statement of Results**. The two differ in legal effect and intended use.

### Official Digital Transcript (My eQuals)

Since 2019, ANU has worked with the My eQuals platform, letting graduates generate a **digital transcript** that meets the standards of the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA). The platform supports PDF downloads, and the file embeds a digital signature and a tamper-evident verification code. In its 2022-updated explanatory notes on application materials for overseas qualification recognition, the CSCSE states that electronic transcripts exported directly from My eQuals are accepted as recognition materials, provided the file keeps its original digital signature intact.

### Paper Transcript Application Process

Students can request a paper transcript through the ANU ISIS system; processing usually takes 5 to 10 business days. Paper transcripts are issued by ANU Student Administration and carry the University's embossed seal and the Registrar's signature. Note that a **paper transcript** is treated as a copy once the envelope has been opened, and some notaries require the original to remain sealed. ANU's official fee is AUD 35 per paper transcript (2024 rate); electronic transcripts are free.

## Translation Requirements and NAATI Certification

The CSCSE requires foreign-language documents in overseas qualification recognition materials to be translated by a **qualified translation agency or translator**. In Australia, the most widely recognised credential is NAATI (National Accreditation Authority for Translators and Interpreters) certification.

### NAATI-Certified Translators

NAATI is Australia's only officially recognised certification body for translators and interpreters. A NAATI-certified translator (Certified Translator) may sign and stamp translations, making them legally equivalent to an **official translation**. When arranging a transcript translation, ANU international students should confirm that the translator's NAATI credential number can be publicly verified on the NAATI website (naati.com.au). According to NAATI's 2023 annual report, 1,247 translators in Australia hold certification in both Chinese and English, with about 30% based in the Canberra region.

### What a Compliant Translation Must Include

A compliant NAATI translation must include: the translator's signature, the NAATI credential number, the certification validity period, the date of translation, and the English statement "This translation is an accurate translation of the original document". The translation must be bound or affixed to the original, with the translator's seal stamped across each page. A translation missing any of these elements may be judged an **invalid translation** during CSCSE review, interrupting the recognition process.

## Levels of Notarisation and How to Arrange It

Depending on the purpose of the document, transcript notarisation can be done at three levels: **Notary Public**, **DFAT Apostille** and **Consular Legalization** by Chinese consulates in Australia. China officially joined the Convention Abolishing the Requirement of Legalisation for Foreign Public Documents (the Hague Apostille Convention) in March 2023, and since 7 November 2023 the Apostille process has applied between China and Australia.

### Notary Public

This level suits documents used within Australia, such as applications to domestic institutions or employers. Students can take the original transcript and the NAATI translation to a Notary Public in the Canberra region. ANU's on-campus legal service (ANU Legal Workshop) can notarise documents for currently enrolled students at around AUD 60 to 90 per document (2024 on-campus price list). The notary verifies that the original and the translation match and stamps the document.

### Hague Apostille

If the transcript will be used in China, since November 2023 students can submit the NAATI translation and the original directly to Australia's Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) or an authorised agency for an **Apostille**. The DFAT Canberra office is on John McEwen Crescent; processing takes 5 to 10 business days and costs AUD 85 per document (2024 rate). Once the Apostille is issued, the document can go straight to the CSCSE without further authentication by Chinese consulates in Australia. For cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%9B%BD%E9%99%85%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E6%88%90%E7%BB%A9%E5%8D%95%E5%AE%98%E6%96%B9%E7%BF%BB%E8%AF%91%E4%B8%8E%E5%85%AC%E8%AF%81%E5%8A%9E%E7%90%86) to settle their foreign exchange.

### Chinese Consulate Authentication (Now Simplified)

Before November 2023, China was not a party to the Hague Convention, so transcripts first had to be authenticated by DFAT and then receive consular legalisation from a Chinese consulate in Australia. Since the Convention took effect, this step has been removed. However, if the document is needed for judicial proceedings or a specific government program in China, some bodies may still ask for additional **consular legalisation**; students should confirm the exact requirements with the recipient in advance.

## Timeline and Cost Estimates

The full translation and notarisation process usually takes 3 to 6 weeks, depending on the route chosen and how quickly each agency works.

### On-Campus Timeline

Start about 8 weeks before graduation. ANU Student Administration needs 5 to 10 business days to process a transcript request; NAATI translators typically deliver in 3 to 5 business days after taking on the job; notary appointments take 1 to 2 weeks. If you choose the DFAT Apostille, allow an extra 5 to 10 business days. The **shortest total time** is therefore about 4 weeks, stretching to 8 weeks at peak periods (the June-July and November-December graduation seasons).

### Cost Summary

Based on 2024 published rates, a typical transcript — from ordering to notarisation — costs roughly: AUD 35 for a paper transcript, AUD 40 to 80 for a NAATI translation (charged per page), AUD 60 to 90 for the notary, and AUD 85 for the DFAT Apostille. That comes to about AUD 220 to 290 in total. Choosing an electronic transcript and printing it yourself saves the AUD 35 paper fee. Some NAATI translators offer online services, which can shorten the timeline further.

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

According to the CSCSE's 2023 FAQ on recognition applications, about 40% of rejected transcript translation and notarisation cases stem from non-compliant translations and 30% from choosing the wrong level of notarisation.

### Common Translation Mistakes

The most frequent errors include: the translator failing to sign in the pinyin of their Chinese name, an expired or unverifiable NAATI number, and a translation not bound to the original. On receiving the translation, students should immediately verify the translator's credential status on the NAATI website. **NAATI certification** is valid for 3 years, so confirm the credential was current on the day of translation. Another common issue is omitting the Chinese names of courses — for example translating "COMP1100" only as "Computer Science" without keeping the original course code, which stops the recognition officer from matching the transcript content.

### Choosing the Wrong Level of Notarisation

Some students wrongly assume that ordinary notarisation is enough for qualification recognition in China. In fact, since November 2023 the CSCSE only accepts transcripts with an **Apostille** or consular legalisation. Ordinary notarised documents have no legal effect in China. Confirm the exact acceptance criteria with the recognition body before submitting, to avoid doing the process twice.

## Alternative: ANU On-Campus Translation Services

The ANU Language Centre offers limited translation services, mainly for internal administrative documents; it does not specialise in transcript translation. ANU International Student Services can also help students access a recommended list of NAATI translators in the Canberra region, updated each semester and covering 15 to 20 certified translators. The list is available on the ANU website under "International Student Support" in the "Current Students" section. Note that ANU does not offer Chinese translation of transcripts — students must commission an external NAATI translator themselves.

## FAQ

### Q1: How long is a NAATI translation of an ANU transcript valid?

A NAATI translation itself has no fixed expiry date; its legal effect depends on the translator holding **valid certification** on the day of translation. However, the CSCSE generally requires the translation to have been issued within 6 months of the recognition application. If the translation is more than 1 year old, it is best to have it redone. According to 2023 recognition data, about 8% of rejected cases were due to translations issued more than 12 months earlier.

### Q2: Can an electronic transcript be used directly for qualification recognition in China?

Yes, provided two conditions are met: the transcript must be exported directly from the My eQuals platform, and the file must remain **PDF with a digital signature**. Screenshots or scanned printouts are not accepted. The CSCSE's 2022-updated materials note that an electronic transcript must show the institution name, student name, qualification details and award date.

### Q3: After getting an Apostille, do I still need to visit a Chinese consulate in Australia?

Since the Convention Abolishing the Requirement of Legalisation for Foreign Public Documents took effect between China and Australia on 7 November 2023, the Apostille has replaced consular legalisation. Students no longer need extra authentication from Chinese consulates in Australia. However, if the document is needed for court proceedings or a specific government tender in China, some bodies may still require **consular legalisation** — check with the recipient in advance.
