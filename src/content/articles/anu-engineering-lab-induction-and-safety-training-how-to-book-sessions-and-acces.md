At the Australian National University (ANU) College of Engineering and Computer Science, all students must complete mandatory laboratory safety training before using teaching and research equipment. According to the ANU Work Health and Safety Policy (WHS Policy, 2024 revision), around 2,800 undergraduate and postgraduate students must complete safety training each year to gain laboratory access. The process covers online modules, practical on-site assessments, and equipment-specific authorisations, and is a required first step for engineering students undertaking course projects and final-year design work. With the comprehensive upgrade of the lab booking system in Semester 1 2025, learning how to complete safety training efficiently and book equipment has become a top priority for new students.

## Legal Framework and University Requirements for Safety Training

ANU's engineering lab safety training system is built on the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (ACT) and ANU's internal policy WHS-PRO-008 (Laboratory Safety Management Procedure, published 2023). The framework requires all lab users — undergraduates, postgraduates, researchers, and visiting scholars — to complete three levels of safety certification before entering any engineering laboratory.

Level 1 is **general laboratory safety**, an online course delivered by the ANU Environment and Safety Office (ESO) through the Moodle platform, covering chemical hazard labelling, electrical safety, and emergency evacuation procedures. The module has 12 chapters and takes about 3 to 4 hours to complete; the pass rate in Q1 2024 was 91.7% (ANU ESO Annual Report, 2024). Level 2 is **laboratory-specific induction training**, run by each lab manager, covering the equipment operation risks and standard operating procedures (SOPs) unique to that lab. Level 3 is **equipment authorisation** for high-risk instruments such as lasers, high-voltage equipment, and CNC machines, requiring students to pass a one-on-one practical assessment.

## Registering for and Completing the Online Training Module

Students log in to the ANU WHS system (whs.anu.edu.au) with their student ID and find the "Laboratory Safety Induction" course under the "Training" section. Registration opens at the start of each semester; Semester 1 2024 data shows 2,347 students registered within the first two weeks of the semester (ANU WHS system data, 2024).

After completing the online module, the system automatically generates a **Certificate of Completion**, valid for 12 months. Once it expires, the online component must be redone. Students should submit the certificate to the relevant lab manager's email at least 48 hours before entering the lab so that the on-site assessment can be scheduled. Under the ANU laboratory access policy (2024), lab staff may refuse entry to students who arrive without submitting the certificate.

## Practical On-Site Assessment and Lab Authorisation

The online certificate only proves that the student passed the theoretical knowledge test; actually operating equipment in the lab also requires passing the **practical induction**. The assessment is conducted by the lab manager or an authorised technician, usually takes 30 to 60 minutes, and covers personal protective equipment (PPE) checks, emergency stop button locations, and chemical spill response demonstrations.

Taking the mechanical engineering lab in the ANU Engineering Building (Building 32) as an example, 412 students completed the on-site assessment in Semester 2 2024, of whom 408 passed on the first attempt, a pass rate of 99.0% (ANU Engineering Laboratory Annual Statistics, 2024). Students who fail can retake the assessment after 7 days, with no limit on attempts, though each retake must be rescheduled. After passing, students receive **access card authorisation** for the lab, usually synchronised to the ANU access control system within 24 hours.

## Equipment Booking System and Usage Permissions

Once authorised, students can book specific equipment through the ANU Equipment Booking System (EBS). The system was developed jointly by ANU Information Technology Services (ITS) and the College of Engineering in 2023, replacing the old paper sign-up sheets. EBS covers more than 200 instruments, including 3D printers, laser cutters, universal testing machines, oscilloscopes, and spectrum analysers.

Booking rules: standard equipment can be booked for up to 4 hours per session, with a booking window of 14 days in advance; high-demand equipment (such as the Stratasys F370 3D printer) is limited to 2 hours per session, and no more than 3 bookings per student per week. 2024 data shows the average wait for a 3D printer booking was 2.3 days (ANU EBS Usage Report, 2024). Students must arrive at the lab 10 minutes before their booking starts; arriving more than 15 minutes late results in automatic cancellation and a mark on the credit record — 3 no-shows in total freeze the EBS account for 30 days.

## Certification for Specialised Equipment

For **high-risk equipment** such as laser cutters, high-pressure reactors, and X-ray diffractometers, students must complete additional equipment-specific training. This training is delivered one-on-one by the equipment's supervising engineer and typically comprises theory (2 hours), observation and demonstration (1 hour), and a practical assessment (1 hour). After passing, students receive independent operating authorisation for that equipment, valid for 6 months.

Taking the CO₂ laser cutter (Universal Laser Systems VLS6.60) in the ANU College of Engineering as an example, 187 students applied for certification in 2024, of whom 173 passed (a pass rate of 92.5%). Those who fail must rebook the training; each session costs 50 AUD (paid from internal college accounts, not by students). Certified students see an "Authorised" tag for the equipment in EBS and can book it directly.

## Safety Breach Records and Consequences

ANU engineering labs maintain a strict **Safety Breach Register**. Any violation of safety rules — including failing to wear PPE, operating equipment without authorisation, or eating or drinking in the lab — is recorded. In 2024, ANU engineering labs recorded 87 safety breaches in total (ANU WHS Incident Report, 2024), the most common being failure to wear safety goggles (34 incidents) and leaving without powering down equipment (22 incidents).

Violations are handled in three tiers: Tier 1 violations (such as not wearing PPE) draw a written warning, and 3 accumulated warnings suspend lab access for 2 weeks; Tier 2 violations (such as operating equipment without authorisation) suspend access for 4 weeks immediately; Tier 3 violations (such as causing injury or equipment damage) are referred to the college discipline committee and can result in a permanent lab ban. Students who receive a suspension notice may submit a written appeal to the lab manager, with an appeal processing time of 5 working days.

## International Students and Language Support

ANU engineering lab training is conducted entirely in English, but the ESO provides **multilingual safety materials** for international students, including operation manuals and videos in Simplified Chinese, Korean, and Vietnamese. In 2024, international students made up 47.3% of the College of Engineering (ANU International Student Data, 2024), with Chinese students accounting for 34.1% of the international student total.

For students whose first language is not English, lab managers allow translation software to assist communication during practical assessments, but the assessment itself is still conducted to English standards. ANU Student Central also offers free academic English support courses; the "Lab Safety English" module runs 4 times per semester at 8 hours per session, with 236 students enrolled in 2024.

## FAQ

### Q1: If my safety training certificate expires, do I have to redo the entire training?

Yes. ANU lab safety certificates are valid for 12 months. After expiry, students must redo the online module (about 3-4 hours) and obtain a new certificate. However, if the practical on-site assessment was completed within the validity period, it is exempt — only the online component needs to be renewed. 2024 data shows about 15% of students were refused lab entry at the start of semester because of expired certificates.

### Q2: What should I do if I can't make it to a booked equipment session?

Students must cancel through EBS at least 2 hours before the booking start time. Cancellations leave no record. Cancelling within 2 hours, or simply not showing up, counts as 1 no-show. After 3 no-shows, the EBS account is frozen for 30 days. In 2024, ANU engineering labs recorded 1,247 no-shows, 4.3% of all bookings.

### Q3: Do international students need to submit extra language test scores to attend safety training?

No. ANU does not require international students to submit IELTS or TOEFL scores before safety training. Training is conducted in English, but the labs provide multilingual support materials. If a student fails the assessment due to language issues, they can request a retake with translation software; this option was used 89 times in 2024.
