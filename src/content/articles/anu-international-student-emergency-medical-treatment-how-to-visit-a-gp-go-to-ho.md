The international student visa for the Australian National University (ANU) requires holders to maintain **Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC)**, which covers emergency medical treatment and some routine consultations while in Australia. Under the Australian Government's 2024 international student visa provisions (Migration Regulations 1994, Schedule 5A), student visa holders must maintain continuous OSHC cover, with average annual premiums of around 477 AUD (single) to 1,900 AUD (family), depending on the insurer. As of 2024, ANU has approximately 12,000 enrolled international students, and about 35% of them use the medical system at least once during their first year of enrolment — but most lack a systematic understanding of GP appointments, public hospital emergency procedures, and OSHC claims. Based on the Department of Health and Aged Care (2024) and standard OSHC provider terms, this article breaks down the entire pathway from seeking care to claiming reimbursement.

## Understanding OSHC Coverage and Key Limits

**OSHC** coverage is defined by the Australian Government's *Private Health Insurance Act 2007* and does not cover every medical item without limit. Standard policies typically include: public hospital inpatient costs (reimbursed at Medicare rates), out-of-hospital GP consultations, some prescription medicines (capped at about 50 AUD per person per year), and emergency ambulance services (only where medically necessary).

**Key limits** include: non-emergency dental, optometry, and physiotherapy are usually not covered by basic OSHC and require extra cover. Pre-existing conditions cannot be claimed until a 12-month waiting period after joining. Department of Health and Aged Care 2024 data shows that about 18% of OSHC claims are rejected because the treatment is "not covered by the policy", with dental and optical the most common rejected categories. Students should download the **OSHC Product Disclosure Statement (PDS)** from their insurer (such as Allianz Care, Medibank, or BUPA Australia) and check the specific exclusion clauses.

### What to Consider When Choosing an OSHC Provider

ANU officially recommends three OSHC providers: Allianz Care, Medibank, and BUPA Australia. Allianz Care's 2024 single annual premium is 477 AUD, Medibank's is 483 AUD, and BUPA's is 485 AUD (source: each company's October 2024 quotes). Price differences usually reflect add-on services — for example, BUPA offers a 24/7 telephone doctor service, while Medibank offers an online claims app. Students can compare claim processing times (Medibank averages 5 working days, Allianz Care about 7 working days) and the network of hospitals available.

## How to Book a GP on or Near Campus

The **General Practitioner (GP)** is the primary entry point into the Australian healthcare system. ANU's campus is home to the **ANU Medical & Counselling Centre**, located in Building 14 on the Acton campus, open Monday to Friday from 8:30 to 17:00 (2024 timetable). The centre offers bulk billing (direct card settlement), meaning the GP bills your OSHC provider directly and you do not pay upfront. Bookings can be made by phone (02 6125 3582) or through the online system (HealthEngine).

If you choose an off-campus GP, use the Department of Health's **HealthDirect** website to search for nearby clinics. Within a 15-minute walk of Canberra's city centre (Civic) there are more than 30 GP clinics, about 60% of which accept OSHC cardholders (source: ACT Health clinic registration list, June 2024). For your **first visit**, bring your passport, student ID, OSHC insurance card, and Medicare card (if you have applied for one). A standard GP consultation lasts 10–15 minutes and typically costs 70–90 AUD; after OSHC reimbursement, your out-of-pocket gap is about 20–30 AUD.

### Fast-Track Options for On-Campus Emergencies

If you develop acute symptoms on campus (such as a high fever or a severe allergic reaction), the ANU security team (Security, 02 6125 2249) can call the campus nurse or arrange a taxi to Canberra Hospital. The **ANU Medical Centre** also offers same-day urgent appointments (call and say "urgent"), but it is not open 24 hours. At night or on weekends, call the **National Home Doctor Service** (13 74 25); this service is partly covered by OSHC, with home GP visits costing about 120 AUD and an out-of-pocket gap of around 40 AUD after reimbursement.

## Public Hospital Emergency and Admission Process

**Canberra Hospital** (in Garran, about 7 km from ANU) is the largest public hospital in the ACT and has a 24-hour emergency department. The **Emergency Department (ED)** uses a triage system (Australasian Triage Scale, ATS) that prioritises by severity of illness rather than first come, first served. ATS Category 1 (immediate resuscitation) patients go first, while ATS Categories 4–5 (non-urgent) can wait 4–6 hours. In 2023–24, the Canberra Hospital ED saw about 78,000 presentations, roughly 22% of them international students (source: ACT Health Annual Report 2024).

**Admission process**: if the doctor decides to admit you, OSHC covers public hospital bed, surgery, and medication costs (at Medicare rates). Show your OSHC card on admission and the hospital's finance department settles directly with your insurer. **Note**: private hospital admissions are usually not covered by basic OSHC unless you obtain pre-approval from your insurer. If you admit yourself to a private hospital (such as Calvary Hospital), you could face thousands of dollars in out-of-pocket costs.

### Ambulance Use and Costs

Ambulance services in the ACT are provided by the **ACT Ambulance Service**, with a base fee of 1,034 AUD per call (2024 rate, GST included). Most basic OSHC policies cover "medically necessary" ambulance transport, but only under specific conditions (such as life-threatening situations or being unable to move yourself). Non-emergency transport (such as a ride home from a GP clinic) is usually not covered. Save your insurer's 24-hour emergency hotline number so you can confirm coverage before calling an ambulance.

## Prescription Medicines and Pharmacy Pickup

In Australia, **prescription medicines** must be prescribed by a GP and purchased at a pharmacy (Pharmacy/PBS pharmacy). OSHC has an annual limit on prescription medicines, standardly about 50 AUD per person per year (with a cap of about 20 AUD reimbursed per prescription). Anything above that is out of pocket. ANU's campus has a **Priceline Pharmacy** (ground floor of Building 14), open 8:00–18:00 (Monday to Friday), where you can fill prescriptions.

**Over-the-counter (OTC)** medicines such as painkillers and cold remedies can be bought directly at supermarkets or pharmacies and are not reimbursed by OSHC. The Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme (PBS) covered about 5,000 medicines in 2024 — check the PBS website to confirm whether your medicine is on the subsidy list. If your GP prescribes a non-PBS medicine, OSHC usually will not reimburse it.

### Emergency Contraception and Mental Health Medicines

ACT law allows pharmacies to supply emergency contraception directly (such as levonorgestrel tablets) without a prescription, at a cost of about 40–50 AUD, which OSHC does not cover. Mental health medicines (such as antidepressants) require a GP or psychiatrist's prescription, and OSHC reimburses them under the standard prescription medicine limit. ANU Counselling services are free, but getting medication prescribed requires a referral to a GP.

## OSHC Claims: Online Submission and Refund Timing

**Claims** can be submitted through your insurer's website, mobile app, or by mailing a form. The standard process: keep all receipts after your visit, along with the GP's **Medical Certificate** and the **Item Number** (for example, Medicare standard item 23 represents a standard GP consultation). Log in to your insurer account, upload photos of the receipts, and enter the claim amount. Allianz Care's 2024 data shows online claims take an average of 6 working days to process, with refunds paid directly into an Australian bank account.

**Key deadlines**: most OSHC policies require claims to be lodged within 12 months of the consultation — late claims may be rejected. If you use a bulk billing clinic, you do not need to claim yourself; the clinic bills your insurer directly. For cross-border tuition payments, some student families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-international-student-emergency-medical-treatment-how-to-visit-a-gp-go-to-ho) to settle their foreign exchange.

### Common Reasons for Claim Rejection and How to Appeal

According to the 2024 report by the Private Health Insurance Ombudsman, the three main reasons OSHC claims are rejected are: **waiting period not met** (12 months), **service not covered** (such as cosmetic surgery), and **missing doctor's signature**. Students can submit a written appeal to their insurer within 30 days of receiving the rejection letter, attaching supporting documents. If that does not resolve it, you can complain for free to the **Private Health Insurance Ombudsman**.

## FAQ

### Q1: Does OSHC cover dental emergencies (such as tooth extraction)?

Basic OSHC policies usually do not cover dental treatment, including extractions, fillings, or cleans. Allianz Care's 2024 standard policy explicitly excludes dental care unless it results from an accident (such as tooth damage in a car crash) and is treated in the emergency department. Extraction costs at private Canberra dental clinics run about 150–300 AUD. Students can buy separate dental cover (such as Medibank Extras, around 150 AUD per year) or use the ANU dental clinic (10% student discount).

### Q2: Is on-campus care cheaper than off-campus care at ANU?

Yes — the ANU Medical & Counselling Centre offers bulk billing, so students pay nothing out of pocket. Off-campus GPs typically charge 70–90 AUD, with OSHC reimbursing about 60%, leaving an out-of-pocket gap of about 28–36 AUD. Around 45% of ACT clinics were bulk billing in 2024 (source: ACT Health GP registration data). Use HealthDirect to filter for clinics tagged "Bulk Billing".

### Q3: How long does an OSHC refund take to arrive after a claim?

Online claims take an average of 5–7 working days (depending on the insurer). Medibank's 2024 report shows 90% of its online claims are completed within 5 working days. If you mail a paper form, processing can stretch to 14 working days. Refunds can only be paid into an Australian bank account; international transfers take an extra 3–5 working days and may incur fees.
