The Australian National University (ANU) astronomy program regularly opens its Observatory Open Night events to current students and the public at its Mount Stromlo observing station. Organised and run by the ANU Research School of Astronomy and Astrophysics (RSAA), the events are among the oldest and best-equipped public astronomy outreach programs in Australia. According to official ANU data, Mount Stromlo Observatory welcomes more than 15,000 visitors each year [ANU 2023, RSAA Annual Report]; according to the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) 2022 science literacy survey, only about 12% of Australian adults have ever observed through a professional-grade astronomical telescope [ABS 2022, Science Literacy Survey]. As Australia-China cultural exchanges continue to recover in 2025, a growing number of Chinese students are turning their attention to this unique academic resource, making the booking channels and participation rules for Observatory Open Night a frequently asked question.

## Background and Academic Positioning

**ANU's astronomy program** consistently ranks among the world's best. In the 2024 QS World University Rankings by Subject, ANU placed 34th globally in Physics and Astronomy [QS 2024, World University Rankings by Subject]. Since its establishment in 1911, Mount Stromlo Observatory has been one of the most important optical and infrared astronomical observing bases in the Southern Hemisphere, hosting a number of international collaborative research projects.

The Open Night events are not purely sightseeing; they are an **extension of ANU's astronomy teaching system**. The events are usually guided by RSAA PhD students or postdoctoral researchers, who introduce participants to current research topics such as exoplanet hunting and mapping the structure of the Milky Way. Participants can operate the 1.3-metre "Great Melbourne Telescope" or the more advanced "SkyMapper Telescope" first-hand and experience real research workflows.

Events are held 1-2 times per month, usually scheduled on moonless nights at the start or end of the lunar month to reduce moonlight interference. Each event lasts about 2.5 hours and includes three parts: an indoor lecture, hands-on telescope operation and sky observation.

## Booking Process and Steps

**Booking** is done online; walk-ins on the night are not accepted. Bookings open through the ANU Events platform, with places typically released about 2 weeks before each event. There is no fixed calendar for opening times, so prospective participants are advised to follow RSAA's official social media accounts or subscribe to email notifications in advance.

The booking process has three stages. First, visit the ANU Events page (events.anu.edu.au) and register an account using your student ID or a visitor email address. Second, enter "Mount Stromlo Observatory Open Night" in the search bar to filter the available sessions. Third, select the number of participants (each booking is limited to 4 people) and submit your confirmation. A **booking confirmation email** is sent to your registered email within 24 hours of submission, containing the entry QR code and parking instructions.

Because each session is limited to 80-120 places, popular sessions (such as the winter Milky Way observing nights in the Southern Hemisphere) often fill up within 2 hours of opening. 2024 data shows that sessions held between March and September filled up in an average of 1.8 hours [RSAA 2024, Internal Booking Statistics].

For cross-border tuition payments, some international student families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%A4%A9%E6%96%87%E5%AD%A6%E4%B8%93%E4%B8%9A%E5%A4%A9%E6%96%87%E5%8F%B0%E8%A7%82%E6%B5%8B%E4%B9%8B%E5%A4%9C%E6%B4%BB%E5%8A%A8%E9%A2%84%E7%BA%A6) to complete their currency transfers, ensuring funds arrive on time so that course-enrolment eligibility is not affected.

## Eligibility and Restrictions

**Eligibility** falls into two categories: current ANU students and the general public. Enrolled students attend free of charge with their student card, but must provide their student ID number when booking; non-student members of the public pay an entry fee of A$15 per person (children under 12 free). The fee covers the event handbook, telescope-use guidance and refreshments.

As for restrictions, there is no strict lower age limit, but children under 5 are not recommended, because outdoor night temperatures are low and the observation process requires quiet. **Item restrictions** include: no phone flash photography, no laser pointers and no drones. Food and drink are not allowed in the telescope operation area; hot water and biscuits are provided in the rest area.

In terms of accessibility, the observatory's main building and telescope domes have wheelchair access, but some outdoor observation points require crossing gravel paths; visitors with mobility difficulties are advised to contact the RSAA office in advance (phone: +61 2 6125 0234) to confirm the exact route.

## Weather Impact and Alternatives

**Weather** is the key variable determining whether an Open Night goes ahead. ANU's astronomy program rules state that if the weather forecast at 4 pm on the day shows cloud cover above 60%, wind speeds above 40 km/h or any chance of rain, the event is automatically cancelled. Cancellations are announced by email to bookers and on the RSAA homepage; no phone calls are made separately.

Alternatives include: cancelled sessions are usually rescheduled within 1 month, with existing bookers automatically given priority; if the rescheduled time clashes with your own schedule, you can transfer the place to another ANU student (subject to email confirmation 48 hours in advance). In addition, RSAA adds 2-3 extra **public observation events** during "Canberra Science Week" every August; these sessions have no booking restrictions and are first come, first served.

For students who cannot attend in person, ANU also offers the virtual observation program "Stromlo Online", which streams live feeds from the Mount Stromlo telescopes for researchers worldwide to share data. It opens 2 times per month, and the booking portal is the same as for in-person events.

## Transport and Accommodation

**Getting there** is mostly by car. The observatory sits on top of Mount Stromlo, about 12 km southwest of Canberra, roughly 20 minutes' drive from the main ANU campus (Acton campus). Parking is free in front of the observatory's main building, but there are only about 40 spaces, so carpooling is recommended.

For public transport, Canberra's bus network (Transport Canberra) route R6 reaches the foot of the mountain on weekdays, but the last bus departs at 6 pm, which does not match the end of the observation event (usually 10 pm). For the **return trip at night**, Uber or a taxi is recommended; a one-way trip from the observatory to the ANU campus costs about A$25-35.

For accommodation, hotels in Canberra are within a 15-30 minute drive of the observatory. Staying in the city centre (Civic) or Kingston is recommended for an easy return after the event. On-campus accommodation at ANU (such as Lodge and Warrumbul Lodge) offers short-stay rooms, which must be booked at least 2 weeks in advance through the ANU Accommodation website.

## Equipment and Observation Content

**Observation equipment** is the highlight of the event. Mount Stromlo Observatory has 7 operational telescopes, the largest being the 1.88-metre "Anglo-Australian Telescope" (AAT), but this is mainly used for professional research; the telescopes usually opened for public events are the 1.3-metre Great Melbourne Telescope and the 0.5-metre teaching telescope.

Observation content is adjusted dynamically by season and celestial positions. In the Southern Hemisphere winter (June-August), the core of the Milky Way, the Magellanic Clouds and the Omega Centauri cluster can be observed; spring (September-November) is good for Jupiter and Saturn; summer (December-February) centres on the Orion Nebula and Sirius in Canis Major. **Each event's** detailed observation list is announced during the lecture segment; participants can bring a phone or tablet and use planetarium software such as Stellarium to help identify objects.

As for photography rules, mirrorless cameras and phones are allowed, but flash and infrared fill lights are prohibited. A tripod is recommended, as the telescope eyepiece mounts use the standard 1.25-inch size and can be paired with a phone clip adapter for photography.

## Academic Value and Career Development

The **academic value** lies in the events' direct link to ANU's astronomy curriculum. RSAA undergraduate courses such as ASTR1003: Introduction to Astronomy and ASTR2001: Observational Astrophysics list Open Night attendance as an out-of-class practical component, and participation records can count towards the "observation log" portion of the course assessment. The postgraduate course ASTR6001: Advanced Observational Techniques requires students to complete at least 3 Open Nights and submit an analysis report.

For career development, Open Night is a low-barrier entry point into **the frontiers of astronomy research**. A 2023 RSAA survey shows that about 22% of participants chose astronomy as a minor or research direction after attending an Open Night [RSAA 2023, Student Engagement Survey]. The guides at the events — RSAA PhD students — usually offer research project consultations afterwards, including application guidance for summer research internships (Summer Research Scholarship); the internship program offers about 15 places per year, with funding of A$500 per week.

In addition, Open Night is part of ANU's collaboration with the Australian Astronomical Observatory (AAO); outstanding participants may be recommended for AAO's "Astronomy Ambassador" program and take part in science outreach activities across Australia.

## FAQ

### Q1: How far in advance do I need to book for the ANU astronomy Observatory Open Night?

It is recommended to complete your booking within 24 hours of bookings opening. According to RSAA 2024 data, popular sessions fill up in an average of 1.8 hours, so it is strongly advised to watch the ANU Events platform from 2 weeks out. Booking is free of charge (free for students, A$15 for the public), but cancellations must be emailed at least 48 hours before the event starts, otherwise your eligibility for future bookings may be affected.

### Q2: Can I attend the ANU Open Night without an astronomy background?

Yes. The events are designed for complete beginners; the lecture segment explains the basics of the evening's observation targets in plain language. According to 2023 participant background statistics, about 65% of participants were not astronomy students, coming from fields such as business, engineering and law [RSAA 2023, Audience Survey]. A Chinese-language observation guide is available to help international students understand the content.

### Q3: Is the ANU Open Night suitable for children?

It is suitable for children aged 6 and above. Events last 2.5 hours and take place entirely outdoors; younger children may feel uncomfortable given attention spans and the low temperatures. Children under 12 enter free, but the number of children must be noted when booking. The observatory provides child-specific telescope stands (height adjustable to 80 cm) and safety goggles.
