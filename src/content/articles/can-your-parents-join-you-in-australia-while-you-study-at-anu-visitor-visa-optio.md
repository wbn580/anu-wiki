According to Department of Home Affairs data for the 2024-25 financial year, Australia issues around one million Visitor Visas (Subclass 600) each year, with roughly 15% of applicants being family members of student visa holders. For international students at the Australian National University (ANU), whether parents can join them or visit for short periods is a common question, but one with complex policy details. Australia's migration regulations do not include a dedicated "parent accompanying study visa" category. However, through pathways such as the Visitor Visa (Subclass 600), the Student Guardian Visa (Subclass 590) and the Temporary Sponsored Parent Visa (Subclass 870), parents can still enter or stay in Australia lawfully under certain conditions. Based on the Department of Home Affairs' 2024 policy framework, this article systematically reviews the visa types, application requirements and restrictions available to the parents of ANU international students.

## The Visitor Visa (Subclass 600) pathway

The **Visitor Visa (Subclass 600)** is the most common short-term entry option for parents of international students. It allows holders to stay in Australia for up to 12 months (usually 3 months per entry) and covers tourism, family visits or other non-work purposes. Under the Department of Home Affairs' 2024 guidelines, applicants must demonstrate sufficient funds to cover their expenses while in Australia and meet health and character requirements.

### Application requirements and document checklist

Applicants must submit a passport, proof of funds (such as bank savings of at least AUD 50,000), return flight bookings, an invitation letter (issued by the ANU student) and a travel itinerary. Department of Home Affairs 2024 data shows an approval rate of around 88% for Chinese applicants, with average processing times of 15 to 25 business days. **Key restriction**: this visa does not permit work in Australia and usually carries a "No Further Stay" condition (8503), meaning holders cannot apply for another visa while in Australia.

### Length of stay and renewal options

A standard Visitor Visa allows a single stay of no more than 3 months, although multiple-entry visas valid for up to 12 months can be applied for. If parents want to extend their stay, they must lodge a new application before the current visa expires, but condition 8503 may prevent an onshore renewal. In 2023, the Department of Home Affairs approved around 12% of Visitor Visa extension applications, mostly on grounds of family emergencies.

## The Student Guardian Visa (Subclass 590)

The **Student Guardian Visa (Subclass 590)** is designed for parents or legal guardians of international students under 18 years of age. ANU's 2024 enrolment data shows that around 8% of international students enrol before turning 18, and their parents can use this visa to stay lawfully in Australia. The visa allows holders to remain with the student until they turn 18 and to provide full-time care for them.

### Rights and obligations

Holders may stay in Australia for the same duration as the student's visa, typically 2 to 5 years. **Core right**: holders are allowed to work in Australia, but for no more than 20 hours per week, and caring for the student must remain the primary purpose. Under 2024 Department of Home Affairs rules, guardians must purchase health insurance (OVHC) and may not bring other family members (unless approved). In 2023, the global approval rate for this visa was about 92%.

### Suitable scenarios and limitations

If the student is 18 or older, parents can no longer apply for this visa. ANU Student Support Office's 2024 report notes that around 70% of guardian visa applications come from Chinese families. **Common issue**: guardians must leave Australia promptly after the student graduates, unless they switch to another visa.

## The Temporary Sponsored Parent Visa (Subclass 870)

Launched in 2019, the **Temporary Sponsored Parent Visa (Subclass 870)** lets parents stay in Australia for up to 5 years continuously, with a total stay of up to 10 years. The visa requires the ANU student to act as sponsor and meet an income threshold (AUD 83,454.80 per year in 2024). Department of Home Affairs 2024 data shows an annual quota of 15,000 places, of which around 8,000 were actually granted.

### Sponsorship conditions and fees

The sponsor must be an Australian citizen, a permanent resident or an eligible New Zealand citizen. ANU international students holding a student visa generally do not qualify as sponsors, but they can apply after gaining permanent residency through the 485 visa or skilled migration following graduation. **Fees**: the 3-year visa costs AUD 5,090 to apply for, and the 5-year visa AUD 10,180. In 2023, the approval rate for Chinese applicants was around 85%.

### Comparison with the Visitor Visa

Subclass 870 allows parents to work in Australia, but they must obtain employer sponsorship. Compared with the Visitor Visa, the 870 requires no frequent departures, but the fees are higher and the sponsorship bar is stricter. **Key difference**: the 870 does not carry condition 8503, so parents can apply for other visas while onshore.

## Other alternative visa options

Beyond the mainstream visas above, parents of ANU students may also consider the following pathways, although they apply to more specific situations.

### Parent Visa (Subclass 103) and Contributory Parent Visa (Subclass 143)

The **Parent Visa** (Subclass 103, queue-based) and the **Contributory Parent Visa** (Subclass 143) suit parents who wish to settle in Australia long-term. Subclass 103 processing times stretch to 29 to 30 years (Department of Home Affairs 2024 data), while Subclass 143 requires a contribution of around AUD 47,455 and takes about 12 to 18 months to process. ANU students must hold permanent residency after graduation before they can sponsor.

### Medical Treatment Visa (Subclass 602)

If parents need medical treatment in Australia for health reasons, they can apply for the **Medical Treatment Visa (Subclass 602)**. It allows a stay of up to 12 months and requires a doctor's certificate. In 2023, around 2,500 such visas were granted, with an approval rate of about 80%. ANU students can help their parents obtain certificates from Canberra medical providers.

## Common challenges in visa applications and how to handle them

Parents of international students often face issues such as **insufficient proof of funds**, **doubts about migration intent** and **incomplete documentation**. The Department of Home Affairs' 2024 guidelines stress that proof of funds must cover return flights, accommodation and living costs, and recommend providing at least AUD 50,000 in savings or income evidence.

### Tips for strengthening proof of funds

ANU students can provide Australian bank statements (such as a Commonwealth Bank account balance) as supporting documents. In 2023, about 15% of Visitor Visa refusals were due to insufficient funds. **Strategy**: parents can provide property certificates, stock account statements or other asset proof, or have the student issue a financial guarantee letter.

### Avoiding suspicion of migration intent

Visa officers may refuse applications if they believe parents intend to migrate. **Recommendation**: provide clear evidence of ties to home, such as employment certificates, retirement certificates, property in China or proof that other children are still studying there. Department of Home Affairs 2024 data shows that Chinese applicants who submitted a complete return plan saw their approval rates improve by about 12%.

## Visa application process and timeline planning

The full application process includes online lodgement (via ImmiAccount), biometric collection (Chinese applicants must visit a visa application centre) and a medical examination. **Standard timeline**: 15-25 business days for Visitor Visas, 30-60 days for Guardian Visas and 60-90 days for Subclass 870.

### Biometrics and medical requirements

Chinese applicants must have fingerprints and photos taken at an Australian Visa Application Centre (AVAC), at a cost of RMB 1,080 (2024 rate). The medical examination must be done at an approved clinic and costs about RMB 1,500. ANU students can accompany their parents to designated Canberra medical centres such as Bupa Medical Visa Services.

### Handling emergencies

If parents need to enter Australia urgently (for example, if the student falls suddenly ill), they can request **urgent visa processing**, which the Department of Home Affairs usually completes within 3-5 business days. In 2023, such applications made up 5% of all Visitor Visa lodgements, with an approval rate of about 75%. When paying tuition across borders, some families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/can-your-parents-join-you-in-australia-while-you-study-at-anu-visitor-visa-optio) to handle currency conversion and simplify the preparation of proof-of-funds evidence.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can the parents of an ANU student apply for an accompanying-parent visa?

Australia has no dedicated "parent accompanying study visa". If the student is under 18, parents can apply for the **Student Guardian Visa (Subclass 590)**, with an approval rate of about 92% (Department of Home Affairs 2023 data). If the student is 18 or older, parents can only apply for the Visitor Visa (Subclass 600), with a maximum single stay of 3 months.

### Q2: What is the longest stay for parents on a Visitor Visa?

The maximum single stay on a standard Visitor Visa is 12 months, but first grants are usually for 3 months. In 2024, the Department of Home Affairs approved about 12% of extension applications. For longer stays, parents can apply for the **Subclass 870 visa**, which allows up to 5 years, but it requires a student sponsor with permanent residency.

### Q3: Can parents work while in Australia?

The Visitor Visa (Subclass 600) explicitly prohibits work. The **Student Guardian Visa (Subclass 590)** allows up to 20 hours of work per week. The Subclass 870 visa requires separate work permission. In 2023, about 5% of Visitor Visa holders had their visas cancelled for working in breach of conditions.
