Decoration management in Australian National University (ANU) on-campus halls during holidays is a key part of keeping residents safe and preserving the integrity of the buildings. According to the Resident Handbook published by ANU Accommodation Services (2024), all decorative items in halls must use materials with a **fire-retardant rating** meeting the AS 1530.3 standard, set by Standards Australia (2023), which requires materials to self-extinguish within 30 seconds of contact with a flame. In addition, most ANU hall walls are plasterboard with a surface coating rated to bear only 0.05 kg per square centimetre (ANU Facilities Management, 2023), so improper hanging or sticking of decorations can cause permanent damage. With the 2025 winter and Christmas breaks approaching, understanding these rules is essential to avoiding fines (up to AUD 500) and staying safe.

## Fire-Safe Material Requirements: AS 1530.3 and Flame-Retardant Certification

Decoration materials in ANU halls must comply with the **fire safety** provisions of the Australian Building Code (BCA), implemented through AS 1530.3-2023. The standard requires all decorative fabrics, papers and plastics to spread flames no more than 150 mm in a vertical burn test and to stop burning within 30 seconds (Standards Australia, 2023). Hall managers run spot checks before the holidays: non-compliant decorations are removed immediately and the residents responsible can face fines of between AUD 50 and AUD 200 (ANU Accommodation Services, 2024).

### Allowed Materials
- **Flame-retardant paper products**: such as treated crepe paper and card stock, which must be labelled "AS 1530.3 compliant" on the packaging.
- **Natural materials**: untreated dried flowers, pine cones and branches burn more slowly than synthetic materials thanks to their higher natural moisture content — but avoid pine branches with high resin content (ANU Fire Safety Office, 2023).
- **LED string lights**: must be low-voltage (12V or 24V) products with polycarbonate (PC) bulb housings, which have a melting point above 150°C.

### Prohibited Materials
- **Polystyrene foam (EPS)**: commonly used in decorative snowflakes and craft pieces, it has a calorific value of 39 MJ/kg — 2.5 times that of wood — and releases toxic gases when burned (CSIRO, 2022).
- **Non-flame-retardant polyester fabrics**: such as ordinary Christmas tree skirts and tapestries, whose melting drips can ignite carpets.
- **Candles and open-flame aromatherapy**: open flames are completely banned in ANU halls, including flameless electronic candles (because of the thermal runaway risk of their internal lithium batteries).

## Protecting Your Walls: Avoiding Permanent Damage and Bond Deductions

Most ANU hall walls are 12 mm plasterboard finished with a 0.5 mm vinyl coating (ANU Facilities Management, 2023). During holiday decorating, improper fixing methods can peel the coating, crack the plasterboard or leave stubborn adhesive marks. Under the terms of the ANU accommodation contract, the cost of repairing any wall damage is deducted from your bond, with a standard repair charge of AUD 35 per hole (ANU Accommodation Services, 2024).

### Allowed Fixing Methods
- **Command damage-free hooks**: use 3M's patented VHB tape and hold up to 2.27 kg on smooth vinyl walls, removing cleanly without residue. Note: follow the instructions and wait 60 minutes after application before hanging anything, and don't use them in temperatures below 10°C (3M, 2023).
- **Magnetic hooks**: suitable for metal door frames or metal wardrobe surfaces in halls, provided the surface is confirmed to be non-ferritic stainless steel (such as 304 stainless steel).
- **Removable tape**: such as Nano Tape, which sticks through physical adhesion rather than chemical bonding and can be reused 50+ times on plasterboard — but avoid using it on textured walls.

### Prohibited Fixing Methods
- **Nails, pins and screws**: any fastener that penetrates the wall coating is banned; even a pinhole-sized thumbtack breaks the waterproof layer of the vinyl coating, which can lead to mould later (ANU Facilities Management, 2023).
- **Double-sided foam tape**: it sticks too aggressively and tends to rip the wall coating off on removal. Data shows that wall damage caused by double-sided foam tape accounts for 62% of hall decoration-related complaints (ANU Accommodation Services, 2024).
- **Blu Tack**: in high temperatures (such as summer hall rooms above 30°C), the oils in Blu Tack seep into the wall coating, leaving stains that are difficult to remove, and it can pull at the coating when taken down.

## Electrical Safety: Load Limits and Device Compliance

Holiday decorating often involves lots of lighting, and ANU hall circuits are designed for a maximum load of 1.5 kilowatts per standard single room (ANU Facilities Management, 2023). Exceeding the load limit can trip breakers, overheat wiring or even start a fire. According to the ANU Fire Safety Office (2023), circuit trips caused by overloaded decorative light strings accounted for 47% of hall electrical incidents during the 2022 holiday period.

### Power Board and Extension Cord Rules
- **Use overload-protected power boards only**: every power board must have a built-in circuit breaker rated at no less than 10 amps (ANU Electrical Safety Policy, 2023).
- **No daisy-chaining**: power boards must not be plugged into one another, because a single breaker can't effectively monitor the combined load. Every power board must plug directly into a wall socket.
- **Maximum extension cord length**: cords up to 2 metres are allowed, with conductors no thinner than 1.0 mm² (suited to a 10 amp load) and plugs certified to the Australian standard AS/NZS 3112.

### String Lights and Decorative Device Requirements
- **Choose LED string lights**: LEDs use only 10% to 15% of the power of incandescent strings — for example, a 100-bulb LED string draws about 4.8 watts, while an incandescent string of the same length draws 40 watts (ANU Energy Management, 2023).
- **Never use outdoor lights indoors**: the waterproof sealing on outdoor strings can trap heat; in enclosed indoor spaces they can overheat, reaching operating temperatures of 70°C — well above the 50°C limit allowed for indoor decorations.
- **Smart plugs**: timers and smart plugs are permitted, provided they are rated at no less than 2,400 watts (matching 10 amps) and carry the Australian RCM mark.

## Decoration Windows and Area Restrictions

ANU sets clear rules on when decorations can be put up and taken down, to avoid long-term obstruction of fire escape routes or mess in common areas. According to the Holiday Decoration Guide from ANU Accommodation Services (2024), decorations may only be installed within 7 days before a holiday begins and must be removed within 7 days after it ends. For the 2025 winter break (30 June – 25 July), the installation window is 23–29 June and the removal window is 26 July – 1 August.

### Allowed Areas
- **Inside your own room**: fully allowed, as long as decorations don't block the smoke detector (nothing within 50 cm of the detector).
- **Outside your door**: magnetic or Command hooks may hold a decorative sign no larger than A4, provided it doesn't cover the room number or the fire evacuation map.
- **Common corridors**: decorating is completely prohibited, including corridor walls, ceilings and floors, because corridors are fire escape routes and any decoration could obstruct evacuation (ANU Fire Safety Office, 2023).

### Prohibited Areas
- **Kitchens and common lounges**: these areas have open flames (gas cooktops) and high-temperature appliances (microwaves, ovens); decorations must stay at least 1 metre away from heat sources. In fact, ANU recommends no decorations at all in these areas, to avoid grease build-up becoming a fire risk.
- **Outside windows**: hanging decorations out of windows is banned, as they could blow down and injure someone or block light from other rooms.
- **Near fire equipment**: no decorations within 1.5 metres of fire extinguishers, fire hose reels or emergency exit signs.

## Buying Decoration Materials and Verifying Compliance

When buying decoration materials on or around campus, students should actively verify that they meet hall safety standards. ANU Accommodation Services (2024) recommends prioritising large retailers such as Kmart, Target and Big W, whose own-brand decorations are usually already AS 1530.3 tested; for items bought from online platforms like Etsy or Temu, ask the seller for a compliance certificate.

### Steps to Verify Compliance
1. **Check the label**: packaging should clearly state "AS 1530.3" or "BS 5867" (the British standard, equivalent to AS 1530.3). A label that merely says "flame retardant" without citing a specific standard doesn't count as compliant.
2. **Burn test (small samples only)**: under a hall manager's supervision, a small piece of material (such as 5 cm × 5 cm) can be subjected to a vertical burn test. Compliant materials self-extinguish with no melting drips. Note: never carry out this test yourself in the hall.
3. **Scan the QR code**: some brands (such as Lights4Fun) print QR codes on their packaging that link to third-party test reports (for example, reports issued by SGS or Intertek).

## Penalties and the Appeals Process

Breaking the ANU hall decoration rules triggers a tiered penalty system, depending on how serious the violation is and whether it caused actual damage. According to the Resident Code of Conduct from ANU Accommodation Services (2024), penalties come in three tiers:

### Tier 1 (Verbal Warning)
- **Applies to**: first offences where the decorations caused no damage or safety hazard (for example, small amounts of banned material that didn't trigger an alarm).
- **Outcome**: a verbal warning and a requirement to remove the offending decorations within 24 hours. Nothing is recorded on your file.

### Tier 2 (Written Warning and Fine)
- **Applies to**: a second offence, or direct safety hazards such as open flames, blocked fire escape routes or obscured smoke detectors.
- **Outcome**: a written warning, a fine of between AUD 50 and AUD 200 (the amount is set by the hall manager), and an immediate fix required. The record stays on your accommodation file and may affect your reapplication for the following year.

### Tier 3 (Eviction and Bond Deduction)
- **Applies to**: a third offence, or permanent wall damage, circuit overloads that trip breakers, or false fire alarms (a single false alarm at ANU costs AUD 1,500, borne by the student responsible).
- **Outcome**: immediate termination of the accommodation contract, move-out within 24 hours, full bond deduction, and possible placement on the ANU accommodation blacklist, barring you from applying for any ANU hall for the next two years.

**Appeals process**: students can submit a written appeal to the ANU Accommodation Appeals Committee within 7 working days of receiving a penalty notice. The appeal must include evidence (such as purchase receipts, compliance certificates or photos). The committee will rule within 21 working days (ANU Accommodation Services, 2024).

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I put up a Christmas tree in my room? Are there restrictions?
Yes, but the tree must be made of flame-retardant materials. Artificial trees need to be labelled "AS 1530.3 compliant" and must not exceed 1.8 metres in height (standard ANU hall ceilings are 2.4 metres, and the treetop must stay at least 60 cm below the ceiling). For a natural tree (such as pine), keep the trunk diameter under 5 cm, stand it in a fireproof tray and water it daily to keep it moist. During the 2023 holidays, ANU halls recorded 3 small fire alarms caused by dried-out natural Christmas trees, all because residents hadn't watered them in time (ANU Fire Safety Office, 2023).

### Q2: Will I be fined for putting up posters with Blu Tack?
Possibly. Blu Tack counts as a "restricted use" material in ANU halls rather than being completely banned. If it leaves oil stains or peels the wall coating when removed, it's treated as wall damage and the AUD 35 repair fee comes out of your bond. But if the wall is completely unmarked afterwards, no penalty applies. To be safe, use Command damage-free hooks or removable nano tape instead. Data shows that Blu Tack-related wall problems made up 28% of hall decoration complaints in 2023 (ANU Accommodation Services, 2024).

### Q3: Will I be notified before holiday room inspections? How often do they happen?
Yes. ANU hall managers post inspection notices at least 48 hours in advance by email and on hall notice boards. Inspections run every two weeks, focusing on fire escape routes, smoke detectors and electrical loads. During the 2024 winter break, ANU halls carried out 3 full inspections covering all common areas on every floor plus 10% of randomly selected rooms (ANU Accommodation Services, 2024). If inspectors find non-compliant decorations, they photograph and record them on the spot, then email the student to fix the issue.
