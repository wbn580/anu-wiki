The Australian National University (ANU) Fenner School of Environment & Society operates a campus agricultural plot of about **2.5 hectares** within the Acton campus, serving as the core outdoor laboratory for the university's environment and sustainability science teaching. Since 2017, when the plot was formally placed under Fenner School management, it has hosted **more than 400** undergraduate and postgraduate students per year for fieldwork courses, and it produces publicly accessible crop yield and soil data. According to the ANU 2023 Campus Sustainability Annual Report, the organic farming trial area has recorded positive growth in soil organic carbon for 5 consecutive years, making it one of the few agricultural platforms in Australian universities that serves teaching, research and community engagement at the same time. For students considering or already enrolled in ANU environmental science, geography or sustainable agriculture, knowing how to book a visit, join Planting Days and obtain yield data is a key step to making full use of campus resources.

## Tour Booking Process and Opening Hours

Fenner School Agricultural Plot tour bookings come in two types: **teaching group bookings** and **public open day bookings**. Teaching groups (such as course classes or research teams) must submit a written application through the Fenner School administrative office, lodged by ANU staff members or course coordinators at least **14 working days** in advance. The booking form must state the number of visitors (maximum 25 people per group), the planned activities (such as soil sampling or crop identification) and the equipment support required (such as spades or pH meters). According to the ANU 2024 Campus Facility Usage Policy, the approval rate for teaching bookings in 2023 was 92%, with the main reasons for rejection being timetable clashes (6%) and weather warnings (2%).

### Public Open Days and Individual Visits

Individual visitors (non-ANU members) can book the monthly public open day through the "Campus Events" section of the ANU website. In 2024, open days are held on the first Saturday of each month from 10:00–12:00, limited to **15 people** per session on a first-come, first-served basis. The tour route is fixed and led by a Fenner School PhD student or staff member, lasting about 90 minutes and covering the crop rotation area, the compost station and the rainwater harvesting system. The average attendance rate for public open days in 2023 was 78%, with the November session recording the highest fill rate (94%) thanks to its harvest-season activities.

### Booking Channel for Special Groups

Current ANU students can apply for "independent research access" permission to support personal projects or thesis data collection. Applications must include a research proposal of **no more than 300 words** stating the purpose of the visit, the plot areas required and the expected duration (maximum 4 hours per visit). The approval cycle is 5 working days, and successful applicants receive a temporary access card and the plot safety briefing. In 2023, 47 independent research access applications were approved, spanning 12 different disciplines.

## How to Take Part in Planting Days

Planting Days are community engagement events held 2–3 times per semester by the Fenner School, open to all ANU staff, students and nearby residents. The Planting Days announced for 2024 are 16 March (autumn sowing), 8 June (winter replanting) and 14 September (spring seedling raising). No advance registration is required — participants simply gather at the plot entrance at 09:30 on the day, with activities running until 12:30. Each session is led by a Fenner School technician and typically includes **3 segments**: a plot zone briefing (15 minutes), hands-on planting work (90 minutes), and tool cleaning with a wrap-up (15 minutes).

### Tools and Safety Requirements

ANU provides gloves, spades and seeds for all participants. Participants must wear long trousers and closed-toe shoes (such as sneakers or gumboots), and bring their own sunscreen and drinking water. During the 2023 Planting Day events, 2 minor grazes were recorded, both treated on the spot by technicians with no medical referral needed. Under ANU health and safety policy, all participants must sign a **simple liability waiver** (about 1 minute of reading time) before each event begins.

### Data Recording After Planting Days

After each Planting Day, the Fenner School updates the plot's online planting log within **48 hours**, recording the crop species sown, sowing density (in plants per square metre) and that day's soil temperature (at 10 cm below the surface). These logs are published through ANU's internal "Fenner Farm" platform and are accessible only to ANU email account holders. The autumn 2023 planting log shows that 6 crop species were sown on the plot, including kale (density 4 plants/m²) and peas (density 12 plants/m²).

## How to Access Yield Data

Fenner School Agricultural Plot yield data is published primarily through the **ANU Open Research Data Portal**, under dataset number ANU-2023-AGR-045. The dataset contains annual yield records for 2018–2023, covering single-harvest weights (in kg/m²) and moisture content percentages for 14 crop species. The data is provided in CSV format with an accompanying PDF metadata document describing sowing dates, irrigation volumes and pest and disease records. As of March 2024, the dataset had been downloaded **more than 1,200 times**, with 60% of downloads coming from IP addresses outside the ANU domain.

### Distinguishing Teaching Data from Research Data

The Fenner School draws a clear line between two types of data: **teaching data** is a simplified version collected for coursework, usually covering weekly growth records (plant height, leaf count) for only 3–5 crop species, published through Wattle (the ANU learning management system) and accessible only to enrolled students. **Research data**, by contrast, includes full soil nutrient analyses (nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium content, in mg/kg), daily weather station readings (temperature, rainfall, wind speed) and pest occurrence frequencies. Research data requires an application to the Fenner School data manager, with an approval cycle of 10 working days. In 2023, 23 research data applications were received and 19 approved.

### Data Visualisation Tools

ANU offers an interactive visualisation interface for yield data built on R Shiny, where users can select crop species and time ranges to generate line charts or bar charts. The interface requires no programming background but does require an ANU unified identity login. In 2023, the interface averaged 85 active users per month, with the most frequently queried crops being tomato (34% of queries) and lettuce (28%). In cross-border academic exchanges, some international students who have settled their tuition via channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-fenner-school-agricultural-plot-visits-how-to-schedule-a-tour-participate-in) use their remaining time to join data visualisation workshops.

## Plot Infrastructure and Research Uses

The Agricultural Plot's core infrastructure includes **4 irrigation zones** (drip, sprinkler, manual watering and rainwater harvesting), a 12 m² compost shed and an 8 m² seed storage room. The irrigation system is maintained by ANU Facilities and Services, with an annual water consumption of about 150 cubic metres (2023 figure), 30% of which comes from the rooftop rainwater harvesting system. The plot's soil is the red Chromosol typical of the Canberra region, with a pH of 5.8–6.5 and an organic matter content of about 3.2% (2022 soil survey data).

### Long-Term Experimental Design

The Fenner School has established **3 long-term experiments** on the plot: an organic vs conventional farming comparison (started 2018), a cover crop diversity experiment (started 2020), and an experiment on the impact of urban microclimate on crop growth (started 2022). Each experiment uses 3 replicate plots (4 m² per plot), with soil and plant samples collected every June and December. Data from these experiments has supported the publication of 4 SCI journal papers (as of January 2024), one of which appeared in *Agriculture, Ecosystems & Environment* (impact factor 6.0).

### Collaborative Research Projects

The plot supports the joint ANU–CSIRO (Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation) project "Urban Food System Resilience" (2021–2025), with a project budget of AUD 850,000. During the project, the plot gained 2 automatic weather stations (Campbell Scientific brand) and a soil moisture sensor network (recording data every 15 minutes). In 2023, the project trialled 3 drought-tolerant crop varieties on the plot, one of which — a quinoa variety — maintained 85% of its yield with irrigation reduced by 40%.

## Course Integration and Credit Links

Fenner School Agricultural Plot is a compulsory field teaching site for **at least 5 undergraduate courses** and **3 postgraduate courses**. Undergraduate courses include ENVS2016 (Sustainable Agricultural Systems), ENVS3010 (Urban Environmental Management) and BIOL2201 (Plant Ecology). Each course spends 6–12 hours teaching on the plot, usually spread across 4 field visits. The postgraduate course ENVS8001 (Environmental Management Practice) requires students to complete a 20-hour independent plot project, culminating in a 4,000-word report.

### Credits and Assessment

The fieldwork component of ENVS2016 accounts for **25%** of the final grade, assessed through a plot soil analysis report (15%) and a planting log submission (10%). In 2023, 68 students were enrolled in the course, with an average fieldwork score of 71.4 out of 100. The marking criteria emphasise accuracy of data recording and completeness of method description rather than final yield. Students unable to attend fieldwork for health reasons may apply for a substitute written assignment, but must provide a medical certificate.

### Research Opportunities for Postgraduates

PhD students and research master's students can apply to use the plot as their primary research site. In 2023, 4 PhD students and 2 master's students incorporated the plot into their thesis research, covering topics such as soil microbial diversity, crop water-use efficiency and community participatory agriculture. These studies require approval from the Fenner School research ethics committee and the signing of a **12-month plot use agreement** that specifies data ownership and authorship rules.

## Community Engagement and Volunteer Program

The Fenner School partners with 3 community organisations in the ACT region (including Canberra City Farm and SEE-Change) to run **4 volunteer workdays** each year. Unlike Planting Days, these workdays focus on plot maintenance tasks (such as weeding, repairing irrigation pipes and turning compost piles) rather than planting. In 2023, the volunteer workdays attracted 112 attendances in total, with ANU students making up 65% and community members 35%. No farming experience is required, but participants must be at least 16 years old.

### Volunteer Training and Certification

After their first participation, volunteers can sign up for the Fenner School's **2-hour basic agriculture training**, covering safe operations, basic crop identification and tool use. Volunteers who complete the training receive a participation certificate (which can be recorded in ANU extracurricular activities) and get priority booking for future volunteer workdays. In 2023, 38 volunteers completed the training, 12 of whom later applied for ANU undergraduate programs in environmental science.

### Data Contributions and Feedback

Data collected by volunteers during maintenance activities (such as weed species records and visual soil compaction assessments) is incorporated into the plot's community observation database. The database holds more than 200 records dating back to 2020 and is published through ANU's "citizen science" platform. Volunteers can submit observation data via an online form within 7 days after an event, with each record published after review by a technician. In 2023, community observation data was cited in 1 undergraduate thesis.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can non-ANU students visit the Agricultural Plot?

Yes. Non-ANU students can visit by booking the monthly public open day, limited to 15 people per session at no cost. In 2024, open days are held on the first Saturday of each month from 10:00–12:00. In addition, during the ACT Heritage Festival each October, the plot opens for 1 special visit; the 2023 session hosted 22 participants.

### Q2: How can I obtain yield data from the plot for personal research?

Yield data can be downloaded free of charge from the ANU Open Research Data Portal (dataset number ANU-2023-AGR-045), which contains annual yield records for 14 crop species from 2018–2023. For more detailed research data (including soil nutrients and weather data), you must apply to the Fenner School data manager, with an approval cycle of 10 working days and an approval rate of 83% in 2023.

### Q3: Do I need to register in advance for Planting Days?

No advance registration is needed. Just gather at the plot entrance at 09:30 on the day — no sign-up required. ANU provides gloves, spades and seeds; participants only need to wear long trousers and closed-toe shoes. The Planting Days announced for 2024 are 16 March, 8 June and 14 September, with each session lasting 3 hours.
