The Australian National University (ANU) has operated a revised Annual Progress Review (APR) system for graduate research students since 2022, requiring all students enrolled in research degrees (Doctor of Philosophy / Master of Philosophy) to submit a systematic progress report at the end of each academic year. According to the Higher Degree by Research Progress Management Policy issued by the ANU Graduate Research Office (2023), students who fail to submit their APR by the prescribed deadline risk suspension of their scholarship or termination of candidature. Research Training Program (RTP) data for 2023 shows that around 12% of research students need to enter a remediation process because of academic progress issues, and the APR is the key checkpoint for identifying early risk. For international students, understanding the APR review criteria and document checklist is directly relevant to visa compliance and the timeline for degree completion — a connection made explicit in the Student Visa conditions updated by the Australian Department of Home Affairs in 2024.

## APR Framework and Review Timeline

The **APR review cycle** runs on the academic year, covering progress from 1 January to 31 December each year. Every graduate research student must submit the annual report through ANU's HDR Progress Portal by **31 October** each year. The first APR applies to students who have completed 12 months of enrolment, and thereafter it is annual until thesis submission. Review outcomes fall into three grades: "Satisfactory", "At Risk" and "Unsatisfactory". Under clause 4.2 of the ANU 2023 Progress Management Policy, two consecutive "Unsatisfactory" outcomes trigger candidature termination proceedings.

### Composition of the Review Committee

APRs are assessed by the college's **HDR Committee**. The committee usually comprises the college's HDR convenor, representatives of the supervisory panel and one independent academic member. Supervisors must complete a Supervisor Report before the APR is submitted; this report is one of the core bases for the committee's assessment. The committee issues its written decision within 30 working days of receiving the materials.

### Visa Implications for International Students

The Student Visa (Subclass 500) - Research Sector conditions updated by the Australian Department of Home Affairs in 2024 require visa holders to maintain "satisfactory course progress". If the APR outcome is "Unsatisfactory", the university is obliged to report this to the Department of Home Affairs, which may lead to visa cancellation. ANU's International Student Support office advises students to confirm their progress status with their supervisor at least 4 weeks before the APR deadline.

## Document Checklist and Submission Requirements

APRs are submitted online through the **HDR Progress Portal**, which opens for completion on 1 September each year. The core materials include: the Student Self-Assessment, the Supervisor Report, an Updated Research Plan and a list of Completed Milestones. All files must be uploaded in PDF format with a total size not exceeding 20 MB.

### Writing Your Student Self-Assessment

The self-assessment must answer four standard questions: main research outputs over the past 12 months, difficulties encountered and how they were resolved, goals for the coming year, and whether additional resources are needed. The ANU Graduate Research Office (2024) recommends keeping the self-assessment to **1000-1500 words**, emphasising quantifiable progress indicators such as the volume of data collected, the number of conference papers submitted or the ethics approval numbers obtained.

### Supervisor Report Requirements

Supervisors complete the Supervisor Report independently in the system, including a rating of student progress (on a 1-5 scale) and written comments. The rating dimensions cover: research progress, writing ability, time management and alignment with milestones. ANU policy requires supervisors to complete their assessment **within 14 days** of the student submitting the self-assessment. If a supervisor fails to submit on time, the committee delays the review until the report is received.

## Milestone Completion and Extension Applications

The core basis of the APR review is whether the student has completed each stage milestone in line with the **Research Training Plan**. Milestones include: submission of the literature review (usually at month 6), ethics approval (month 9), confirmation of candidature (month 12), the mid-candidature review (month 24) and submission of the thesis draft (month 36). According to ANU 2023 data, around 78% of "Unsatisfactory" cases stem from missed milestones.

### Extension Application Process

Students who cannot complete a milestone on time because of force majeure (such as health problems or natural disasters) may submit an **Extension Request**. The application must include supporting documents (such as medical certificates) and be submitted no later than 14 working days before the milestone deadline. The ANU Graduate Research Office (2024) stipulates that a single extension may not exceed 6 months, and cumulative extensions across the candidature may not exceed 12 months.

### Remediation Plans and Support Resources

For students graded "At Risk", the committee requires a **Remediation Plan** setting out specific goals for the next 3-6 months. ANU provides supporting resources including the Academic Skills Centre for writing support, the Statistical Consulting Unit and ANU Counselling. In 2023, around 65% of students who participated in a remediation plan returned to a "Satisfactory" rating at the next APR.

## How APR Outcomes Affect Scholarships

ANU **graduate research scholarships** (including the ANU HDR Fee Remission Merit Scholarship and the RTP Stipend) all carry a "satisfactory progress" condition. Under clause 6.1 of the ANU Scholarship Rules (2023), if the APR outcome is "Unsatisfactory", scholarship payments are suspended within 30 days of the committee's decision. During the suspension period, students retain their candidature but must cover tuition and living costs themselves.

### Conditions for Scholarship Reinstatement

Scholarship reinstatement requires two conditions: the committee approves a new remediation plan, and the student achieves a "Satisfactory" rating at the next APR. ANU finance office data shows that in 2022-2023, 47 students had their scholarships suspended for unsatisfactory APR outcomes, and 32 of them had their scholarships reinstated within 6 months through a remediation plan. International students whose scholarship is suspended must also update their evidence of funds with the Department of Home Affairs, otherwise their visa may be affected.

### Special Clauses for External Scholarships

Students holding **external scholarships** (such as the Australia Awards Scholarship from the Australian government) must have their APR outcomes reported to the funding body as well. Different funders handle "Unsatisfactory" outcomes differently; for example, the 2024 guidelines of the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) state that two consecutive "Unsatisfactory" outcomes result in immediate loss of the scholarship. Students should contact the scholarships office to confirm the specific clauses before submitting their APR.

## Changing Supervisors or Colleges and APR Adjustments

Students who apply for a **Supervisor Change** during an APR cycle — because of a shift in research direction or problems with the supervisory relationship — must complete the relevant procedures before submitting the APR. The ANU Graduate Research Office (2024) requires a supervisor change application to include written consent from both the outgoing and incoming supervisors, plus an updated research plan. After the change, the APR review is based on the new supervisor's assessment.

### Cross-College Transfer Process

Students who need to move from one college to another (for example from the College of Science to the College of Engineering) must first obtain approval from the **HDR Convenor** of both colleges. ANU policy requires cross-college transfers to be initiated at least 8 weeks before the APR deadline so the new college has enough time to assess the feasibility of the research plan. In 2023, ANU processed 23 cross-college transfer applications with an average processing time of 45 days.

### How Supervisory Panel Changes Affect the APR

Changes to members of the supervisory panel (including associate and adjunct supervisors) do not require formal approval but must be noted in the APR report. ANU advises students to notify all panel members at least 2 weeks before submitting the APR so they have time to update their assessments. If there is disagreement within the panel (for example over the progress rating), the committee convenes a hearing to decide.

## Appeals and Academic Rights

Students who disagree with an APR outcome may lodge a formal appeal with the college **within 20 working days** of receiving the written decision. Appeals must be submitted in writing to the college's HDR convenor, with supporting evidence (such as additional research outputs or medical certificates). Clause 8.1 of the ANU Appeals Policy (2023) states that grounds for appeal must be procedural error or new evidence, not mere dissatisfaction with an academic judgement.

### College-Level Appeal Process

The college establishes an **Appeal Committee** within 15 working days of receiving the appeal, comprising one independent academic, one student representative and one Graduate Research Office staff member. The committee may uphold the original decision, require a re-review or modify the outcome. In 2023, ANU accepted 34 APR appeals, of which 12 resulted in modified outcomes (including 8 changed from "Unsatisfactory" to "At Risk").

### University-Level Appeal and External Arbitration

Students unsatisfied with the college-level outcome may appeal further to the **ANU Academic Board**. University-level appeals must be lodged within 30 working days of the college decision. Under Australia's Higher Education Standards Framework (TEQSA, 2024), students may also complain to the Commonwealth Ombudsman about procedural issues, although that channel does not deal with academic judgement disputes.

## Time Management Strategies for APR Preparation

Because APR preparation involves multiple parties (student, supervisor and college administration), the ANU Graduate Research Office (2024) recommends a **backwards-planning schedule**: working back from the 31 October deadline, start drafting the self-assessment 8 weeks out (early September), discuss progress with your supervisor 6 weeks out (mid-September), and complete all supporting documents (such as ethics approval updates) 4 weeks out (early October). ### Common Missing-Document Problems

According to analysis of ANU 2023 APR data, the most common missing materials are: an **updated ethics approval certificate** (34% of missing-document cases), a **collaborating institution agreement** (23%), and a **similarity report for the thesis draft** (18%). Students should use the system's built-in Checklist feature to verify every item before submission.

### Contingency Plan for Technical Issues

The HDR Progress Portal can experience access peaks in the last week of October. ANU Information Technology Services (ITS) advises students not to submit on the deadline day itself, and to download the offline system template (PDF format) in advance as a backup. If a system fault prevents submission, students must email all materials to the Graduate Research Office within 24 hours after the deadline, attaching a screenshot of the system error.

## FAQ

### Q1: Will my APR outcome affect my student visa?

Yes. Under the Student Visa (Subclass 500) - Research Sector conditions updated by the Australian Department of Home Affairs in 2024, if the APR outcome is "Unsatisfactory", ANU is obliged to report it to the Department of Home Affairs within 28 days. This can lead to visa cancellation. In 2023, ANU reported 19 APR-related visa risk cases to the Department of Home Affairs, and 14 of those students avoided visa cancellation through a remediation plan.

### Q2: What if my supervisor does not submit the Supervisor Report on time?

ANU policy requires supervisors to complete their assessment within 14 days of the student submitting the self-assessment. If the supervisor is late, the student should first send an email reminder, copying in the college's HDR convenor. If the supervisor still has not submitted within 7 days after the deadline, the committee makes an interim decision based on available materials (the student's self-assessment and milestone completion records), then re-assesses once the supervisor's report arrives. In 2023, around 8% of APRs were deferred because supervisors submitted late.

### Q3: Can I add materials after the APR deadline?

No. ANU requires APR materials to be submitted through the system before 23:59 (Australian Eastern Daylight Time) on 31 October. The system closes automatically after the deadline. If you miss the deadline because of force majeure, you must email an application within 24 hours with supporting documents (such as a medical certificate). In 2023, ANU approved 11 late submission applications, with an average processing time of 5 working days.
