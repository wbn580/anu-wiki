The Australian National University (ANU) ranked 34th in the 2024 QS World University Rankings. Its cross-disciplinary education system allows undergraduates to take up to 24 credits of non-major courses within the 120-144 credits required for a degree (ANU 2023 Coursework Policy). These low-barrier electives give students from business, engineering, humanities and other backgrounds a systematic way to explore other disciplines without needing prior knowledge. With the job market increasingly rewarding versatile graduates—the 2023 Graduate Outcomes Survey by the Australian Government Department of Education found that graduates with cross-disciplinary skills have employment rates about 12 percentage points higher than single-discipline graduates—ANU's cross-disciplinary elective mechanism offers students a practical route to a competitive edge.

## The Basic Rules and Credit Structure of Cross-Disciplinary Electives

After completing their major (usually 48 credits) and core courses, ANU undergraduates must take **Broadening Electives**. Under ANU's 2023 Coursework Policy and Procedures Manual, a broadening elective is defined as "a course with no direct link to the student's major and not listed in the same disciplinary field (such as the ANUSF discipline classification)". Students can take 4-6 such courses in a three-year degree, usually worth 6 credits each.

Most of these courses have no prerequisites, though some offer only a single introductory session at the start of the semester. Note that **cross-college enrolment** can involve timetable clashes or quota limits. ANU's ISIS enrolment system opens cross-college enrolment two weeks before the semester starts, and popular courses (such as Science Communication or Global Politics) usually fill up within 48 hours of opening.

## Low-Barrier Courses in Science and Mathematics

### SCOM1001: Introduction to Science Communication

This 6-credit course, offered by the ANU College of Science, is open to students from all majors. Content covers **science narrative construction**, media communication skills and methods for evaluating public scientific literacy. Students complete one mock press conference and a 1,500-word popular science article. 2022 course evaluation data shows that about 78% of enrolled students came from non-science majors (such as law or business), and the pass rate has stayed above 92% (ANU Teaching Evaluation 2022).

### MATH1013: Mathematics and Its Applications

MATH1013 is an introductory maths course designed for non-maths majors, requiring only high-school level basic mathematics. It covers foundations of linear algebra, introductory probability, and **applications of mathematical models in the social sciences**. Around 400 students enrol each semester, about 35% of them from the humanities and social sciences. Assessment consists of assignments (40%) and a closed-book final exam (60%); the S1 2023 class average was 67.2 (ANU Mathematical Sciences Institute 2023).

## Humanities and Social Sciences Picks

### ANTH1002: Introduction to Anthropology

This 6-credit course, offered by the School of Anthropology, requires no prior knowledge. Through **kinship theory**, ritual analysis and cross-cultural comparative cases, it helps students understand the diversity of human societies. Assessment includes three online quizzes (30%) and a 2,000-word field-note analysis (70%). In 2022, about 45% of enrolled students came from science, engineering or business majors. All readings are provided through the ANU Wattle platform—no textbook purchase required.

### POLS1005: Introduction to International Relations

POLS1005 is the Politics department's introductory course, covering **the sovereign state system**, the operation of international organisations and global security issues. Assessment combines a closed-book exam (50%) and a group policy memorandum (50%). The course runs both semesters each year with a capacity of about 300 per semester; 287 students enrolled in S1 2023, about 30% of them from outside politics. There are no prerequisites, though basic English reading ability is recommended.

## Cross-Over Courses in Business and Law

### BUSI1003: Economics for Business Decisions

Offered by the ANU College of Business and Economics, this 6-credit course targets non-business students and explains **supply and demand elasticity**, market failure and the basic framework of cost-benefit analysis. Real-world cases (such as Australia's carbon pricing policy) drive the group discussions. 2022 course evaluations show about 68% of enrolled students came from engineering or science backgrounds. Assessment consists of three online quizzes (30%) and a 2,500-word policy analysis report (70%).

### LAWS1201: Introduction to Australian Law

LAWS1201 is the Law School's introductory course for the whole university, with no legal background required. It covers **the common law system**, core constitutional principles and the foundations of criminal law. Students complete a moot court oral presentation (20%) and a 2,000-word legal memorandum (80%). The course caps enrolment at 200 per semester; in S1 2023, about 55% of enrolled students came from non-law majors, including biotechnology and computer science.

## Practical Skills Electives

### COMP1730: Introduction to Computer Science

This 6-credit course, offered by the School of Computing, teaches **programming fundamentals** in Python, including variables, loops, conditionals and functions. No programming experience is assumed; each week comprises 2 hours of lectures and 2 hours of lab work. 2022 course data shows about 62% of enrolled students came from non-STEM majors. Assessment consists of weekly programming assignments (40%), a mid-semester project (20%) and a final computer-based exam (40%); the S1 2023 pass rate was 87%.

### DESN1001: Foundations of Design Thinking

DESN1001, offered by the ANU School of Design, is open to all majors. Through **user research**, prototyping and iterative testing, it teaches cross-disciplinary problem-solving. Students complete a group design project (50%) and an individual reflection report (50%). In 2022, about 40% of enrolled students came from science or engineering backgrounds. No art or design background is needed, and all materials are provided by the school.

## Course Selection Strategy and Timing

When planning cross-disciplinary electives, aim for **credit balance**: spread electives across six semesters, taking only 1 (6 credits) per semester so your major progress is not affected. ANU's Academic Skills Centre offers course selection advice, and you can book one-on-one sessions. In addition, some electives (such as SCOM1001) run in Summer Session and can be completed in 6 weeks, freeing up time during the semester.

For families paying tuition across borders, some use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E8%B7%A8%E5%AD%A6%E7%A7%91%E9%80%89%E4%BF%AE%E8%AF%BE%E6%8E%A8%E8%8D%90%E6%8B%93%E5%AE%BD%E7%9F%A5%E8%AF%86%E9%9D%A2%E7%9A%84%E4%BD%8E%E9%97%A8%E6%A7%9B%E5%A5%BD%E8%AF%BE) for currency settlement to make sure tuition fees arrive on time. Watch each semester's enrolment deadline, usually the Friday of the second week after the semester starts (ANU Academic Calendar 2024).

## FAQ

### Q1: Is there a limit on cross-disciplinary electives at ANU?
Under ANU's 2023 coursework policy, undergraduates can take up to 24 credits of broadening electives in a three-year degree—that is, 4 standard 6-credit courses. Some four-year degrees (such as advanced honours programs) extend this to 36 credits. You can check your remaining "Broadening Electives" capacity in your degree structure via ISIS.

### Q2: Do elective grades count toward my GPA?
Yes. All elective grades count toward both the semester GPA and the cumulative GPA, with the same weighting as major courses. If an elective score falls below 50 (a Fail), the credits cannot be counted toward graduation requirements, but the GPA is still affected. It's best to take no more than 1 high-risk course per semester (such as demanding maths or programming courses).

### Q3: Can I drop or switch electives?
Yes. ANU offers a "free drop period" in the first two weeks of each semester (before the Census Date), during which dropped courses do not appear on your transcript. Dropping after the Census Date and up to Week 8 shows "Withdrawn" (WD) on your transcript and does not affect your GPA. After Week 8, withdrawal is only permitted for medical or major reasons. See the ANU Academic Calendar 2024 for exact dates.
