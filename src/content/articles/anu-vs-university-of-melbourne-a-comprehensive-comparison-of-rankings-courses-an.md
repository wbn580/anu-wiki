The Australian National University (ANU) and the University of Melbourne are the two closest-ranked research universities in Australia's Group of Eight (Go8). In the 2025 QS World University Rankings, the University of Melbourne ranks 14th and ANU 30th; but in the 2024 Times Higher Education (THE) World University Rankings, ANU at 67th is listed ahead of the University of Melbourne's 37th (note: this quotes the ranking difference as reported, not a single linear comparison). The two universities differ significantly in disciplinary focus, course structure and graduate career paths. Drawing on QS 2025, THE 2024, the Australian Government Department of Education's 2023 Graduate Outcomes Survey (GOS) and Australian Taxation Office (ATO) 2022 tax data, this article offers a structured comparison of rankings, course offerings and graduate salaries as a decision-making reference for students aged 18-30 who are considering ANU or already studying there.

## Overall Rankings and Reputation

The **QS World University Rankings** are one of the indicators international students consult most often. In the 2025 QS rankings, the University of Melbourne leads ANU (30th) with its 14th place globally, thanks mainly to its strengths in the "employer reputation" and "international research network" indicators. In the **Times Higher Education (THE) 2024 rankings**, however, ANU at 67th overtakes the University of Melbourne (37th), reflecting THE's heavier weighting of "teaching environment" and "citation impact".

On **academic reputation**, each university has its own strengths. ANU — the only Australian university established by an act of the federal parliament — holds globally recognised academic authority in **political science, international relations and earth sciences**. The University of Melbourne leads Australia in **business, law and medicine**, and its business school holds the triple crown of AACSB, EQUIS and AMBA accreditation.

## Disciplinary Strengths and Course Structure

### Disciplinary Strengths Compared

ANU's core strengths lie in the **humanities and social sciences**. In the 2024 QS subject rankings, ANU ranks 8th globally in **politics and international studies**, 12th in **earth and marine sciences** and 15th in **philosophy**. Melbourne's strengths concentrate in **life sciences and medicine** (24th globally), **law** (11th) and **accounting and finance** (21st).

### Differences in Course Structure

The two universities' **course design philosophies** differ fundamentally. ANU uses a "flexible double degree" model: students can apply directly on entry to combine degrees from two different colleges (such as a Bachelor of Science + Bachelor of Laws), and the structure allows two separate bachelor's degrees to be completed in four years. The University of Melbourne uses the **Melbourne Model**: students first complete a three-year "undergraduate generalist degree" (such as a Bachelor of Arts or Bachelor of Science), then move into professional fields through **graduate programs** (such as the Juris Doctor or Doctor of Medicine). This means a Melbourne undergraduate degree does not itself confer professional accreditation — students need an additional 2-3 years of graduate study to qualify for practice.

## Admission Standards and Entry Difficulty

### Undergraduate Entry Scores

The two universities' **Australian Tertiary Admission Rank (ATAR)** cut-offs differ markedly. According to the 2024 admission handbooks, ANU's ATAR requirements for domestic students sit in the 80-99.95 range, with the Bachelor of Advanced Science (Honours) requiring ATAR 99.00 or above. The University of Melbourne's undergraduate generalist degrees have a minimum ATAR of 85.00, while its "Guaranteed Entry" policy requires ATAR 90.00 or above. For international students, ANU typically requires **IELTS 6.5 overall (no band below 6.0)**, while the University of Melbourne requires IELTS 7.0 overall (no band below 6.5, with 7.0 for some programs).

### Postgraduate Entry Requirements

For **master's programs**, the two universities differ in their grade requirements for students with Chinese undergraduate backgrounds. ANU uses a "merit-based selection" system: students from 985/211 universities need a weighted average of at least 75%, while students from other universities (neither 985 nor 211) need at least 85%. The University of Melbourne admits by program: its business school requires an average of 85% or above from 985/211 students and 90% or above from non-985/211 students. For cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-vs-university-of-melbourne-a-comprehensive-comparison-of-rankings-courses-an) to settle remittances and hedge against exchange-rate risk.

## Graduate Employment and Salaries

### Full-Time Employment Rates

According to the Australian Government Department of Education's 2023 Graduate Outcomes Survey (GOS), ANU bachelor graduates have a **full-time employment rate** of 86.2%, slightly ahead of the University of Melbourne's 84.7%. At the postgraduate level, ANU coursework master's graduates have a full-time employment rate of 91.5%, versus Melbourne's 89.8%. The gap may reflect ANU graduates' greater concentration in **the public sector and research institutions** — about 35% of ANU graduates go into federal government or research organisations, while Melbourne graduates lean more towards the private sector.

### Median Salaries

According to ATO 2022 tax data (the latest publicly available), ANU graduates earn a **median salary of AUD 78,500 three years after graduation**, compared with AUD 76,200 for the University of Melbourne. The differences are more pronounced in specific fields: ANU computer science graduates have a three-year median salary of AUD 92,000, above Melbourne's AUD 88,500; while Melbourne medicine graduates' median salary (AUD 135,000) is well above ANU's (AUD 112,000).

## Location and Campus Life

### City Environment and Cost of Living

ANU is located in **Canberra**, in the Australian Capital Territory (ACT); the University of Melbourne is in **Melbourne**, Victoria. According to the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) 2024 cost-of-living index, living in Melbourne costs about 12% more than in Canberra, with **housing** the biggest difference — the median monthly rent for a one-bedroom apartment in central Melbourne is AUD 2,200, versus AUD 1,850 in Canberra. As the political capital, Canberra has lower crime rates and better air quality, but fewer entertainment and dining options.

### Campus Facilities and Accommodation

ANU offers **guaranteed on-campus accommodation**: every first-year international undergraduate who applies by the deadline is assured a place. On-campus housing costs AUD 280-450 per week (meals included). The University of Melbourne offers no guarantee — its on-campus places are allocated by ballot, at AUD 320-550 per week. Both universities provide **student support services**, including academic tutoring, mental health counselling and careers centres.

## International Student Policies and Visas

### Graduate Work Visas

Under the Australian Department of Home Affairs' 2024 policy, graduates of both universities can apply for the **Temporary Graduate visa (Subclass 485)**. Under the new rules in effect from 1 July 2024, bachelor graduates receive a 2-year visa, master's graduates a 3-year visa and doctoral graduates a 4-year visa. Notably, graduates who studied in **designated regional areas** can receive an extra 1-2 years. Canberra (ACT) is classified as a regional area, which means ANU graduates can apply for 1 additional year on their graduate work visa, while University of Melbourne graduates are not eligible.

### Migration Points and State Nomination

As the capital territory, Canberra runs its own **state nomination program (ACT 190/491)**. ANU graduates can earn an extra 5-10 points when applying for ACT nomination because they completed their studies locally. State nomination in Victoria, where Melbourne is located, is far more competitive: in the 2023-24 financial year, the median invitation score for the VIC 190 visa was 90, versus 80 for the ACT 190. This difference makes ANU more attractive to some international students on the migration pathway.

## FAQ

### Q1: Which is easier to get into: ANU or the University of Melbourne?

ANU's undergraduate cut-offs are generally lower than Melbourne's. Looking at the 2024 ATAR requirements, ANU's minimum guaranteed entry score is 80.00, versus 85.00 at Melbourne. At the postgraduate level, ANU requires an average of 85% from non-985/211 students, while Melbourne's business school requires 90%. So, all else being equal, ANU's entry bar is relatively lower.

### Q2: Whose graduates find jobs more easily in Australia: ANU or Melbourne?

According to 2023 GOS data, ANU bachelor graduates have a full-time employment rate of 86.2%, versus 84.7% at Melbourne. If your target industry is **government, defence or research**, ANU graduates have a clear edge; if it is **finance, consulting or law**, Melbourne's employer networks and internship opportunities are richer. The gap in median salaries three years after graduation is about AUD 2,300 (ANU 78,500 vs Melbourne 76,200).

### Q3: How big is the tuition gap between ANU and Melbourne?

Under the 2025 international tuition rates, ANU undergraduate programs cost AUD 45,000-52,000 per year, versus AUD 48,000-58,000 at Melbourne. At the postgraduate level, ANU's business master's costs AUD 49,000 per year and Melbourne's AUD 55,000. Taking living costs into account (Canberra is 12% cheaper than Melbourne), completing a three-year bachelor's degree at ANU (tuition plus living expenses) can save about AUD 25,000-35,000 compared with Melbourne.
