## Key Employment Indicators Across Popular Master's Fields

According to ANU's official graduate employment survey, overall employment rates for master's graduates remain strong, with most fields reporting high full-time employment within months of graduation. The survey shows that graduates in computing, finance, and engineering consistently achieve above-average outcomes when compared with other disciplines.

For salaries, the report indicates that these fields command competitive starting pay. Computing and finance graduates, in particular, often secure positions with higher median salaries than the university-wide average for master's cohorts. Engineering graduates also see robust earnings, though variability by specialisation is common. These findings reflect the strong demand for technical and quantitative skills in the Australian job market.

## Industry Destinations: Local and International Pathways

LinkedIn alumni data provides a complementary view of where ANU master's graduates end up. The data shows that a significant share of computing graduates work in large technology firms, both in Australia and internationally, while finance graduates frequently enter banking, consulting, and other financial services sectors. Engineering graduates are found across construction, mining, and energy industries, with many staying in Australia but also a notable portion moving to overseas roles.

The official survey further distinguishes between domestic and international job markets. For graduates who remain in Australia, employment outcomes are strong across these fields. Among those who go abroad, data suggests that computing and finance graduates are particularly well-represented in global hubs. This comparison highlights how some fields offer more flexibility in geographic mobility.

## Comparing Field Differences for Prospective Students

When evaluating options, prospective students should note differences in outcomes. Both official and LinkedIn data point to computing and finance as top fields for employment and salary, with engineering close behind. However, engineering's industry destinations tend to be more traditional sectors, while computing and finance have broader cross-industry appeal. This distinction can influence decisions based on career preferences.

Other factors, such as geographic location preferences and visa pathways, also matter. The data does not provide details on visa sponsorship, but the information on salaries and industries can help applicants weigh practical outcomes against their long-term goals.

## Enhancing Your Career Prospects While Studying

For those interested in strengthening employability, engaging in practical experiences is beneficial. The data on industry demand suggests that developing technical skills, building a professional network, and seeking internships can improve chances. ANU's location in Canberra offers connections to government and research bodies, which can supplement private-sector pathways.

While the data points to strong outcomes for these fields, individual results vary. Use reports like these to inform your choices, but also consider personal interests and career alignment.
