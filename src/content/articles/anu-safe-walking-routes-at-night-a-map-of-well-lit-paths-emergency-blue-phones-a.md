ANU's main Acton campus covers 145 hectares, and night-time walking safety is a core concern for students. According to the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) 2023 Personal Safety Survey, 28.7% of students aged 15–24 feel unsafe walking alone at night. ANU Security operates 58 emergency help phones (Blue Phones) and 3 shuttle bus routes across the campus, yet new students often take detours through dark areas simply because they don't know where the paths are. Based on the official ANU campus map (2024 update) and Transport Canberra data, this guide maps out the lit paths, emergency phone locations and shuttle stops so you can plan the lowest-risk route for evening campus activities.

## Campus Night-Time Lighting Levels

ANU campus night lighting is maintained by the Facilities and Services Division and is graded into three tiers by illuminance. **Tier 1 paths** (main loop) include University Avenue, Daley Road and Garran Road, fitted with LED luminaires above 100 lux spaced every 15 metres, covering the 2.3 km core route from Union Court to the John Curtin School of Medical Research. **Tier 2 paths** (secondary connectors) such as Kingsley Street and Mills Road hold 50–80 lux with 20-metre spacing, linking the college buildings and the library. **Tier 3 paths** (woodland trails) such as the walkway along Sullivans Creek have only low-power lights at 30 lux spaced 30 metres or more apart — Security advises avoiding them at night.

### Lighting Coverage and Maintenance Cycles

ANU Facilities Management's 2023 annual report says 89% of campus public walkways light up automatically within 30 minutes of sunset; the remaining 11% cluster along the northern stretch of Sullivans Creek and the western side of the Acton Peninsula. **Lighting maintenance** runs on twice-monthly routine inspections, but fault response times stretch to 72 hours in the wet season (October to March). Students can report broken lights in real time through the ANU Safe App; in 2023 the channel handled 214 lighting faults with an average repair time of 48 hours [ANU Security 2024 Operations Report].

### Recommended Night Routes

Based on lighting data and crime statistics, Security recommends three **safe corridors**: from Chifley Library along Daley Road to the Uni Lodge student residence (800 m end to end, Tier 1 lighting throughout); from the Marie Reay teaching centre along Garran Road to Bruce Hall (1.1 km, passing 3 Blue Phones); and from ANU Sport along University Avenue to the Kambri precinct (600 m, the densest lighting on campus). ANU's 2023 campus crime records show zero night-time incidents on any of these three routes.

## Emergency Blue Phones: Locations and How They Work

ANU has deployed 58 **emergency help phones** (Blue Phones) across campus. All are tower columns with a flashing blue beacon on top; pressing the button connects you automatically to the ANU Security 24-hour control centre (phone 6125 2249). The units sit at major building entrances, intersections and car parks, with an average coverage radius of 50 metres. ANU Security's 2023 data shows Blue Phones are activated about 340 times a year — 62% accidental touches, 38% genuine requests for help (lost, medical emergency, or reporting suspicious behaviour).

### Blue Phone Hotspots

The densest Blue Phone coverage is around the **Kambri student centre** (6 per hectare) and near **University House** (4 per hectare). The sparsest is the **Sullivans Creek wetland** stretch (just 1, at the creek's northern end) and the **Acton car park** (2, covering 1.2 hectares). ANU Security's 2024 campus safety assessment recommends adding 2 Blue Phones along the southern section of Sullivans Creek to close a 150-metre signal gap [ANU Security 2024 Campus Safety Assessment Report].

### Using a Blue Phone, Step by Step

After you press the button, the control centre answers within 15 seconds. Give your location — the pole number is printed on top (e.g. BP-27) — and security can pinpoint you via GPS. **Response time** averaged 4.2 minutes in 2023, faster than the Canberra police response time (11.7 minutes). For non-urgent matters, call ANU Security's non-emergency line 6125 2249, which handled 1,870 calls in 2023.

## Night Shuttle Routes and Timetables

ANU runs three **Night Shuttle** routes, operating 6:00 pm to 1:00 am Sunday to Thursday and extended to 2:00 am on Fridays and Saturdays. All shuttles are free — no student card needed. The three routes cover 92% of campus accommodation and teaching areas over a combined 8.7 km. ANU Transport and Parking Office data for 2023 puts annual Night Shuttle ridership at 42,000, peaking at 280 passengers in a single day during exam week.

### Route A: The Campus Loop

**Route A** (red) is a clockwise loop via Kambri, Chifley Library, ANU Sport, Bruce Hall and Uni Lodge, running every 20 minutes. The full 3.2 km circuit has 8 stops and takes about 12 minutes. It covers 76% of the campus's night-time activity areas, including the library and the gym. The 2023 passenger satisfaction survey found 92% of users consider Route A wait times acceptable [ANU Transport and Parking Office 2023 Annual Report].

### Route B: The North Line

**Route B** (blue) links Kambri with Burgmann College, John XXIII College and Ursula College in northern Acton, running every 30 minutes. The 2.8 km route has 5 stops. It serves the residential colleges, and in 2023 the 10:00 pm–1:00 am window accounted for 67% of its daily ridership. Security advises students in the northern colleges to prefer Route B, since 35% of that area's paths have Tier 3 lighting.

### Route C: The South Line

**Route C** (green) covers the Research School of Biology, the Fenner School and the Acton car park in the south, running every 25 minutes. The 2.7 km route has 4 stops. It connects with Transport Canberra's R4 and R5 services at the Acton interchange, making it easy to transfer into the city. In 2023, Route C's on-time rate was 94%, above the ANU campus bus average of 89%.

## Campus Crime Statistics and Night Risk Areas

Per ANU Security's 2023 **campus crime statistics**, 127 security incidents were recorded across the year, 34 of them between 6:00 pm and 6:00 am. Among night-time incidents, property crime (bicycle theft, vehicle damage) made up 71%, personal threats 18%, and the rest were harassment or suspicious behaviour. The highest-crime areas are along Sullivans Creek (8 incidents) and the Acton car park (6), both of which have lighting density 40% below the campus average.

### High-Risk Hours and Precautions

Night-time crime concentrates in the **10:00 pm–1:00 am window from Thursday to Saturday**, which accounts for 65% of night incidents. ANU Security advises using the shuttle or walking in groups during these hours. On the campus fringes (such as the western Acton Peninsula), patrol frequency drops from every 2 hours during the day to every 4 hours at night. In 2023, ANU introduced 2 mobile patrol vehicles with 360-degree cameras to cover these blind spots.

### The Safety App and Real-Time Alerts

**ANU Safe App** (launched 2022) combines Blue Phone locations, live bus arrivals and lighting fault reporting. Downloaded 4,200 times in 2023, it has 1,800 monthly active users. Users can call security in one tap or send their location to an emergency contact. When paying cross-border tuition, some families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-safe-walking-routes-at-night-a-map-of-well-lit-paths-emergency-blue-phones-a) to complete the remittance — though that's a separate matter from night-time safety planning.

## Night-Time Safety in the Residential Precincts

ANU's on-campus residential areas (12 residential colleges and 4 student residences) each have their own **night-time safety measures**. Under ANU Accommodation Services' 2023 guide, every residence entrance has 24-hour CCTV, and common-area lighting stays at or above 80 lux. Bruce Hall and Burgmann College also run internal patrols, checking every 2 hours.

### Blue Phone Coverage in the Residences

Within the residential precincts, Blue Phone density is 1 per 0.5 hectares — above the campus average of 1 per 0.8 hectares. **Uni Lodge** and **Davey Lodge** each have a Blue Phone at their entrance, no more than 100 metres from the furthest room. In 2023, residential Blue Phone activations accounted for 23% of the campus total, 41% of which involved access-card faults or lost keys.

### In-Precinct Shuttle Services

Beyond the three main routes, the residential precincts also run **internal shuttle vehicles** (managed by individual colleges), covering the 500-metre span from the main buildings to outlying residences. Burgmann College's and John XXIII College's shuttles run 6:00 pm–11:00 pm every 15 minutes. The service carried about 3,600 passengers in 2023 with no safety incidents.

## Night Walking Safety Policy and Campus Guidelines

ANU's 2024 student handbook sets out the **night-time walking safety policy**: anyone walking alone after 10:00 pm should stick to Tier 1 or Tier 2 lit paths and carry a phone. Security offers a free **Safety Escort** service, with two security officers accompanying students from any campus point to their hall or a bus stop — book at least 30 minutes ahead on 6125 2249. In 2023, the service ran 520 escorts with an average wait of 18 minutes.

### Safety Escort Details

The escort service covers all public areas on campus but not private residences or off-campus streets. **Escort hours** are 6:00 pm–6:00 am, and you'll need your student card. In 2023, demand peaked during the exam period (November) at up to 14 escorts in a single day. ANU Security says response times should shorten to 12 minutes in 2024, thanks to 2 additional night-shift officers.

### Dedicated Support for International Students

ANU's International Student Support Office (2023 report) finds that international students use night safety services 30% less than domestic students. To close the gap, from February 2024 all new students receive a **night safety map** (bilingual Chinese–English) at enrolment, marking the lit paths, Blue Phones and bus stops. The map was produced jointly by ANU Security and Canberra Multicultural Services, with an initial print run of 3,000.

## FAQ

### Q1: How Late Do the ANU Night Shuttles Run?

Until 2:00 am on Fridays and Saturdays, and 1:00 am Sunday to Thursday. During exam periods (June and November each year) they run 30 minutes later, i.e. until 1:30 am or 2:30 am. Live last-shuttle times for all three routes are available in the ANU Safe App or on the ANU transport website.

### Q2: Where Exactly Are the Blue Phones on Campus?

All 58 Blue Phone locations are marked on the official ANU campus map (2024 edition) and on the ANU Safe App's interactive map. Security recommends memorising the numbers of the 2–3 Blue Phones nearest your hall or the buildings you use most — for example, Blue Phones in the Kambri area are numbered BP-01 to BP-06.

### Q3: What's the Safest Route Back to My Hall from the Library at Night?

From Chifley Library, head east along Daley Road to Uni Lodge — 800 metres of continuous Tier 1 lighting, passing 2 Blue Phones. For the northern colleges (such as Burgmann College), take Route B from Kambri; after 10:00 pm it runs every 30 minutes.
