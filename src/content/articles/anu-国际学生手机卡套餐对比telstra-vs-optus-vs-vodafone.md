ANU International Student Mobile Plans Compared: Telstra vs Optus vs Vodafone

As of 2025, Australia's three major mobile network operators — Telstra, Optus and Vodafone — together hold about 87% of the national mobile services market (Australian Competition and Consumer Commission, 2024, *Communications Market Report*). For students arriving in Canberra, where the Australian National University (ANU) is based, choosing the right SIM card directly affects how reliably you can stay in touch day to day, navigate campus and reach help in an emergency. As the national capital territory, Canberra has a different base-station distribution from major cities such as Sydney and Melbourne: according to the Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA, 2024, *Mobile Coverage Data*), Telstra's 4G/5G network covers 99.2% of the ACT's inhabited areas, while Vodafone's coverage is about 94.5%. This comparison looks at the three carriers through the lens of real ANU international student use, drawing on official coverage maps, rate tables and user-measured data to weigh up signal performance on and off campus, plan value and long-term cost.

## Network Coverage and Signal Strength Compared

The three carriers deploy base stations differently across Canberra, which directly affects signal stability on the ANU campus and in the surrounding suburbs.

**Telstra** has the most base stations in Canberra, and its 4G network covers every indoor area of the Acton campus (ANU's main campus), including the underground library levels and some laboratories. According to Telstra's official coverage tool (January 2025 data), average 5G download speeds on campus are 312 Mbps, with uploads of 28 Mbps. **Optus** has about 60% as many base stations as Telstra in Canberra, but in the main teaching buildings on campus (such as the Hancock Building and Chifley Library) its average 5G download speed is 245 Mbps. **Vodafone** has the lowest base-station density in the ACT, and in some indoor areas of campus (such as the basement levels of the Marie Reay Teaching Centre) 4G signal can drop to 1-2 bars.

### Performance at the Edges of Campus

At the edges of campus (such as the Bruce Hall residences and along the Acton peninsula foreshore), Telstra's signal stays stable, Optus occasionally fluctuates, and Vodafone may briefly fall back to 3G in some low-lying stretches (ACMA, 2024, *Mobile Coverage Data*). For students who regularly commute between the Canberra city centre and ANU, Telstra's continuous coverage along the bus routes (such as the R2 and R3) beats the other two.

### 5G Availability and Bands

As of 2025, both Telstra and Optus have deployed standalone 5G (SA) on the ANU campus, while Vodafone only offers non-standalone 5G (NSA) in Canberra, with peak rates around 70% of Telstra's (Telstra, 2025, *5G Coverage Map*). For international students with 5G phones, the real-world difference between Telstra and Optus on campus is small, but off campus — for example heading out to the Black Mountain Nature Reserve — Telstra's coverage advantage is more pronounced.

## Prepaid Plans vs Long-Term Contracts: Cost Analysis

For ANU international students, prepaid plans are usually more flexible than long-term contracts, because they don't require an Australian bank account or credit history.

**Telstra**'s prepaid offering spans Telstra Prepaid and the cheaper Belong (Telstra's sub-brand). In Telstra's official prepaid range, the most basic 30-day plan (AUD 30) includes 30 GB of data, unlimited domestic calls and SMS, plus AUD 15 of international call credit (usable for calls to China and other countries). Belong's 30-day plan (AUD 25) provides 20 GB of data, but international calls require a separate top-up pack. **Optus**'s prepaid plan (30 days, AUD 30) includes 40 GB of data, unlimited domestic calls and SMS, plus 300 minutes of international calls (covering 35 countries, including China and India). **Vodafone**'s prepaid plan (30 days, AUD 25) includes 30 GB of data, unlimited domestic calls and SMS, plus 500 minutes of international calls. By unit price, Optus's AUD 30 plan costs AUD 0.75 per GB, less than Telstra's AUD 1.00 and Vodafone's AUD 0.83 (carrier websites, January 2025 data).

### The Economics of Long-Term Contracts

If you plan to study at ANU for 2 years or more, a long-term contract (12-24 months) can lower your monthly bill. Telstra's 12-month contract (AUD 55 per month) offers 100 GB of data and unlimited international calls, but requires a credit check. Optus's 12-month contract (AUD 45 per month) offers 80 GB of data and 500 minutes of international calls. Vodafone's 12-month contract (AUD 40 per month) offers 60 GB of data and unlimited international calls. For new students without an Australian credit history, prepaid remains the safer first choice, since a contract application can be declined or require a deposit (Optus, 2025, *Credit Check Policy*).

### International Calls and Roaming

For students who regularly call family back home, Vodafone's prepaid plan includes the most international call minutes (500), with Optus's 300 minutes in second place. Telstra's AUD 15 international credit works out to roughly 100 minutes of calls to China (at AUD 0.15 per minute). On roaming, both Telstra and Optus support using your plan data free of charge in some Asian countries (such as China and Singapore), capped at 2 GB per day, while Vodafone requires a separate AUD 5 per day roaming pack.

## Student Discounts and Extras

All three carriers offer student-specific discounts or bundles, and ANU international students can verify their status with their student email (@anu.edu.au) to unlock them.

**Telstra** offers a 10% monthly discount on selected prepaid and contract plans through its Telstra Student Hub program. Telstra customers can also use its free public Wi-Fi hotspot network (covering Canberra's city centre and parts of ANU), which helps if you're tight on data. **Optus**'s Optus Student Perks program gives new customers AUD 5 off per month for the first 6 months, plus a 12-month Apple Music subscription (worth about AUD 130). **Vodafone**'s Vodafone Student Offer gives AUD 5 off per month for the first 12 months and unlimited social media data (including WeChat, WhatsApp and Instagram) — though not Chinese apps such as Weibo or Douyin.

### Data Sharing and Multiple Devices

For students who use both a phone and a tablet, Telstra allows data sharing on prepaid plans through its Data Share feature (AUD 5 extra per month), while Optus and Vodafone prepaid plans don't support data sharing by default. On contract plans, both Optus and Vodafone include free data sharing. For cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%9B%BD%E9%99%85%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E6%89%8B%E6%9C%BA%E5%8D%A1%E5%A5%97%E9%A4%90%E5%AF%B9%E6%AF%94telstra-vs-optus-vs-vodafone) to settle their foreign exchange and avoid the high bank transfer fees and exchange-rate losses.

### Emergency Services and Network Priority

In an emergency (such as dialling 000), all three carriers must provide a connection, but Telstra has an "emergency call priority" feature that can put emergency calls through ahead of others during network congestion. Telstra's network also covers the hills around Canberra (such as Mount Ainslie and Mount Stromlo) better than Optus and Vodafone — an important factor if you take ANU outdoor courses or go hiking.

## Measured Speeds and Latency

Based on crowdsourced measurements from ANU students in the fourth quarter of 2024 (source: OpenSignal, 2025, *Australia Mobile Network Experience Report*), here are the average download speeds and latencies for the three carriers in the Canberra region.

**Telstra** averages 298 Mbps down and 32 Mbps up on the ANU campus, with 28 ms latency. **Optus** averages 246 Mbps down, 27 Mbps up and 32 ms latency. **Vodafone** averages 189 Mbps down, 22 Mbps up and 38 ms latency. During peak hours (4 pm to 8 pm), Telstra's speeds drop the least (about 12%), while Vodafone's drop the most (about 25%). For students who need a stable connection for online classes (such as Zoom meetings or ANU's Wattle platform), both Telstra and Optus deliver a smooth experience, but Vodafone can stutter on video in some residential colleges (such as Ursula College).

### Streaming and Gaming

For students using Netflix or YouTube, both Telstra's and Optus's 5G networks support 4K video streaming, while Vodafone can only reliably play 1080p video in parts of campus. On game latency, Telstra's 28 ms suits competitive online games (such as *League of Legends* or *Valorant*), while Vodafone's 38 ms can produce perceptible lag in multiplayer matches.

## Number Porting: Process and Cautions

If you switch carriers, you can keep your existing phone number, but watch the timing and any fees involved.

Australia's mobile number portability (MNP) policy lets you switch within 24 hours. The process: first, get a PAC code (Porting Authorization Code) from your current carrier, either by SMS or through online support. Then give the code to your new carrier along with proof of identity (such as your passport and ANU student ID). Porting usually completes within 2-4 hours, but refunds of prepaid account balances take 5-10 business days. **Telstra**'s porting process is fully online, while **Optus** and **Vodafone** may require an in-store identity check (Optus, 2025, *Number Porting Policy*). If you hold a Chinese phone number, turn off "international roaming" before leaving China to avoid heavy data charges in Australia.

### Porting Fees and Contract Exit

If you're still in a contract, cancelling early means paying 50% of the remaining monthly fees as a penalty (Telstra and Optus policy), while Vodafone charges 25% of the remaining months. Prepaid customers pay no exit fee, but already-paid plan credit isn't refunded. It's a good idea to port before the semester starts (such as the February or July intake) so verification SMS messages during course enrolment aren't affected.

## Using Campus Wi-Fi Alongside Mobile Data

ANU's campus-wide Wi-Fi (eduroam and ANU-Secure) is free for international students, which reduces how much you depend on a mobile data plan.

ANU's Wi-Fi offers peak download speeds of 1 Gbps on the Acton campus (ANU IT Services, 2025, *Wireless Network Performance Report*), enough for HD video conferencing and large file downloads. On a tight budget, you can pick a low-data mobile plan (say 10-20 GB per month) and rely mostly on campus Wi-Fi. One caveat: eduroam is weak at the edges of campus (such as the University House gardens), where you'll fall back on mobile data. **Telstra** prepaid customers can also use its public Wi-Fi hotspots (Telstra Air) at about 200 locations across Canberra's city centre, cutting data costs further.

### Backup SIM Strategy

Some ANU students run a "main + backup" SIM strategy: a prepaid plan from Telstra or Optus (30-40 GB per month) as the main card, and a low-cost MVNO (mobile virtual network operator, such as Aldi Mobile or Boost Mobile) as the backup — these usually ride on Telstra's network at lower rates. For example, Boost Mobile's 30-day plan (AUD 20) gives 15 GB of data on Telstra's network, making it a handy backup data source.

## FAQ

### Q1: Which carrier has the most stable signal on the ANU campus?

Telstra is the most stable both indoors and outdoors on the ANU Acton campus, with average 5G download speeds of 312 Mbps and better underground coverage than Optus and Vodafone. According to ACMA's 2024 report, Telstra covers 99.2% of the ACT, versus 94.5% for Vodafone.

### Q2: As a new student with no Australian credit history, can I sign up for a contract plan?

Yes, but you'll need your passport, visa and ANU offer letter as proof of identity. Optus and Vodafone usually require a deposit (AUD 200-400), while Telstra may simply decline applicants with no credit history. Prepaid plans need no credit check, making them the safer option for new students.

### Q3: Are the "international call minutes" in these plans enough?

For students who call home 1-2 times a week (15-20 minutes each), Vodafone's 500 minutes or Optus's 300 minutes of international calls are usually plenty. If you only use WeChat voice or video calls, you can skip international minutes and cut your monthly bill — for example with Belong's AUD 25 plan.
