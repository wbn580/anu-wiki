ANU Materials Science: Electron Microscope Training and Booking

The materials science program at the Australian National University (ANU) is home to one of the Southern Hemisphere's largest electron microscopy centres — the Australian Centre for Microscopy and Microanalysis (CAM). The centre is equipped with more than 30 high-end electron microscopes, including transmission electron microscopes (TEM) and scanning electron microscopes (SEM), and provides undergraduates, postgraduates, and researchers with a complete booking system covering everything from basic training to independent operation. According to the 2024 QS World University Rankings by Subject, ANU ranks 41st globally in materials science, and its electron microscopy facilities log more than 15,000 hours of use per year [QS, 2024, QS World University Rankings by Subject]. With the rapid expansion of research in nanomaterials and quantum devices, mastering electron microscope operation and the booking process has become one of the core skills for ANU materials science students. This entry systematically covers the training pathway, booking rules, instrument types, and safety regulations, drawing on ANU's official laboratory manuals and 2025 academic calendar data.

## Training System and Certification Levels

ANU's electron microscopy training uses a tiered certification system with three levels ordered by operational complexity. **Level 1 Basic Certification** is open to all materials science undergraduates and covers basic SEM operation and sample preparation; training takes about 8 hours, after which users can book standard SEM instruments. **Level 2 Advanced Certification** covers TEM operation, requires at least 20 hours of hands-on training and a pass on the theory exam, and is valid for 24 months. **Level 3 Independent Researcher Certification** is restricted to PhD students and postdoctoral researchers; applicants must submit a research plan and obtain approval from the facility director, and are then allowed to use high-resolution instruments independently outside working hours. According to ANU CAM's 2024 training statistics, 412 students completed Level 1 certification over the year, and 67% of them booked equipment within the following 6 months [ANU CAM, 2024, Annual Training Report].

### Training Course Schedule

Training courses are held in concentrated blocks at the start of each semester, in sync with the ANU academic calendar. In Semester 1, 2025 (17 February to 1 June), SEM training is scheduled on weekdays from 24 February to 7 March, 9:00 to 17:00 daily. TEM training runs in two sessions: 10–21 March (before the mid-semester exam week) and 5–16 May (before final exams). Each session is limited to 12 participants on a first-come, first-served basis. Applicants must pre-register through ANU's Wattle learning management system and complete the online safety module (about 2 hours) before attending the face-to-face training. In 2024, the Materials Science department optimised training waiting times, cutting the average wait from 18 working days to 11 working days [ANU Materials Science, 2024, Departmental Operations Report].

### Certification Renewal and Upgrades

Level 1 certification has no expiry, but if a user has not used the equipment for 12 consecutive months, they must retake the safety refresher. When a Level 2 certification expires, users can renew it by completing one 4-hour supervised session (assessed by a certified trainer). As for the upgrade pathway, moving from Level 1 to Level 2 requires: 10 hours of accumulated equipment use, a sample preparation report, and a pass on the TEM theory exam (pass mark 70%). 2023 data shows that about 31% of Level 1 users applied for an upgrade within a year [ANU CAM, 2023, User Progression Data].

## Booking System and Operating Procedures

All electron microscopes are managed through ANU's online booking platform, "LabArchives Scheduler". Users log in with their university single sign-on (UNI ID), and the system shows instrument availability and maintenance status in real time. **The basic booking unit is 30 minutes**, with a maximum of 4 hours per booking (Level 1 users) or 8 hours (Level 2 and above). Booking slots for the next 14 days open every Monday at 8:00 am, and popular instruments (such as the JEOL JEM-2100F TEM) are usually fully booked within 2 hours of opening. According to Q4 2024 data, weekend slots have a booking success rate of only 23%, far below the 61% on weekdays [ANU CAM, 2024, Scheduler Usage Analytics].

### Cancellations and Penalties

Cancellations must be made through the system at least 4 hours before the start time; otherwise the slot is recorded as a "No-Show". Three no-shows in a single semester trigger a 14-day suspension of booking privileges. In addition, if an incident occurs during instrument use — such as sample contamination or mishandling of the vacuum system — the user must submit an incident report to the CAM management office within 24 hours. In 2024, 27 operational incidents were recorded, 5 of which put instruments out of service for more than 48 hours; the certification levels of the users involved were temporarily downgraded.

### Instrument Types and Typical Applications

CAM manages a total of 31 electron microscopes, divided into three categories by function. **High-resolution SEMs** (such as the Zeiss UltraPlus) are suited to surface morphology observation with a resolution of 0.8 nanometres, and are commonly used for analysing metal fracture surfaces. **Environmental SEMs** (such as the FEI Quanta 450) can image under low-vacuum or humid conditions, making them suitable for biomaterials and hydrated samples. **Aberration-corrected TEMs** (such as the JEOL ARM200F) achieve atomic-level imaging with resolution below 0.1 nanometres and are the core tool for quantum dot and two-dimensional materials research. Each instrument has its own training module, and users must pass the corresponding certification before booking it.

## Sample Preparation Requirements and Standards

Sample quality directly affects image quality and equipment safety. ANU CAM has established strict preparation standards: all samples must pass a "three-stage check" before loading — visual inspection, dimensional measurement, and conductivity testing. **Metal samples** must be mounted in conductive resin and polished to a surface roughness below 0.25 micrometres. **Powder samples** must be dispersed in ethanol and drop-cast onto copper grids, with agglomerated particle diameters not exceeding 50 micrometres. **Biological samples** must undergo critical point drying and gold coating; otherwise they will deform from dehydration in the high-vacuum environment. The preparation room is on the ground floor of the CAM building and is equipped with ion millers, ultramicrotomes, and plasma cleaners. Users can use these free of charge (preparation slots must be booked in advance).

### Common Violations and Handling

The most common violations in 2024 were: sample size exceeding the specimen stage limits (42% of all violations), failure to secure samples with conductive tape (28%), and undeclared residual magnetic materials (15%). The first occurrence of each violation draws a written warning; a second occurrence suspends equipment access for 30 days. For magnetic materials, CAM requires users to use dedicated magnetic sample holders and to note the material type and residual magnetisation strength in the booking remarks. In 2024, an incident involving undeclared neodymium-iron-boron powder damaged an SEM objective lens; the repair cost AUD 12,000, and the user involved was permanently banned from using CAM equipment.

## Safety Regulations and Emergency Procedures

Electron microscope operation involves high voltage (up to 200 kV), X-ray radiation, and liquid nitrogen coolant. ANU CAM has developed detailed safety procedures based on the Australian Radiation Protection and Nuclear Safety Act (ARPANSA, 2015). **Before entering the lab**, users must wear anti-static lab coats and safety goggles and confirm they are not carrying magnetic items (such as credit cards or phones). While an instrument is running, the operating room door must remain closed, and entry is strictly forbidden while the red light is on. Liquid nitrogen refills require two people working together, with each person filling no more than 10 litres at a time to prevent cryogenic burns. CAM conducted 4 emergency drills in 2024, with an average evacuation time of 2 minutes 15 seconds, better than the ANU laboratory standard of 3 minutes [ANU Safety Office, 2024, Emergency Drill Report].

### Radiation Monitoring and Dose Limits

TEM instruments generate X-rays while running. CAM has installed 3 radiation monitoring points around each instrument, with data uploaded to the central system every 15 minutes. A user's cumulative annual radiation dose must not exceed 1 millisievert (the public limit); users who operate TEMs must wear personal dosimeters, read monthly. In 2024, the highest personal dose among all users was 0.23 millisieverts, well below the limit. If a radiation leak occurs, the automatic alarm system triggers within 5 seconds and simultaneously cuts the high-voltage supply. CAM undergoes ARPANSA compliance inspections every quarter and recorded no violations across 2024.

## Booking Fees and Funding Support

For materials science students, booking fees for basic instruments (such as SEMs) are already included in tuition and require no additional payment. **TEM and high-resolution instruments** are billed hourly: AUD 30 per hour for Level 2 users and AUD 15 per hour for Level 3 users. Aberration-corrected TEMs cost more: AUD 80 per hour (Level 3) or AUD 120 per hour (non-materials-science users). Fees are settled monthly through ANU's internal account system and can be deducted from research grants or student accounts. In 2024, CAM provided about AUD 450,000 in fee waivers for materials science students, funded mainly through ANU's Research Infrastructure Fund [ANU Research Office, 2024, Infrastructure Funding Report].

### Scholarships and Subsidy Programs

PhD students can apply for the "CAM User Grant" for up to AUD 2,500 per year in instrument-use subsidies, submitting a research plan and budget statement. In addition, ANU partners with the Australian Research Council (ARC) on the "Linkage Equipment Access" program, which provides free instrument time for interdisciplinary collaborations. In 2024, 18 materials science PhD students received subsidies, with an average grant of AUD 1,800. Application deadlines are 31 March and 30 September each year, with results announced within 6 weeks.

## Academic Calendar and Key Dates

ANU operates on a two-semester system, and electron microscopy training and bookings are closely tied to the academic calendar. **Key 2025 dates** include: Semester 1 (S1) runs from 17 February to 1 June, and Semester 2 (S2) from 21 July to 2 November. The training registration window opens 3 weeks before each semester begins (S1: 27 January; S2: 30 June). Equipment maintenance is concentrated in the breaks between semesters: winter maintenance (2 June to 20 July) and summer maintenance (3 November to 16 February the following year), during which most instruments cannot be booked. During exam weeks (S1: 2–6 June; S2: 3–7 November), CAM opens only basic SEM instruments, and daily booking hours are reduced to 9:00–16:00.

## FAQ

### Q1: How long does ANU electron microscopy training take?

From registration to Level 1 certification takes about 2 weeks on average, including the 2-hour online safety module, 8 hours of face-to-face training, and one practical assessment. 2024 data shows that 86% of applicants passed the assessment on their first attempt. Level 2 certification requires an additional 20 hours of training plus the theory exam, for a total cycle of about 6 weeks.

### Q2: Can students outside materials science book the electron microscopes?

Yes, but they must apply through CAM's "interdisciplinary user" channel. Non-materials-science users must complete Level 1 training, and booking fees are increased by 50% (for example, SEM use goes from free to AUD 15 per hour). In 2024, interdisciplinary users accounted for 18% of CAM's total bookings, coming mainly from the chemistry and physics departments.

### Q3: Are the electron microscopes available during exam weeks?

During exam weeks (2–6 June and 3–7 November 2025), CAM opens only basic SEM instruments, with daily operating hours shortened to 9:00–16:00. TEM and high-resolution instruments are not bookable, as they undergo annual maintenance. It is recommended that users complete critical experiments at least 2 weeks before exam week.
