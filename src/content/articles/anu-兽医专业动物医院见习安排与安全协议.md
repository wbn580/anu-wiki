Clinical placements for the Australian National University (ANU) Doctor of Veterinary Medicine (DVM) program are built around mandatory rotations at the on-campus animal hospital. Students must complete at least **1,200 hours** of supervised clinical placement before graduating (Australian Veterinary Industry Commission 2023 accreditation standards). The University Veterinary Teaching Hospital, ANU's animal hospital, is the only tertiary referral teaching hospital in the ACT, seeing more than **8,000 cases** a year on average (ANU Animal Hospital 2023 operating report). Following the 2024 update to internship safety guidelines by the Australian Veterinary Registration Commission (AVBC), the university adjusted its placement safety protocols accordingly, covering three core areas: biosecurity, radiation protection and animal behaviour risk assessment.

## Placement structure: rotation schedule and credit requirements

### Core rotation modules
The fourth (clinical) year of the ANU DVM program consists of 8 mandatory rotation modules, each lasting 2–4 weeks. Students complete, in sequence, **small animal internal medicine**, **small animal surgery**, **equine medicine**, **production animal medicine**, **emergency and critical care**, **anaesthesia and analgesia**, **diagnostic imaging** and **public health and epidemiology**. At the end of each module, the Clinical Supervisor conducts an objective structured clinical examination (OSCE); students who do not pass must retake the module before graduation.

### Time allocation and caseload requirements
According to the ANU School of Veterinary Medicine 2024 course handbook, students work **35–40 hours** a week at the animal hospital, including night shifts and weekend duty. Each student must independently complete at least **50 cases** of full history-taking, physical examination and initial diagnostic planning, of which **15 cases** must include surgical assistance records. The caseload must cover canine patients (no fewer than 20 cases), feline patients (no fewer than 15 cases), equine patients (no fewer than 10 cases) and production animals (no fewer than 5 cases).

## Safety protocols: a three-tier protection system

### Biosecurity level management
The ANU Animal Hospital operates a three-tier protection system in line with Australian biosecurity standards (AS/NZS 2243.3:2022). **Tier 1 protection** applies to routine companion animals (dogs and cats) and requires isolation gowns, latex gloves and protective face shields. **Tier 2 protection** applies to suspected zoonotic cases (such as leptospirosis and salmonellosis) and adds N95 respirators and single-use waterproof aprons. **Tier 3 protection** is reserved for suspected rabies cases (although the ACT is a rabies-free region, animals arriving from high-risk countries must be isolated); these are handled in negative-pressure isolation rooms with positive-pressure respirators.

### Radiation safety and imaging work
During the diagnostic imaging rotation, students must complete **ANU radiation safety online training** (updated annually) before they may enter the radiology area. The training covers the ALARA (As Low As Reasonably Achievable) principle, dose monitoring (annual effective dose limit of 1 mSv for the student cohort) and the pregnancy declaration procedure. According to the ANU Work Health and Safety (OHS) 2023 report, the median radiation exposure for students is **0.08 mSv/year**, well below the statutory limit.

## Animal behaviour risk assessment

### Pre-procedure assessment process
Before any animal enters a clinical procedure, the attending veterinarian must complete a **Behavioural Risk Score (BRS)** on a scale of 1–5. Animals scoring 1–2 (low risk) may be handled by students independently; score 3 (moderate risk) requires handling under the supervision of a technical staff member; scores 4–5 (high risk) require chemical restraint (sedation or anaesthesia) before contact. A 2023 internal audit at the ANU Animal Hospital found that **72%** of canine cases were rated 1–2, **18%** rated 3, and **10%** required chemical restraint.

### Bite and scratch reporting system
All injuries to students involving animals (including bites, scratches and kicks) must be reported through the ANU OHS online system within **24 hours**. In the 2022–2023 academic year, the School of Veterinary Medicine recorded **37** animal-related injuries to students, of which **14** were dog bites (37.8%), **11** were cat scratches (29.7%), and the remainder were horse kicks or cattle butts. All injured students receive a tetanus and rabies post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) assessment, with costs covered by the university's workers' compensation insurance.

## Infection control and vaccination requirements

### Mandatory immunisation schedule
All students entering clinical placements must complete the following vaccinations and submit proof of immunity: **tetanus** (booster within 10 years), **hepatitis B** (3-dose series plus antibody titre testing), **rabies** (pre-exposure prophylaxis, 3-dose series) and **Q fever** (1 dose, for students on production animal rotations). Under the ANU Health Service 2024 policy, students who have not completed Q fever vaccination may not enter cattle, sheep or pig housing areas.

### Post-exposure procedures
If a needlestick injury, mucosal exposure or bite occurs, students should immediately rinse the wound for **at least 15 minutes** (with running water or saline) and then go to the ANU Student Health Centre or the Canberra Hospital emergency department for post-exposure assessment. The university runs a **24-hour** occupational exposure hotline staffed by infection control nurses. In 2023 the university handled **8** needlestick injuries, none of which resulted in seroconversion.

## Night and weekend duty safety

### Duty staffing
During night duty (17:00–08:00) and weekend duty (08:00–17:00), the animal hospital keeps at least **1** registered veterinarian, **1** registered veterinary nurse and **2** senior students (fourth year) on site. Junior students (third year) may only assist during daytime hours. Students on duty must wear a **personal alarm** and carry a **two-way radio**, reporting their location to the duty supervisor every 2 hours.

### Emergency evacuation plan
The ANU Animal Hospital maintains a **three-tier emergency evacuation plan**: Tier 1 (fire/explosion) — all personnel evacuate via 4 emergency exits to designated assembly points; Tier 2 (uncontrolled animal attack) — immediately initiate chemical restraint procedures and evacuate non-essential personnel; Tier 3 (natural disaster such as flooding) — move animals to the second-floor wards and cut all power on the ground floor. The plan is reviewed annually by the ACT Emergency Services Agency (ESA); the most recent drill took place in February 2024, with **48** participating students and an average evacuation time of **3 minutes 42 seconds**.

## FAQ

### Q1: Are there additional language requirements for international students doing the ANU veterinary animal hospital placement?
Yes. International students must provide an IELTS overall score of 7.0 (no band below 7.0) or equivalent before enrolment. In addition, students must pass the **Clinical English Communication Assessment** before starting placements. The assessment is run by the ANU Language Centre at the end of third year; students who fail must complete a **6-week** intensive language course (cost AUD 2,400) and cannot otherwise progress to the fourth-year clinical rotations.

### Q2: If I am bitten by an animal during placement, will the university cover the medical costs?
Yes. All animal-related injuries sustained during placement are fully covered by ANU Workers' Compensation, including emergency treatment fees, the tetanus vaccine, rabies PEP and follow-up psychological counselling. In 2023 the university paid an average of **AUD 1,850** per incident. Students pay nothing out of pocket.

### Q3: Is there any leave during the ANU animal hospital placement? Can I go back to China?
The placement schedule follows the ANU academic calendar. There are no summer or winter breaks during the fourth-year clinical rotations; the hospital only closes briefly over **Christmas** (25 December – 1 January) and **Easter** (4–7 April). Students may travel home during these closures, but must submit a leave request to the Clinical Coordination Office at least **4 weeks** in advance. Total leave across the year may not exceed **10 working days**, otherwise graduation will be delayed.
