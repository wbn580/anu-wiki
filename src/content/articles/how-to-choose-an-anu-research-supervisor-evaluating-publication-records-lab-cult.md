Choosing the right research supervisor is one of the most critical academic decisions in postgraduate study at the Australian National University (ANU). As a member of Australia's Group of Eight (Go8), ANU ranked 34th in the 2024 QS World University Rankings and has more than 3,200 postgraduate supervisors [QS, 2024]. However, according to data released by Australia's Department of Education in 2023, the average PhD completion time is about 4.2 years, and supervisor fit is a core variable in how efficiently students finish [Australian Department of Education, 2023]. Drawing on publicly available academic metrics and internal ANU resources, this article offers prospective postgraduate students a framework for evaluating supervisors across three dimensions: publication records, lab culture and supervision style.

## Evaluating a supervisor's publication record

**The publication record** is a direct measure of a supervisor's academic activity and research impact. ANU requires every Primary Supervisor to have published at least 5 peer-reviewed papers in the past five years, a threshold that ensures sustained research output.

### Balancing quantity and quality

When evaluating, look at both **publication volume** and **citation metrics**. Use Google Scholar or Scopus and focus on the h-index — a metric that counts the number of papers cited at least h times. For ANU professors in the humanities and social sciences, an h-index of 15-25 is a good level; in science, technology, engineering and mathematics (STEM), 30-50 is common. Bear in mind that h-indexes vary widely across disciplines, so cross-disciplinary comparisons are of limited value.

### Checking publication trends over the past three years

A supervisor's **recent publication trend** is more informative than lifetime output. Using the ANU Research Portal or Scopus, filter the supervisor's publication list from 2021 onwards and look for: continued first-author or corresponding-author output, the JCR quartile (Q1/Q2) of the publishing journals, and whether there is a record of co-authoring with graduate students. If output has dropped sharply in the last three years, or papers mostly appear in lower-quartile journals, the supervisor's research activity may be declining.

## Understanding lab culture and research environment

**Lab culture** directly affects a graduate student's day-to-day experience and mental health. In ANU's 2023 postgraduate satisfaction survey, around 78% of PhD students said the lab atmosphere was a key factor in whether they persisted with their studies [ANU, 2023].

### Observing team structure and student backgrounds

Use the lab website or ResearchGate to see the supervisor's current student list. An ideal team includes **graduate students at different stages** (from first-year PhDs to senior students) as well as postdoctoral researchers. Also note the diversity of student backgrounds — a team dominated by one nationality or institution may indicate rigid communication styles that hinder academic exchange.

### Learning about graduation timelines and outcomes

ANU publishes **PhD completion data** for each college every year. Through the ANU Academic Services Office, you can obtain the median time-to-degree for students supervised by a given supervisor over the past five years. If the average completion time for the supervisor's students exceeds 4.5 years (the standard ANU PhD duration is 4 years), or there are many withdrawals, treat it as a warning sign. Graduate destinations (academia, industry, government agencies) also reflect the supervisor's industry network.

## Analysing supervision style

**Supervision style** is a soft factor that shapes research efficiency, and ANU officially advises students to have at least one face-to-face or video conversation before formally confirming a supervisor.

### Micromanagement vs. hands-off supervision

During the conversation, observe how much control the supervisor exerts over the **research process**. Micromanaging supervisors tend to demand detailed weekly progress reports and strictly prescribe experimental steps; hands-off supervisors give students considerable freedom but may be slow to provide feedback. A survey by the ANU Postgraduate and Research Students' Association (PARSA) found that around 62% of students prefer "balanced" supervision — regular meetings (every two weeks) while letting the student lead the research direction [PARSA, 2023].

### Gauging feedback speed and communication channels

Ask the supervisor how long they typically take to respond to **draft chapters**. Most ANU supervisors commit to written feedback within 2-4 weeks, though actual turnaround varies with individual workloads. Ask to see sample emails between the supervisor and current students (with the students' consent), or contact current students directly to learn the real feedback cycle. When paying cross-border tuition, some student families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/how-to-choose-an-anu-research-supervisor-evaluating-publication-records-lab-cult) to complete the remittance.

## Using official ANU resources for due diligence

ANU offers several official channels to help students evaluate supervisors systematically. Making full use of these resources reduces information asymmetry.

### Using the ANU Research Portal database

The ANU Research Portal holds **personal profiles** for all academics, including research interests, recent publications, supervision history and awards. The data is maintained directly by each college and updated quarterly. When searching, check the "Supervision" tab, which lists the names of the supervisor's current students and their research project titles.

### Reading student evaluations and anonymous feedback

ANU's postgraduate association (PARSA) publishes a **supervision survey** every year covering communication, professional support and career guidance. The 2023 survey collected 1,247 valid responses, with around 15% of supervisors rated below 3 out of 5 [PARSA, 2023]. The data is available under the "Supervision" section of the PARSA website, but note that ratings based on very small samples may not be statistically significant.

## Spotting red flags

During your evaluation, certain **red flags** deserve attention. They may signal future collaboration difficulties.

### Beware of high student turnover

If more than 30% of a supervisor's PhD students **change supervisors or drop out** within two years, it usually points to systemic problems in the lab or in the supervision. ANU's PhD program allows students to change supervisors without giving a reason within the first 12 months, but frequent changes may reflect the supervisor's lack of available time or a clash of communication styles.

### Watch for administrative workload

Supervisors who serve as **deans, heads of school** or directors of large research centres may have significantly less time available for supervision. Internal 2023 statistics from the ANU Vice-Chancellor's Office show that supervisors with administrative roles average just 1.5 hours of one-on-one supervision per week, far below the 3.2 hours for those without such roles [ANU, 2023]. At the interview, ask directly about the supervisor's administrative workload and how it affects supervision time.

## Key questions to ask when interviewing a supervisor

Before formalising the relationship, run a structured interview to gather key information. The questions below help verify what your preliminary research has shown.

### On meeting frequency and format

Ask the supervisor: "How often do you usually meet with your graduate students, and are meetings scheduled or on demand?" Most ANU colleges recommend **fortnightly** one-on-one meetings of 30-60 minutes. Also confirm the format — in person, video or hybrid — and whether the supervisor allows students to reach them at short notice in an emergency.

### On authorship and publication policy

Clarify the supervisor's conventions on **author ordering**. ANU's academic integrity policy states that graduate students who are the main contributors should be listed as first authors, though practice can vary by discipline in reality. Ask the supervisor for their publication list from the past three years and calculate the proportion of papers with graduate students as first authors. If that proportion is below 50%, the supervisor may tend to claim student work for themselves.

### On career development support

Ask whether the supervisor provides **conference funding**, industry internship referrals or teaching opportunities. ANU gives each PhD student around $2,000 per year in conference travel support, and some supervisors add top-ups from project funds for international conferences. Learning what share of a supervisor's former students went on to postdoc positions or faculty roles is an indirect gauge of how effective their professional network is.

## FAQ

### Q1: How do I judge whether a supervisor's h-index is high enough?

The h-index should be assessed in the context of the discipline. At ANU, an h-index of 15-25 in the humanities and social sciences usually indicates an influential scholar; in STEM, 30-50 is common. But the h-index grows with academic age — for early-career supervisors (within 5 years of earning their PhD), an h-index of 5-10 is still normal. Use Scopus for the most accurate h-index data: the database covers papers from more than 7,000 publishers and is updated quarterly [Scopus, 2024].

### Q2: What is a normal average time-to-degree for a supervisor's students?

The standard ANU PhD duration is 4 years, but the median actual completion time is about 4.2 years [Australian Department of Education, 2023]. If the average completion time for a supervisor's students exceeds 5 years, or the completion rate is below 60%, treat it as a red flag. You can get specific figures through the "Supervision" tab on the ANU Research Portal or by contacting your college's graduate research coordinator directly.

### Q3: How long do I have to change supervisors if I'm unhappy?

ANU allows graduate students to change their Primary Supervisor without giving a reason within **12 months** of enrolment. The process requires a written application to the college graduate research committee and usually takes 2-4 weeks to process. After 12 months, changing supervisors requires solid grounds (such as inadequate supervision or a change of research direction). A 2023 survey by the ANU postgraduate association found that around 11% of PhD students changed supervisors at least once during their studies [PARSA, 2023].
