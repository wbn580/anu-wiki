The Australian National University (ANU) operates two medical service centres on campus serving all enrolled students: the ANU Health and Wellbeing Centre (Acton campus) and the ANU Medical Centre (Kambri campus). Under the Australian Government's Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) framework (2024 revision), international students with OSHC can use their insurance card for electronic billing (bulk billing) at the point of care, with no out-of-pocket cost for GP consultations. According to 2023 data from the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW), the average wait time for a GP at university campus clinics across Australia is 15 to 30 minutes; ANU's clinics use an online booking system to keep waiting times under 20 minutes. Based on ANU's official policies and current Australian Department of Health regulations, this article sets out the appointment process, fee structure and insurance rules.

## Booking channels and how they work

ANU's on-campus clinics offer **three booking channels**, all of which require identity verification through your student account or a medical platform. The online booking system is HealthEngine, covering all GP and nurse appointment slots at both clinics.

**Online booking** — after registering a HealthEngine account with your ANU student email, you can view available slots for the next 7 days. The system sends an SMS reminder 24 hours before your appointment; cancellations must be made at least 2 hours in advance. The 2024 ANU Student Experience Survey found that 72% of respondents made their first appointment online.

**Phone booking** — for urgent symptoms or when the online system is down. The Acton clinic can be reached on +61 2 6125 3598 and the Kambri clinic on +61 2 6125 2211. When booking by phone you'll need your student ID number and OSHC insurance card number, and the operator will tell you the expected wait time.

**Walk-in** — only for urgent cases, and same-day doctor availability is not guaranteed. The clinic's front-desk nurse performs a triage assessment; if your symptoms don't meet the urgent threshold, you'll be advised to book a slot for the next day. The ANU Medical Centre's 2023 annual report notes that walk-in patients wait on average 47 minutes longer than patients with appointments.

### What to bring to your first appointment

For your first visit, bring the following:
- **Student ID** (ANU ID Card) or the student number on your offer letter
- **OSHC insurance card** (physical or digital)
- **Passport** (for identity verification only; the clinic stores it encrypted)
- **Previous medical records** (if you have any, upload PDFs in advance through HealthEngine)

Patients who don't bring their insurance card must pay the GP fee out of pocket and then claim reimbursement from their insurer with the receipt. Under the Australian Department of Health's Medicare Benefits Schedule (MBS, November 2024 edition), the standard GP fee is 102.85 AUD, with a maximum rebate of 42.85 AUD.

## Consultation fee structure

Whether international students pay at ANU's on-campus clinics depends on whether the OSHC bulk billing option applies. Bulk billing means the clinic bills your insurer directly and you pay nothing on the day.

**GP consultations** — with bulk billing, the consultation fee is 0 AUD. If the clinic doesn't offer bulk billing, you pay **102.85 AUD** (2024 MBS rate) and then submit a claim to your OSHC provider. Allianz Care Australia (ANU's designated OSHC provider) reimburses 100% of the MBS fee, i.e. 42.85 AUD per visit, leaving an out-of-pocket gap of 60 AUD.

**Specialist referrals** — you must first be assessed by a GP; specialist fees are not covered by bulk billing. Dermatology, for example, costs about 280 to 350 AUD for an initial consultation; OSHC typically reimburses the MBS fee of 85.40 AUD, leaving about 200 to 270 AUD out of pocket. According to 2023 statistics from the Australian Private Health Insurance Association (PHIAC), the average out-of-pocket gap for specialists accounts for 68% of the consultation fee.

**Nurse services and vaccinations** — the on-campus clinics offer free flu vaccinations (April to June each year); HPV vaccines cost about 210 AUD for the vaccine itself (two doses). Wound care, blood pressure checks and other nurse services are free under bulk billing.

### What OSHC covers

ANU requires all international students to hold **OSHC insurance**; in 2024 the annual single-person premium was 608 to 1,024 AUD (depending on provider and level of cover). Allianz Care Australia's standard OSHC covers the following:
- GP consultations (100% covered under bulk billing)
- Public hospital admission (shared ward)
- Selected prescription medicines (up to 50 AUD per prescription)
- Ambulance services (fully covered in emergencies)

Not covered: dental, optometry, physiotherapy and non-urgent private hospital admission. Under the Australian immigration department's student visa condition 8501 (2024), your visa can be cancelled if you don't hold valid OSHC.

## Scope of clinic services

ANU's on-campus clinics provide **comprehensive primary care**, covering acute illness, chronic disease management, mental health and preventive care. Both clinics are staffed by registered nurses and GPs; the Kambri clinic additionally offers pathology blood collection.

**Mental health services** are among the most-used services by international students. The clinics have psychologists on site (referral from a GP required); initial consultations last 60 minutes, and fees can be rebated for up to 10 sessions under the Medicare mental health program (Better Access Initiative). The 2023 ANU Student Health Survey found that 34% of international students had used on-campus mental health services in the previous 12 months.

**Travel medicine and vaccinations** — the clinics offer pre-departure consultations, covering vaccines such as yellow fever, typhoid and hepatitis A. Vaccine costs are paid out of pocket: a yellow fever vaccine, for instance, costs 85 to 120 AUD per dose. The clinics also provide free COVID-19 vaccinations (continuing under the Australian Immunisation Program in 2024).

**Sexual health services** include free condoms, sexually transmitted infection (STI) testing and emergency contraceptive prescriptions. STI tests (urine and blood) are free under bulk billing, with results usually available through HealthEngine within 3 business days.

### Specialist referrals and external partnerships

When the on-campus clinics can't manage a complex case, the GP issues a **referral letter** to Canberra Hospital (3.2 km from the Acton campus) or Calvary Public Hospital. Referral letters are valid for 12 months and allow you to book any specialist. ANU clinics have a fast-track agreement with the Canberra Sexual Health Centre, so STI-related referrals can be seen within 48 hours.

## Cancellation and lateness policy

ANU's clinics enforce strict **appointment management rules** to reduce wasted slots. Cancellations must be made at least 2 hours before the appointment, via HealthEngine or by phone; otherwise the appointment is recorded as a no-show.

**Running late** — if you're more than 10 minutes late, the clinic may ask you to rebook or have a nurse do an initial assessment. Internal data from the ANU Medical Centre shows that about 15% of appointments in 2023 were rescheduled because of lateness. After 3 consecutive no-shows, your account is flagged and you can only book by phone for the next 30 days.

**Emergency cancellations** — if you can't attend because of sudden illness or an emergency, call the clinic to explain; the no-show record is then removed. Cases requiring supporting documents (such as a hospital emergency department receipt) account for 23% of emergency cancellation requests.

## After hours and emergencies

ANU clinic **opening hours** are Monday to Friday 8:30-17:00 (closed on public holidays). If you need medical care outside these hours, use the alternatives below.

**After Hours GP** — use HealthEngine to book an after-hours clinic in the Canberra area, such as the Canberra After Hours Medical Service (in Phillip, 8.5 km from campus). In 2024 this service charges 120 AUD per visit, with OSHC reimbursing the MBS fee of 42.85 AUD.

**Emergency services** — life-threatening situations (such as breathing difficulties, severe bleeding or chest pain) call for an immediate call to 000 for an ambulance. ANU campuses have automated external defibrillators (AEDs) at the entrances of 12 buildings; ANU Security (phone 6125 2249) can tell you where they are. According to 2023 statistics from the Australian Ambulance Association, the average ambulance response time in the Canberra region is 9.4 minutes.

**Mental health crisis lines** — Lifeline (13 11 14) and Beyond Blue (1300 22 4636) are available 24 hours a day. ANU's student support team provides telephone crisis intervention after hours (6125 2442).

## Paying and claiming reimbursement

For consultations that aren't bulk billed, you **pay first and claim later**. Payment methods include Visa, Mastercard, American Express and UnionPay (supported at the Kambri clinic). Claims with Allianz Care Australia can be submitted through its website or mobile app.

**Claim processing times** — after submitting online, funds usually arrive within 5 to 10 business days. Allianz's 2024 customer satisfaction report shows that 92% of claims were processed within 7 business days. You'll need: the clinic's itemised receipt (with Medicare item numbers), a photo of your OSHC insurance card and your bank account details.

**Large expenses** — for specialist surgery or hospital admission, you can apply for pre-approval. Pre-approval takes 2 to 5 business days; once approved, the insurer pays the hospital directly. When paying tuition across borders, some families use professional channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%9B%BD%E9%99%85%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E6%A0%A1%E5%86%85%E8%AF%8A%E6%89%80%E9%A2%84%E7%BA%A6%E6%B5%81%E7%A8%8B%E4%B8%8E%E5%B0%B1%E8%AF%8A%E8%B4%B9%E7%94%A8) to complete the currency conversion — the same payment logic applies to transferring medical fees across borders.

## FAQ

### Q1: Do international students have to pay to see a GP at ANU's on-campus clinics?

With valid OSHC insurance and when the clinic offers bulk billing, the GP consultation fee is 0 AUD. If the clinic doesn't offer bulk billing, you pay 102.85 AUD (2024 MBS rate) upfront, then claim 42.85 AUD back from Allianz Care Australia, for an actual cost of 60 AUD. Bring your insurance card to your first visit to make sure you qualify for bulk billing.

### Q2: Can I see someone about mental health at ANU's on-campus clinics?

Yes. The clinics provide counselling services, but you'll need a referral letter from a GP first. Under the Medicare mental health program, you can claim up to 10 rebated sessions per year (60 minutes each). The 2023 ANU Student Health Survey found that 34% of international students had used on-campus mental health services. After hours, call Lifeline (13 11 14) for urgent support.

### Q3: Can I be seen at an ANU clinic without my OSHC insurance card?

You can still be seen, but bulk billing won't apply. You'll pay the GP fee out of pocket (102.85 AUD) and then claim from your insurer with the receipt. If you provide your insurance card details within 30 days of the visit, some clinics can re-bill the consultation as bulk billing. Not holding OSHC may breach student visa condition 8501, so activate your cover within 7 days of arriving in Australia.
