The Honours year at the Australian National University (ANU) is a crucial bridge between undergraduate study and a research doctorate, and its application process underwent significant changes in 2024, including a unified deadline and stricter grade thresholds. Under the Honours Policy released by ANU in 2024, applicants must reach a weighted average mark of at least 70% (GPA 5.0/7.0) by the end of their third undergraduate year, and places in the Honours year (usually 1 year) are capped by each college's supervisor capacity. In 2023, ANU awarded about 420 Honours degrees, roughly 15% of that year's undergraduate graduates (source: ANU Planning & Performance Measurement, 2024 Annual Report). With competition for Australian Research Training Program (RTP) funding intensifying, understanding the application timeline, thesis proposal conventions and supervisor allocation mechanisms is directly relevant for students planning a research degree.

## Eligibility and Grade Thresholds

**Eligibility for an Honours place** is based primarily on undergraduate academic performance and course structure. ANU requires applicants to have completed at least 96 units in the relevant discipline area (equivalent to 3 years of full-time study) with a weighted average mark (WAM) of no less than 70 (corresponding to GPA 5.0). For some highly competitive disciplines, such as Economics (Honours) and Physics (Honours), the effective admission cutoff is usually above 75 (GPA 5.5).

### Prerequisite Course Requirements

Beyond the overall score requirement, applicants must have completed specific prerequisite courses during their undergraduate degree. At the **ANU College of Science**, for example, the 2024 *Science Honours Handbook* explicitly states that applicants to Chemistry Honours must have completed the two core courses CHEM2201 and CHEM2202 with a grade of at least 65 (Credit) in each. In the social sciences, such as Politics Honours, at least one research methods course (e.g., POLS2001) is required with a grade of at least 70.

### Additional Conditions for International Students

International students must also satisfy English language requirements: an overall IELTS score of 6.5 (with no band below 6.0), or an equivalent TOEFL/PTE score. Some colleges (such as the College of Law) may require IELTS 7.0. According to the Australian Department of Education's 2023 *International Student Data*, about 22% of ANU international students choose to apply for an Honours year, with an average acceptance rate of around 65% — lower than the 78% for domestic students.

## Application Timeline and Process

**Honours applications** are usually split into two stages: an Expression of Interest (EOI) and the formal application. ANU uses a single online Application Portal; the 2024 application window runs from 1 August to 31 October (for intake in Semester 1 of the following year). Some colleges offer mid-year intake (Semester 2), with a deadline of 30 April.

### Key Dates

- **1 August**: the application portal opens; students can submit an EOI and upload preliminary materials.
- **15 September**: colleges release the first round of supervisor lists and available projects (Supervisor List & Available Projects).
- **31 October**: formal applications close; a complete thesis proposal must be submitted.
- **Mid-December**: admission outcomes are released, including supervisor allocation notices.

### Document Checklist

Applications must include the following documents: an official academic transcript, a personal statement (no more than 500 words), two academic reference letters (written by undergraduate lecturers or research supervisors), and a draft research proposal (Research Proposal Draft, usually 1,500–2,000 words). According to the ANU Graduate Research Office's 2024 *Application Checklist*, incomplete applications are automatically deferred to the next round rather than rejected outright.

## Thesis Proposal Guidelines

The **thesis proposal** carries the most weight in the Honours assessment, accounting for roughly 40% of the admission score. ANU colleges impose uniform formatting requirements, but the specific content varies by discipline. The proposal must include the following core elements: a research question, a literature review, a methodology, an expected timeline and references.

### Research Question and Literature Review

The research question must be clear and workable, and aligned with the supervisor's research interests. The ANU College of Science's 2024 *Research Proposal Guidelines* recommend that the literature review cite at least 15 peer-reviewed journal articles, with articles published in the last 5 years making up no less than 60%. Social science proposals, by contrast, emphasise clarity of the theoretical framework — a Politics Honours proposal, for example, must explicitly engage at least 3 major theoretical traditions.

### Methodology and Feasibility

The methodology section must spell out the data sources, sample size and analytical techniques. At the **ANU College of Engineering and Computer Science**, for example, more than 80% of successful 2023 proposals included a concrete experimental design or a link to a code repository (such as a GitHub repo). The feasibility assessment should include a time budget: the Honours year is typically 48 weeks, with no more than 20 weeks for data collection and at least 12 weeks reserved for thesis writing.

## Supervisor Allocation

**Supervisor allocation** is the heart of the ANU Honours application and is run by each college's Honours Coordinator. The process follows a 'mutual selection' principle: students list up to 3 preferred supervisors (in priority order) in their application, and supervisors decide whether to take them on based on their own supervisory capacity and how well the project matches their interests.

### Supervisor Capacity and Matching

Each supervisor typically takes on at most 2–3 Honours students per year (source: ANU College of Science, 2024 *Supervisor Capacity Policy*). In 2023, about 65% of applicants university-wide were matched to their first-choice supervisor, 25% to their second or third choice, and the remaining 10% were allocated by the Honours Coordinator to supervisors with spare capacity. Of the students allocated this way, about 40% ended up changing their research direction.

### Cross-College Supervision

Some projects cut across disciplines and are eligible for co-supervision. Applicants must clearly name a primary supervisor and a secondary supervisor in the proposal, and the two must come from different colleges or research centres. Under the ANU 2024 *Joint Supervision Guidelines*, Honours students with cross-college supervision receive an extra 500 Australian dollars in research funding to cover inter-campus travel or lab material costs.

## Assessment Criteria and Admission Decisions

**Honours admission assessment** is handled by each college's Honours Committee using a 100-point scoring system. The scoring dimensions are: academic performance (30%), thesis proposal quality (40%), reference letters (20%) and interview performance (10%). Some colleges (such as the ANU College of Business and Economics) require all shortlisted candidates to attend a 15-minute interview focused on research motivation and the feasibility of the proposal.

### Score Weights and Thresholds

Academic performance is converted from the weighted average mark: 70–74 earns a base score (60 points), 75–79 a good score (75 points), and 80 and above an excellent score (90 points). The thesis proposal is scored independently by two assessors and the average is taken; if the two scores differ by more than 15 points, a third assessor is brought in. The final admission cutoff is adjusted dynamically by each college based on that year's applicant numbers — in 2023 the university-wide average cutoff was 72 (out of 100), with 75 for the College of Science and 68 for the College of Arts and Social Sciences.

### Waitlist and Deferral

Applicants who miss the direct admission cutoff can be placed on a waitlist. The waitlist stays valid until 4 weeks before enrolment; if an admitted student gives up their place, waitlisted applicants are offered it in score order. Under the ANU 2024 *Admissions Policy*, about 15% of Honours places are ultimately filled from the waitlist. Applicants can also request deferral to the following academic year by submitting a deferral application with reasons (such as insufficient research preparation or a supervisor time conflict); the approval rate is about 60%.

## Special Considerations for International Students

**International students applying for Honours** must also factor in visa and financial requirements. Under Australian immigration rules, the Honours year falls under the 'higher education' sector of the student visa (Subclass 500), and the visa period usually covers the entire Honours year (12 months). After admission, applicants must provide evidence of funds covering at least the tuition fees (about 45,000 Australian dollars for the 2024 Honours year) plus living costs (about 21,000 Australian dollars per year) (source: Australian Department of Home Affairs, 2024 *Student Visa Financial Capacity*).

### Scholarships and Funding

ANU offers a limited number of scholarships for international Honours students, including the ANU Honours Scholarship (10,000 Australian dollars per year) and college-specific awards such as the College of Asia & the Pacific Honours Award (5,000 Australian dollars per year). In 2023, the competition ratio for international student scholarships was about 8:1 (source: ANU Scholarships Office, 2024 *Annual Report*). Applicants must submit their scholarship application at the same time as their Honours application — the deadlines are identical.

### Pathways After Graduation

After completing the Honours degree, international students can apply for the Temporary Graduate visa (Subclass 485), valid for 2–4 years depending on qualification level and field of study. According to the Department of Education's 2023 *Graduate Outcomes Survey*, the medium-term employment rate (3 years after graduation) for Honours graduates is 92%, above the 86% for bachelor's graduates.

## FAQ

### Q1: How is an Honours degree different from a standard bachelor's degree in course structure?

An Honours degree adds a research-intensive year on top of the standard bachelor's degree, usually comprising one advanced research methods course (6 units) and a research thesis (24 units, roughly 15,000–20,000 words). A standard bachelor's degree normally does not require an independent research project. ANU rules set the total load for the Honours year at 48 units (1 year), versus 144 units (3 years) for a standard bachelor's degree.

### Q2: Can I still apply for Honours if my undergraduate weighted average is below 70?

Applicants below 70 are usually not eligible to apply directly, but they can consider the Honours Preparation Year — taking an extra 6–12 months of relevant courses to reach 70 before applying. In 2023, about 8% of ANU Honours students entered through this pathway. In addition, some colleges allow an outstanding thesis proposal (scored at 80 or above) to waive the grade threshold, but this requires special approval from the Honours Committee.

### Q3: Can I apply directly for a PhD after completing the Honours year?

Yes — an Honours degree (at least an IIA class, i.e. above 75) normally satisfies the minimum entry requirements for Australian PhD programs. Under the ANU 2024 *PhD Admission Policy*, an IIA Honours degree qualifies for direct PhD entry without additional master's coursework. In 2023, about 35% of new ANU PhD students held an ANU Honours degree.
