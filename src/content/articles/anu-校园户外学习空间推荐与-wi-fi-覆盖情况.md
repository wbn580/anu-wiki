The main campus of the Australian National University (ANU) covers around 145 hectares and has more than 20 designated outdoor study spaces, with campus Wi-Fi covering over 95% of outdoor teaching areas. According to the Campus Network Coverage Report published by ANU Information Technology Services in 2023, more than 1,200 Wi-Fi access points are deployed across the university, and average signal strength in outdoor areas stays above -65 dBm — enough to support video conferencing and online classes. In parallel, a 2022 review by the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA) noted that outdoor study spaces have become an important complement to students' informal learning, and ANU's provision in this area ranks among the best of the Group of Eight (Go8). For students seeking flexible study environments in Canberra's changeable climate, this data-driven facilities planning offers a quantifiable basis for choosing where to work.

## Outdoor Study Space Distribution and Types

ANU's outdoor study spaces sit mainly along both sides of the campus central axis, including along University Avenue on the **Acton campus**, beside Sullivans Creek and in the various college courtyards. According to the Campus Space Usage Guide produced by ANU Facilities and Services in 2024, these spaces fall into three types: **covered open pavilions** (such as the pavilion south of Chifley Library), **open lawns and stepped seating** (such as the Union Court lawn area), and **semi-enclosed study pods with power outlets** (such as the outdoor study pods in the Kambri precinct). Every type has at least 2 Wi-Fi access points to ensure network continuity.

### Overview of Key Outdoor Study Areas
- **Kambri cultural precinct**: 6 semi-enclosed study pods, each with 4 USB charging ports and a Wi-Fi coverage radius of 30 metres.
- **Sullivans Creek riverside walkway**: 8 benches and 3 groups of picnic tables; measured average signal strength of -60 dBm, suitable for small-group discussion.
- **Lawn east of Hancock Library**: about 500 square metres, with 2 high-power outdoor access points supporting 50 simultaneous device connections.

### Seasonal Usage Advice
Canberra winters (June–August) average a minimum temperature of 1 °C, while summer highs (December–February) can reach 35 °C. ANU Facilities and Services recommends choosing covered seats near building walls (such as south of Chifley Library) in winter to make use of radiant building heat; in summer, the shaded areas along Sullivans Creek are recommended — the tree canopy there covers about 40%, making it 2–3 °C cooler than open lawns.

## Wi-Fi Coverage Technology and Performance Metrics

The ANU campus Wi-Fi network uses a dual-SSID architecture with **eduroam** and **ANU-Secure**, managed centrally by Aruba wireless controllers. According to the Annual Network Performance Report from ANU's Digital Campus Office (2023), 284 outdoor access points are deployed across outdoor areas, supporting 802.11ac Wave 2 and 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6). Field measurements show that in high-traffic areas such as Kambri Square, a single access point can carry 128 concurrent connections, with average download speeds of 150 Mbps and upload speeds of 80 Mbps.

### Signal Strength Heat Map
ANU Information Technology Services publishes a signal coverage heat map every quarter. Data from Q1 2024 shows the **outdoor study spaces** with the most stable signals are:
- **University House courtyard**: signal -55 dBm, latency < 10 ms
- **Fellows Oval lawn, west side**: signal -62 dBm, but signal drops to -75 dBm in parts of the north side due to tree cover
- **Poplar Grove avenue**: signal -58 dBm, but bandwidth is limited because 3 access points share the same backhaul link

### Network Congestion Periods
According to ANU ITS traffic statistics, **weekdays 11:00–14:00** (lunchtime) are the peak period for outdoor Wi-Fi use, when uplink bandwidth utilisation in the Kambri area can reach 85%. Students who need high-bandwidth tasks (such as video uploads) should avoid these hours or use a wired connection (such as the RJ45 ports provided in the Kambri study pods).

## Power and Supporting Infrastructure

Whether an outdoor study space can actually be used effectively depends largely on **power access** and the availability of **shade and rain protection**. ANU Facilities and Services' 2024 audit report shows that 68% of outdoor seating areas on campus are within 15 metres of a building power outlet, but only 22% of seats have outdoor weatherproof power boxes directly available. The breakdown is as follows:

### Outdoor Seating with Power
- **Kambri study pods**: all 6 pods have 4 × 240V outlets and 4 USB-A ports, supporting fast charging of up to 65W
- **Pavilion south of Chifley Library**: 4 of the 8 seats can connect to weatherproof outlets on the building wall
- **North side of the Marie Reay building**: the stepped seating area has only 2 shared outlets — bring your own extension lead

### Shade and Rain Protection
In 2023 ANU completed an upgrade of the **Union Court area**, adding 3 new retractable canvas canopies covering around 200 square metres. In addition, the 3 wooden pavilions beside Sullivans Creek were treated with waterproof coating in early 2024, able to withstand Canberra's average annual rainfall of 616 mm. For uncovered areas, students can plan ahead using the real-time weather radar map published on the ANU website (data provided by the Australian Bureau of Meteorology).

## Quiet Study and Group Collaboration Zones

ANU divides its outdoor study spaces by acoustic environment into **quiet zones** and **collaboration zones** — a classification proposed and implemented by the Student Representative Council in 2022. According to a 2023 survey by the ANU Student Experience Office, 72% of students surveyed said clear zoning helps improve study efficiency. The zoning rules are as follows:

### Quiet Study Zones (noise < 50 dB)
- **Lawn east of Hancock Library**: amplified sound is prohibited; noise-cancelling headphones recommended
- **University House courtyard**: individual reading and writing only; group discussion prohibited
- **Poplar Grove avenue**: "quiet hours" signage (weekdays 9:00–17:00)

### Collaboration Zones (noise 50–70 dB permitted)
- **Kambri Square**: 4 whiteboards and 2 sets of movable tables and chairs, supporting group projects
- **Sullivans Creek picnic tables**: conversation at normal volume allowed, but playing music is prohibited
- **Fellows Oval lawn**: outdoor classes or society activities permitted, but must be booked in advance through the ANU space booking system

### Zone Violation Handling
ANU Security Services patrols 3 times a week and issues reminders in areas exceeding noise limits. In 2023 a total of 47 verbal warnings were issued, 80% of them concentrated at Kambri Square. Students can report noise issues in real time through the ANU SafeZone app.

## Accessibility and Special Needs

ANU is committed to ensuring all outdoor study spaces meet the accessibility requirements of the **Disability Discrimination Act (DDA)** and the **Building Code of Australia (BCA)**. According to the Accessibility Audit Report by ANU's Equity and Inclusion Office (2024), 92% of outdoor study areas on campus are reachable by wheelchair, but 8% of areas (such as some lawn ramps) still have gradients steeper than 1:14.

### Accessibility Facilities List
- **Kambri study pods**: 900 mm entrance width, 1,500 mm internal turning radius, low-level power outlets
- **Pavilion south of Chifley Library**: non-slip concrete floor; wheelchairs can reach 4 of the seats directly
- **Sullivans Creek riverside walkway**: some sections are only 1.2 metres wide; wheelchairs must detour to the main path on the north side

### Sensory-Friendly Spaces
In 2023 ANU set up its first sensory-friendly study area in **Poplar Grove**. The area avoids highly reflective metal surfaces and has low-allergen plantings (such as lavender and rosemary). Wi-Fi signal strength there is optimised to stay at -60 dBm, and no amplified broadcast equipment is installed.

## Weather and Microclimate Effects

Canberra's climate directly affects how usable the outdoor study spaces are. Based on the **Bureau of Meteorology (BOM)** 1991–2020 climate normals, Canberra averages 2,807 hours of sunshine a year, but spring (September–November) average wind speeds reach 17 km/h, with a 12% chance of afternoon thunderstorms. Data from ANU's campus microclimate monitoring project (launched 2023) shows that perceived temperature can differ by as much as 5 °C between different areas.

### Wind Environment Assessment
- **Fellows Oval lawn**: open and exposed; winter wind speeds are 30% higher than in surrounding areas, feeling 2–3 °C colder
- **Sullivans Creek riverside**: buffered by riparian vegetation, wind speeds reduced by 40%; a good choice on windy days
- **Kambri Square**: buildings create a wind tunnel effect; afternoon winds can reach 25 km/h, so it is best avoided between 14:00–16:00

### UV Protection
The ANU Health and Safety Office recommends SPF 30+ sunscreen when studying outdoors for more than 30 minutes. The canopies in the **Union Court area** block 95% of UVB radiation, while tree canopy over the lawns provides only about 60% coverage. Students can check the real-time UV index via the ANU weather app (based on BOM data).

## Booking and Usage Rules

Most ANU outdoor study spaces operate on a **first-come, first-served** basis, but some high-demand areas must be booked in advance through the ANU space booking system (SpaceBook). According to ANU Student Services usage data for 2024, the Kambri study pods average 89% occupancy on weekdays, dropping to 45% on weekends.

### Bookable Spaces
- **Kambri study pods**: max 2 hours per booking, bookable up to 7 days ahead, limited to 1 booking per person per day
- **Fellows Oval lawn**: bookings only for society activities; apply 14 days in advance and pay a $50 deposit
- **Sullivans Creek picnic tables**: no booking needed, but arrive early during peak hours (11:30–13:30)

### Code of Conduct
- Smoking and vaping are prohibited in outdoor study spaces (ANU is a smoke-free campus)
- Food scraps must be cleaned up by users; violators face a $200 fine (under ANU Ordinance 12)
- Using amplified sound equipment requires written permission from the ANU Events Management Office

## FAQ

### Q1: Does the outdoor Wi-Fi on the ANU campus support Zoom video conferencing?
Yes. According to ANU ITS field tests in 2023, in the main outdoor study areas such as Kambri Square and the University House courtyard, Zoom 720p video calls have a packet loss rate below 0.5% and an average latency of 12 ms. However, in the weaker-signal area west of the Fellows Oval lawn, using mobile data as a backup is recommended.

### Q2: Do the outdoor study spaces have fixed opening hours?
All outdoor study spaces are open 24 hours, but the study pods with power (such as those in the Kambri area) switch off part of their lighting between 22:00–06:00. ANU Security Services data shows that in 2023, usage of outdoor study areas at night (18:00–06:00) was only 12% of daytime levels.

### Q3: How can I check the current Wi-Fi signal strength in an outdoor study area?
Students can view the real-time signal heat map through the "campus map" feature of the official ANU App. The data updates every 15 minutes and covers all outdoor areas with deployed access points. March 2024 data shows user-reported accuracy averaging 94%.
