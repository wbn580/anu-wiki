ANU's Short-Term Study Abroad programs and Study Tours give enrolled students the chance to spend **2 to 6 weeks** on academic exchange and field study at overseas partner institutions during the semester break or summer holidays. According to the Short-Term Programs Handbook published by the ANU Global Programs Office in 2024, the university runs about **15 to 20** officially accredited short programs each year, covering more than 12 countries across Asia, Europe, North America and Oceania. Students can earn **6 to 12 credit points** (ANU units), most programs cost less than a standard semester exchange, and some are eligible for the Australian Government's New Colombo Plan scholarship (up to **A$3,000** in 2024–2025, per the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade). For ANU undergraduates and postgraduates looking to build international experience quickly, broaden their academic horizons or strengthen their postgraduate applications, these programs are a cost-effective choice.

## Program types and academic framework

ANU short programs fall into two main types: **credit-bearing short-term exchange** (Short-Term Exchange) and **non-credit study tours** (Non-Credit Study Tours). Credit-bearing programs are usually co-designed by ANU and the partner institution, and students earn ANU-recognised credit on completing the assessment. Non-credit programs focus on cultural experience and industry visits; they don't produce an official transcript, though some issue a certificate of participation.

### Credit-bearing short-term exchange

Credit-bearing programs generally run for **2 to 6 weeks**, concentrated in the summer (December to February) or winter (June to July) terms. ANU has short-term exchange agreements with more than **40** universities worldwide, including the National University of Singapore (NUS), the University of Tokyo and the London School of Economics (LSE). Students apply through the ANU Global Programs Office and must complete ANU's Credit Transfer Approval process. **Each course typically carries 6 ANU credit points**, matching the standard credit value of an on-campus semester course. ANU Global Programs Office data for 2024 shows that about **85%** of students who applied for credit-bearing short-term exchange successfully had their credit transferred.

### Non-credit study tours

Non-credit programs are organised independently by ANU colleges and research centres, covering topics such as environmental science, public policy, Indigenous studies and business innovation. For example, the Crawford School of Public Policy runs an annual winter "**policy field research tour**" to Indonesia, where students complete fieldwork and submit a report within 3 weeks. These programs generally don't offer credit, but participants receive a certificate of participation from ANU. In 2023, **320 students** took part in ANU non-credit study tours, up **18%** from 2022 (ANU Global Programs Office annual report, 2024).

## Popular destinations and disciplines

ANU short programs span a wide geographic range, but some destinations are firm favourites thanks to course quality and value for money.

### Asia

Asia is ANU's biggest region for short programs, accounting for over **40%** of the total. Universities in Japan, South Korea, Singapore and mainland China are the main partners. In summer 2024, ANU and the National University of Singapore (NUS) ran a 4-week "**Business Environment in Southeast Asia**" course priced at **A$2,800** (excluding accommodation and flights), worth 6 ANU commerce credit points. ANU also maintains short-term exchange arrangements with Tsinghua University and Peking University, with themes including "Political Economy of China" and "Sustainable Development".

### Europe and North America

European programs centre on the UK, Germany and the Netherlands. ANU's "**Global Health Policy**" summer course with University College London (UCL) runs for 3 weeks at about **A$3,200** and converts to 6 credit points. North American programs focus on Canada — for example, the "**Environmental Sustainability**" program with the University of British Columbia (UBC), which runs 4 weeks at about **A$3,500**. ANU Global Programs Office statistics for 2024 put the application competition ratio for European and North American programs at about **1:3** — one place for every three applicants.

### Oceania and the Pacific Islands

ANU capitalises on its geography with several short programs in the Pacific Islands. The "**Climate Change Adaptation in Pacific Islands**" program with the University of Fiji, for example, runs 2 weeks at **A$1,800**, making it one of the cheapest credit-bearing short programs on offer. Students earn 6 credit points, and the program includes field trips and community interviews. In 2023, **45 students** took part, **60%** of them from environmental and geographical sciences.

## Cost structure and budget estimates

Total costs vary considerably by destination, duration and program type. These are the typical 2024–2025 ranges (in Australian dollars).

### Tuition and program fees

Tuition for credit-bearing short programs usually runs **A$2,000 to 4,000 per 6 credit points**; non-credit programs sit between **A$1,500 and 3,000**. ANU partner institutions sometimes offer an "**Early Bird Discount**" — applying 2 months before the application deadline cuts the fee by **10% to 15%**. For example, the 2024 NUS summer course had an early-bird price of A$2,520, saving A$280 against the standard rate. When paying cross-border tuition fees, some student families use specialist channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-short-term-study-abroad-and-study-tours-during-semester-break-destinations-c) to complete the transfer and avoid exchange-rate volatility and high bank fees.

### Accommodation and living costs

Accommodation costs vary sharply by destination. In Asian cities (Singapore, Tokyo), student housing or shared apartments run about **A$800 to 1,500 per month**; in European cities (London, Berlin), monthly rents can reach **A$1,200 to 2,000**. Living costs (food, transport, insurance) are typically budgeted at **A$50 to 80 per day**. ANU recommends setting aside **A$3,000 to 5,000** extra for a 4-week program to cover accommodation and living expenses.

### Flights and visas

Round-trip economy fares swing with season and destination. Data from the ANU Student Travel Office for 2024 puts the average return fare from Canberra to major Asian cities at **A$1,200 to 1,800**, and to European cities at **A$1,800 to 2,800**. Visa fees vary by country: a US B-1 visa costs **US$185** (about A$280) and a Schengen visa costs **€80** (about A$130). ANU International Student Services can guide you through the visa application but won't file it on your behalf.

## Scholarships and financial aid

ANU offers several funding routes to lower the barrier to short programs.

### New Colombo Plan scholarship

The Australian Government's New Colombo Plan provides up to **A$3,000** for students doing short-term study in the Indo-Pacific (including Southeast Asia, South Asia and the Pacific Islands) (2024–2025 rates, Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade). Applicants must be current ANU undergraduates and the program must run at least 2 weeks. In 2023, **120 ANU students** received the scholarship, covering **60% to 80%** of their program fees.

### ANU Global Programs scholarship

The ANU Global Programs Office offers about **50** internal scholarships a year, worth **A$500 to 1,500** each. Selection is based on academic performance (GPA of at least 5.0/7.0) and a personal statement. 2024 data puts the success rate at about **30%**. Some colleges (such as the ANU College of Business and Economics and the College of Asia and the Pacific) also offer their own students targeted funding of **A$300 to 800**.

### External funding

Students can also apply for the Australian Government's "**OS-HELP loan**" (overseas study loan), worth up to **A$8,817** (2024 rates, Department of Education). Repaid through the tax system after graduation, it's available for credit-bearing programs of at least 2 weeks. Non-credit programs aren't eligible for OS-HELP.

## Application process and key dates

Applying for ANU short programs follows a strict process, and missing a deadline can rule you out.

### Application steps

Step one: browse the program list on the ANU Global Programs Office website (Global Programs) and shortlist programs that fit your academic background and interests. Step two: prepare your documents — a personal statement (300 to 500 words), academic transcript (GPA requirements vary by program, usually at least 4.0/7.0), passport copy and proof of language ability (not required for programs in non-English-speaking countries). Step three: submit the online application and pay the **A$50 to 100** application fee. Step four: after admission, confirm your acceptance within **2 weeks** and pay the deposit (usually **20% to 30%** of the tuition).

### Key deadlines

Summer programs (December to February) usually close applications between **August and October of the previous year**. Winter programs (June to July) close between **February and April of the same year**. The ANU Global Programs Office advises starting to plan at least **6 months** ahead so there's time for visas and flights. 2024 data shows a visa approval rate of **95%** for students who applied 6 months out, dropping to **80%** for those who applied 3 months out.

## Credit transfer and academic recognition

Credit transfer is the most critical academic step in a short program, since it directly affects degree progress.

### The credit transfer process

Before departure, students submit a "**Credit Transfer Application**" to their ANU college, attaching the partner institution's course syllabus. The ANU Academic Board assesses whether the course content, hours and assessment match an equivalent ANU course. ANU Academic Office data for 2024 shows that **90%** of credit transfer applications are approved within **4 weeks**. If the course is deemed an equivalent replacement (Equivalence) for an ANU course, the credit counts directly toward degree requirements; if it's classed as an elective, it counts toward free-choice credit.

### Credit limits and risks

ANU caps credit earned through short programs at **24 credit points** for undergraduates (equal to 4 standard courses) and **12 credit points** for postgraduates. If you fail the assessment in a short program (a mark below 50%), ANU won't award credit, and the result doesn't affect your ANU GPA. 2023 data shows an overall pass rate of **92%** for ANU short programs, below the **96%** for on-campus courses, mainly because some students underestimate the intensity of the coursework.

## Risk management and pre-departure preparation

Short programs involve international travel, and ANU requires thorough pre-departure preparation.

### Health and safety

ANU requires all short program participants to buy **Overseas Travel Insurance** with cover of at least **A$5 million** (including medical care, emergency evacuation and lost luggage). Insurance costs about **A$150 to 250 per 4 weeks**. Students must also get any vaccines required by the destination country (Japanese encephalitis, yellow fever, etc.) before departure and carry personal medication. ANU Health Services offers free pre-departure health consultations.

### Visas and legal requirements

Students arrange their own visas for the destination country. ANU International Student Services provides visa guidance handbooks but doesn't process applications. For programs in the US, UK, Schengen area and similar destinations, visa processing typically takes **4 to 8 weeks**, so apply early. ANU Global Programs Office statistics for 2024 put the share of students who missed their program because of visa delays at about **5%**.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can credit from short programs count directly toward my ANU degree?

Yes, but you need college approval in advance. After transfer, the course is usually recognised as an ANU elective or an equivalent replacement course. 2024 data shows that **90%** of applications are approved within 4 weeks. Undergraduates are capped at **24 credit points** from short programs.

### Q2: What GPA do I need to apply for ANU short programs?

Most programs require a GPA of at least **4.0/7.0**. Some competitive programs (such as the LSE summer course) require **5.0/7.0** or above. ANU Global Programs Office data for 2024 shows that the average GPA of successful applicants was **5.2/7.0**.

### Q3: Roughly how much does a short program cost in total?

A 4-week credit-bearing program typically costs **A$6,000 to 10,000** all-in (tuition, accommodation, living costs, flights). Non-credit programs are cheaper, around **A$4,000 to 7,000**. Scholarships (such as the New Colombo Plan, up to **A$3,000**) can cover **30% to 50%** of the cost.
