The **Multi-Faith Centre**, officially opened by the Australian National University (ANU) in 2015, stands on Kingsley Street in the Acton campus at the centre of the university. With a floor area of about 400 square metres, it is the university's core facility for supporting an international student body of more than 30% (ANU 2023 annual report data) and the local multi-faith community. According to the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) 2021 census, more than 28% of Canberra residents report no religious affiliation, while Christianity (about 46%), Islam (about 3.5%), Buddhism (about 2.8%) and Hinduism (about 2.2%) are the main faith groups. The centre provides **dedicated prayer rooms, retreat spaces and chaplaincy services**, and coordinates religious holiday arrangements, aiming to create an inclusive spiritual support environment for about 26,000 enrolled students (ANU 2024 student statistics).

## Multi-Faith Centre facilities and prayer room layout

### Main prayer room and gender separation

The centre's main prayer room holds about 60 people and features **movable partitions** to support collective worship for different faiths including Islam, Christianity and Buddhism. The space has a **Qibla direction indicator**, with separate male and female prayer areas in line with Islamic requirements. The floor is carpeted, and lockers are available for storing personal belongings (bring your own lock). The centre is open daily from 7:00 to 22:00, with possible changes on public holidays — check the ANU Estate Management website for details.

### Auxiliary spaces and facilities

The centre includes a **Meditation Room** of about 30 square metres with cushions and low-level lighting, suitable for personal meditation or small group activities (maximum 8 people). An **Ablution Room** next to the prayer room is equipped with hot and cold water taps and a floor drain, meeting Islamic purification needs. The centre also provides **accessible access**, including wheelchair-accessible prayer areas and toilets, ensuring convenience for students with disabilities.

### Other campus prayer points

Beyond the Multi-Faith Centre, ANU maintains **small prayer rooms** in several college buildings, such as the **Coombs Building (College of Arts and Social Sciences)** and the **Peter Baume Building (College of Law)**, all equipped with prayer mats and direction indicators. These spaces are usually not staffed, so students coordinate usage times among themselves. The campus map (ANU Interactive Map) marks all prayer points; new students are advised to check opening status in person during their first week.

## Chaplaincy services

### Resident chaplain team

The ANU chaplaincy team consists of **6 resident chaplains** representing Christian (Anglican, Catholic, Uniting Church), Islamic, Buddhist and Jewish traditions. Each chaplain offers at least **4 hours of one-on-one consultation** per week, usually in the chaplaincy office at the Multi-Faith Centre. Students can drop in during designated times without an appointment or submit an online booking request through the ANU Chaplaincy website. Services cover **spiritual guidance, crisis intervention and grief support**, and chaplains help students with faith-related academic arrangements (such as exam time adjustments).

### Interfaith dialogue programs

Each semester the centre runs **interfaith dialogue events**, such as the "Faith Table" series, inviting students from different backgrounds to share their religious practices. In 2023, 12 events were held with more than 300 student attendances (ANU Chaplaincy 2023 Activity Report). These events aim to foster **understanding between religions** and reduce misunderstandings arising from cultural differences. Notices are published via ANU student email and campus posters, and events are usually free.

### Emergency spiritual support

During **mental health crises** or major events (such as natural disasters or family emergencies), the chaplaincy team provides a 24-hour emergency phone line (+61 2 6125 2211, routed to the on-duty chaplain). The service operates in coordination with ANU Health & Wellbeing, ensuring students receive initial spiritual support before seeking professional psychological counselling. According to the ANU 2023 student wellbeing survey, about 12% of respondents had used chaplaincy services, and 85% of them said they found it "helpful".

## Religious holiday observances and academic arrangements

### Official religious calendar

ANU publishes a **Religious Observance Calendar** each year listing about 20 major religious festivals, including Eid al-Fitr (Islam), Easter (Christianity), Yom Kippur (Judaism) and Diwali (Hinduism). The calendar is developed by the ANU Office of Multicultural Affairs in consultation with religious groups and is integrated into the ANU academic calendar system. Students can apply for **exam time adjustments** or **assignment extensions** by submitting a request through the ANU Access & Inclusion platform at least 14 days before the festival, with supporting evidence (such as a letter from a religious leader).

### Facility adjustments during festivals

During major religious festivals the Multi-Faith Centre extends its opening hours. For example, during Ramadan 2024 the centre opened from 4:00 am to 10:30 pm to support students performing Fajr (dawn prayer) and Iftar (breaking the fast). The centre also provides **simple meals and drinking water**, organised by ANU Muslim Students' Association (MSA) volunteers. During **Christmas and Easter**, special masses are held at the centre, led by resident chaplains with community members invited to join.

### Academic exemption process

Under the ANU **Academic Policy and Procedures Handbook** (2024 edition), students missing classes or exams due to religious festivals must complete a "Religious Observance Form" and attach proof of the festival dates. The application is reviewed by the course coordinator and usually results in approval for a **make-up exam or alternative assignment**. If the coordinator refuses, students can appeal to the college's Associate Dean (Student Affairs). ANU explicitly prohibits discrimination against students on religious grounds; violations may lead to disciplinary action.

## Interfaith community and support networks

### Student religious societies

ANU recognises more than 15 **student religious societies**, including the ANU Muslim Students' Association (MSA), the ANU Christian Fellowship (CF) and the ANU Buddhist Society. These societies run regular activities each semester, such as **Quran study circles, Bible study groups and meditation workshops**. Activities are usually held at the Multi-Faith Centre or in college classrooms; some require a small membership fee (usually AUD 5-20 per semester). The society list is available on the ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) website.

### Multicultural dining

The **Kambri precinct dining hall** next to the Multi-Faith Centre offers options meeting various religious dietary requirements, including **Halal-certified food** (supplied by ANU catering provider Compass Group) and **vegetarian/vegan choices**. Menus are labelled with allergens and religious dietary markers such as "Halal" or "suitable for vegetarians". Students can view daily menus and nutrition information through the ANU Food App.

### Interfaith resource centre

Inside the Multi-Faith Centre is a **small library** holding about 200 religious texts, including the Bible, the Quran, Buddhist scriptures and the Bhagavad Gita, with multilingual versions (Chinese, Arabic, Hindi and more). The space also has **computer terminals** for accessing online religious resources (such as Bible Gateway and Quran.com). Borrowing is free with registration, and the loan period is two weeks.

## Enrolment and navigation advice

### O-Week activities

Each February the ANU Multi-Faith Centre holds an open day during **O-Week** with **guided tours** and a meet-the-chaplains session. New students can learn about prayer room locations, society activities and the religious festival application process. Sessions usually run Monday to Friday from 10:00 am to 3:00 pm with no booking required. International students are advised to bring their **passport or student ID** to register for facility use.

### Cross-border payments and financial support

For religious-background international students who need to pay tuition or living costs, the ANU Finance Office accepts a range of payment methods including bank transfer and credit card. ### Accommodation and faith needs

ANU on-campus residences (such as Bruce Hall and Burgmann College) offer **religion-friendly accommodation options**, including single-gender floors and vegetarian meal plans. Students can note faith needs on their accommodation application, and residence management will do its best to accommodate. For example, **Burgmann College** has a small prayer room and permits personal worship in rooms (subject to noise rules). Accommodation fees vary by room type; in 2024, single rooms cost about AUD 350-450 per week.

## FAQ

### Q1: What are the Multi-Faith Centre's exact opening hours? Is it open on public holidays?

The Multi-Faith Centre is normally open daily from 7:00 to 22:00. On public holidays (such as Christmas and New Year's Day), hours are shortened to 9:00-17:00. 2024 data shows the centre was open 358 days of the year, closing only 7 days (including New Year's Day and Christmas). Students should confirm same-day hours via the ANU Estate Management website or by calling +61 2 6125 2211.

### Q2: How do I apply to reschedule an exam for a religious festival?

Submit a "Religious Observance Form" through the ANU Access & Inclusion platform at least 14 days before the festival, attaching proof of the festival (such as a letter from a religious leader or a screenshot of the official calendar). Under ANU 2023 policy, the approval rate is about 92%; unapproved cases are usually due to late applications. Make-up exams are typically scheduled within two weeks after the festival.

### Q3: Does ANU provide halal food? Which restaurants on campus are halal-certified?

Yes. The Compass Group supplier at the ANU Kambri dining hall provides halal-certified food, including **halal burgers, grilled meats and curries**. In addition, "The Bowery" restaurant at **Union Court** and **Fellows Café** also offer halal options. All halal food carries a "Halal" label; in 2024 there were 6 halal-certified dining outlets on campus.
