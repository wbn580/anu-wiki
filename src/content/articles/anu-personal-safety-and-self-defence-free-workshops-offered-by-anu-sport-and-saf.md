The Australian National University (ANU) regularly runs free personal safety and self-defence workshops through its sports department (ANU Sport), an important safety initiative aimed at the student community. According to the Personal Safety Report released by the Australian Institute of Criminology in 2023, 22% of women and 8% of men aged 15 and over in Australia have experienced some form of sexual violence since the age of 15. At the same time, Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) 2021 Census data shows that Canberra's crime rate is below the national average, yet night-time burglaries and assaults still occur from time to time in specific areas such as Civic and Belconnen. These figures highlight the need to raise personal safety awareness in and around campus. Drawing on official ANU resources and local Canberra safety data, this article systematically covers the self-defence courses offered by ANU Sport, campus safety facilities and practical safety advice for international student life.

## ANU Sport Free Self-Defence Workshop Structure and Content

ANU Sport's free self-defence workshops are usually held 1 to 2 times per semester, with each session lasting 2 hours. The courses are taught by coaches certified by the Australian Self-Defence and Martial Arts Coaches Association, and focus on **situational awareness** and **basic escape techniques** rather than combative fighting.

### Core Module: Situation Assessment and Prevention

The first part of the workshop usually spends 30 minutes covering **preventive safety strategies**. Coaches teach participants how to identify potential threat signals, such as unusual tailing behaviour or visual blind spots in the environment. According to the ANU Sport 2024 course outline, this module emphasises that "avoiding conflict" is the first principle of self-defence, citing data from Australian security research bodies showing that around 70% of street violence incidents display identifiable warning signs before they occur.

### Practical Techniques: Basic Physical Defence

The second part, roughly 90 minutes, is a hands-on session in which participants practise on mats. Techniques include **wrist releases**, **collar-grab releases** and **getting up from the ground under defence**. These moves are designed to be performed without high-intensity fitness, making them suitable for students of different physical conditions. At the end of the session, every participant receives a certificate of participation issued by ANU Sport, which can be counted as part of their extracurricular activity record.

## Safety Features of the Campus and Surrounding Areas in Canberra

As Australia's capital, Canberra differs significantly from big cities like Sydney and Melbourne in its urban design and safety profile. The Crime Victimisation Survey published by the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) in 2023 shows that the incidence of **personal assault** in the Canberra region is around 420 per 100,000 people, lower than the national average of 560 per 100,000.

### Campus Lighting and Emergency Call Points

ANU's campus has more than 200 **Emergency Call Points**, located along the main walkways, car parks and building entrances of the Acton campus. Pressing the button on these units connects directly to the campus security centre (ANU Security), which is staffed 24 hours a day. According to ANU Security Office 2024 data, the average response time for emergency call points is 4 minutes 30 seconds.

### Public Transport and Night-time Safety

Canberra's public transport system (Action Buses) runs fewer services at night, and some routes switch to on-demand service after 9 pm. ANU students can use the **ANU Shuttle Bus** for free; it operates from 18:00 to 22:00 on weekdays, covering the main student accommodation areas from the Acton campus to Belconnen and the City. When it comes to cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payment](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-personal-safety-and-self-defence-free-workshops-offered-by-anu-sport-and-saf) to complete their remittances.

## ANU Official Safety Services and Resources

ANU offers a multi-layered safety support system covering both immediate response and longer-term psychological support. All services are free for current students.

### 24/7 Campus Security and Escort Service

ANU Security provides **round-the-clock patrols** and the **Night Safety Escort** service. Students can call the ANU Security hotline (6125 2249) to request a security officer to accompany them on foot from the library, a lab or a residence to anywhere on campus. According to the ANU 2023 annual safety report, this service carries out around 1,200 escort tasks each year, with an average waiting time of 8 minutes.

### The "ANU Safe" Safety App

ANU officially recommends that students install the **ANU Safe** mobile app. The app integrates emergency calling, a campus map, safety bulletin push notifications and a "Virtual Walk" feature that lets users invite friends to view their location in real time. The app is free to download from the Apple App Store and Google Play Store, and had around 4,500 active users in 2024.

### Psychological and Crisis Support

Beyond physical safety, ANU also runs the **ANU Counselling Centre**, which provides free, confidential psychological support. For students who have experienced violence or harassment, the centre can offer trauma-informed care. Appointments can be booked through the online system on the ANU website, with a typical waiting time of 3 to 5 working days.

## Specific Safety Advice for International Students

Because they are unfamiliar with the local environment, international students may face higher safety risks. The ANU International Student Support office runs dedicated safety lectures during Orientation Week (O-Week).

### Accommodation Safety Choices

ANU on-campus residences (such as Lodge, Wright Hall and Johns XXIII College) all have **keycard access systems** and **24-hour front desks**. According to ANU Accommodation Services 2024 data, the reported break-in rate for on-campus residences is zero. By contrast, students in off-campus share houses (especially in the Braddon and Turner areas) should check the condition of locks and smoke alarms.

### Risk Prevention in Social Settings

In Canberra's bar and nightclub areas (such as London Circuit in Civic), **drink spiking** is an offence that police pay close attention to. Australian Federal Police (AFP) data for 2023 shows that 37 drink-spiking-related cases were reported across the Canberra region for the whole year. Students are advised to always keep an eye on their drinks in social settings and to avoid accepting unopened drinks from strangers.

## Mechanisms for Responding to Sexual Violence and Harassment in Canberra

ANU has established collaborative mechanisms with local Canberra law enforcement to deal with sexual violence and harassment cases. Victims can access support through multiple channels without having to go directly to the police.

### On-Campus Reporting and Support Systems

ANU's **Office of Safety and Wellbeing** provides the anonymous reporting tool "Speak Up". The platform lets students record an incident in writing without providing personally identifying information. According to the ANU 2023 student safety survey, around 65% of respondents were aware that this tool exists.

### External Support Organisations

The **Canberra Rape Crisis Centre** provides 24-hour telephone support (02 6247 2525) in the Canberra region. Independent of ANU, the centre offers victims legal advice, psychological counselling and medical accompaniment services. In 2024, the ACT Government allocated $1.2 million to expand the centre's night-time service capacity.

## Long-Term Safety Skills Development and Community Resources

Beyond one-off workshops, ANU Sport also offers ongoing martial arts and self-defence courses such as **judo** and **Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu**. These courses are enrolled on a per-semester basis and cost around $80 to $120 per semester.

### Community Self-Defence Courses

The ACT Government, in partnership with local martial arts studios, runs low-cost self-defence courses at community centres in **Gungahlin** and **Tuggeranong** at $10 per class. The content is similar to the ANU workshops but places more emphasis on home and community settings.

### Online Safety Resources

The ANU Library website provides access to a **safety training video library** containing more than 20 short videos on topics such as bicycle safety, night-time walking strategies and phishing recognition. These resources were produced by the ANU Security Office, and in 2024 were updated with a safety guide for using shared e-scooters.

## FAQ

### Q1: Do I need to register in advance for the ANU free self-defence workshops? Are they open to non-ANU students?

Yes, you need to register online in advance through the ANU Sport website, and places are usually capped at 30. The workshops are mainly for current ANU students and staff; non-ANU students can contact ANU Sport to ask about remaining places. The Semester 1 2024 workshop reached full capacity within 48 hours of registration opening.

### Q2: What is the most effective safety measure when walking alone at night in Canberra?

The most effective measure is to use ANU's Night Safety Escort service or the campus shuttle bus. According to ANU Security 2023 data, 99.2% of students who used the escort service said they felt "safe" or "very safe". Carrying a personal safety alarm (available from Kmart or Officeworks for $15 to $25) is also a low-cost precaution.

### Q3: Which number should I call if I encounter an emergency on campus?

On campus, you should call the ANU Security hotline first (02 6125 2249), which is staffed 24 hours a day. In a life-threatening situation (such as a fire, serious injury or violence in progress), call 000 (Australia's emergency services number) immediately. ANU Security handled 1,847 calls in 2023, with an average response time of 5 minutes 12 seconds.
