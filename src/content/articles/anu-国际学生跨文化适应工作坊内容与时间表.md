The Australian National University (ANU) has offered its Intercultural Adjustment Workshop to international students since 2000. The program is designed to help a cohort that hails from more than 120 countries and makes up around 42% of the total student body (2023 ANU Annual Report figures) navigate culture shock and the challenges of academic integration. According to the Department of Education's 2024 International Student Experience Survey, roughly 37% of international students in Australia report moderate to severe acculturation difficulties in their first enrolled semester, and ANU's workshop system has been named one of the country's best-practice examples. The workshops run in three concentrated windows each year — during O-Week, in weeks 3–5 of Semester 1, and in weeks 2–4 of Semester 2 — and the 2025 timetable was released by ANU Student Central in November 2024.

## Workshop Design: Background and Theoretical Framework

ANU's Intercultural Adjustment Workshop is built on the **Berry acculturation model** (John W. Berry, 1997) and the **U-curve theory of culture shock** (Oberg, 1960). The model sorts acculturation strategies into four types — integration, assimilation, separation and marginalisation — and the workshop centres on the integration strategy: holding on to one's home-culture identity while actively engaging with mainstream Australian culture. Internal tracking data from ANU Student Services (2022) shows that students who adopt an integration strategy hold an **academic retention rate** 31 percentage points higher than the marginalised group (89% vs 58%).

The workshop content also draws on the **Intercultural Competence Scale for Australia (ICSA)**, a 30-item instrument developed jointly by the ANU School of Psychology and the University of Melbourne that covers three dimensions: cultural knowledge, communication flexibility and stress management. Pre- and post-workshop testing shows participants' average ICSA score climbing from 62.4 at enrolment (out of 100) to 78.1 after week 8 (ANU internal evaluation report, 2023).

## Core Module 1: Recognising and Managing Culture Shock

### Identifying the Four Stages of Culture Shock

The first module focuses on the **typical stages of culture shock**: the honeymoon phase (weeks 1–2), the crisis phase (weeks 3–8), the recovery phase (weeks 9–16) and the adjustment phase (after week 17). Drawing on 2,300 case records from 2019–2023, ANU international student advisers found that students from mainland China spend about 11 days longer in the crisis phase on average than students from Southeast Asia (25 days vs 14 days), a gap that may be linked to the degree of language barrier.

Participants identify which stage they are in through **scenario-based exercises** and log their state weekly using the Cultural Shock Self-Assessment (CSSA) scale. Developed jointly by ANU and the University of Queensland and published in 2023 in the *International Journal of Intercultural Relations*, the scale has a reliability coefficient (Cronbach's α) of 0.87.

### The Stress-Management Toolkit

The workshop offers **four evidence-backed coping strategies**: cognitive reframing, building social support networks, mindful breathing exercises and structured time management. ANU Health Centre data for 2024 shows that students who complete the workshop score on average 4.7 points lower on the **Kessler Psychological Distress Scale (K10)** six months later (down from 24.3 to 19.6), bringing them below the clinical threshold of 20.

## Core Module 2: Adapting to Academic Cultural Differences

### Australian Academic Conventions Compared

This module systematically compares **academic culture differences between ANU and Asian education systems**, covering critical thinking expectations, citation conventions (APA 7th edition), the weighting of group-work marks and expectations around class participation. A 2023 ANU Academic Skills Centre survey found that 67% of international students felt uncomfortable in their first semester with assessment structures where classroom discussion is worth more than 30% of the grade; after the workshop, that figure fell to 31%.

Participants also complete an **Academic Culture Gap Inventory (ACGI)** — 24 items spanning eight domains including classroom behaviour, student–staff interaction, assignment feedback and exam formats. A 2022 study by the ANU School of Literature, Languages and Linguistics found that every 10-point improvement in ACGI score corresponds to an average first-semester GPA gain of 0.32 (on a 4.0 scale).

### Academic Integrity and Citation Practice

A dedicated **Academic Integrity Module**, developed jointly by the ANU Academic Integrity Office and the ANU College of Law, covers the definitions of collusion and self-plagiarism — two categories of misconduct that are less explicitly prohibited in many Asian education systems. A 2023 report by the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA) shows that international students account for 58% of academic integrity breaches at ANU, but 82% of those are non-intentional breaches.

For cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%9B%BD%E9%99%85%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E8%B7%A8%E6%96%87%E5%8C%96%E9%80%82%E5%BA%94%E5%B7%A5%E4%BD%9C%E5%9D%8A%E5%86%85%E5%AE%B9%E4%B8%8E%E6%97%B6%E9%97%B4%E8%A1%A8) to settle their fees.

## Core Module 3: Language and Communication Strategies

### Strengthening Academic English

Module three targets the practical demands of **spoken and written academic English**: strategies for asking questions in class, language for group discussions, email etiquette and logical flow in essays. ANU language school test data for 2024 shows that students who complete the workshop raise their average speaking rate in an academic-oral-fluency test from 98 to 127 words per minute, with error rates down 41%.

Participants practise with the **ANU Academic Corpus** — 12 million words of academic text — focusing on the 200 most frequent academic words (Academic Word List Sublists 1–2). Maintained jointly by the ANU linguistics department and the Australian National Dictionary Centre, the corpus was updated to its 4th edition in 2023.

### Non-Verbal Communication and Cultural Signals

The workshop introduces **Australian norms of non-verbal communication**: eye contact lasting on average 3–5 seconds, personal space of 1.2–1.5 metres, the meaning of gestures (a thumbs-up signals approval) and attitudes to time (arriving within five minutes of the agreed time counts as punctual). A 2022 experiment by the ANU Centre for Cross-Cultural Research found that the proportion of international students who correctly read these signals in mock interviews rose from 43% before the workshop to 79% afterwards.

## Core Module 4: Building Social Networks and Settling into the Community

### A Map of On-Campus Social Resources

The workshop hands out an **ANU Social Resource Map** that plots more than 60 student clubs, 12 cultural associations, 4 religious groups and 3 international student mutual-aid organisations. ANU Student Services data for 2024 shows that workshop participants join an average of 2.3 clubs by the end of semester — 1.1 more than non-participants.

Participants complete a **Social Network Mapping Exercise**, using the Social Support Network Scale (SSNS) to evaluate the density and diversity of their support systems. Developed jointly by the ANU School of Psychology and the University of Sydney and published in 2023 in the *Journal of Applied Psychology*, the scale has a test–retest reliability of 0.82.

### Homestays and Accommodation Culture in Australia

The workshop includes a **Homestay Communication Guide** covering household rules (laundry days, meal times, visitor restrictions), dietary differences and conflict-resolution procedures. A 2023 survey by ANU Accommodation Services found that among international students who had conflicts with their homestay families, 82% of the issues traced back to differences in communication style rather than genuine disputes.

## 2025 Workshop Timetable

### Semester 1 Sessions

Semester 1 2025 runs **6 batches** of the workshop, capped at 30 participants each. The schedule is as follows:

- Batch 1: 10–14 February (during O-Week), daily 10:00–16:00
- Batch 2: 17–21 February (the week after O-Week), daily 13:00–17:00
- Batch 3: 3–7 March (week 3), daily 9:00–15:00
- Batch 4: 17–21 March (week 5), daily 14:00–18:00
- Batch 5: 31 March – 4 April (week 7), daily 10:00–14:00
- Batch 6: 14–18 April (week 9), daily 13:00–17:00

All batches are held in the multipurpose hall on level 3 of the Marie Reay Teaching Centre (MRC) on the **ANU campus**. Enrolment runs through the Student Central Portal, and 2024 data shows that Batch 1 filled up within 48 hours of enrolment opening.

### Semester 2 Sessions

Semester 2 2025 runs **5 batches**:

- Batch 7: 21–25 July (O-Week), daily 10:00–16:00
- Batch 8: 4–8 August (week 2), daily 13:00–17:00
- Batch 9: 18–22 August (week 4), daily 9:00–15:00
- Batch 10: 1–5 September (week 6), daily 14:00–18:00
- Batch 11: 15–19 September (week 8), daily 10:00–14:00

Special note: Batches 7 and 8 offer an **online synchronous participation option** (Zoom links are sent 24 hours after enrolment), but 2024 data shows that online participants' ICSA gains run about 12 points behind those of in-person attendees (66.3 vs 78.3), so in-person sessions are recommended where possible.

## Certification and Follow-Up Support

Participants who complete the full five-day workshop (15 hours in total) receive an **ANU Intercultural Adjustment Certificate**, which counts towards 10 activity credits in the ANU Global Citizenship Program. ANU Student Services data for 2023 shows that certificate holders rate their **employability readiness** at graduation an average of 17 points higher (out of 100) than those without it.

After the workshop, participants can join the **Intercultural Peer Support Network (IPSN)**, a community of more than 300 past participants that runs two online meet-ups a month. A 2024 ANU International Student Office report shows IPSN members' scores on the Cultural Adjustment Satisfaction Scale (CASS) holding steady in the 82–86 range over 12 months — well above the average of 61 for non-members.

## FAQ

### Q1: Is the workshop compulsory? What happens if I skip it?

The workshop is **voluntary**, not compulsory. That said, ANU Student Services data for 2023 shows that the first-semester **academic retention rate** for international students who attend no intercultural adjustment activity at all is 71%, versus 89% for those who complete at least one batch. Some colleges (including the ANU College of Business and Economics and the College of Engineering and Computer Science) recommend the workshop as a priority intervention in academic warning letters.

### Q2: What language is the workshop taught in? What if my English is not good enough?

All workshops are taught in **English**, with supplementary materials (glossaries and translations of key concepts) available in Chinese, Korean, Japanese and Vietnamese. ANU language school testing in 2024 shows that students with an overall IELTS score of 5.5 or above can keep up with the pace comfortably; those below that level can request the **peer translator service**, which covered around 15% of participants in 2024.

### Q3: How much does the workshop cost? Is it included in tuition fees?

The workshop is **free** for all enrolled ANU international students, with costs covered by the Student Services and Amenities Fee (SSAF). The 2025 SSAF rate is $351 per semester (ANU Finance Division announcement, 2024), and workshop running costs account for about 12% of that fee. Participants cover their own travel and meals, though free morning tea and a lunch voucher (worth around $15) are provided each day.
