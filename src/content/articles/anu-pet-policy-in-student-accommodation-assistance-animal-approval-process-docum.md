The Australian National University (ANU) manages pets in its on-campus accommodation under the legal framework of the Domestic Animals Act 2013, which prohibits keeping pets in all student residence buildings except for formally approved Assistance Animals. According to the Accommodation Terms and Conditions released by ANU Accommodation Services in 2023, approval of an assistance animal requires submission of at least 3 separate documents, including a letter of certification from a medical practitioner, an animal training qualification certificate and an accommodation application form, with the approval cycle usually taking 14 to 28 working days. Meanwhile, Australian Human Rights Commission 2022 data shows that around 1.7% of enrolled students across Australian higher education institutions have a certified assistance animal registered. As a member of the Group of Eight (Go8), ANU saw its assistance animal applications grow by 37% between 2021 and 2023. This policy framework directly affects around 6,200 on-campus residents (ANU 2024 on-campus accommodation capacity data) and involves the signing of Flatmate Agreements, making it one of the most talked-about accommodation management issues among ANU students today.

## Legal Definition and Categories of Assistance Animals

**Assistance Animal** is strictly defined in ANU policy as an animal as defined in Section 4 of the Domestic Animals Act 2013 — that is, an animal that has been professionally trained to perform specific tasks for a person with a disability. This definition excludes Emotional Support Animals and ordinary pets. Under Section 9 of the Australian Government's Disability Discrimination Act 2005, only dogs and certified Miniature Horses may enter ANU accommodation facilities as assistance animals.

### Distinguishing Assistance Animals from Emotional Support Animals

ANU Accommodation Services' 2023 policy document states clearly that an **Emotional Support Animal** is not considered an assistance animal and therefore has no legal right to enter student residences. This distinction stems from differences between the Australian legal system and the US Fair Housing Act — Australian law does not include emotional support animals within the scope of Reasonable Adjustment for disability. ANU students who need an emotional support animal must apply for off-campus accommodation and check for themselves whether the lease agreement allows pets.

### Special Rules for Miniature Horses as Assistance Animals

ANU policy permits certified **Miniature Horses** as assistance animals, but with strict facility conditions. Under ANU Accommodation Services' 2023 technical assessment standards, a miniature horse must be no taller than 86 cm (34 inches) and weigh no more than 45 kg (99 lbs), and the residence room must be on the ground floor with a passage leading directly to an external exit. As of 2024, no miniature horse assistance animal has ever been approved in ANU on-campus accommodation (ANU Accommodation Services internal statistics).

## Approval Process and Documentation Requirements

ANU's assistance animal approval uses a **three-stage review mechanism**: applicants must submit preliminary documents, animal assessment materials and an accommodation adjustment plan in turn. According to the ANU Accommodation Services 2023 Assistance Animal Application Guide, the full process takes an average of 21 working days, with document correction being the main cause of delay, accounting for 62%.

### Stage 1: Medical Certification and Disability Verification

Applicants must provide a **Medical Certificate** issued by a registered general practitioner (GP) or specialist, which must state clearly: (1) that the applicant's disability diagnosis meets the definition in the Disability Discrimination Act 2005; (2) the specific tasks the assistance animal performs (such as guide dog duties, seizure alert or mobility assistance); and (3) the necessity of the animal's presence in the accommodation space. The certificate is valid for 6 months and must be reissued when it expires. ANU Student Health and Wellbeing Centre 2023 data shows that around 41% of applications are returned for correction because the medical certificate does not clearly specify the animal's tasks.

### Stage 2: Animal Training and Behaviour Assessment

Applicants must submit an **animal training certificate** issued by Assistance Animals Australia or an equivalent accredited body, proving that the animal has completed at least 120 hours of professional training and passed a public behaviour test. The certificate must include the animal's vaccination records, health examination certificate and desexing status. ANU Accommodation Services reserves the right to require an on-site assessment of the animal, carried out by a veterinarian appointed by ANU Security, at the applicant's expense; the standard assessment fee for 2024 is $245.

### Stage 3: Accommodation Adjustment and Flatmate Agreement

Once approved, applicants must sign an **Accommodation Adjustment Agreement**, which covers: (1) the animal must wear an identifying vest in common areas (hallways, kitchen, laundry); (2) the applicant must clean up the animal's waste immediately; (3) the animal must not enter the private spaces of flatmates who have not approved it. When it comes to cross-border tuition payments, some international student applicants use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payment](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-pet-policy-in-student-accommodation-assistance-animal-approval-process-docum) to complete their remittances, so that accommodation deposits and adjustment fees can be paid on time.

## Flatmate Agreements and Balancing Residency Rights

ANU accommodation policy requires assistance animal applicants to obtain **written consent** from their flatmates, signed as an annex to the Flatmate Agreement. According to the ANU Accommodation Services 2023 Flatmate Agreement template, the annex covers three parts: the animal's range of activity, allocation of cleaning responsibilities and an emergency evacuation plan. Flatmates may withdraw consent within 14 days of signing; after withdrawal, the applicant must move to a single-occupancy unit within 28 days.

### What Happens if a Flatmate Refuses Consent

If a flatmate refuses to sign the consent form, ANU Accommodation Services will initiate a **Reasonable Adjustment Assessment**. The assessment panel consists of one representative from Accommodation Services, one ANU Disability Services coordinator and one student representative. According to ANU's 2022 internal case statistics, of 34 flatmate refusal cases, 19 (55.9%) resulted in the applicant being moved to a single room, 8 (23.5%) resulted in the flatmate being moved to another room, and 7 (20.6%) were dismissed because the applicant could not demonstrate the necessity of the animal.

### Animal Allergies and Conflicts with Reasonable Adjustment

When a flatmate provides a doctor-certified **animal allergy certificate**, ANU policy prioritises the allergic flatmate's right to health. Under ANU Accommodation Services' 2023 policy, an allergic flatmate may ask for the assistance animal to be confined to the applicant's room, or ask the applicant to pay for deep cleaning of common areas at least twice a week (the 2024 standard fee is $45 per clean). If the two parties cannot reach an agreement, ANU will arrange for one of them to move to another residence unit, with the moving costs borne by ANU Accommodation Services.

## Unauthorised Pet Keeping and Penalties

ANU accommodation policy classifies **keeping animals without approval** as a Serious Breach. Under clause 7.2 of the ANU Residential Conduct Code, a first offence may result in a $200 fine and an order to remove the animal within 48 hours; a second offence leads to termination of the accommodation contract, and the breach record is kept on the student's ANU file, which may affect future accommodation applications.

### Handling Animal Noise and Cleanliness Complaints

**Animal noise complaints** are handled jointly by ANU Accommodation Services and ANU Security. According to ANU's 2023 complaint statistics, among assistance-animal-related complaints, noise complaints accounted for 34%, cleanliness complaints 41% and aggressive behaviour complaints 25%. The complaint process is: a first complaint triggers a written warning; a second complaint requires the animal to undergo a behaviour assessment; a third complaint can initiate a review of the assistance animal's eligibility.

### Reporting Obligations for Animal Injury Incidents

Under Section 28 of the Domestic Animals Act 2013, any **animal injury incident** (including bites or scratches that break the skin) must be reported to the ACT Animal Management Authority within 24 hours. ANU Accommodation Services' 2023 policy requires that after an injury incident, the assistance animal's accommodation approval is automatically suspended until the ACT Government completes its investigation and issues a safety assessment report — a process that usually takes 30 to 60 working days.

## Special Policies for International Students

**International students** applying for an assistance animal must additionally submit proof of visa compliance. According to the Department of Home Affairs' 2023 Student Visa Conditions, holding an assistance animal does not affect an international student's visa status, but students must ensure the animal's import complies with the Biosecurity Act 2021. ANU International Student Services 2023 data shows that international students account for 27% of assistance animal applicants, with students from the United States, the United Kingdom and Canada making up 68% of international applicants.

### Import Quarantine Requirements for Animals

International students bringing an assistance animal into Australia from overseas must comply with the **import quarantine requirements** of the Australian Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry (DAFF). Under the DAFF 2023 Import Conditions for Pets, dogs and cats must complete a rabies vaccination at least 180 days before departure and undergo 10 to 30 days of quarantine in an approved facility upon arrival. Quarantine costs are borne by the applicant, with the 2024 standard fee ranging from $1,800 to $4,500 per animal depending on the length of quarantine.

### Impact of Visa Status Changes

When an international student's **student visa** changes (for example, to a Temporary Graduate visa, subclass 485), the assistance animal's accommodation approval automatically lapses. Under ANU Accommodation Services' 2023 policy, after a visa change the student must resubmit an accommodation application within 14 days, with the applicable accommodation terms based on the new visa category. If a student can no longer continue their accommodation because of visa issues, ANU Disability Services can help arrange accessible off-campus accommodation.

## Alternatives if the Application is Rejected

If an assistance animal application is rejected, ANU offers **alternative reasonable adjustments**. Under Section 21 of the Disability Discrimination Act 2005, ANU has an obligation to provide reasonable adjustments that do not involve an animal, including but not limited to: (1) allocating a ground-floor room to reduce mobility barriers; (2) providing accommodation quota for a Personal Care Assistant; (3) installing an emergency call system. ANU Disability Services' 2023 report shows that around 23% of assistance animal applications are ultimately resolved through alternative adjustments.

### Off-Campus Accessible Accommodation Resources

ANU Accommodation Services partners with the ACT Government's **Accessible Housing Register** to provide off-campus accommodation recommendations for applicants who are not approved. In 2024 the register listed 127 accessible rental properties in the ACT, of which 42 allow assistance animals. ANU can provide a one-off moving subsidy — the 2024 standard is $800 — to cover the deposit and the first month's rent.

### Appeals and External Review Channels

Applicants dissatisfied with an ANU decision can lodge a **disability discrimination complaint** with the Australian Human Rights Commission. According to the Commission's 2022 annual report, among the disability discrimination complaints against higher education institutions it accepted, assistance-animal cases took an average of 12 months to process, with a 47% mediation success rate. ANU's internal appeal process requires a written appeal to be submitted within 20 working days of the decision notice, and the ANU Student Grievance Office must issue a review decision within 30 working days.

## FAQ

### Q1: Are cats or dogs allowed in ANU residences as ordinary pets?

No. All ANU on-campus residence buildings strictly enforce the **no-pets policy**; only formally approved Assistance Animals may enter residences. Under clause 8.3 of the ANU Accommodation Services 2023 Accommodation Terms and Conditions, ordinary pets (including cats, dogs, hamsters, birds, etc.) must not be brought into residence areas under any circumstances, and keeping one without approval can result in a $200 fine and termination of the accommodation contract. Students who want to keep a pet must choose off-campus rental housing that allows pets.

### Q2: How long does it take to get an assistance animal application approved?

The full approval process takes an average of **21 working days**, but the actual time varies with how complete the documentation is. According to ANU Accommodation Services' 2023 internal statistics, the fastest approval took 9 working days (complete documents and no flatmate objection), and the slowest 64 working days (involving document corrections, animal assessment and flatmate negotiations). Applicants should submit their application at least **8 weeks** before moving in to avoid accommodation scheduling conflicts.

### Q3: Can an applicant stay in the original residence if the flatmate refuses to sign the agreement?

Not directly. If a flatmate refuses to sign the Flatmate Agreement annex, ANU will initiate a reasonable adjustment assessment, which typically results in the applicant being moved to a single room (55.9% of cases) or the flatmate being moved to another room (23.5%). According to ANU's 2022 internal case statistics, the proportion of applicants who stayed in their original room with the flatmate forced to accept was zero. Applicants must complete the move within 14 days of receiving the assessment outcome notice.
