The Australian National University (ANU) ranked 30th in the 2025 QS World University Rankings and is Australia's only national university established by an act of the federal parliament. For international students, the university's scholarship system covers tuition reduction, living allowances, and research support. In 2024, ANU awarded more than AUD 12 million in international student scholarships (ANU, 2024, Scholarships Annual Report). According to Australian Department of Education 2023 data, applications for ANU international scholarships grew 18% year on year, making competition increasingly intense (Department of Education, 2023, International Student Data). Drawing on ANU's official policies and historical admissions data, this article systematically sets out the scholarship types, application deadlines, eligibility thresholds, and personal statement strategies, offering a practical application guide for students aged 18–30 who are currently enrolled at or planning to enrol at ANU.

## Scholarship Types and Coverage

ANU offers three main scholarship categories for international students, aimed at undergraduate, postgraduate coursework, and research degree applicants respectively. The funding amounts, application processes, and competition levels differ significantly across the categories.

### ANU Chancellor's International Scholarship

This scholarship is open to all international undergraduate and postgraduate coursework applicants and offers two levels of support: **a full tuition fee waiver** (covering the entire degree) or a **50% tuition fee waiver**. For the 2025 intake, ANU plans to award about 200 scholarships, roughly 60 of them full waivers. There is no separate application — every international student who submits a program application by the designated deadline is automatically entered into the review pool. Academic merit is the core criterion: undergraduate applicants need an ATAR of 98.0 or equivalent (for example, a Chinese Gaokao score at least 120 points above the first-tier cutoff for their province), and postgraduate applicants need an undergraduate average of 85% or higher (equivalent to a GPA of 6.0/7.0 on ANU's 7-point scale).

### ANU College of Business and Economics International Scholarship

This scholarship is aimed specifically at business and economics applicants and is administered independently by the ANU College of Business and Economics (CBE). It provides a **tuition fee reduction of AUD 10,000 per year**, renewable for up to 4 years (undergraduate) or 2 years (master's). In 2024, 85 scholarships were awarded, about 40% of them to applicants of Chinese nationality. When submitting their program application, applicants must tick the "CBE Scholarship" option and also submit a **500-word personal statement**. The college's review committee weighs academic performance (60%), personal statement quality (30%), and extracurricular activities (10%).

### ANU HDR Fee Remission Merit Scholarship

For Master of Philosophy (MPhil) and Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) applicants, ANU offers a **full tuition fee waiver** plus a **living allowance of about AUD 35,000 per year** (2025 rate, adjusted for CPI). The scholarship is administered centrally by ANU's HDR (higher degree research) office. There is no fixed cap on the number of awards, but the actual number granted each year is constrained by each college's research funding. In 2023, the scholarship supported 412 international research students, with the College of Engineering and Computer Science accounting for the largest share (28%). Applicants must submit a research proposal, letters of recommendation, and an academic CV together with their research degree application.

## Eligibility and Hard Requirements

Each scholarship category has explicit academic benchmarks, language requirements, and nationality restrictions. Applicants should check each one before submitting materials to avoid wasting an application on eligibility grounds.

### Academic Benchmarks

The **Chancellor's International Scholarship** requires undergraduate applicants to provide their high school certificate and standardised test scores (such as A-Level, IB, or Gaokao): A-Level results must reach at least A*AA, IB at least 42 points, and the Chinese Gaokao at least 120 points above the first-tier cutoff for the applicant's province. Postgraduate applicants must provide their full undergraduate transcript with an average of no less than 85% (or GPA 6.0/7.0). The **CBE International Scholarship** has a slightly lower academic bar: undergraduate applicants need an ATAR of 95.0 or equivalent, and postgraduate applicants an average of 80% or higher. The **HDR Fee Remission** has no uniform cutoff, but colleges typically expect applicants to have averages of 85% or higher in both undergraduate and master's studies, and to have secured written acceptance from a supervisor.

### English Language Requirements

All scholarship applicants must meet ANU's English language requirements for admission. **IELTS**: overall 6.5 or higher, with no band below 6.0. **TOEFL**: overall 80 or higher, with writing at least 20 and speaking at least 18. **PTE Academic**: overall 64 or higher, with no communicative skill below 55. Some colleges (such as Law and Medicine) require more — IELTS overall 7.0 with no band below 6.5. Language scores must have been obtained within 2 years before the scholarship application deadline; expired scores are not accepted.

### Nationality and Visa Restrictions

**International student scholarships** are open only to applicants who are not Australian citizens, not Australian permanent residents, and not New Zealand citizens. Applicants must hold or plan to apply for an **Australian student visa (Subclass 500)**. Some scholarships (such as the CBE International Scholarship) further require that applicants have not already completed an equivalent or higher degree in Australia. In addition, the **Chancellor's International Scholarship** does not accept applicants who already hold other full funding (such as government-sponsored scholarships).

## Application Timeline and Deadlines

ANU scholarships combine rolling review with fixed deadlines. Applications that miss a deadline are automatically moved to the next round, but the places may already be allocated by then.

### Round One (Early Bird)

**Deadline**: 31 August each year (for February entry the following year). This round covers all scholarship types. The advantage of applying early: the **Chancellor's International Scholarship** allocates about 50% of its places in round one, and the review cycle is shorter (4–6 weeks). Applicants must complete their program application and upload all materials — including transcripts, language scores, and recommendation letters (where applicable) — by 31 August. ANU sends out round-one scholarship outcomes by mid-October.

### Round Two (Standard)

**Deadline**: 15 December each year (for February entry the following year) or 31 May (for July entry the following year). Round two typically offers the places left over from round one. In 2024, for example, the **Chancellor's International Scholarship** awarded only 82 places in round two, 38% fewer than in round one. The CBE International Scholarship splits its places more evenly between the two rounds (about 40:45). The HDR research scholarship accepts applications all year round, but you should apply 6–9 months before enrolment so your supervisor and college have enough time to assess the research proposal.

### Round Three (Top-Up)

**Deadline**: 31 January each year (for February entry) or 15 July (for July entry). The top-up round only applies to scholarships that were not fully allocated in earlier rounds. In the 2023 top-up round, the **CBE International Scholarship** had 12 places left and the **Chancellor's International Scholarship** had 5. Review in the top-up round is faster (2–3 weeks) and fewer people compete, making it a good option for applicants who were not ready earlier.

## Personal Statement Strategies

A personal statement is a mandatory document for the CBE International Scholarship and some college scholarships, and it is the **key basis on which the review committee distinguishes between applicants**. An effective personal statement should contain three core elements: academic motivation, career plans, and potential to contribute.

### Structure and Word Count

The CBE International Scholarship requires **no more than 500 words**; other scholarships usually accept 300–800 words. Use a three-part structure: an opening paragraph (100 words) stating your motivation and directly explaining why you chose ANU and this program; a middle section (300 words) presenting your academic background and research interests with specific course names, professors' research areas, or project experience; and a closing paragraph (100 words) setting out your career goals and how the scholarship would concretely support your development. Avoid hollow phrases like "I am very passionate"; replace them with specific statements such as "My undergraduate thesis on X topic led me to Y conclusion, which aligns with Professor Z's work on field A".

### Highlighting Academic Fit

The review committee cares most about how well you match ANU's academic resources. In your personal statement, mention the research areas or course names of **at least 2–3 ANU professors** and explain why those resources are indispensable to your academic development. For example: "Dr. Jane Smith's research on Southeast Asian economic integration directly connects to my fieldwork in Vietnam, and her course ECON8032 would provide the quantitative methods needed for my proposed thesis." Specific references like this markedly boost the credibility of a statement.

### Avoiding Common Mistakes

According to ANU's official review feedback published in 2024, the three most common problems in personal statements are: **over-generalisation** (such as "I want to change the world" without a concrete path), **failing to mention ANU-specific resources** (writing "I love Australia" without mentioning ANU's libraries, laboratories, or research centres), and **grammar and formatting errors** (such as typos or not using 12-point font as required). Before submitting, check the statement with Grammarly or a professional proofreading service, and ask a current ANU student or alumnus for feedback.

## Recommendation Letters and Supporting Materials

HDR research scholarships and some college scholarships require recommendation letters. The quality of the letters directly affects the outcome, especially the **consistency between the research proposal and the recommendation letters**.

### Choosing Your Referees

ANU requires **2 recommendation letters**, at least one from an academic referee (such as an undergraduate or master's supervisor). Referees should have had direct academic interaction with you and be able to describe your research ability, capacity for independent work, and academic potential in concrete terms. Avoid lecturers who only know your course grades; prefer supervisors who guided your thesis, project, or experiments. Letters should be printed on the referee's institutional letterhead and include their signature and contact details.

### What a Strong Letter Should Contain

An effective letter should include: the referee's relationship with you and how long they have known you (for example, "I supervised X's honors thesis for 12 months"); your performance on specific projects (for example, "X independently developed a Python model that improved prediction accuracy by 15%"); and an assessment of your academic potential (for example, "X is among the top 5% of students I have supervised in 10 years"). Avoid vague statements like "X is a hardworking student" and use verifiable, specific examples instead.

### The Research Proposal

Research scholarship applicants must submit a **1,000–2,000 word** research proposal covering the research question, literature review, methodology, and expected outcomes. The proposal should clearly state its connection to a specific ANU supervisor or research centre, for example: "This project builds on the work of the ANU Centre for Applied Macroeconomic Analysis, specifically Dr. John Smith's 2023 paper on inflation dynamics." The proposal should be reviewed and confirmed as feasible by your intended supervisor before submission.

## Application Process and Document Checklist

A complete scholarship application involves online submission, document upload, and follow-up. Missing any key document will delay the application or get it rejected outright.

### Using the Online Application System

All scholarship applications are submitted through the **ANU Application Portal** (apply.anu.edu.au). Applicants first create an account, select their program, and tick the "Apply for Scholarship" option. The system then generates a document checklist automatically, including: academic certificates and transcripts (notarised or WES-certified), language test scores, a passport copy, a personal statement (where applicable), and recommendation letters (uploaded directly by referees). Files must be in PDF format, each no larger than 5 MB.

### Notarisation and Certification Requirements

**Chinese academic certificates** must be certified by the China Higher Education Student Information and Career Center (CHSI) or by an Australian qualification assessment body (such as VETASSESS). **Transcripts** must be provided in a bilingual Chinese-English version stamped with the official school seal. **Language scores** must be sent directly to ANU by the testing organisation (IELTS via the IELTS TRF system, TOEFL via the ETS system). Notarisation usually takes 2–4 weeks, so prepare well in advance.

### Follow-Up and Checking Results

After you submit, ANU sends an Acknowledgement of Application by email within 4–6 weeks. You can track your document review status in real time through the Application Portal. Scholarship outcomes are announced 8–10 weeks after the deadline. **Applicants who are not selected** can request to join the waitlist, but waitlist places are usually scarce — in 2024, only 12% of waitlisted applicants for the Chancellor's International Scholarship were eventually offered a scholarship.

## FAQ

### Q1: Is there a hard minimum IELTS requirement for ANU international scholarships? Can I still apply if my overall score meets the bar but one band is 0.5 short?

All ANU scholarships require **an overall IELTS score of at least 6.5 with no band below 6.0**. If any band is below 6.0, the application is considered ineligible even if the overall score meets the requirement. Under ANU's 2024 language policy, language scores cannot be submitted after the application — you must provide a complete, valid score report by the deadline. Take the test 6–8 months in advance to leave time for a retake.

### Q2: What does the automatic review mechanism for the Chancellor's International Scholarship mean? Do I need to submit a separate application?

This scholarship **requires no separate application**. All international students who submit an ANU program application by the designated deadline are automatically entered into the review pool. ANU's Admissions Office ranks applicants by academic performance (such as ATAR, Gaokao scores, or undergraduate GPA), and the top 200 applicants qualify for review. In 2024, the automatic review pool contained 4,800 applicants; 200 scholarships were ultimately awarded, an award rate of about 4.2%.

### Q3: Will citing ANU professors' research areas in my personal statement look like I'm trying to flatter the committee? How do I cite naturally?

No, as long as the citations are **genuine and specific**. The committee wants to see that you have a deep understanding of ANU's academic resources. The right approach: choose professors whose work is highly relevant to your research interests and cite their specific papers or courses (for example, "Professor X's 2023 paper on Y method directly applies to my proposed research on Z"). Avoid citing more than 5 professors, or well-known scholars unrelated to the program you're applying for. ANU's 2024 internal review report found that personal statements citing 2–3 professors with sound connections scored 15% higher on average.
