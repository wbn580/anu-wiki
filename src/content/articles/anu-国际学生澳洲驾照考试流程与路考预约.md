ANU International Student Guide: the Australian Driver Licence Test Process and Driving Test Booking

As of 2024, data from Access Canberra, the Australian Capital Territory (ACT) road transport authority, shows that around 3,200 international students apply each year to convert to an ACT licence or sit a local licence test, of whom about 40% are enrolled at the Australian National University (ANU) [ACT Government, 2024, Annual Driver Licensing Report]. Unlike other Australian states and territories, the ACT has specific conversion and testing rules for international students holding overseas licences: if you hold a valid overseas licence (non-English), you may drive legally in the ACT for up to 3 months, after which you must apply for a local licence. For ANU students, mastering the full licence test process — from the theory test to booking the driving test — is a key step towards joining the local road system. Based on official ACT regulations, this article systematically covers the full process for ANU international students, from confirming eligibility to passing the driving test, with practical booking details.

## Confirming Eligibility and Overseas Licence Conversion Rules

Before starting the licence application, ANU international students must first confirm whether their overseas licence is eligible for conversion. ACT law divides overseas licence holders into two categories: licences from "designated countries" (such as the United Kingdom, Germany and Japan) can be converted directly without testing; licences from non-designated countries (including mainland China) require passing the full knowledge test, hazard perception test and driving test.

**Students holding a valid overseas licence (non-designated country)** may drive during the first 3 months after arriving in the ACT using their original licence and an officially recognised translation. After this period, they must apply for an ACT licence. If the overseas licence expired more than 5 years ago, they must start again from the L-plate (learner licence) stage.

### Translation Requirements and Recognised Providers

All non-English licences must be accompanied by a translation certified by **NAATI (the National Accreditation Authority for Translators and Interpreters)**. ANU students can obtain translations through on-campus services or online platforms at a cost of about $25-40 per page [NAATI, 2024, Translator Finder Database]. Access Canberra does not accept uncertified self-translations or ordinary notarised translations.

### Preparing Identity Documents

Before applying, prepare four types of identity documents: your passport, valid visa (electronic or paper), the original overseas licence with its translation, and proof of address (such as an ANU student card or bank statement). Proof of address must show a local ACT residential address, and documents from two different sources are usually required.

## The Driver Knowledge Test (DKT) Process

The ACT's **Driver Knowledge Test (DKT)** is the first step towards obtaining a learner licence (L-plate). The test covers road rules, road signs and safe driving knowledge, with 45 multiple-choice questions; a pass requires at least 36 correct answers (80%). The test can be taken in Chinese or English, but ANU students are advised to take the English version to become familiar with local terminology.

### Booking the Test and Fees

The DKT can be booked online through the Access Canberra website, or in person at the Civic office (about a 15-minute walk from the ANU main campus). Booking requires personal identity details and visa information. The test fee is $48.75 (2024 rate), which includes one test attempt and one free retest if you fail the first time [ACT Government, 2024, Driver Licensing Fees Schedule].

### Study Resources and Learning Materials

Access Canberra offers a free online practice test covering more than 200 real questions. ANU students can also borrow the ACT Road Rules Handbook from the campus library, or practise with third-party apps (such as the "Australian Driving Test" series). It is recommended to complete at least 5 practice tests and reach a consistent accuracy of 85% or above before booking the real test.

## The Hazard Perception Test (HPT)

After passing the DKT, candidates must complete the **Hazard Perception Test (HPT)** within 12 months before they can book the driving test. The HPT is a computer-based simulation in which candidates must identify potential hazards in video scenarios and respond to them, with 28 scenarios in total; scoring is based on reaction time and accuracy. The pass mark is 80% (correctly identifying at least 22 scenarios).

### Test Content and Practice Methods

Key HPT focus areas include judging vehicle gaps, spotting pedestrians appearing suddenly, and assessing intersection risks. ANU students can buy the official practice DVD from the Access Canberra website ($15) or practise on online simulation platforms (such as "Driving Test Australia"). It is recommended to complete at least 10 full practice runs in the simulated environment to get used to judging hazards in different weather and road conditions.

### Booking and Test Fees

The HPT is also booked through the Access Canberra online system and costs $39.75. The test takes about 45 minutes, and results are given on the spot. If you fail, you must wait at least 7 days before retaking the test; the retest fee is unchanged.

## Booking and Preparing for the Driving Test

The **Driving Test** is the final step towards independent driving. In the ACT, the test is conducted by an Access Canberra-accredited examiner and lasts about 35-45 minutes, covering basic vehicle operation, compliance with road rules and independent driving decision-making. Before booking the driving test, ANU students must have held a learner licence (L-plate) for at least 3 months and completed at least 50 hours of supervised driving (including 10 hours at night).

### Vehicle Requirements and Test Routes

The test vehicle must meet ACT safety standards: valid registration (Rego), a passed vehicle inspection (an eSafety inspection report is required if the car is more than 3 years old), and a dual brake pedal (if using a driving school vehicle). ANU students can use a driving school vehicle (about $80-120 per hour) or their own car. ACT driving test routes typically include residential areas, main roads, roundabouts and freeway sections, with a focus on lane changing, reverse parking and three-point turns.

### How to Book and Waiting Times

Driving test bookings are made through the Access Canberra website or by phone (13 22 81); book 4-6 weeks in advance as slots are limited. The test fee is $104.75. ANU students can choose the Civic, Belconnen or Tuggeranong testing centres, with Civic being the closest to campus. For cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%9B%BD%E9%99%85%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E6%BE%B3%E6%B4%B2%E9%A9%BE%E7%85%A7%E8%80%83%E8%AF%95%E6%B5%81%E7%A8%8B%E4%B8%8E%E8%B7%AF%E8%80%83%E9%A2%84%E7%BA%A6) to settle foreign exchange, but driver licence test fees must be paid directly to Access Canberra.

## On the Day of the Driving Test: Procedure and Marking

On the day of the test, candidates must bring their learner licence, identity documents (passport or ANU student card) and the booking confirmation. Before the test starts, the examiner checks basic safety items such as the vehicle's lights, brakes and tyres. Throughout the test, the examiner sits in the front passenger seat and records marks on a tablet. The **marking criteria** are divided into three categories: vehicle control (20 points), compliance with road rules (40 points), and decision-making and safety awareness (40 points). The total is 100 points and the pass mark is 90 (allowing at most 10 points to be lost).

### Common Deductions and How to Handle Them

Official ACT data shows the most common deductions are: failing to check blind spots (2 points each time), speeding (up to 5 points in cumulative deductions) and failing to give way (an immediate fail) [ACT Government, 2024, Driving Test Statistics Report]. ANU students are advised to take at least 2 mock driving tests before the real one (about $100-150 each), with a driving instructor marking like a real examiner.

### Test Results and Next Steps

The examiner tells you the result on the spot. If you pass, you can immediately apply for a provisional licence (P-plate) valid for 3 months, and the full licence will be posted to your registered address within 2-4 weeks. If you fail, you must wait at least 14 days before rebooking; the retest fee is $78.50.

## Provisional Licence (P-plate) and Converting to a Full Licence

After passing the driving test, ANU international students receive a **Provisional Licence (P-plate)**. The P-plate has two stages: P1 (red P-plate) and P2 (green P-plate). While holding a P1 licence, drivers must observe the following restrictions: a maximum speed of 90 km/h, no mobile phone use (including hands-free) and a zero blood alcohol limit. The P1 licence is valid for 18 months, after which it can be upgraded to P2.

### Requirements for a Full Licence

After holding a P2 licence for 12 months, you can apply to convert to a Full Licence if you have no serious traffic offence record. For ANU international students, if you have lived in the ACT for less than 3 years, the Full Licence's validity will be tied to your visa validity. After graduating and leaving Australia, the licence can be kept for use as an international licence.

### Special Notes for International Students

ANU international students should note that traffic offences committed while driving on an overseas licence in the ACT will affect visa applications. Under Australian immigration rules, accumulating 12 demerit points or committing serious offences such as drink-driving can lead to visa cancellation [Department of Home Affairs, 2024, Migration Regulations 1994]. Students are advised to strictly follow road rules and take out car insurance that includes third-party liability cover.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I convert a mainland China licence directly to an Australian licence in the ACT?
No. Mainland China is not on the ACT's list of "designated countries" for licence conversion without testing. ANU students with a mainland China licence must pass all three tests — the DKT, HPT and driving test — to obtain an ACT licence. However, for the first 3 months after arrival, you can drive legally with a NAATI translation.

### Q2: Can I cancel or reschedule a driving test booking?
Yes. Driving test bookings can be cancelled or rescheduled free of charge through the Access Canberra website more than 48 hours before the test time. Cancelling within 48 hours incurs a charge of 50% of the test fee (i.e. $52.375). If you neither cancel nor attend, the full fee is charged and you must rebook.

### Q3: Which driving test centre do ANU students use most?
The Civic centre is the most used by ANU students, about a 15-minute walk from the main campus. Its test routes cover the residential areas around ANU and the Northbourne Avenue main road, with moderate difficulty. 2024 data shows the pass rate at the Civic centre is about 62%, slightly higher than the ACT average of 58% [ACT Government, 2024, Driving Test Statistics Report].
