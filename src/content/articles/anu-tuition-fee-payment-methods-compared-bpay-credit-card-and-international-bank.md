The Australian National University (ANU) enrolled more than 25,000 students in 2024, around 40% of them international (Department of Education, 2023, International Student Data Annual Report). Paying tuition is a financial step every enrolled student has to complete, and ANU offers three main channels: BPAY, credit card and international bank transfer. According to the Student Payment Guide published by the ANU Finance Office in 2024, the method you pick can add between 0% and 3.5% in extra fees, which directly affects what you actually pay each semester. Drawing on official ANU policy and the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission's (ACCC) 2023 Cross-Border Payment Costs Report, this article compares the three methods on arrival times, fees and process so students can make the most cost-effective decision.

## BPAY: The Main Choice for Domestic Students

**BPAY** is an electronic bill payment service built by Australia's banking system and is one of ANU's recommended payment methods. It is available to students with an Australian bank account and can be completed through personal internet banking or a mobile banking app.

### How BPAY Works and How Long It Takes

Students generate a payment reference with a specific **Biller Code** and **Customer Reference Number** in the ANU ISIS system. After entering these two codes in internet banking, funds usually reach the ANU account within 1 to 2 business days. AusPayNet 2023 data shows BPAY processes more than 12 million transactions a day on average, making the system highly reliable.

### BPAY Fees

BPAY charges students no extra fees, and ANU does not add a surcharge for BPAY payments either. The Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA, 2022, Payments System Board Annual Report) notes that BPAY's operating costs are borne by the merchant (in this case ANU), so it is a zero-surcharge option for students. One caveat: international students who initiate a BPAY payment from a non-Australian bank account may be charged a cross-border processing fee by intermediary banks, typically between $5 and $20 AUD.

## Credit Card: Instant Confirmation but the Highest Cost

**Credit card** is the fastest option in ANU's payment system, usually confirmed instantly. ANU accepts Visa, Mastercard and American Express, but processing fees vary by card type.

### Credit Card Fee Breakdown

ANU charges an explicit **1.5% to 3.5%** service fee on credit card payments, depending on the card brand. According to the ANU Finance Office's 2024 fee schedule, Visa and Mastercard attract a 1.5% processing fee while American Express is 3.5%. Taking the 2024 Master of Commerce tuition of $48,000 AUD as an example, paying by Visa adds $720 AUD, while American Express adds $1,680 AUD. The Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC, 2023, Consumer Credit Report) warns that the surcharge on tuition payments can trigger credit card interest — if you don't pay the balance in full within the interest-free period, the real cost rises further.

### When Credit Card Makes Sense

Credit card suits students who need urgent confirmation on the due date itself, or those who can offset the surcharge with credit card points. Be aware, though, that some banks classify tuition payments as **cash advance** transactions, which can accrue interest from the transaction date at rates typically between 15% and 22% (RBA, 2023, Credit Card Interest Rate Statistics). Check the transaction category with your issuing bank before paying.

## International Bank Transfer: The Standard Cross-Border Route

**International bank transfer** (also called wire transfer or SWIFT transfer) is one of the main ways international students pay ANU from overseas accounts. It moves funds over the SWIFT network from the student's home bank to ANU's holding account in Australia.

### Transfer Process and Arrival Time

Students download ANU's **receiving bank details** from ISIS, including the bank name (Commonwealth Bank of Australia), BSB code, account number and SWIFT code. Transfers typically take 3 to 5 business days from initiation to arrival, though slow intermediary banks can stretch this to 7 business days in extreme cases. The World Bank (2023, Remittance Prices Worldwide database) puts the average arrival time for incoming international wire transfers to Australia at 4.2 business days.

### Hidden Costs

An international bank transfer is made up of three cost components: the **sending bank's fee** (usually $15 to $50 AUD), **intermediary bank charges** ($10 to $30 AUD) and the **receiving bank's processing fee** (ANU charges nothing extra). On top of that, the exchange rate offered by the sending bank typically includes a margin of 1% to 3% over the mid-market rate. The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC, 2023, Cross-Border Payment Costs Report) points out that a $10,000 AUD international wire transfer can carry total costs of $250 to $400 AUD — the equivalent of 2.5% to 4.0% of the tuition amount. ## Side-by-Side Comparison of the Three Methods

The table below compares BPAY, credit card and international bank transfer across five key dimensions, based on the ANU Finance Office's 2024 Student Payment Guide and AusPayNet's 2023 operating data.

- **Extra fees**: 0% · 1.5% to 3.5% · 2.5% to 4.0% (including hidden exchange margins)
- **Arrival time**: 1 to 2 business days · instant · 3 to 5 business days
- **Who it suits**: Australian bank account holders · all credit card holders · global bank account holders
- **Exchange rate transparency**: no currency conversion involved · depends on the issuing bank's rate · low transparency, includes margins
- **Refund difficulty**: easy, refunded to the original account · more complex, must contact the issuing bank · more complex, bank statements required

## How to Choose a Payment Method

Pick the method that best fits your account type and payment window. **BPAY** is the cheapest option but only works with an Australian bank account. International students who can act at least 5 business days before the deadline can still consider **international bank transfer**, accepting the uncertainty around exchange rates and fees. **Credit card** is only worth it for emergencies or for users whose points fully offset the surcharge.

### Installment Plans

ANU lets students apply for an **installment plan** at the start of each semester, splitting tuition into 3 to 4 payments. Each installment carries an extra $50 AUD administration fee (ANU Finance Office, 2024). Installments don't restrict your payment method — you can still use BPAY, credit card or international bank transfer for each one.

### Exchange Rate Lock Tools

For students using international bank transfer, some banks offer **forward rate lock** services that let you buy currency at an agreed rate 1 to 30 days before paying. National Australia Bank (NAB, 2023, Foreign Exchange Services Handbook) shows the service costs around 0.5% to 1.0% of the transaction amount — less than the exchange margin on a standard wire transfer.

## FAQ

### Q1: What is the latest I can pay ANU tuition?

ANU tuition is due 28 days before the start of each semester (ANU Academic Calendar, 2024). Students who miss the deadline are disenrolled and may face a $250 AUD reinstatement fee. Exact dates are listed under the "Financial" page in ISIS.

### Q2: Which payment method is cheapest for international students?

For international students with an Australian bank account, BPAY is the zero-cost option. For students paying directly from overseas, specialist tuition payment platforms usually cost less than a traditional wire transfer. The ACCC's 2023 report puts the average cost of a traditional wire transfer at 3.2%, while specialist platforms can bring it down to 1.5% to 2.0%.

### Q3: Do credit card tuition payments earn points?

Sometimes — but note that some issuers classify tuition as a cash advance transaction, which earns no points. Under ANZ's 2024 credit card terms, some cards explicitly exclude tuition payments from their rewards programs. Read your issuer's points terms before paying, or call customer service to confirm.
