The Humanities Research Writing Bootcamps at the Australian National University (ANU) are an intensive writing support program run jointly by the Academic Skills Centre and the College of Arts and Social Sciences. Since the program launched in 2018 it has run 3 to 4 sessions per year, each lasting 5 working days, and has supported more than 1,200 graduate researchers and early-career researchers in total. According to ANU's 2023 internal evaluation report, participants produced an average of 1,500 words per day during the bootcamp, a 275% increase over their non-bootcamp average of 400 words per day. With academic publishing growing ever more competitive — Times Higher Education (2024) data shows the average time to complete a humanities PhD globally has reached 8.2 years — structured writing interventions like this offer measurable, evidence-backed value for shortening thesis timelines and improving publication output.

## Bootcamp Schedule Structure and Timing

### The Standard 5-Day Schedule Template

Each bootcamp follows a fixed daily time-block design: 9:00–12:00 is the **silent writing block**, during which participants must switch off all digital notifications and stay off social media; 1:00–3:00 is the **peer review session**, with groups of 4 to 5 people allocated by disciplinary proximity; and 3:30–4:30 is the **skills workshop**, covering topics such as reference management, argument construction and academic English. The official schedule from the ANU Academic Skills Centre (2024) shows at least 5.5 hours of effective writing time per day, broken up by two 15-minute breaks.

### 2025 Schedule and Registration Windows

The three bootcamps announced for 2025 are: Session 1, 17–21 February (registration closes 3 February); Session 2, 23–27 June (registration closes 9 June); and Session 3, 20–24 October (registration closes 6 October). Each session is capped at 30 participants on a first-come, first-served basis. Registration goes through the **dedicated registration link** on the ANU Academic Skills Centre website and requires a research abstract of no more than 200 words plus a statement of your current writing stage. Statistics from ANU Student Administration (2024) show the average waitlist for the last 3 sessions was 22 people, so interested students should complete registration within 48 hours of registration opening.

## Registration Process and Eligibility

### Who Can Apply and Prerequisites

The bootcamps are open to current ANU PhD candidates, Master of Philosophy (MPhil) students and early-career postdoctoral researchers. Applicants must meet the following conditions: they have passed institutional human ethics approval and are at the **writing stage** after data collection; and they have at least one full draft chapter or a draft journal article. The eligibility notes from the ANU Academic Skills Centre (2024) explicitly state that students who have not yet started writing are not prioritised, because the bootcamps are designed to advance existing manuscripts rather than launch new projects.

### Registration Materials

The registration form requires three uploads: 1) a research abstract (within 200 words, covering the core argument and current chapter structure); 2) a **letter of support** signed by your supervisor, confirming that you are at the writing stage and that attending the bootcamp will not interfere with other academic commitments; and 3) a writing sample of no more than 5 pages (a draft chapter is acceptable). The ANU Graduate Research Office's (2023) registration guide stresses that all materials must be merged into a single PDF file named 'Surname_Bootcamp_2025.pdf'. Confirmation emails usually arrive within 5 business days of submission; if you do not receive one, contact academicskills@anu.edu.au.

## Sample Workshop Materials and Teaching Resources

### Thesis Argument Map

The bootcamp provides a standardised **thesis argument map** template: on one A4 page participants lay out the research question, the central claim, 3 to 5 supporting points, and the evidence source for each point. The training manual from the ANU humanities writing teaching team (2024) shows the template is adapted from the Toulmin model of argument and suits humanities disciplines such as history, anthropology and literary criticism. In one documented case, a philosophy PhD student used the template to restructure a muddled chapter into a logically coherent chain of argument within 3 days, and the resulting submission was later accepted by *Philosophical Studies*.

### Peer Review Rubric

The **peer review rubric** handed out at each bootcamp covers 5 assessment dimensions: clarity of argument (weight 25%), sufficiency of evidence (weight 25%), structural coherence (weight 20%), academic language (weight 15%) and citation accuracy (weight 15%). Each dimension uses a 1-to-5 Likert scale, with an open comment box alongside. Internal evaluation by the ANU Academic Skills Centre (2023) found that after the rubric was introduced, participants' rating of peer feedback 'actionability' rose from 3.2 (out of 5) before the bootcamp to 4.1 after.

## Writing Output Tracking and Outcomes

### Daily Writing Output

Based on tracking data from the ANU Academic Skills Centre (2024) across all 4 bootcamps in 2023, 120 participants produced an average of 7,500 words over the 5 days (median 6,800). **Humanities PhD students** had the highest daily output at 1,800 words, well above the 1,200 words for social science students. The difference is attributed to the nature of humanities disciplines, which rely more on independent writing than on data analysis. A comparison group of PhD students who did not attend the bootcamp produced an average of only 2,000 words over the same 5 days.

### Long-Term Publication Impact

A 12-month follow-up study by the ANU College of Arts and Social Sciences (2024) found that 68% of bootcamp participants submitted a journal article within 12 months of completing the program, versus 41% of non-participants. The **first-submission acceptance rate** was 29% for participants and 17% for non-participants. The study also noted that 52% of participants completed a full draft of their PhD thesis within 6 months of the bootcamp, significantly ahead of the 33% in the control group. These figures indicate that short, intensive writing interventions have a lasting positive effect on research output.

## Venue, Facilities and Technology

### Physical Space and Equipment

The bootcamps are held in the **Hancock Library** seminar room on level 3 (Room 3.02) at the ANU main campus. The space seats 35 and is equipped with 24 individual power outlets, two 65-inch interactive displays and high-speed Wi-Fi (measured download speeds of at least 200 Mbps). The ANU Library (2024) facilities guide notes that the room uses movable acoustic panels, so it can be switched into fully soundproof mode during silent writing blocks. Participants bring their own laptops, but the bootcamp loans external monitors, keyboards and mice — 10 sets, first come, first served.

### Digital Tools and Software Support

During the bootcamp, temporary licences for the following software are provided free of charge: Scrivener (academic writing software, 3-month trial), Zotero (reference manager, including 1 GB of cloud storage) and Grammarly Premium (grammar checking, 30-day trial). The software list from ANU Information Technology Services (2024) shows these licences are issued via bootcamp-specific activation codes and are valid only for the duration of the bootcamp. Participants can also access the Academic Skills Centre's **online writing resource library**, which holds more than 50 video tutorials and 20 PDF templates covering everything from literature review to conclusion writing.

## Cross-Disciplinary Collaboration and Networking

### Discipline Grouping and Cross-Disciplinary Dialogue

The bootcamps use a mixed grouping strategy: morning silent writing blocks group participants by disciplinary proximity (e.g., history with anthropology, literature with philosophy), while afternoon feedback sessions randomly mix participants from different disciplines. The evaluation report from the ANU Academic Skills Centre (2024) notes that this design lets participants get precise in-discipline feedback while also spotting blind spots in their own arguments through **cross-disciplinary perspectives**. One participant's feedback read: 'Questions from a sociology colleague made me realise my historical analysis was missing structural factors.'

### Alumni Network and Follow-Up Support

After the bootcamp, all participants automatically join the **ANU Humanities Writing Bootcamp Alumni Network**, which keeps daily conversation going through a Slack channel and holds a monthly online writing group meeting. Data from the ANU Alumni Office (2023) shows the network now has more than 400 active members, about 35% of whom co-authored a paper with fellow members within 12 months of completing a bootcamp. The bootcamp also offers individual writing consultations for 3 months after the program (2 sessions per person, 30 minutes each), booked through the Academic Skills Centre's online system.

## FAQ

### Q1: Are the bootcamps open to international students? Is there an extra fee?

Yes — the bootcamps are open to all enrolled ANU graduate students, including international students. The cost is already covered by your annual tuition fees; there is no extra charge. According to the ANU Student Fees and Scholarships Office (2024), international students on a student visa can take part in this kind of academic activity without affecting their visa compliance. Note, however, that the bootcamps do not provide accommodation or transport subsidies — participants arrange their own.

### Q2: If I miss the registration deadline, is there still a waitlist chance?

Yes. Each bootcamp's waitlist is usually kept open until 5 business days before the start. Data from the ANU Academic Skills Centre (2024) shows that across the last 3 bootcamps, an average of 8 waitlisted applicants eventually got a place, mostly because original registrants cancelled due to academic clashes. Waitlist applications go through the same registration link, with 'waitlist request' noted in the remarks field. It is also worth watching for the next bootcamp's registration window — the gap between sessions is about 4 months.

### Q3: Can I leave midway through the bootcamp if an urgent research task comes up?

Yes, but you must notify the bootcamp coordinator at least 24 hours in advance. Under the participation policy of the ANU Academic Skills Centre (2024), participants who leave early do not receive a completion certificate and must submit a short exit statement (within 100 words) explaining the reason so future scheduling can be improved. Data shows the early withdrawal rate across the 4 bootcamps in 2023 was 6.8%, mainly due to supervisor-requested experimental data collection or conference submission deadline clashes.
