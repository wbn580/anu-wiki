In its 2023 Academic Integrity Report, the Australian National University (ANU) recorded that it handled more than 420 academic misconduct allegations over the year, with around 35% of cases ultimately entering the formal hearing process. According to the National Guidelines for Academic Integrity issued by the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA) in 2022, universities must establish independent academic misconduct hearing mechanisms to safeguard procedural justice. For ANU students, understanding the hearing process and appeal strategies is key to protecting their academic rights.

## Definition and Classification of Academic Misconduct

ANU defines **academic misconduct** as any conduct that seeks to gain an unfair advantage in academic activities, and classifies it into three levels. Under the ANU Academic Integrity Policy (2024 revision), **Level 1 violations** cover minor issues such as improper citation and unauthorised collaboration, and are usually handled by the course convenor; **Level 2 violations** involve more serious conduct such as plagiarism and resubmission of one's own work, and must be referred to the College Academic Integrity Committee; **Level 3 violations** cover serious fraud such as contract cheating and data falsification, and go straight to the University Discipline Committee.

ANU's 2023 annual academic integrity statistics show that of all 420 allegations, cases involving **contract cheating services** rose from 8% of the total in 2021 to 17% in 2023, making them the fastest-growing category of violation. The University has partnered with Turnitin to deploy AI-writing detection tools, and identified 37 assignments suspected of being AI-generated in the first quarter of 2024.

## Reporting and Preliminary Investigation Stage

Academic misconduct is usually detected by teaching staff through **Turnitin similarity reports** or classroom observation. ANU requires staff to issue a Preliminary Investigation Notice to the student within 10 working days of identifying a suspected violation, setting out the specific allegations and the evidence. The student must submit a written response within 14 days of receiving the notice.

Under clause 5.2 of the ANU Student Discipline Rules 2023, during the preliminary investigation stage students have the right to inspect all evidence relating to the allegations, including the full similarity report, exam invigilation footage and communication records. 2023 data shows that around 45% of cases were dismissed at the preliminary investigation stage due to insufficient evidence or a reasonable explanation provided by the student, and never proceeded to a formal hearing.

## Formal Hearing Process

If the preliminary investigation does not reach a resolution, the case proceeds to a formal hearing. ANU's **academic misconduct hearings** are conducted by a panel of three members, at least one of whom must come from outside the student's own college. According to ANU's official process documents, the hearing is divided into four stages: opening statements, evidence review, witness questioning and closing statements.

The student has the right to bring one **support person** to the hearing, who may be a fellow student, a staff member, or an academic advocacy adviser from the student union. ANU's student union (ANUSA) provided more than 80 hearing accompaniment services in 2023. Hearings are usually completed within 30 to 60 minutes, and the panel issues a written decision within 10 working days, including the finding and any penalty imposed.

## Possible Penalties

ANU penalties for academic misconduct depend on the level of the violation and the seriousness of the circumstances. Common penalties for **Level 1 violations** include deduction of marks on the assignment (up to 40%), resubmission of the work, or a written warning; **Level 2 violations** may involve a downgrade of the course grade (for example, from HD to Fail) or a zero mark for the course; penalties for **Level 3 violations** range from suspension for one semester to permanent exclusion.

The ANU Discipline Committee's 2023 annual report shows that of the cases that reached a formal hearing, approximately 62% resulted in a finding of misconduct. Of these, **contract cheating cases** attracted the harshest penalties: 92% of upheld contract cheating findings resulted in at least one semester of suspension, and 12% led to permanent exclusion. The University also records an "academic misconduct" notation on the transcript, which is retained for five years after graduation.

## Appeal Mechanism and Time Window

Students who are dissatisfied with a hearing decision may appeal to the **ANU Academic Appeals Committee** within 20 working days of receiving the decision. Under the ANU Appeals Policy 2024, an appeal must be based on one of the following three grounds: a procedural error, the emergence of new evidence, or a penalty that is clearly unreasonable.

An appeal must be submitted in writing with supporting material. 2023 data shows that ANU received 37 appeals relating to academic misconduct, of which 8 (approximately 21.6%) were upheld in part or in full. The category with the highest **appeal success rate** was procedural error, accounting for 62% of upheld appeals. Students can submit an appeal online through the "Appeals" module of the ANU student portal; a $50 appeal fee applies, which is refunded in full if the appeal succeeds.

## External Appeal Channels

If internal appeals do not produce a satisfactory outcome, students may lodge a complaint with the **Commonwealth Ombudsman**. The Ombudsman has the power to investigate whether ANU's administrative decisions are fair, but its 2022-2023 annual report shows that complaints involving academic misconduct accounted for only 6% of all higher education complaints, and the Ombudsman cannot overturn academic decisions — it can only recommend that the university reconsider the case.

Another channel is to apply for judicial review through the **ACT Civil and Administrative Tribunal** (ACAT). ACAT can review whether ANU has complied with the procedural requirements of the Student Discipline Rules. According to ACAT's 2023 case statistics, there were 3 applications involving ANU academic misconduct, of which 1 was remitted for rehearing due to procedural defects.

## Prevention Measures and Resources

ANU offers a range of resources to help students avoid academic misconduct. The **Academic Skills Centre** provides free one-on-one tutoring, serving more than 2,300 students in 2023. The University also runs a compulsory online academic integrity course, which achieved a completion rate of 97% in the first quarter of 2024.

## FAQ

### Q1: How long does it take to get a result from an ANU academic misconduct hearing?
After the formal hearing concludes, the panel usually issues its written decision within 10 working days. For complex cases or Level 3 violations, this may extend to 15 working days. ANU's average decision period in 2023 was 12.3 working days.

### Q2: What evidence can a student view after being accused of academic misconduct?
Students have the right to view all evidence relating to the allegations, including the full Turnitin similarity report, exam invigilation footage, class records and email communications. ANU requires the University to provide the evidence package at least 5 working days before the hearing.

### Q3: How long does an academic misconduct record stay on the transcript?
ANU retains academic misconduct records for five years from the date of graduation. The record appears on the transcript only as an internal code and does not affect overseas qualification recognition. If a student later applies for postgraduate study or a job after graduating, some employers or institutions may ask to see the full transcript.
