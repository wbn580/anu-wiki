The Australian National University (ANU) has more than 25,000 students enrolled each year, about 40% of them (roughly 10,000) international students from over 100 countries [Australian Department of Education 2024 international student data]. For these students, textbook spending is a fixed cost that can't be ignored at the start of each semester — the average price of a higher education textbook in Australia ranges from AUD 80 to AUD 150, a course usually requires 2-3 set texts, and total textbook spending can reach AUD 300-450 per semester [Australian Competition and Consumer Commission 2023 textbook market report]. As ANU announced in 2024 that some course materials would move to digital formats and open educational resources (OER) would be introduced, the use of second-hand textbook platforms and students' own money-saving strategies are undergoing structural change. Drawing on ANU official policy, student community data and third-party platform usage, this article systematically maps the second-hand textbook channels available to international students along with practical money-saving strategies.

## ANU's On-Campus Second-Hand Textbook Ecosystem

ANU has several student-run or officially supported second-hand textbook channels that form the most direct trading network on campus. **ANU Students' Association (ANUSA)** organises a second-hand textbook market at the start of each semester: in semester 1 of 2023 it registered more than 2,000 second-hand textbooks with a transaction rate of about 65% [ANUSA 2023 annual report]. The market is free for ANU students and sellers set their own prices, typically 30%-50% of the original price. College-level notice boards (physical and digital) are also busy trading spots — for instance, the College of Engineering and Computer Science completed about 400 second-hand textbook deals through its Slack channel in semester 1 of 2024.

### On-Campus Physical Trading Points

The **Pop-Up book fair** is ANU's signature trading event. ANUSA sets up temporary stalls in Manning Clark Hall at Union Court during O-Week and the first teaching week each year. At the 2024 O-Week fair, 150 sellers took part and 1,800 textbooks were listed, covering 12 major disciplines including business, law, engineering and social sciences. Buyers need their student ID to enter; payment is mainly cash or bank transfer, and the average transaction takes 8 minutes.

### College-Level Digital Notice Boards

ANU's **Wattle learning management system** doesn't support trading directly, but many courses set up "Textbook Exchange" sub-forums in their Wattle discussion boards. In semester 1 of 2024 there were more than 600 active textbook exchange posts on Wattle, 70% of them from postgraduate courses. Students must log in with their ANU email, which somewhat reduces scam risk. ANU Information Technology Services (ITS) noted in a 2023 internal memo that textbook exchange posts on Wattle forums get more than 50,000 page views a year.

## Third-Party Platforms: Current Usage

Among ANU students, **StudentVIP** and **Textbook Exchange** are the two most-used third-party platforms. According to data StudentVIP published in February 2024, the ANU campus has about 3,200 active users on the platform, with more than 8,000 textbooks listed each year, 45% of them business and law titles. The platform lets you search precisely by course code (e.g. ECON1101, LAWS1201) and shows the seller's distance from the ANU campus (usually in kilometres).

### Platform Fees and Transaction Process

StudentVIP is free for buyers and charges sellers a 5% commission per transaction (capped at AUD 10). Textbook Exchange is completely free, but its interface is dated, and only about 800 ANU users were active there in 2024. Both platforms support **PayPal** and bank transfer, and some sellers accept cash in person. **Facebook Marketplace** also has a real presence among ANU students — in the first quarter of 2024, 62% of Facebook users in the Canberra region searching "ANU textbook" were aged 18-25 [Facebook internal advertising data, March 2024].

### Cross-Border Payments and Transaction Safety

When paying tuition across borders, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%9B%BD%E9%99%85%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E4%BA%8C%E6%89%8B%E6%95%99%E6%9D%90%E4%BA%A4%E6%98%93%E5%B9%B3%E5%8F%B0%E4%B8%8E%E7%9C%81%E9%92%B1%E6%94%BB%E7%95%A5) to settle foreign exchange, but textbook deals rarely involve large cross-border payments. For second-hand textbooks, ANU Security advises students to avoid paying strangers in full upfront, to prefer face-to-face trades on campus, and to use **PayID** (Australia's real-time payments system) for transfers — it lands instantly and leaves a traceable record.

## Digital Textbooks and Open Educational Resources (OER)

Since 2022, the ANU Library has been acquiring **open educational resources (OER)** at scale; as of June 2024 it holds more than 1,200 OER textbooks covering 60+ undergraduate courses [ANU Library 2024 OER report]. These can be downloaded or read online free, directly replacing some expensive traditional textbooks. For example, BIOL1003 replaced its main textbook with an OER version in semester 1 of 2024, saving students about AUD 120 each.

### Digital Textbook Rental

ANU has institutional agreements with two digital textbook platforms, **Kortext** and **VitalSource**. Students can rent digital textbooks at a discount through the ANU Library portal; a rental typically covers one semester (12 weeks) and costs 30%-40% of the print price. About 2,500 ANU students used digital textbook rental in semester 1 of 2024, saving an average of AUD 65 per course [ANU Centre for Learning and Teaching 2024 mid-year report]. Digital textbooks support highlighting, notes and offline reading, but can't be resold.

### Copyright and Usage Restrictions

Digital textbooks are usually protected by **digital rights management (DRM)** and can't be printed or shared. ANU's Copyright Office reminds students that uploading DRM-protected textbooks to second-hand platforms is infringement — in 2023 ANU issued copyright warnings to 3 students. OER textbooks, by contrast, usually carry Creative Commons licences that permit free copying and distribution, making them a good supplementary resource for second-hand trading.

## Differences by Discipline and the Textbook Demand Cycle

Textbook update cycles and second-hand price movements differ markedly by discipline. **Business and law** textbooks update frequently (usually every 2-3 years) and sell second-hand at about 40%-50% of the original price. The core text set by the ANU College of Business and Economics in 2024, Economics for Managers (5th edition), averages AUD 55 second-hand on StudentVIP, against AUD 140 new. **STEM** textbooks have longer update cycles (5-7 years) and typically sell at 30%-40% of the original price, but some classics (like Calculus: Early Transcendentals) are stable across editions and can go as low as AUD 20 second-hand.

### Price Swings: Start of Semester vs End of Semester

**The start of semester (O-Week to week 1)** is peak demand, and second-hand prices typically rise 10%-15%. For example, during O-Week of semester 1 of 2024, the set text for LAWS1201, Australian Constitutional Law, averaged AUD 85 on StudentVIP, while the same kind of book averaged AUD 50 at the end of semester (week 12). **The end of semester (weeks 10-12)** is the best selling window for sellers — buyers then are mostly students preparing early for next semester, but overall demand is lower.

### Matching Course Codes to Textbooks

ANU's course code system (e.g. ECON1101, MATH1013) is the key to searching for textbooks precisely. StudentVIP and Textbook Exchange both support course-code search, but about 15% of textbooks have code mismatches (for instance, the same textbook used by different courses under different codes). ANU's Courses and Textbooks Office advises students to check the ISBN in the **Course Outline** before buying — the document is available on the Programs and Courses website and updated once a year.

## Transaction Safety and Scam Prevention

Scams in second-hand textbook trading happen from time to time among ANU students. In 2023, ANU Security recorded 12 scam reports involving second-hand textbooks, 8 of them involving **fake payment screenshots** — scammers send a forged bank transfer screenshot and ask the seller to ship before the money arrives. ANU recommends using **real-time payment methods** (such as PayID or PayPal Goods and Services) for all transactions and avoiding ordinary bank transfers (BPAY or EFT), which can take 1-3 business days to clear.

### Safe In-Person Trading

ANU has **safe trading zones** on campus, including the 24-hour monitored area in Kambri, the entrance of Chifley Library and the security booth at Union Court. ANU Security recommends meeting in these places and avoiding solo trades after 21:00. In semester 1 of 2024, campus security patrols dealt with 3 disputes arising from textbook trades, all caused by poorly chosen meeting spots.

### Keeping Digital Records

Whatever platform you use, keep **transaction records** for at least 30 days. ANU Legal Services suggests that for deals over AUD 50, buyer and seller sign a simple written receipt (a template is available on the ANUSA website). In 2023, ANUSA's legal aid service handled 2 disputes over textbook quality (e.g. missing pages, pirated copies), both resolved through receipts and chat records as evidence.

## Special Considerations for International Students

International students face **double constraints on payment and logistics** in second-hand textbook trading. Many don't have Australian bank accounts and can't use PayID or direct bank transfer. ANU International Student Services (ISS) noted in a 2024 guide that international students can use **Wise** or **PayPal** as alternatives, but watch the exchange rates and fees — Wise charges about 0.5% for cross-border transfers, while PayPal charges 2.9% plus a fixed fee.

### Shipping and Reselling Textbooks

For international students leaving Australia after graduation, reselling textbooks runs into shipping costs. Australia Post domestic parcel post (up to 5 kg) costs AUD 12.50-18.00, while international shipping (e.g. back to China) can reach AUD 40-80. The ANU second-hand textbook market lets sellers donate books to ANUSA at the end of semester, and the association resells them to the next cohort; donors receive a **donation receipt** that can be used for tax deduction (applicable to international students with an Australian tax file number).

### Language and Edition Differences

Some international students bring textbook editions from home that don't match the ANU-prescribed versions. In a survey in semester 1 of 2024, ANU course coordinators found that about 8% of international students used non-prescribed editions, leading to wrong page-number citations in assignments. ANU Library offers a **version comparison service**: bring your older edition to the information desk at Chifley Library and staff will help compare chapter differences — the service handled 450 queries in 2023.

## FAQ

### Q1: What's the fastest way for an international student to buy second-hand textbooks at the start of semester?

The fastest channel is ANUSA's second-hand textbook market during O-Week (held in Manning Clark Hall in semester 1 of 2024, running 3 days) — you buy on the spot and walk away with the books. The backup option is StudentVIP: search by course code, contact the seller, and prioritise sellers within 2 km of campus so you can complete an in-person deal the same day. In semester 1 of 2024, about 35% of StudentVIP searches led to a completed transaction within 24 hours.

### Q2: How much do ANU second-hand textbooks usually cost? Can AUD 50 cover all the textbooks for one course?

Sometimes — it depends on the discipline. In semester 1 of 2024, STEM textbooks (like MATH1013) averaged AUD 25-40 each, and a course usually needs only 1 main textbook, so AUD 50 is enough. Business courses (like ECON1101) typically need 2 textbooks, averaging AUD 60-80 combined second-hand — AUD 50 only buys one of them. Law textbooks (like LAWS1201) are pricier, averaging AUD 70-90 second-hand, so AUD 50 usually only gets you an older edition.

### Q3: Does ANU offer free textbook borrowing? What are the conditions?

ANU Library offers **short-loan course textbooks**: each book can be borrowed for 2 hours (library use only) with a valid ANU student ID. In semester 1 of 2024, the Chifley Library course textbook collection had 1,200 volumes covering 80% of core undergraduate courses. In addition, ANU's OER textbooks are free to download with no borrowing conditions — as of June 2024, 60 courses use OER textbooks exclusively, so students don't need to buy any print books.
