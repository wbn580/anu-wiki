There is no single best personal loan broker in Australia. The right broker for you is the one whose licence you can verify, whose written quote you can compare line by line, and whose recommendations match your income, visa status and deposit. This guide walks through how to run that check yourself, step by step.

## 本文要点

- Start by verifying the broker's credit licence on the ASIC public register before you share documents.
- Ask for a written quote that separates the interest rate, upfront fees, ongoing fees and any exit costs.
- Check current rate benchmarks on the RBA statistics tables so you know what a competitive offer looks like.
- Confirm whether lender's mortgage insurance applies to your deposit level before you commit.
- Keep your own copy of every document you sign and every figure you are quoted.

## Step one: work out what you actually need

Before you contact anyone, write down three things: how much you want to borrow, how long you want the loan for, and whether the purpose is a home purchase, a refinance or something else. Personal lending in Australia covers a wide range, and a broker who mostly writes home loans may not be the right fit for a small unsecured loan.

Decide too whether you want a broker who compares many lenders or one who works with a smaller panel. Neither is automatically better. What matters is whether the broker can explain, in writing, why a particular product was recommended over the alternatives they had access to.

If you are buying property rather than borrowing against an existing asset, your residency status changes the picture. FIRB explains that foreign persons and temporary residents usually need foreign investment approval to buy residential property in Australia, and temporary residents are generally limited to new dwellings or vacant land for construction. Established second-hand dwellings are usually restricted. Approval requirements and any exemptions should be checked item by item against FIRB's current published rules.

Conclusion: a clear written brief of your borrowing goal makes every later comparison faster and harder to fudge.

## Step two: verify the broker before you share anything

ASIC handles credit licensing and responsible lending regulation in Australia. Its MoneySmart website publishes official guidance on applying for home loans and checking fees, and borrowers can use ASIC's public registers to confirm that a credit licensee is registered.

Ask the broker for their credit licence number or their credit representative number, then look it up yourself. A broker operating under someone else's licence should be able to tell you which licensee they represent. If they cannot or will not, that is a reason to stop.

Membership of a professional body is a separate matter from licensing. Industry associations run their own membership and certification programs, and those are not the same thing as an ASIC credit licence. A broker may hold both, one or neither. Do not treat a membership badge as a substitute for the licence check.

Conclusion: licence verification takes a few minutes and is the single highest-value check you can run.

## Step three: understand what moves the rate

The Reserve Bank of Australia sets the cash rate target, which is the benchmark for interbank lending and feeds into bank funding costs. On 11 August 2026 the RBA board decided to keep the cash rate target unchanged at 4.35 per cent. The current level and the history of changes are published on the RBA's cash rate page.

The rate you are offered is not the cash rate. The RBA notes that a bank's published home loan rate also reflects operating costs, risk premiums and competitive pressure, which is why quotes differ between institutions. The RBA's statistics tables publish monthly F-series data, including weighted average rates for housing loans split by owner-occupier and investor, and by variable and fixed, plus bank funding costs. Those tables are the cleanest public benchmark for judging whether a quoted rate sits high or low.

APRA supervises banks and other deposit-taking institutions and publishes prudential requirements and data relating to housing lending, including serviceability buffer expectations that banks must apply when assessing a loan. That is one reason two borrowers with similar incomes can be assessed differently.

Conclusion: use the RBA tables as your reference point, then judge each quote against them rather than against advertising.

## Step four: build a document file that survives scrutiny

Lenders typically assess the source and verifiability of your income, your visa or residency status, your deposit size and loan-to-value ratio, and your capacity to repay. Borrowers with overseas income are generally asked for verifiable income evidence, and the specific standard varies by institution.

Set up a folder with your identification, income evidence, existing debt statements and deposit records. Keep the original files and a dated copy of everything you send. If a broker tells you a document is not needed, ask them to confirm that in writing.

Lender's mortgage insurance usually applies when your deposit falls below the lender's threshold. The threshold and the premium are set by the lender's current policy, so ask for the figure in writing rather than relying on a general rule of thumb.

Conclusion: a complete, dated file reduces the chance that a conditional offer collapses late in the process.

## Step five: compare quotes on the same page

Ask every broker you speak to for a written quote covering the same items so the comparison is real. At minimum you want the loan amount, whether the rate is fixed or variable, how long the rate is valid, the repayment frequency, all fees including any early repayment penalty, and whether an offset account is included.

Before signing, obtain the written loan contract and check those items against what you were told verbally. Where a figure differs, ask for the reason in writing. The contract and the lender's current disclosure are what govern, not the summary in an email.

If you are comparing a broker against going direct to a lender, use the same checklist for both. The four major banks, Commonwealth Bank, Westpac, ANZ and NAB, all publish their home loan products, rates and application conditions on their own websites, and their policies for non-resident or overseas-income borrowers differ from each other. Check each bank's current published page rather than assuming a policy carries across.

Conclusion: identical line items on every quote is the only way to tell whether one offer is genuinely cheaper.

## Step six: run a final check before you commit

Work through a short closing checklist. Confirm the licence check is done and recorded. Confirm the rate type and how long the quoted rate holds. Confirm every fee, including exit costs. Confirm whether lender's mortgage insurance applies and at what amount. Confirm the repayment amount and frequency against your own budget, not the broker's summary.

Keep a dated record of what you were told and by whom. If any answer is vague, ask again in writing. A broker who is comfortable being pinned down in writing is usually the one worth continuing with.

Conclusion: the final check is not a formality, it is where most avoidable surprises are caught.

## Common questions

Is there one best personal loan broker in Australia?

No. Brokers differ by lender panel, specialisation and the type of borrower they handle well. The workable approach is to verify licensing, compare written quotes on identical items and check the numbers against public benchmarks.

How do I check whether a broker is licensed?

Ask for their credit licence or credit representative number and look it up on ASIC's public registers. MoneySmart publishes guidance on home loan applications and fee checking that is useful alongside that check.

Does a broker's industry membership mean they are licensed?

No. Industry membership and certification programs are separate from the ASIC credit licence. A broker may hold one, both or neither, so verify the licence independently.

Where can I see what a competitive rate looks like?

The RBA statistics tables publish monthly F-series data on weighted average housing loan rates and bank funding costs. Compare any quote against those published figures rather than against advertising.

What should I check before signing?

Get the written contract and confirm the loan amount, rate type, rate validity period, repayment frequency, all fees including early repayment penalties, and offset account terms. Where the contract differs from what you were told, ask for the reason in writing.
