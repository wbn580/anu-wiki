The Australian National University (ANU) sets a clear **Census Date** and **Withdrawal Deadlines** each semester; withdrawing before or after these dates produces different academic record grades — WD (Withdrawn without failure), WN (Withdrawn with failure) — and corresponding financial liability. According to the Australian Department of Education's 2023 Higher Education Student Data Collection, about 12% of undergraduate students nationwide had incurred financial liability or academic penalties in at least one course for missing the census date【Department of Education, 2023, Higher Education Student Data Collection】. ANU's 2024 Student Enrolment Policy further stipulates that students who withdraw after the census date not only bear the full tuition fee, but their transcript is permanently marked WN, which directly affects weighted average (GPA) calculations【Australian National University, 2024, Student Enrolment Policy】. Understanding these three time points and their consequences is the basic operational knowledge every ANU student must master when selecting courses and adjusting their semester plans.

## What the Census Date Is and What It Does

The **Census Date** is the most important administrative deadline in the ANU academic calendar, usually falling in weeks 4-5 after teaching begins each semester. In Semester 1 2024, for example, the census date was 31 March 2024【ANU, 2024, Academic Calendar】. Before this date, students can withdraw from courses freely, without any academic record or financial liability.

The census date's core function is to lock in a student's official enrolment status. After the census date, ANU submits the student's **Confirmation of Enrolment (eCoE)** data to the Australian Government, which is used to calculate Commonwealth Supported Places (CSP) and the disbursement of tuition loans (HELP loans). Under the Australian Government's 2023 Higher Education Support Act, students who withdraw after the census date lose their **CSP subsidy eligibility** for that course and must bear the full tuition fee themselves【Australian Government, 2023, Higher Education Support Act】.

In addition, the census date is a critical point for international student visa compliance. The Australian Department of Home Affairs requires international students to be enrolled in at least **48 credit points** per semester (usually 3 standard courses); withdrawing after the census date can leave students short of credit points and trigger the risk of student visa cancellation. ANU International Student Services advises students to complete all course adjustments at least **14 days** before the census date.

## Withdrawal Grades: The Difference Between WD and WN

### WD (Withdrawn without failure)

**WD** means "withdrawal without academic failure" and only occurs when a withdrawal is requested after the census date but **before the end of teaching week 8** (i.e., before the withdrawal deadline). WD does not affect the student's **GPA**, but it is permanently displayed on the transcript as a "WD" mark. ANU's 2024 Academic Assessment Policy explicitly states that WD does not count toward the number of failed courses and does not affect academic progress assessment【ANU, 2024, Academic Assessment Policy】.

Students applying for WD must submit the request through the **ISIS (Interactive Student Information System)** and do not need to provide a special reason. However, note that WD still carries partial financial liability — students must pay **25%** of the course tuition as an administrative fee (for example, under 2024 rates, if an international student's tuition for a 6-unit course is about AUD 5,000, the WD fee is AUD 1,250).

### WN (Withdrawn with failure)

**WN** means "academic failure caused by not withdrawing within the required time", and arises from any withdrawal after the withdrawal deadline but before the end of the semester. WN is treated as a **Fail**, counted directly into GPA calculations, and is equivalent to a score of 0. According to ANU's 2024 Student Progress Policy, one WN course lowers a student's semester GPA by about **0.5 to 1.0 grade points** (depending on the total credit points that semester) and may trigger an academic warning【ANU, 2024, Student Progress Policy】.

WN withdrawal incurs no additional administrative fee, but the student must still pay the full tuition for the course. For international students holding a **student visa (Subclass 500)**, WN or fail records in two consecutive semesters may lead the university to report the student to the Department of Home Affairs, affecting visa renewal.

## Financial Liability: Tuition Refunds and Debt Calculations

Students who withdraw before the census date receive a **100% tuition refund** (minus an administrative fee of about AUD 200). Withdrawing after the census date but before the withdrawal deadline (i.e., a WD withdrawal) means the student bears **25% of tuition**, with the remaining 75% refundable or convertible into credit. Withdrawing after the withdrawal deadline (i.e., a WN withdrawal) means the student bears **100% of tuition**, with no refund entitlement at all.

For domestic students using **HELP loans**, withdrawing after the census date causes the loan amount for that course to be counted into the student's **HELP debt**, and it cannot be undone by withdrawing. Data from the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) shows that in FY2022-2023, about **34,000** university students nationwide incurred an average of **AUD 2,300** in additional HELP debt due to withdrawing after the census date【ATO, 2023, Student Loans and Repayment Statistics】.

International students should pay particular attention: tuition debt arising from withdrawing after the census date must be settled before the end of the semester, otherwise ANU will suspend the student's **enrolment rights for future semesters** and may withhold the release of transcripts. ## Key Dates and Operating Procedures

### Semester timeline

The timeline for a standard ANU semester is as follows (using Semester 1 2024 as an example):
- **Teaching start date**: 19 February 2024
- **Census date**: 31 March 2024 (Sunday of week 6)
- **Withdrawal deadline**: 28 April 2024 (Sunday of week 10)
- **Semester end date**: 2 June 2024

### Operating procedures

To withdraw before the census date, students can do it directly through the **ISIS system** with no approval needed. To apply for a WD withdrawal after the census date but before the withdrawal deadline, submit a "Withdrawal after Census Date" application in ISIS, and the system will automatically calculate the fees payable and generate an invoice. For a WN withdrawal after the withdrawal deadline, students must submit a written application to **Student Central**, attaching supporting documents for special circumstances (such as medical certificates or family emergencies), which is executed only after approval by the academic committee.

## Special Circumstances and Exemption Applications

ANU allows students to apply for withdrawal exemptions on grounds of **force majeure or serious personal reasons**, including but not limited to: serious illness (doctor's certificate required), death of an immediate family member, serious psychological problems (psychologist's report required), or academic error (such as misleading course information). Exemption applications must be submitted within **28 days** after withdrawal, with complete supporting documents. Under ANU's 2024 Special Circumstances Policy, once an exemption application is approved, a WN can be changed to WD, or a WD can be changed to a full refund【ANU, 2024, Special Circumstances Policy】.

For students who are forced to withdraw for **academic progress reasons** (such as a GPA below 4.0 for two consecutive semesters), ANU automatically marks the withdrawal as WN, and the exemption policy does not apply. These students must restore their enrolment eligibility through the **Academic Progress Appeal** process.

## Special Notes for International Students

International students hold a **Subclass 500 student visa** and must strictly comply with the enrolment requirements of the **Department of Home Affairs**. Withdrawing after the census date and dropping below **48 credit points per semester** is treated as a breach of visa conditions. ANU International Student Services recommends that international students confirm before the census date that their **enrolled credit points** satisfy visa requirements, and keep written records of all withdrawal applications.

In addition, if withdrawal extends a student's **course completion time**, the student must apply for a new **eCoE** at least **6 weeks** before the visa expires, otherwise they may face the risk of becoming unlawful non-citizens. According to 2023 Department of Home Affairs data, about **7.2%** of student visa cancellation cases are directly related to withdrawing after the census date【Department of Home Affairs, 2023, Student Visa Cancellation Report】.

## Common Misconceptions and Practical Advice

Many students assume that "withdrawing before the census date leaves no trace", but in fact, even when withdrawing before this date, the ISIS system still generates an **internal operation log** for school audit purposes. However, this log does not appear on transcripts or academic records.

Another common misconception is that "WD withdrawal does not affect scholarship eligibility". Some ANU scholarships (such as the **ANU Chancellor's International Scholarship**) require students to complete at least **75% of enrolled courses** each semester; a WD withdrawal reduces credit points and may trigger scholarship suspension clauses. Scholarship holders are advised to contact the **Scholarships Office** before withdrawing to confirm the impact.

Practical advice: students should check their course enrolment status at least once in **week 3** of each semester to confirm that no courses were missed or wrongly selected. The **"Enrolment Check"** function in ISIS automatically detects insufficient credit points or course clashes.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I get a tuition refund if I withdraw after the census date?

Withdrawing after the census date (i.e., a WD withdrawal) refunds **75% of tuition**, with the student paying 25% as an administrative fee. Withdrawing after the withdrawal deadline (i.e., a WN withdrawal) refunds no tuition at all — the student bears 100% of the cost【ANU, 2024, Fees and Refund Policy】.

### Q2: How much does a WN withdrawal affect my GPA?

A WN withdrawal counts as **0 points** in the GPA calculation. For a 6-unit course, if the semester's total is 24 credit points, one WN lowers the semester GPA by about **0.5 to 1.0 grade points** (depending on other course results). WN in two consecutive semesters triggers an academic warning【ANU, 2024, Student Progress Policy】.

### Q3: Will my visa be cancelled if I withdraw as an international student?

Withdrawing after the census date and dropping below **48 credit points per semester** may be treated as a breach of visa conditions. According to 2023 Department of Home Affairs data, about **7.2%** of student visa cancellation cases are related to this【Department of Home Affairs, 2023, Student Visa Cancellation Report】. Contact ANU International Student Services before withdrawing to confirm the visa implications.
