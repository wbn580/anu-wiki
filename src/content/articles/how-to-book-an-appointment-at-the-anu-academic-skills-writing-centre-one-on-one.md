The Academic Skills Writing Centre at the Australian National University (ANU) offers current students free one-on-one writing feedback and thesis editing support. According to ANU's official 2024 data, the Centre handles more than 3,000 appointments per semester on average, with around 65% of bookings coming from postgraduate students (including coursework and research students) [ANU, 2024, Academic Skills Annual Report]. In the 2025 QS World University Rankings, ANU placed 30th, and its academic writing support system was rated by Times Higher Education as one of the five most comprehensive among Australian universities [THE, 2024, Student Support Services Review]. For students facing thesis deadlines or looking to improve the quality of their academic writing, mastering the booking process is the first step to accessing this key resource.

## Booking system overview

The ANU Academic Skills Writing Centre uses a unified online booking system, and all services are managed through the "Academic Skills" module integrated into the **ANU student portal** (ISIS). The system opens booking slots for the coming two weeks every Monday at 9:00 am (Australian Eastern Standard Time), with each appointment lasting 45 minutes. According to the 2023 Student Experience Survey, the average wait time for an appointment was 4.2 working days, extending to 7.8 working days in peak periods (Weeks 4–8 and Weeks 12–14) [ANU, 2023, Student Experience Survey].

### Eligibility and limits

All enrolled ANU students — including undergraduates, coursework postgraduates and research degree candidates — are eligible. Each student can book at most 8 one-on-one sessions **per semester**, and research students (PhD and MPhil) can get an additional 2 appointments dedicated to their thesis. Appointment records are kept in the system for 12 months, and students can review past appointment content and advisor feedback at any time.

### Cancellation and lateness policy

If you cannot attend, you must cancel through the system at least 4 hours before the appointment starts. Arriving more than 10 minutes late counts as a missed appointment and is counted against the semester limit. 2024 data shows that about 12.7% of appointments were marked as "no-show" because students neither cancelled nor attended [ANU, 2024, Academic Skills Usage Data].

## Step-by-step booking guide

A successful booking takes five standard steps and usually no more than 8 minutes in total. The system requires you to log in with your **ANU student email** (@anu.edu.au); third-party email addresses cannot access the booking interface.

### Step 1: Log in and navigate

After logging in via ISIS (isis.anu.edu.au), find the "Academic Skills" link under the "Student Services" menu. Once the page loads, click the "Book an Appointment" button and the system takes you to the booking calendar. First-time users must complete a short questionnaire (about 2 minutes) covering their current course codes, writing stage (such as outline, draft or revision) and the type of help needed.

### Step 2: Choose a service type

The booking system offers three main service categories: **General Writing Feedback** (for essays, reports and literature reviews), **Thesis Editing** (for thesis chapters or complete drafts) and **Oral Presentation** (for speech practice and slide feedback). After choosing a category, the system shows available advisors, each with their specialisation listed (such as social sciences, natural sciences, humanities and arts).

### Step 3: Upload your materials

Before confirming the booking, you must upload at least one **writing sample** (.docx or .pdf, maximum file size 20MB). For Thesis Editing, a continuous text of no more than 5,000 words is recommended. The system prompts you to note the aspects you want the advisor to focus on (such as structure, argumentation, grammar or citation style). After the 2024 system upgrade, a new "AI-assisted writing declaration" checkbox was added, requiring you to confirm that AI tool use has been disclosed in line with ANU's academic integrity policy.

### Step 4: Confirm and reminders

Once submitted, the system generates an 8-digit confirmation code and sends it to your ANU student email. You can view, modify or cancel appointments on the "My Appointments" page. Automatic reminder emails are sent 24 hours and 1 hour before the appointment. When it comes to paying cross-border tuition fees, some families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/how-to-book-an-appointment-at-the-anu-academic-skills-writing-centre-one-on-one) to complete currency conversion.

## What one-on-one feedback covers

One-on-one feedback is the Centre's core service, delivered by trained Academic Skills Advisors. These advisors are all current or former **senior postgraduate students** at ANU holding qualifications in TESOL, applied linguistics or related fields. Before starting, each advisor completes a 40-hour training program and passes a simulated tutoring assessment.

### Focus areas

In a 45-minute session, advisors typically cover three main dimensions: **structure and logic** (argument development, paragraph transitions and the chain of reasoning), **language and expression** (sentence variety, academic vocabulary and grammatical accuracy) and **citation standards** (consistency checks for APA, Harvard, Chicago and other formats). According to 2024 internal evaluations, advisors gave specific citation-format suggestions in about 78% of appointments [ANU, 2024, Service Impact Report].

### Feedback format and records

After the session, the advisor provides a written feedback summary (about 300–500 words) sent to your student email via the system. The summary has three parts: key findings (2–3 main improvement points), specific examples (quoting original passages with revised versions) and suggested next steps (recommended online resources or focus for the next appointment). Students can review past feedback through the system for 14 days after the appointment.

## Thesis editing service details

The Thesis Editing service is designed for research degree candidates and applies to every stage of the thesis — from the proposal to the final draft before submission. The difference from general writing feedback is that advisors make **deeper text-level revisions**, including sentence restructuring, paragraph reorganisation and terminology consistency checks.

### Scope and limits

Under ANU's academic integrity policy, the Centre's editing service is limited to the **language and structural level**; it does not involve substantive changes to research content, data analysis or the direction of the argument. Advisors will not add or remove academic arguments or alter the accuracy of cited references. Each appointment processes at most 5,000 words (about 15–20 pages of double-spaced text); longer manuscripts require multiple appointments. 2024 data shows that research students used the thesis editing service an average of 3.2 times to polish a complete thesis [ANU, 2024, Thesis Support Statistics].

### Priority booking

During the end of semester (Weeks 12–16) and the two weeks before thesis submission deadlines, the system runs a priority queue for **Thesis Submission** appointments. Students must upload "thesis submission deadline evidence" (such as a screenshot of the HDR milestone email) when booking, and the system automatically assigns the earliest available slot. In the 2024 peak period, the average wait for priority appointments fell to 2.1 working days, compared with 6.4 working days for non-priority ones.

## On-campus and online service options

The ANU Academic Skills Writing Centre offers three service modes: **on-campus (face-to-face)**, **online video (Zoom)** and **asynchronous written feedback**. Students can choose whichever suits their preferences and location.

### On-campus locations

On-campus appointments take place on level 2 of the **Hancock Building** (building 124), in rooms 2.12 to 2.16. The area has 5 private tutoring rooms, each equipped with an external monitor, whiteboard and WiFi access. Opening hours are Monday to Friday, 9:00 am to 5:00 pm (Australian Eastern Standard Time). 2024 data shows that about 58% of appointments chose the on-campus mode, with a higher share (72%) among research students [ANU, 2024, Service Modality Data].

### Online service standards

Online appointments run over Zoom; students click the meeting link generated by the system 5 minutes before the appointment starts. Advisors share their screens to show edits, and students are asked to keep their cameras on for better interaction. In the asynchronous written feedback mode, advisors return comments as annotations on the uploaded document within 72 hours; this mode does not include a live discussion component.

## Common questions and tips for getting the most out of it

To maximise the value of an appointment, students can use several strategies. **Preparation is key**: read through your draft at least once before the appointment and mark the passages you are unsure about. According to 2023 user feedback, well-prepared students received an average of 7.3 substantive revision suggestions per appointment, compared with just 3.1 for unprepared students [ANU, 2023, User Feedback Analysis].

### Materials preparation tips

Advisors recommend uploading **complete chapter drafts** rather than isolated paragraphs, because structural problems often only become visible when the text is read as a whole. Attaching the marking rubric or your supervisor's writing requirements also helps the advisor give more targeted feedback. For international students, especially non-native English speakers, listing 3–5 of your most persistent writing problems in advance (such as article use, clause structure or academic vocabulary choices) can significantly improve the session's efficiency.

### Strategies for multiple appointments

With a maximum of 8 appointments per semester, spread them out: use Weeks 3–4 for feedback on an early draft of a course paper, Weeks 7–8 for mid-term revisions, and Weeks 11–12 for final papers or the thesis final draft. Research students should concentrate their appointments in the final two months before thesis submission to ensure key chapters are fully edited.

## FAQ

### Q1: How long does it take to get feedback after booking?

For one-on-one sessions, feedback is given verbally at the end of the 45-minute appointment, and the written summary is sent to your ANU student email within 24 hours. For the asynchronous written feedback mode, advisors complete annotations and return the document within 72 hours. Average response times in 2024: 0.8 hours for on-campus, 1.2 hours for online, and 58.3 hours for asynchronous mode [ANU, 2024, Service Response Time Data].

### Q2: Can non-native English speakers book dedicated language support?

Yes. The Centre has a service category designed specifically for **international students**, and all advisors are trained in teaching writing to second-language learners. 2024 data shows that around 67% of appointments came from non-native English speakers, with Chinese students making up the largest share (31%). This service carries no extra fee and uses the same booking system as regular appointments.

### Q3: Can I rebook if I miss an appointment?

No. A missed appointment (arriving more than 10 minutes late or not attending) is recorded as a "no-show", counts against the 8-session semester limit and cannot be rebooked. You need to book a new slot through the system. 2024 data shows about 12.7% of appointments were marked as no-shows, with the highest rate (18.3%) in Weeks 6–8. If you cannot attend, cancel at least 4 hours in advance so the slot can be released to other students.
