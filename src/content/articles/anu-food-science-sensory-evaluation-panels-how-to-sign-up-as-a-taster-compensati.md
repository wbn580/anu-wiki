The ANU Food Science Sensory Evaluation Panel is a research and practice platform under the university's College of Health and Medicine that recruits enrolled students and staff to take part in sensory tests of food and beverage taste, smell and texture. According to the official ANU 2024 Sensory Panel Recruitment Guide, more than 1,200 participations occur across various tests each year, covering dairy products, beverages, baked goods and functional foods. The ANU sensory evaluation program strictly follows Australian Standard AS 2542.1-2014 (sensory analysis methodology) and is subject to ethical review by Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ). For ANU students aged 18 to 30, taking part in sensory evaluation is not only a way to earn extra income (compensation ranges from AUD 15 to AUD 40 per hour) but also a window into cutting-edge food science research.

## How the Sensory Evaluation Panel Works

The ANU Food Science Sensory Evaluation Panel is run by the Sensory Laboratory of the Food Science department, which is equipped with 10 individual sensory testing booths, each fitted with a computer-assisted data collection system (Compusense Cloud). Tests generally fall into two types: **analytical tests** (such as discrimination tests and descriptive analysis) and **affective tests** (such as liking ratings and purchase intent surveys). Analytical tests require participants to have basic sensory discrimination ability, while affective tests only require subjective feedback.

### Test Frequency and Duration

According to the ANU Sensory Laboratory's Semester 1 2025 schedule, 4 to 6 tests are held per week on average, with each test lasting anywhere from 15 to 60 minutes. Peak periods cluster in the middle of the semester (weeks 4 to 10), when food science postgraduate projects typically need large amounts of data. Tests are mostly scheduled between 10 am and 4 pm, though some projects may include evening sessions (such as beer flavour testing).

### Participant Database Management

ANU maintains its participant database in the RedCap system; after registration, participants receive email invitations. The database tags participants by **allergy history**, **dietary restrictions** (such as vegetarian or gluten-free) and **sensory sensitivity** (such as sensitivity to bitterness). 2024 database statistics show about 2,800 active registrants, of whom 65% are undergraduates, 20% postgraduates and 15% staff.

## How to Register as a Taster

Registration is fully online — no interview or pre-screening required. Visit the ANU Sensory Panel website (sensory.anu.edu.au), click the "Register as a Panelist" button and fill in the basic information form, including name, student ID, contact details, year of birth and dietary preferences. Once registration is complete, the system automatically sends a confirmation email containing a **unique participant number** (PID) used for all future test check-ins and compensation records.

### Initial Screening Tests

Some analytical tests require participants to pass a basic sensory screening test. Screening tests typically include a **taste recognition test** (identifying the five basic tastes: sweet, sour, salty, bitter and umami) and an **odour recognition test** (identifying common aromas such as lemon, mint and vanilla). Screening tests are free to attend; those who pass are marked as "Qualified Panelist" and receive priority invitations to higher-paying analytical projects. Those who do not pass can still take part in affective tests.

### Email Invitations and the Booking System

After registration, participants receive test invitation emails from sensory@anu.edu.au from time to time. Each email includes the test name, duration, compensation amount and a booking link (using the ANU Booking Calendar system). Bookings are first-come, first-served, with 12 to 20 places typically open per test. Participants must confirm their booking 24 hours before the test starts, otherwise the place is released to the waitlist.

## Compensation Rates and Payment Methods

The ANU Sensory Panel's compensation rates are tiered by test duration and type, with all compensation paid by bank transfer through the **ANU procurement and payment system**. Under the compensation policy updated in January 2025, affective tests (15 to 30 minutes) pay AUD 15 to AUD 20; analytical tests (30 to 60 minutes) pay AUD 25 to AUD 40; tests involving **special samples** (such as high-concentration capsaicin or alcoholic beverages) pay 20% to 50% more.

### Payment Cycles and Tax Notes

After a test is completed, compensation is deposited into the participant's Australian bank account within 14 working days. When cumulative annual compensation exceeds AUD 300, ANU issues a Payment Summary to the participant, which must be declared in the annual tax return. International students should note that, under the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) 2024 guidance, this income counts as "other labour income" and does not qualify for the tax-free threshold. For cross-border tuition payments, some student families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-food-science-sensory-evaluation-panels-how-to-sign-up-as-a-taster-compensati) to complete their remittances.

### Cancellation Policy

Participants who need to cancel a booked test must do so through the Booking Calendar system at least 2 hours before the test starts. Three accumulated No Shows result in the account being temporarily frozen for 30 days. Absences due to illness or emergencies can be excused by emailing sensory@anu.edu.au.

## Laboratory Safety and Code of Conduct

The ANU Sensory Laboratory complies with Australian Standard AS 2542.1-2014 and ANU health and safety policy. All participants must sign an **informed consent form** before entering the laboratory, confirming they understand the test content, potential allergens and their right to withdraw. The laboratory prohibits bringing food, drinks (other than test samples) or phones into the testing booths.

### Personal Hygiene Requirements

Participants must avoid smoking, chewing gum or consuming caffeinated drinks within 30 minutes before a test. On test days, participants must not wear **strong perfume** or **scented skincare products**, as these can interfere with the accuracy of odour tests. The laboratory provides single-use mouthwash for cleansing the palate between tests.

### Allergens and Medical Declarations

Known allergens (such as dairy, nuts and gluten) must be truthfully declared at registration. Test invitation emails clearly mark the allergen categories that may be present in the samples. For tests involving **alcohol** or **capsaicin**, participants must complete a health questionnaire before check-in to confirm they have no relevant contraindications. The laboratory is equipped with adrenaline auto-injectors (EpiPen) and first-aid kits, and all staff hold first-aid certificates.

## Common Test Types and Sample Scope

The ANU Sensory Panel covers a wide range of food categories. According to 2023–2024 project records, test samples include **dairy products** (yoghurt, cheese, ice cream), **beverages** (coffee, tea, juice, beer), **baked goods** (bread, biscuits, cakes) and **functional foods** (protein bars, probiotic drinks). Some tests involve imported foods, which must meet Australian biosecurity import conditions.

### Blind Testing and Coding Systems

All samples are presented under three-digit random codes, so participants cannot know the brand or product source. The laboratory uses FIZZ software to generate codes, ensuring codes are never repeated across tests. Blind testing is designed to eliminate the influence of brand preference on sensory evaluation, and the data is used only for academic research or product development.

### Data Use and Privacy Protection

Test data is used only for internal ANU academic research or joint projects with industry partners (such as CSIRO and the Australian Food Innovation Centre). Participants' personal information is stored separately from test data and anonymised. Under the protocol approved by the ANU Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC), data is retained for 7 years and then destroyed.

## Benefits of Participation and Academic Value

Beyond direct financial compensation, taking part in sensory evaluation lets participants accumulate **research participation credits** (some undergraduate courses such as FOOD3001 require a certain number of research practice hours). For food science students, this offers hands-on exposure to sensory analysis instruments (such as electronic noses and texture analysers) and experimental design processes. Students from other disciplines can also learn about consumer behaviour research methods through participation.

### Industry Recognition

ANU Sensory Laboratory data has been cited by the Australian Food Industry Association (AFIA) as a reference for product development. For students who later seek jobs in the food industry, sensory evaluation experience can be a plus on applications for **consumer insights** or **quality control** roles. In 2024, ANU partnered with Woolworths on a consumer test of low-sugar yoghurt, and the results directly influenced recipe adjustments in that product line.

## FAQ

### Q1: Will participating in sensory evaluation tests affect my student visa?
No. Under the Australian Department of Home Affairs 2024 visa conditions, student visa holders may work no more than 48 hours per fortnight. Sensory evaluation compensation counts as "research participation remuneration" rather than formal employment income, and each test typically lasts only 1 hour, so the cumulative annual hours stay far below the 48 hours/fortnight limit. However, annual compensation above AUD 300 must be declared for tax.

### Q2: If I am allergic to a certain food, can I still take part in tests?
Yes, but you must declare your allergens in detail at registration and only sign up for tests explicitly marked as free of that allergen. ANU Sensory Laboratory 2024 data shows about 18% of test projects offer gluten-free, lactose-free or vegan options. Invitation emails list all known allergens, and participants are expected to check them themselves.

### Q3: How long after a test will I receive compensation, and how do I check payment status?
Compensation usually arrives within 14 working days after the test. Participants can check payment status through the ANU Payment Portal (log in to ISIS and enter the Finance module). If payment has not arrived after more than 21 working days, email sensory@anu.edu.au with your PID number to enquire.
