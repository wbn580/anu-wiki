In 2023 the Australian National University (ANU) had 4,532 doctoral students in total, of whom about 43% were international (ANU Planning & Statistical Services, 2023). One of the key stages of a PhD application — the pre-application inquiry email — is written well or poorly, and its quality directly affects supervisor response rates. According to *Nature*'s 2022 global PhD survey, about 67% of doctoral students made initial contact with potential supervisors by email before applying, and supervisor fit is a core factor in doctoral completion rates (the Australian average is about 60%). Drawing on ANU's official admissions guides and academic publishing conventions, this article sets out structured email-writing techniques and supervisor matching strategies to help applicants communicate more effectively in the highly competitive PhD application process.

## The core purpose of the pre-application inquiry email and when to send it

A **pre-application inquiry email** is essentially an academic cover letter. Its primary purpose is to demonstrate to a prospective supervisor that you have independent research potential and to show a precise fit with the supervisor's research direction. ANU colleges typically require applicants to obtain a supervisor's preliminary agreement (a Letter of Support) before formally submitting a PhD application — a step that is especially important in the College of Engineering and Computer Science (CECS) and the College of Asia and the Pacific (CAP).

Timing should follow the academic calendar. ANU PhD applications are open year-round, but review committees typically process applications in concentrated rounds in February, May and September each year. Emails should be sent **8-12 weeks** before the deadline to give supervisors enough time to review materials. In 2024 the average processing time for international PhD applications at ANU was 14 weeks (ANU Admissions Office, 2024 Annual Report). Sending too early (for example, 6 months ahead) may mean supervisors cannot yet confirm their funding situation; sending too late risks missing scholarship deadlines.

### The timing window for supervisor responses

According to informal statistics on ANU's 2023-2024 PhD application data, emails sent on Tuesday to Thursday mornings between 9 and 11 am (Australian Eastern Time) received the highest response rates, at about 38%. Emails sent on Friday afternoon saw response rates drop below 12%. This pattern tracks academics' working rhythms — most professors deal with administrative tasks on Mondays and tend to read fewer new emails on Fridays.

## A structured framework for the pre-application inquiry email

An effective email should contain five core paragraphs and stay within **300-400 words** in total. For emails over 500 words, the full-read rate among supervisors drops to 41% (based on a 2023 *Journal of Academic Communication* survey of 200 professors).

**Paragraph 1: state who you are and your research intent.** Get straight to the point with your name, current degree and the ANU doctoral program you intend to apply for (for example, PhD in Biological Sciences). Cite a paper the supervisor published within the last 2 years, for example: "I am writing to express my interest in pursuing a PhD under your supervision, particularly inspired by your 2023 paper in *Nature Ecology & Evolution* on 'Climate-driven shifts in alpine plant phenology'."

**Paragraph 2: show research ability and relevant experience.** Summarise your master's thesis or published outputs in 2-3 sentences, with concrete data — for example, "My master's thesis analysed 1,200 soil samples across 6 sites, achieving a 94% data completion rate." Avoid vague claims like "I have extensive experience."

### Aligning research interests precisely with the supervisor's direction

**Paragraph 3: propose an initial research idea.** The idea must have a workable intersection with the supervisor's existing research projects. For example, if the supervisor studies "Antarctic ice-core palaeoclimate reconstruction", you could propose "using machine learning methods to optimise denoising algorithms for ice-core oxygen isotope data". This paragraph should include 1-2 specific technical terms (such as "Radiocarbon dating calibration" or "Finite element modelling") to demonstrate depth of knowledge in the field.

**Paragraph 4: explain the basis for the match.** List 2-3 publications or funded projects from the supervisor's lab over the last 3 years (from the ANU research portal or Google Scholar) and explain why your skill set would complement the existing team. For example: "Your ARC Discovery Project DP220103456 on 'Urban heat island mitigation' aligns with my experience in GIS-based microclimate modelling using ENVI-met software."

When it comes to cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use professional channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%8D%9A%E5%A3%AB%E7%94%B3%E8%AF%B7%E5%A5%97%E7%A3%81%E4%BF%A1%E6%92%B0%E5%86%99%E6%8A%80%E5%B7%A7%E4%B8%8E%E5%AF%BC%E5%B8%88%E5%8C%B9%E9%85%8D%E7%AD%96%E7%95%A5) for settlement, but payment issues are usually not something to discuss at the PhD application stage.

**Paragraph 5: attachments and next steps.** List the materials you have attached (CV, transcript, writing sample) and suggest a concrete time for the next contact, for example: "I will follow up in two weeks if I have not heard from you regarding a potential Zoom meeting."

## Supervisor matching strategies: from research direction to funding status

**Supervisor matching** should not stop at a superficial overlap of research interests. ANU supervisor selection involves three dimensions: research direction alignment, funding sustainability, and supervision style compatibility.

**Research direction alignment** requires reading at least 10 papers the supervisor has published in the last 5 years and analysing their research trajectory. For example, if the supervisor published mainly methodological papers in 2020-2022 and shifted to applied research after 2023, your research proposal should lean towards the applied side. The ANU research portal (Researchers.anu.edu.au) lists each supervisor's full publication record and H-index data; in 2024 the average H-index of ANU PhD supervisors was 28 (Scopus data).

### Finding funding information from public sources

**Funding sustainability** is a step international students often overlook. ANU supervisors typically fund doctoral stipends through Australian Research Council (ARC) Discovery Projects, collaborative projects with the Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation (CSIRO), or industry funding. Applicants can search the ARC grants database (arc.gov.au/grants) by supervisor name to see currently active projects and how many years remain. For example, a 3-year ARC Discovery Project (DP23xxxxx) started in 2023 and ending in 2026 means a student starting in 2025 would enjoy the full 3 years of funding support. If the supervisor has no active projects, funding must come from college scholarships (such as the ANU HDR Fee Remission Merit Scholarship) or external scholarships (such as the China Scholarship Council, CSC).

## Preparing the attachments

The quality of attachments shapes the first impression. An **academic CV** should be 2-3 pages and include: education (with GPA and class rank), publications (with DOI or arXiv links), conference presentations (noting oral/poster), technical skills (such as Python, R, MATLAB, LaTeX), and 2-3 referees (whose consent you have obtained in advance). ANU's minimum GPA requirement for PhD applicants is 5.0/7.0 (equivalent to an Australian Credit), but popular supervisors typically screen for applicants with a GPA of 6.0/7.0 or above.

A **research proposal** is not required at the inquiry stage, but attaching a one-page preliminary proposal can noticeably improve response rates. It should include: the research question (1-2 sentences), methodology (3-5 sentences), expected data sources (such as ANU's supercomputer cluster, the Australian Synchrotron, or the National Herbarium), and 3-5 key references (including 1-2 by the supervisor).

### Choosing a writing sample

A **writing sample** should be a published journal article or a chapter of your master's thesis. If you have no formal publications, a revised coursework paper is acceptable, provided the formatting is proper (abstract, references, numbered figures and tables). ANU's College of Asia and the Pacific (CAP) typically expects writing samples of at least 3,000 words, while the College of Engineering and Computer Science (CECS) places more weight on the completeness of technical appendices.

## Common mistakes and academic etiquette

The most common mistakes in pre-application emails include: mass-sending (without personalising the salutation), over-selling (such as "I will revolutionise your lab"), and ignoring the supervisor's administrative role. **Mass emails** are easy to spot — supervisors can tell immediately from the unedited "Dear Professor [Last Name]" template traces. A 2023 internal survey at the ANU College of Engineering found that mass emails received a response rate of only 4%, versus 31% for personalised emails.

**Academic etiquette** requires: a formal salutation ("Dear Professor [Last Name]" rather than "Dear Dr." or "Hi"); a full signature at the end of the email (including degree, institution and ORCID); avoiding sending on weekends or public holidays; and, if there is no reply within 3 weeks, one polite follow-up email of no more than 50 words. The follow-up should include new information, such as "I have since submitted my manuscript to *Journal of Geophysical Research*" or "I have been awarded the ANU International Travel Grant."

### What to do if the supervisor does not reply

If two emails go unanswered, consider whether: the supervisor is on research leave (ANU professors typically take 6 months of research leave every 3 years); the supervisor has stopped taking students (check the "Current Students" list on the ANU research portal); or the research direction is genuinely mismatched. At that point, turn to other supervisors in the same college, or contact the Higher Degree Research (HDR) Convenor to ask for alternative recommendations.

## Strategies for cross-disciplinary applications

ANU's 2023-2027 strategic plan explicitly prioritises interdisciplinary research, in areas such as "climate and health" and "data science and Indigenous knowledge". **Cross-disciplinary applicants** need to cite key literature from both disciplines in the email and explain how the methods will be integrated. For example, when applying in "environmental economics", you could cite both an econometrics paper from the ANU Crawford School of Public Policy and an ecosystem services assessment paper from the Fenner School of Environment and Society.

Cross-disciplinary emails should avoid jargon stacking. In paragraph 3, phrases such as "bridge the gap between" or "integrate [Discipline A] methods with [Discipline B] frameworks" work well. Applicants may contact two supervisors at once (a primary and an associate supervisor), stating clearly: "I am writing to both Professor Smith and Professor Jones, as my proposed research sits at the intersection of their respective expertise in..."

### The order of contact for joint supervision

If the target lab operates co-supervision, contact the primary supervisor first and only approach the associate supervisor after receiving a positive response. Under ANU doctoral supervision policy, the primary supervisor carries more than 70% of the supervision responsibility, so their opinion is decisive. When mentioning the associate supervisor in the email, use "with the potential for co-supervision by" rather than "I also contacted".

## FAQ

### Q1: How long without a reply before a pre-application email counts as failed?

According to ANU tracking data on 500 applicants in 2023, about 62% of replies arrive within 5-10 working days of sending. If there is no reply within 3 weeks, send one follow-up. If another 2 weeks pass with no reply (5 weeks in total), it is advisable to approach other supervisors. Note that ANU's summer break (the Christmas holiday period) runs from mid-December to mid-January, during which response rates drop by about 80%.

### Q2: Should I attach a full research proposal to the email?

No. At the inquiry stage, a 1-page preliminary research idea (about 300-400 words) is enough, not the full 10-page proposal. In the formal ANU PhD application, research proposals are 2,000-3,000 words, but the purpose of the inquiry email is to "spark interest" rather than "submit a final version". Attaching a full proposal can actually reduce reading rates because of its length.

### Q3: I have no publications. What should I write?

Focus on the research design of your master's thesis, the scale of data collection (e.g., "conducted 50 semi-structured interviews" or "analysed 200 GB of satellite imagery") and your technical skills. Among students who entered ANU PhD programs in 2022, about 34% had no formal publications before enrolment (ANU HDR Annual Report, 2022), yet they demonstrated solid methodological training in their inquiry emails. You can also attach an unpublished coursework paper as a writing sample, noting "Unpublished coursework paper, available upon request."
