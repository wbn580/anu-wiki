Field sampling sea trips are a core practical component of ANU's Marine Science undergraduate and postgraduate curriculum, involving around 120 undergraduates and 40 postgraduates each year. Under the Australian Maritime Safety Authority (AMSA, 2023) *National Standard for Commercial Vessels*, all ANU teaching and research vessels must hold a Class 1C or 1D commercial operations certificate. Sea-going activities are concentrated in the non-monsoon period from February to November each year; individual trips typically last 6 to 12 hours, with the furthest voyages reaching about 50 nautical miles offshore. Since the Australian Marine Sciences Association (2024) made field safety training a compulsory module in the 2024 Australian university marine science course accreditation framework, ANU has systematically upgraded its pre-departure safety preparation, risk assessment and emergency procedures, making this topic highly relevant in the current academic environment.

## Pre-Departure Mandatory Training and Certification

All students participating in field sampling must complete the **ANU Marine Safety Training (MST)** before going to sea. Designed by the Australian National University Safety & Wellbeing Office (ANU Safety & Wellbeing, 2023), the course runs for 8 hours in total and includes a written examination and a practical assessment. Training covers identification of shipboard safety equipment, emergency signal response, personal flotation device (PFD) donning procedures and maritime radio communication protocols. Students who pass receive a **Maritime Safety Operations Certificate** valid for 12 months; no one without this certificate is permitted on board.

### Swimming Competency Test Requirements

Under the ANU Marine Science Department's 2024 *Sea Trip Safety Manual*, all students must complete a **200-metre continuous swim test** within 30 days before departure. The test is held at the ANU campus swimming pool under the supervision of a certified lifeguard. Students who fail may sit one resit; those who fail again must enrol in a 4-week intensive swimming course. This requirement applies to all sea-going activities, including nearshore intertidal sampling.

### Medical Declaration and Insurance

Within 72 hours before each trip, students must submit an **electronic medical declaration form** covering 12 items including current medications, allergy history, epilepsy, asthma and cardiovascular conditions. ANU requires all international students to hold **Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC)** that includes coverage for overseas study activities, while domestic students must purchase additional marine accident insurance through ANU's partner insurers, with a minimum coverage of A$500,000.

## Survey Vessel Specifications and Safety Equipment Configuration

ANU's Marine Science program mainly uses two survey vessels: the **RV Kulu** (12 m long, 12 passengers) and the **RV Malu** (18 m long, 24 passengers), both of which completed safety equipment upgrades in 2021. Under the Australian Maritime Safety Authority (AMSA, 2023) *National Standard*, each vessel's **safety equipment inventory** includes: one auto-inflating PFD per person (with a 406 MHz personal locator beacon), two 6-person life rafts, EPIRB emergency position-indicating radio beacons (2 per vessel), and VHF marine radios (covering channels 16 and 67). In addition, the RV Malu is fitted with an AIS automatic identification system transmitter, allowing it to be tracked by other vessels and coast stations within a 10-nautical-mile range.

### Communications and Navigation Systems

Both vessels are fitted with **Garmin GPSMAP 8612 navigation systems** supporting real-time chart updates and waypoint marking. Within 5 nautical miles of the coast, the shipboard VHF radio can contact the **Australian Coast Guard (AMSA Rescue Coordination Centre)** directly; beyond that range, satellite phones (Iridium 9575) serve as the backup means of communication. A 2023 ANU internal audit showed that all sea trips maintained a radio position report every 30 minutes.

### Emergency Medical Equipment

Each vessel carries a **marine first-aid kit** (meeting AMSA Class 1C/1D standards) containing tourniquets, epinephrine auto-injectors (EpiPen), antihistamines, burn dressings and an automated external defibrillator (AED). At least one crew member holds the **HLTAID014 Provide Advanced First Aid** certificate, accredited by the Australian Skills Quality Authority (ASQA, 2024) and valid for 3 years.

## Sea Trip Declaration and Weather Assessment

All sea trips must be submitted through the ANU Marine Science Department online system at least **72 hours** before the planned departure time via the **Sea Trip Declaration Form**, covering the route, sampling point coordinates, estimated return time and the list of participants. The form is reviewed by the departmental safety committee; once approved, it is automatically copied to the ANU Safety & Wellbeing Office and the regional office of the Australian Maritime Safety Authority.

### Weather Window Criteria

Under the ANU 2024 *Sea Trip Operating Procedures*, trips are approved only when **wave height ≤ 1.5 m**, **wind speed ≤ 20 knots** (about 37 km/h) and **visibility ≥ 5 nautical miles**. Weather data comes from the coastal waters forecasts issued by the **Bureau of Meteorology (BOM, 2024)**, updated every 6 hours. If conditions deteriorate at sea to wave heights above 2.0 m or wind speeds above 25 knots, the captain has the authority to terminate sampling immediately and return to port.

### Tide and Current Calculations

Sampling plans must refer to the tide tables for the target area in the **Australian National Tide Tables (2024)**, with sampling windows normally set for periods when tidal current velocity is below 1.5 knots (about 0.77 m/s). For voyages involving benthic trawling or rocky reef sampling, a safe working window within 2 hours either side of low tide must also be calculated.

## Field Sampling Procedures and Division of Roles

A typical sampling trip lasts 8 to 10 hours and is divided into the **transit phase**, the **sampling phase** and the **return phase**. Students are organised into groups of 3 to 4, each supervised on site by a graduate teaching assistant (TA) or postdoctoral researcher. Sampling equipment includes CTD profilers (measuring conductivity, temperature and depth), Van Veen grab samplers, plankton nets (200 µm mesh) and handheld multi-parameter water quality meters (YSI ProDSS).

### Sampling Point Layout and Recording

Sampling point positions are fixed using **differential GPS (DGPS)** with an accuracy of ±1 m. For each point, the time, coordinates, water depth, substrate type and weather conditions are recorded, and the data is entered into the ANU Marine Science Data Management System (MDS). Over 2023, ANU's Marine Science program completed data collection at 187 sampling points, covering Jervis Bay Marine Park, Batemans Bay and nearshore areas of the Tasman Sea.

### Sample Processing and Preservation

Biological samples (such as zooplankton and benthic invertebrates) are fixed on site with **4% formaldehyde solution** or **95% ethanol**, and labelled with the sample number, date and GPS coordinates. Water samples are collected using Niskin bottles, dispensed into 500 mL polyethylene bottles, stored in coolers at 4°C, and analysed for nutrients within 24 hours of returning to the laboratory. All sample transport must comply with the **Australian Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry (DAFF, 2024)** regulations on cross-regional transport of biological samples.

## Emergency Response and Incident Reporting

ANU's Marine Science program has established a **three-tier emergency response mechanism**, with the captain, the departmental safety committee and the ANU Safety & Wellbeing Office each responsible for different levels. Tier 1 (handled on board) is executed by the captain and covers mild seasickness, cuts or equipment faults; Tier 2 (requiring shore-based support) is coordinated by the departmental safety committee, for example moderate injuries or mechanical failures; Tier 3 (major incidents) is taken over by the ANU Safety & Wellbeing Office and involves man overboard, fire or vessel sinking.

### Man Overboard Procedure

Before each voyage, the captain conducts a **man overboard (MOB)** drill covering casualty location (marking the GPS waypoint with the MOB button), vessel turning (the Williamson turn) and rescue ladder deployment. According to the ANU 2023 safety report, all crew must pass a practical MOB assessment before departure, with the average response time kept within 3 minutes.

### Incident Reporting and Statistics

All incidents and near misses must be submitted through the ANU incident reporting system within **24 hours** of returning to port, including a description of the event, the people involved, environmental conditions and corrective actions. Between 2022 and 2024, ANU's Marine Science program reported 14 incidents: 8 cases of mild seasickness, 3 equipment faults, 2 minor cuts and 1 allergic reaction, with no serious injuries or fatalities. These figures are compiled regularly by the ANU Safety & Wellbeing Office (2024) and submitted to the Australian Maritime Safety Authority.

## Post-Trip Data Management and Safety Audits

After returning from sea, all data must be uploaded to the ANU Marine Science Data Management System (MDS) within **48 hours** and quality-reviewed by the course coordinator. Review criteria include GPS coordinate consistency, sample number continuity and completeness of field records. Non-compliant data points are flagged and require re-sampling; 2023 data shows that about 5.2% of sampling points had to be recollected due to data quality issues.

### Equipment Maintenance and Disinfection

All sampling equipment is **cleaned and disinfected** within 24 hours of returning to port to prevent biofouling and cross-contamination. CTD profilers and Niskin bottles are soaked in 10% hydrochloric acid solution for 15 minutes and then rinsed with deionised water; nets and grabs are rinsed with high-pressure water and left to dry. Maintenance records are archived by the ANU marine technical team, with a full calibration carried out every quarter.

### Safety Audits and Improvements

The ANU Safety & Wellbeing Office conducts **one comprehensive safety audit** of the Marine Science program's sea-going activities each year, with the results submitted as a report to the departmental academic committee. The main improvements identified in the 2023 audit included increasing the number of shipboard AEDs (from 1 to 2) and upgrading the VHF radios to digital mode. All of these improvements were fully implemented by February 2024.

## FAQ

### Q1: What personal gear do students need to bring for ANU Marine Science sea trips?

Students must bring their own **personal flotation device (PFD)** (auto-inflating or foam models; ANU offers a rental service at A$15 per trip), non-slip deck shoes, a waterproof jacket (Gore-Tex material recommended), sunscreen (SPF50+) and 2 litres of drinking water. ANU provides all professional sampling equipment and first-aid supplies. The 2024 *Sea Trip Safety Manual* requires that students do not board in jeans or cotton clothing, as these absorb water and increase the risk of going overboard.

### Q2: How are trips adjusted if bad weather is encountered?

ANU uses a **72-hour weather window** mechanism: if forecasts show wave heights above 1.5 m or wind speeds above 20 knots, the course coordinator will notify students 24 hours before the planned departure that the trip is cancelled or postponed. In 2023, a total of 11 trips were cancelled for weather reasons, accounting for 15.7% of planned voyages. Cancelled trips are usually rescheduled to the next available weather window, no later than 14 days after the original date.

### Q3: Do international students need additional visas or insurance to join sea trips?

International students holding an **Australian student visa (Subclass 500)** can join all nearshore sea trips (within 50 nautical miles of the coast) without any additional visa. For insurance, OSHC covers basic medical care, but **marine accident insurance** must be purchased separately; ANU recommends buying it through partner insurer Allianz Global Assistance at around A$120 per year, with A$500,000 in coverage including search and rescue costs and medical evacuation.
