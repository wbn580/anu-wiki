Laboratory reports are a core component of assessment in the Australian National University (ANU) chemistry program, accounting for **25% to 40%** of the final grade in most chemistry courses. According to the Teaching and Assessment Policy released by the ANU College of Science in 2023, about **1,200** undergraduates must complete at least 6 standard-format laboratory reports each semester. The standards system draws directly on the industry standards of the Royal Australian Chemical Institute (RACI), and is designed to develop students' skills in data recording, quantitative analysis and scientific writing. Notably, in 2024 the ANU Chemistry Department updated its report marking criteria, raising the weight of data processing and error analysis from 15% to 20%, reflecting a stronger emphasis on experimental reproducibility. For students planning to apply to graduate school or enter a research career, mastering these standards is not just the threshold for passing courses — it is also preparatory training for future academic publication.

## Report structure standards

ANU chemistry laboratory reports follow a fixed chapter sequence; deviating from the structural order results in a **format deduction** (usually 5% of the total mark). Every report must include a cover page stating the course code, experiment number, submission date and collaborators (for group experiments).

### Title and abstract

The title must accurately reflect the core content of the experiment — for example, "Determination of the molar absorption coefficient of the Fe(II)–phenanthroline complex using UV–visible spectrophotometry". The abstract is limited to **200 words** and must include the purpose of the experiment, key methods, main quantitative results (including error ranges) and conclusions. According to the ANU Chemistry Department 2022 *Laboratory Report Marking Guide*, an abstract missing quantitative data loses 50% of the marks for that section outright.

### Introduction and methods

The introduction must provide background theory and cite at least 2 peer-reviewed references (such as the *Journal of Chemical Education*). The methods section must be written in the third person, past tense, and state the purity grade of all reagents (e.g. "analytical reagent AR grade") and the instrument models (e.g. "Shimadzu UV-2600 spectrophotometer"). ANU Chemistry Department 2023 internal statistics show that about 34% of mark deductions result from key instrument parameters being omitted from the methods section.

## Data presentation and figure standards

Data presentation is one of the highest-weighted modules, worth **25%** of the total mark. All raw data must be recorded in pre-numbered tables with units and uncertainties. Figures must follow these standards:
- **Axes**: variable names and units must be labelled (e.g. "Concentration (mol·L⁻¹)"), with a uniform font size of 10 pt Times New Roman
- **Error bars**: standard deviation (SD) or confidence intervals (95% CI) must be shown; figures missing error bars score zero for that figure
- **Linear fits**: the regression equation and R² value must be displayed, and the fit line must not be forced through the origin

### Numbering rules for tables and figures

Tables and figures are numbered separately and consecutively (Table 1, Table 2; Figure 1, Figure 2) and cited in order in the text. According to the ANU Academic Skills Centre 2024 *Scientific Writing Guide*, each error in the citation order between figures and text costs 1 mark (maximum 5 marks).

## Error analysis and data processing

The 2024 updated marking criteria raise the weight of error analysis to **20%**. Students must complete the following calculations:
- The mean, standard deviation (SD) and relative standard deviation (RSD) of all measured values
- 95% confidence intervals using the **t distribution** (when n ≥ 3)
- The uncertainty of final results via error propagation formulas (such as Δz = √[(∂z/∂x)²Δx² + (∂z/∂y)²Δy²])

### Outlier handling

If a data point deviates from the linear fit line by more than 2 times the residual standard deviation, you must note whether a **Grubbs test** was used to exclude it. The ANU Chemistry Department 2023 laboratory manual explicitly states that deleting data points without a statistical test is treated as academic misconduct, and that section scores zero.

## References and citation format

References must use **ACS (American Chemical Society) format** — numbered in order of first citation in the text, with full entries listed at the end. ### Weighting of citation types

- Textbooks: at most 1, and it must be a designated reference (such as *Atkins' Physical Chemistry*)
- Journal articles: preference is given to RACI journals (such as the *Australian Journal of Chemistry*) or ACS journals
- Web sources: only university official websites or government agency data may be cited (such as the CSIRO spectral database); sources like Wikipedia may not be cited

## Marking criteria and deduction items

ANU chemistry laboratory reports are marked on a **100-point scale**, with the final grade converted according to the course weighting. The marking rubric is divided into five dimensions:

- **Structure and format**: 10% · complete sections, clear page numbers, no grammatical errors
- **Data presentation**: 25% · standard tables, proper figures, correct error bars
- **Error analysis**: 20% · complete statistical calculations, reasonable confidence intervals
- **Discussion and conclusions**: 25% · interpretation of results, comparison with the literature, analysis of limitations
- **References**: 10% · correct ACS format, sufficient number of citations
- **Timely submission**: 10% · 5 marks deducted for each 24 hours late; zero after 72 hours

### Common deduction points

A 2023 ANU Chemistry Department sample analysis of 450 laboratory reports identified the **three most frequent deduction items**:
- Failure to convert units (28%)
- Figures missing error bars (22%)
- Discussion section not citing the literature (19%)

## Submission and similarity checking

All laboratory reports must be submitted through the **ANU Wattle system** by 23:59 (Australian Eastern Standard Time) on the 7th day after the lab class. The system automatically runs a Turnitin similarity check; reports with a similarity rate above **25%** trigger an academic integrity review. Under 2024 ANU College of Science rules, a first offence above the threshold may be revised and resubmitted (with a maximum deduction of 10 marks), while a second offence scores zero outright and is referred to the Student Disciplinary Committee.

### Individual reports for group experiments

Even when the experimental data comes from group work, every student must write their own report independently. The discussion section must note that "the experimental data for this experiment were collected jointly by group members [names]", but the analysis, calculations and conclusions must be your own. If two reports are found to have more than 30% textual similarity in the discussion section, both students are treated as committing academic misconduct.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I generate figures directly in Excel for the lab report?

Yes, but you must adjust the formatting manually. Excel's default charts do not meet ANU standards: change the axis font to Times New Roman 10 pt, add error bars (using the "custom error bars" function) and remove the default gridlines. ANU Chemistry Department 2023 statistics show that about 65% of students lost marks for not adjusting Excel's default formatting, with an average deduction of 3.2 marks.

### Q2: What confidence interval should I use in error analysis?

The standard requirement is a **95% confidence interval** using t-distribution critical values (when n ≥ 3). For example, if the mean of 5 measurements is 0.245 mol·L⁻¹ with a standard deviation of 0.012 mol·L⁻¹, then 95% CI = 0.245 ± (2.776 × 0.012/√5) = 0.245 ± 0.015 mol·L⁻¹. Failing to state the confidence level costs 2 marks.

### Q3: What should I do if the experimental data clearly deviate from the expected theoretical values?

You must report the raw data truthfully and analyse the reasons for the deviation in the discussion section (such as instrument calibration error, insufficient reagent purity, differences in operating time, etc.). Under the ANU Chemistry Department 2024 marking criteria, proactively identifying and discussing systematic errors earns a **3-mark bonus**, but falsifying data results in a zero for that report and triggers an academic investigation.
