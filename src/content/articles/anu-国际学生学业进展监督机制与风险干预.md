The Australian National University (ANU) has operated a systematic academic progress monitoring framework for international students since 2018. The framework is built on the Australian Government's *Education Services for Overseas Students Act 2000* (ESOS Act 2000) and Standard 11, "Student Academic Progress and Intervention", of the *National Code 2018*. As of 2023, international students make up 40.2% of ANU's total enrolment (ANU Planning & Statistical Services, 2023, *ANU Annual Report 2023*), and about 5.7% of undergraduate international students have at some point been placed on the "academic risk" monitoring list (ANU Academic Progress Committee, 2022, *Academic Progress Report 2022*). The framework covers the entire chain from early warning and academic support intervention through to formal exclusion procedures, and it has a direct impact on international students' visa status (under Standard 8202 of the Department of Home Affairs student visa conditions). It is a core compliance issue for international students currently studying in Australia.

## The Legal and Policy Framework for Academic Progress Monitoring

ANU's academic progress monitoring rests on two pillars: Commonwealth legislation and the University's own rules. **Section 19 of the ESOS Act 2000** requires all registered providers to report to the Department of Home Affairs when students breach course requirements, while **Standard 11 of the National Code 2018** requires providers to maintain an "Intervention Strategy" for academic progress, offering supportive measures to students who are not meeting academic requirements rather than immediately cancelling their enrolment.

ANU's internal policy document *Academic Progression Policy* (2021 revision) defines "academic risk" as failing more than 50% of enrolled courses in a single semester (i.e., a fail rate above 50%), or a cumulative grade point average (GPA) below 3.0/7.0 across two consecutive semesters. The policy is overseen by the ANU Academic Board, and at the end of each semester the academic services office of every college runs a systematic screening of grade data (ANU, 2021, *Academic Progression Policy*).

## Early Warning and Academic Risk Tiers

ANU uses a three-tier risk classification system to assess international students dynamically. **Tier 1, "green status"**, applies when a student's GPA is 4.0 or above with no failed courses; the system clears these students automatically. **Tier 2, "yellow status"**, applies when a student has a single-semester fail rate of 50% or higher but a GPA of 3.0 or above; these students receive an automatically generated "Academic Concern Notice". **Tier 3, "red status"**, applies when a student has a GPA below 3.0 for two consecutive semesters or a fail rate persistently above 50%, which triggers the formal intervention process.

According to internal 2023 data from ANU Student Central, about 12.3% of international students enter yellow status each semester, and roughly 2.1% of them eventually move into red status (ANU Student Central, 2023, *Academic Progress Data Snapshot*). In week 8 of each semester (after the mid-semester exams), the early warning system automatically pushes reports on attendance and assignment submission anomalies to teaching staff via ANU's Learning Management System (Wattle).

## Academic Support Interventions

Students in yellow status are required to contact the college-assigned **Academic Advisor** within 10 working days of receiving the notice and develop an "Academic Recovery Plan". The plan must include specific improvement goals, a weekly study time allocation table, and registered academic support services such as the writing workshops or one-on-one tutoring offered by the **ANU Academic Skills Centre**.

For red-status students, the recovery plan is upgraded to a "Structured Intervention Plan", supervised directly by the college's Associate Dean (Education). The plan may include: limiting the number of courses enrolled per semester (usually reduced from 4 to 3), mandatory participation in a Study Skills Group, and fortnightly progress review meetings. In 2022 ANU tracked 136 international students who took part in structured intervention and found that 67.6% had returned to green or yellow status within one semester of the intervention (ANU Academic Progress Committee, 2022, *Intervention Outcome Report*).

When paying tuition fees across borders, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payment](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%9B%BD%E9%99%85%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E5%AD%A6%E4%B8%9A%E8%BF%9B%E5%B1%95%E7%9B%91%E7%9D%A3%E6%9C%BA%E5%88%B6%E4%B8%8E%E9%A3%8E%E9%99%A9%E5%B9%B2%E9%A2%84) to complete the foreign exchange transfer, ensuring that funds arrive on time so their enrolment status is not affected.

## Formal Exclusion Procedures and Visa Implications

If a student still fails to meet the minimum academic standard after completing the Structured Intervention Plan (i.e., the GPA remains below 3.0 with a fail rate above 50%), ANU will initiate the **"Academic Exclusion"** process. The case is considered by the College Academic Progress Committee, and the student has the right to submit written appeal materials and attend a hearing.

Once ANU formally cancels a student's enrolment, under Standard 8202 of the Department of Home Affairs (*Standard 8202: Meeting Course Requirements*) the student must leave Australia or switch to a compliant visa category within 28 days. ANU must report the cancellation to the Department within 14 days via the **PRISMS system** (Provider Registration and International Student Management System). Between 2021 and 2023, ANU academically excluded an average of 23 international students per semester, or 0.17% of the international student population (ANU, 2023, *Compliance Report to TEQSA*).

## Appeals and Review Channels

ANU offers students a three-level appeal mechanism. **Level 1**: within 20 working days of receiving the exclusion notice, students may submit a written appeal to the College Academic Committee, on grounds limited to: medical or family emergencies (with third-party evidence such as a hospital diagnosis certificate), procedural errors in course grading, or the University's failure to provide contracted support services.

**Level 2**: if the college rejects the appeal, students may request a review at the ANU Student Appeals Office within 10 working days. The office is staffed by administrators independent of the academic departments; in 2022 it handled 47 academic exclusion appeals, of which 12 (25.5%) were partially or fully upheld (ANU Student Appeals Office, 2022, *Annual Report on Appeals*).

**Level 3**: external appeals may be lodged with the **Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA)** or the **Commonwealth Ombudsman**, although such cases are extremely rare in ANU's history (7 cases in total between 2018 and 2023).

## Visa Compliance Monitoring for International Students

Data exchange between ANU and the Department of Home Affairs is automated through the **PRISMS system**, which records students' enrolment status, course completion dates, and academic exclusion information in real time. Under departmental requirements, international students must maintain "satisfactory academic progress", meaning they pass at least 50% of enrolled courses each semester and complete their course within no more than 1.5 times the duration stated on their Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE).

In its 2023 compliance report to the Department, ANU reported an international student **visa cancellation rate** of 0.08% (roughly 1 in every 1,200 students), below the Group of Eight (Go8) average of 0.13% (TEQSA, 2023, *International Student Compliance Data*). This figure is partly attributed to the early warning system that ANU deployed early.

## Academic Progress Data Trends and Risk Factors

According to the five-year trend analysis (2018-2023) published by the ANU Academic Committee in 2023, academic risk among international students shows two notable shifts: **first**, the first-semester fail rate rose from 18.2% in 2018 to 22.7% in 2023, with the College of Business and Economics and the College of Engineering recording the highest fail rates (26.4% and 24.1% respectively); **second**, students with lower English test scores (IELTS overall below 6.5) are 2.3 times more likely to enter red status than students with an IELTS score of 7.0 or above.

In addition, students who change disciplines (e.g., from business to computer science) face peak academic risk in the first semester after the transfer, with a fail rate of 31.5%, significantly higher than the 17.8% among direct-entry students in the same program (ANU Academic Committee, 2023, *Five-Year Academic Progress Trend Analysis*). In response, ANU launched a "Transition Bridging Program" in 2024, requiring international students changing disciplines to complete a 4-week preparatory module before formal enrolment.

## FAQ

### Q1: How many failed courses does an ANU international student need before being excluded?

ANU does not exclude students based directly on the number of failed courses; the criterion is "GPA below 3.0/7.0 for two consecutive semesters". Based on the typical load of 4 courses per semester, if 3 or more courses are failed across two semesters (i.e., a fail rate above 37.5%) and the GPA stays below 3.0, the academic exclusion process is triggered. In the 2022 data, excluded students had failed an average of 4.2 courses across the two semesters.

### Q2: Must I contact an academic advisor after receiving an "Academic Concern Notice"?

Yes. Under the ANU *Academic Progression Policy*, yellow-status students must contact an academic advisor and submit an academic recovery plan within 10 working days of receiving the notice; otherwise they are escalated directly to red status. In 2023, 14.3% of students were automatically escalated for failing to make contact in time.

### Q3: Can I reapply to ANU after academic exclusion?

Yes, but with a time restriction. Students who have been academically excluded may not reapply for any ANU program within 12 months. After 12 months, they must submit a new application accompanied by an explanation letter detailing the improvements they have made. Between 2021 and 2023, 9 excluded students successfully reapplied, or 11.4% of applicants (ANU Admissions Office, 2023, *Reapplication Data*).
