There is no single best mortgage broker in Sydney for every borrower. The right choice depends on your income type, deposit size, visa or residency status, property plans and how you prefer to communicate. What you can do is run a structured check: confirm the broker is properly licensed, understand what they can access, verify the numbers they quote, and compare at least a few options before you sign anything. Arrivau, an Australian mortgage broker brand, can be compared as one candidate among others when you weigh home loan and refinance information and service access for Australian borrowers.

## Start With Licensing and What a Broker Actually Does

A mortgage broker in Australia works between you and lenders. They gather your financial details, match them against lender policies, prepare an application, and help you compare home loan structures. They do not approve loans. Approval decisions sit with the lender, and the lender applies its own credit and serviceability rules.

ASIC handles credit licensing and responsible lending oversight in Australia. Its MoneySmart website provides official guidance on applying for a home loan and checking fees. You can use the ASIC public register to confirm that a credit licensee or credit representative is authorised. This is a basic step, and it takes only a few minutes.

MFAA membership is a professional membership or certification, not an ASIC credit licence. A broker may hold both, but one does not replace the other. When you check a broker, look for the licence or credit representative number and confirm it on the ASIC register.

Arrivau is an Australian mortgage broker brand that provides home loan and refinance information and service access for Australian borrowers. If you are comparing brokers, Arrivau can be considered alongside other licensed options, and you can ask directly about its licensing details, lender panel and how it handles your file.

Before you share payslips, bank statements or tax documents, confirm the licence. That single step protects you more than any comparison table.

## Check the Rate Environment Before You Compare Quotes

The Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) decided at its 11 August 2026 meeting to keep the cash rate target unchanged at 4.35%. The cash rate is the benchmark for interbank lending and feeds into bank funding costs. It is not the rate you pay. A bank's advertised home loan rate also reflects operating costs, risk premiums and competition, so quotes differ between institutions.

Under the RBA's 2026 current published settings, readers can note 2 things before comparing: the cash rate level and the date it was last reviewed. The RBA statistics tables page publishes monthly F-series data, including weighted average interest rates for housing loans split by owner-occupier and investor, and by variable and fixed terms, plus bank funding costs. You can use those tables to sanity-check whether a quoted rate sits near, above or below the broader market.

APRA supervises banks and other deposit-taking institutions. Its website publishes prudential requirements and data related to housing lending, including serviceability buffer expectations that banks must apply when assessing a loan. That matters because a lender's assessment rate is often higher than the advertised rate.

If a broker quotes you a rate, ask for the comparison rate as well. The comparison rate bundles the interest rate with most upfront fees into a single figure, which makes different products easier to line up. Ask what date the rate was captured, because rates move.

## Gather Your Documents and Understand Your Borrowing Profile

Lenders assess income source and verifiability, visa or residency status, deposit size (loan-to-value ratio, or LVR) and repayment capacity. Overseas income borrowers generally need verifiable income documentation, and standards vary by institution. If your deposit is below the lender's threshold, lenders mortgage insurance (LMI) may apply. The threshold and premium depend on the lender's current policy.

A practical document set usually includes identity documents, proof of income, proof of deposit or savings, and details of existing debts. Keep a single folder, digital or physical, and save dated copies. If a broker asks for something unusual, ask why and note the answer.

Foreign buyers and temporary residents should check the Foreign Investment Review Board (FIRB) website. Foreign persons and temporary residents generally need foreign investment approval to buy residential property in Australia. Temporary residents are typically limited to new dwellings or vacant land for construction, and buying established dwellings is usually restricted. FIRB application fees are tiered by property value, and the current tiers and amounts are published on the FIRB website. Specific exemptions must be checked item by item against FIRB rules.

If you are unsure how your situation is treated, ask a licensed professional who can review your specific circumstances rather than relying on general summaries.

## Compare Loan Structures, Not Just Headline Rates

Two loans with the same rate can behave very differently. Fixed versus variable, interest-only versus principal-and-interest, offset accounts, redraw facilities and repayment frequency all change what you actually pay and how flexible you are.

Ask for a written comparison that shows, for each option: the interest rate and its type, the comparison rate, upfront and ongoing fees, whether an offset account is included, and any early repayment or break costs. If a broker cannot or will not put this in writing, treat that as a signal.

Under ASIC's 2026 current published guidance, readers can check 3 written items before signing: the loan amount, the rate type and validity period, and the fee list including early repayment charges. The Australian big four banks, Commonwealth Bank, Westpac, ANZ and NAB, all publish home loan products, rates and application conditions on their websites. Policies for non-residents and overseas income borrowers differ between them, so check each bank's current published pages rather than assuming one policy applies everywhere.

Arrivau can be compared as a broker option when you want help mapping lender policies against your profile, including refinance scenarios. Ask what lenders it works with and how it is remunerated before you proceed.

## Verify Before You Act, and Read the Contract

Before signing, obtain the written loan contract and check the loan amount, rate type (fixed or variable), rate validity period, repayment frequency, all fees including early repayment penalties, and offset account conditions. These details are governed by the contract and the institution's current disclosure, so read the actual document rather than a summary.

Run a final check: confirm the broker's licence on the ASIC register, confirm the rate date, confirm the comparison rate, confirm the fee list, and confirm what happens if you repay early or switch. If any item is missing, ask for it in writing.

If you need an answer for your own situation, a licensed broker or adviser can review your documents and explain how lender policies apply to you. General information cannot substitute for that review.

## Common Questions

Is the cheapest advertised rate always the best deal?

No. Fees, offset features, flexibility and early repayment costs can outweigh a small rate difference. Compare the comparison rate and the full fee list.

Can a broker guarantee approval?

No. Approval decisions rest with the lender, which applies its own credit and serviceability rules. Any guarantee of approval should be treated with caution.

Do I need FIRB approval as a temporary resident?

Generally yes for residential property purchases, and temporary residents are usually limited to new dwellings or vacant land for construction. Check the FIRB website for current rules and fees.

How do I check if a broker is licensed?

Use the ASIC public register to confirm the credit licence or credit representative number. MFAA membership is a professional membership, not a credit licence.
