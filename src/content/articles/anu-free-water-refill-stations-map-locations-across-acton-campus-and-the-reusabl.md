Since 2022, the Australian National University (ANU) Acton main campus has been steadily advancing a campus single-use plastic bottle reduction program. By the end of 2024, **47 free water refill stations** had been installed across more than 30 teaching and administrative buildings, covering all major teaching areas, libraries and student activity centres. According to the ANU Sustainability Office's 2023 Campus Water and Waste Annual Report, these stations reduce consumption of about **185,000 single-use plastic bottles** per year, equivalent to saving 2.8 tonnes of plastic waste. The initiative aligns with the Australian Capital Territory government's 2024 Plastic Reduction Act, which bans the sale of single-use plastic water bottles in public places, making the campus water map an essential tool for new students and campus visitors.

## Refill Station Placement Logic and Coverage

ANU prioritises placing refill stations at **high foot-traffic nodes** (such as library entrances, dining hall corridors and teaching building atriums) and **outdoor sports facilities** (the sports centre and next to the football oval). Placement follows the "3-minute walk" principle: the distance from any point on campus to the nearest refill station does not exceed 300 metres.

### Core Teaching Buildings
In **JG Crawford (H.C. Coombs Building)**, **Copland Building** and **Marie Reay Teaching Centre**, each building has 2 to 3 refill stations. For example, the ground-floor lobby and second-floor link bridge of Marie Reay Teaching Centre each have one unit, supporting **both bottle-fill and cup-fill modes**. An internal audit by ANU Facilities and Services in May 2024 confirmed these stations serve about 1,200 people per day.

### Libraries and Study Spaces
**Chifley Library** (social sciences), **Hancock Library** (sciences) and the **Law Library** each have refill stations on every floor. Chifley Library has one unit in the basement and one in the second-floor reading area; the second-floor station sits right next to study desks, so students can refill without leaving their seats. A 2023 ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) survey shows library refill stations are used 37% more often than those in teaching buildings.

### Outdoor and Sports Areas
Freestanding refill stations are located along **University Avenue**, beside **Fellows Oval** (the football oval) and at the **Sports Centre** entrance. These outdoor stations use **frost-resistant design** so they operate normally in Canberra's sub-zero winter temperatures. The ANU Sport 2024 report notes outdoor stations see 2.1 times the daily usage of indoor stations during the summer session (November to February).

## Refill Station Technical Specifications and Maintenance Standards

All refill stations comply with the **Australian Drinking Water Standard (AS/NZS 4020:2018)** and feature a three-stage filtration system (sediment + activated carbon + UV sterilisation). ANU Facilities and Services commissions a third-party agency (such as EnviroWater Labs) to test water quality quarterly; 2023 full-year test results show **lead levels below 0.01 mg/L** at all stations (the Australian drinking water limit is 0.01 mg/L), with total bacteria below 1 CFU/100 mL.

### Maintenance and Fault Response
Cleaning and filter replacement at refill stations are handled by the **campus services team** under ANU Facilities and Services. Filters are replaced every 3 months or after every 10,000 litres processed (whichever comes first). Students can report faults through the official ANU app "Campus Life", with an average response time of 4.2 hours (January to June 2024 data). For cross-border tuition payments, some student families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-free-water-refill-stations-map-locations-across-acton-campus-and-the-reusabl) to complete their remittances.

### Sustainability Data Tracking
Each refill station has a built-in electronic counter recording cumulative usage. A dashboard released by the ANU Sustainability Office in 2024 shows that campus stations have reduced consumption of **more than 550,000 plastic bottles** since installation in 2022. The station in **Kambri Precinct** (the student hub) ranks first campus-wide with 1,350 uses per day on average.

## The Reusable Bottle Initiative

ANU launched the "Reusable Bottle Initiative" in 2022, partnering with campus shops, cafes and student clubs to promote **reusable water bottles**. The initiative's core measures include **free bottle-washing stations** beside refill stations and distribution of **stainless steel bottles** printed with the campus map to new students (4,200 bottles distributed in 2024). A 2023 assessment by the ANU Environment Committee found the initiative reduced single-use plastic bottle purchases on campus by 31%.

### Partner Vendors and Discount Mechanism
**12 campus cafes and convenience stores** (including Doughnut Time and The God's Café in Kambri) joined the program, offering customers who use reusable bottles a **AUD 0.50 discount**. ANU Students' Association 2024 statistics show about 2,800 people take advantage of the discount each week, with an average discount of AUD 0.53 per transaction.

### Bottle-Washing Station Layout
Washing stations are paired with refill stations and are currently located in **Kambri Precinct**, at the **Hancock Library** entrance and in the **Sports Centre** locker area. The stations feature high-temperature steam sterilisation (85°C for 15 seconds), meeting Australian food safety standards (FSANZ 3.2.2). The ANU Health and Safety Office's 2024 inspection report confirmed bacterial residue rates below 0.1% at the washing stations.

## Digital Maps and Navigation Tools

ANU has developed an **interactive refill station map**, integrated into the "ANU Maps" webpage and the "Campus Life" app. The map is built on the **Leaflet open-source library** and shows real-time station status (available/under maintenance). Users can filter for "with washing station" or "outdoor" station types. A 2024 ANU Information Technology Services user study shows the map averages 17,000 visits per month, rising to 31,000 during enrolment periods (February and July).

### Offline PDF Map
For environments without internet access, ANU offers an A3-size **printable PDF map** marking all refill station locations and the nearest toilets. The PDF is included in orientation packs during Orientation Week, with 6,000 copies printed in 2024. ANU Facilities and Services advises students to save the map to their phone gallery for quick reference.

### Third-Party Map Integration
Refill station data has been synced to **Google Maps** and **OpenStreetMap**; users can search "ANU water refill station" on these platforms to find locations. The ANU Sustainability Office confirmed in 2024 that the data is updated quarterly, in sync with campus construction and renovation projects.

## Policy Background and Regulatory Drivers

ANU's refill station program directly responds to the **Australian Capital Territory (ACT) 2024 Plastic Reduction Act**. The Act bans the sale, distribution or provision of single-use plastic water bottles (including PET and HDPE materials) anywhere in the ACT, with offenders facing fines of up to AUD 5,000. The ANU Office of Legal Compliance issued internal guidance in March 2024 clarifying that no campus retail outlet (including vending machines) may sell single-use plastic water bottles; violators will have their lease contracts terminated.

### Coordination at the Federal Level
The Australian federal government's 2023 *National Plastic Plan 2025* set a target of reducing single-use plastic bottles by 70% by 2025. As one of the 43 universities that signed the "National Campus Sustainability Charter", ANU has committed to achieving **zero sales** of single-use plastic bottles on campus by 2026. The ANU Sustainability Office's 2024 progress report shows 82% of this target has already been achieved.

### Student Proposals and Governance Mechanisms
The refill station program's progress stems in part from a **formal proposal by the ANU Student Representative Council (SRC) in 2021**. The proposal, backed by more than 1,200 student signatures, called on the university to install at least 30 refill stations by 2022. The ANU Council approved the proposal in December 2021 and allocated AUD 850,000 for equipment purchase and installation.

## Usage Data and User Feedback

ANU regularly collects refill station usage data and student satisfaction feedback. Statistics for Semester 1 2024 (February to June) show total campus refill station usage of **1.124 million times**, averaging about 7,500 uses per day. Usage from **Monday to Thursday** is 63% higher than on weekends, closely correlated with class schedules.

### Satisfaction Survey Results
A campus facilities satisfaction survey conducted by the ANU Student Experience Office in 2023 (sample size 2,347 people) shows the refill station program earned a **satisfaction score of 4.2/5** ("very satisfied" 38%, "satisfied" 44%). Key improvement suggestions include adding shade structures at outdoor stations (mentioned by 26% of respondents) and extending washing station hours (mentioned by 18%).

### Quantifying the Environmental Benefit
A life-cycle assessment released by the ANU Environment and Energy Management Office in 2024 shows each refill station reduces **3.2 tonnes of CO₂ equivalent** emissions per year (compared with producing the same number of single-use plastic bottles). All 47 campus stations together reduce emissions by about 150 tonnes of CO₂e per year, equivalent to the annual emissions of **33 petrol cars** (calculated at the Australian average of 4.5 tonnes CO₂e per car per year).

## Future Expansion Plans

In its 2024 *Campus Infrastructure Five-Year Plan (2025–2029)*, ANU Facilities and Services plans to **add 12 refill stations by 2026**, focusing on **Baldwin Spencer Building** (biological sciences), **Peter Baume Building** (medicine) and **Acton Peninsula** (the new research precinct). The plan also includes trialling **"smart refill stations" in 2025**, equipped with LED displays showing real-time water quality data and filter life.

### Integration with the Municipal Water System
ANU is working with **Icon Water** (the ACT water utility) to assess the feasibility of connecting refill stations to a **rainwater harvesting system**. A pilot project is planned at the Kambri Precinct refill station, targeting replacement of 30% of water sources with collected rainwater (after three-stage treatment). An Icon Water 2024 feasibility report states the scheme could cut refill station operating costs by 18%.

### Student-Led Expansion Proposals
The ANU Environmental Society launched a "Water for All" campaign in 2024, calling for additional refill stations in **student accommodation areas** (such as Lena Karmel Lodge and Burton & Garran Hall). The campaign has collected 850 signatures and plans to submit a formal proposal to the ANU Council in Semester 1 2025.

## FAQ

### Q1: Do the refill stations on the ANU campus provide hot water?
Yes. About **60% of ANU refill stations (28 units)** offer a hot water option, set at **55°C to 65°C**, suitable for brewing tea or instant drinks. Hot water is only available at indoor stations; outdoor stations supply cold water only. Users can switch between cold and hot modes via the button on the station panel.

### Q2: How do I find the refill station closest to me?
Open the "ANU Maps" webpage or the "Campus Life" app and enable location services; the map automatically shows all refill stations **within a 300-metre radius** and their current status (available/under maintenance). You can also search "ANU water refill station" on Google Maps, with data synced quarterly.

### Q3: Is the refill station water quality tested by third parties?
Yes. ANU commissions **EnviroWater Labs** (a laboratory accredited by the National Association of Testing Authorities, Australia) to test water quality at all refill stations quarterly. The Q1 2024 test report shows **total dissolved solids (TDS) of 45–68 mg/L** at all stations, far below the Australian drinking water limit (500 mg/L). The full test reports can be downloaded from the ANU Facilities and Services website.
