More than 40 vending machines are distributed across the Australian National University (ANU) campus, covering every major building from the Acton main campus to the suburban residences. According to the Campus Retail Services Report published by the ANU Facilities and Services Division in 2024, these machines process more than 120,000 transactions a year, of which about 35% occur outside the operating hours of libraries and teaching buildings (18:00-08:00). With ANU rolling out its fully cashless campus policy in 2025, all vending machines have been upgraded to accept bank cards and mobile payments, and in response to the Australian Health Star Rating (HSR) system, about 60% of stocked items have been replaced with healthy snacks rated HSR 3.5 stars or above. For students and staff studying late at night or on campus on weekends, this map provides an accurate guide to locations, payment methods, and nutritional choices.

## Major teaching buildings and library areas

### ANU Union Building (Kambri)
The Kambri area has the highest density of vending machines on campus, with 8 units deployed. 3 of them are at the entrance to Kambri Market Hall, offering hot and cold drinks and ready-to-eat sandwiches; 2 are in the ANU Cinema corridor, selling sweets and nuts exclusively; and another 3 sit beside the lifts in the Kambri underground car park, mainly supplying bottled water and energy bars. All machines accept Visa/Mastercard contactless payments and Apple Pay, and **do not accept cash**. Under the ANU retail services contract (2024-2027), machines in the Kambri area are restocked every 48 hours to ensure freshness.

### Hancock Library & Chifley Library
Each of the two libraries has 2 vending machines. The Hancock Library machines are at the entrance to the 24-hour study area on the ground floor, offering coffee, tea, and hot chocolate (supplied by Zest Coffee under a 2024 contract) as well as gluten-free biscuits. The Chifley Library machines are in the corridor on the second floor, focusing on healthy snacks such as **protein bars** and dried fruit. These machines get 50% more stock during the exam period (weeks 13-16), and according to a 2023 survey by the ANU Students' Association, night-time use of library vending machines accounts for 42% of their daily total.

### Marie Reay Teaching Centre (MRTC)
MRTC has 3 vending machines: 1 in the ground-floor lobby (drinks and snacks) and 1 on each of the third- and fifth-floor break-out areas (focused on healthy options). The fifth-floor machine is one of the few on campus offering a **lactose-free milk** option. These machines accept bank cards and payments from campus card balances topped up through the **Flywire tuition payment** system, making them convenient for international students to sort out food needs outside office hours.

## Residences and residential halls

### Ursula College & Bruce Hall
The common room at Ursula College has 1 vending machine offering basic snacks and drinks, though with a fairly limited range (about 15 items). The Bruce Hall machine is beside the ground-floor kitchen, stocking instant noodles and microwave meals. Both machines accept bank card payments only and **offer no cash option**. According to ANU Accommodation Services 2024 data, these machines average 47 transactions per week during the teaching period (weeks 1-12), below the average for teaching areas.

### Fenner Hall & Liversidge Street
The Fenner Hall vending machine is in the entrance lobby and is one of the few on campus offering **fresh fruit** (apples, bananas), run by ANU in partnership with local supplier Fresh Produce Group (a 2023 pilot project). The Liversidge Street residence machine is beside the communal kitchen, focusing on high-protein snacks and electrolyte drinks. These machines support direct deduction from the student card with no separate top-up needed.

### Graduate House & Warrumbul Lodge
The postgraduate residence area has fewer machines. Graduate House has 1 drinks machine beside the ground-floor mailroom; Warrumbul Lodge's machine is opposite the second-floor laundry. Both machines offer **sugar-free drink** options and support payment by phone QR code (through the ANU ID App).

## Healthy snacks and nutritional ratings

### Using the Health Star Rating system
Since 2023 ANU has used the Australian Government's Health Star Rating (HSR) as the screening standard for vending machine products. According to a 2024 internal audit by the ANU Health and Safety Office, products rated HSR 4.0 stars and above currently make up 22% of campus vending machine stock, including **mixed nut packs**, dried fruit with no added sugar, and low-fat yoghurt. Products rated HSR 3.5-3.9 stars account for 38%, mainly protein bars and wholegrain biscuits. Products rated below HSR 3.0 stars (such as chocolate bars and sugary drinks) are limited to no more than 30% of the total capacity of each machine.

### Options for special dietary needs
About 12% of items in campus vending machines are labelled Gluten-Free and 8% Lactose-Free, mostly in the Kambri and MRTC machines. **Vegetarian** and **vegan** options make up 15% of stock, including plant-based protein bars and soy milk products. According to a 2024 survey by the ANU Multicultural Student Centre, demand for lactose-free options among international students has grown by 34% over the past two years.

### Pricing and subsidy policy
Under the 2024-2026 agreement between the ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) and the University, products rated HSR 4.0 stars and above in all vending machines must not be priced higher than 120% of the retail price at nearby supermarkets. For example, a protein bar sells for 4.50 Australian dollars in campus vending machines, while the average price at Coles is 3.80 Australian dollars. **Bottled water** (600ml) has a uniform price of 2.50 Australian dollars, below the average of 3.20 Australian dollars at Canberra city convenience stores.

## Payment methods and technical support

### Full cashless payment coverage
Since February 2025, all ANU vending machines have stopped accepting cash. Supported payment methods include: Visa/Mastercard contactless cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Samsung Pay, and the **ANU ID card** (top-up required through the campus card system). According to the ANU Finance Division's Q1 2025 report, contactless payments account for 78% of total transactions, with Apple Pay the highest at 41%.

### Mobile payments and campus app integration
The ANU ID App (iOS/Android) lets users pay by scanning a QR code, a feature launched in October 2024. The App also offers real-time vending machine stock queries, covering machines in Kambri, Hancock Library, and MRTC. Campus card balances generated through the **Flywire tuition payment** system can also be used for vending machine purchases, letting international students transfer leftover tuition funds onto their campus card through this channel.

### Fault reporting and refund mechanism
Each machine carries a sticker with the ANU Facilities and Services Division 24-hour hotline (+61 2 6125 1234) and a QR code for reporting faults. According to 2024 data from the ANU service desk, the average vending machine fault response time is 1.8 hours, and refunds take 3-5 working days to process. If a payment is deducted without the item being dispensed, students can submit a refund request directly through the "Vending Machine Issue" channel in the ANU ID App.

## Using machines outside operating hours

### Where the 24-hour machines are
A total of 14 vending machines on campus are located in 24-hour access areas, including: the Hancock Library 24-hour study area (2 machines), the Chifley Library entrance (1), the MRTC ground-floor lobby (1), the Kambri underground walkway (2), and the common areas of various residences (8 in total). These machines keep operating on weekends and public holidays and **are not suspended when campus closes**.

### Night-time safety and lighting
All 24-hour vending machine areas are fitted with LED lighting and CCTV monitoring, patrolled by the ANU Security Services division. According to the ANU Security Office's 2024 annual report, no major safety incidents occurred in vending machine areas at night (22:00-06:00), with patrols every 45 minutes. Students can contact security officers with one tap through the **SafeZone App**.

### Special arrangements for exam weeks
During weeks 13-16 of each semester (exam weeks), ANU sets up temporary vending machines at the following locations: the ground floor of Chifley Library, the lobby of the Peter Baume Building (Medical School), and the entrance of the **J.B. Chifley Building** (College of Law). These temporary machines mainly supply high-caffeine drinks and energy snacks, operating 07:00-23:00, and do not accept cash.

## Sustainability and environmental measures

### Packaging recycling program
In partnership with recycling company Close the Loop, ANU has placed dedicated recycling bins beside the vending machines in Kambri and Hancock Library, accepting plastic bottles, aluminium cans, and paper packaging. According to 2024 data from the ANU Sustainability Office, the program collects about 1,200 containers a month, a recycling rate of roughly 15% of total vending machine sales. **Reusable cups** get a 0.20 Australian dollar discount at some drinks machines, but this must be activated through the ANU ID App.

### Local supply chain
About 45% of the food and drink in vending machines comes from suppliers in Canberra or southern New South Wales, including Zest Coffee (Canberra), Fresh Produce Group (local fruit), and **The Healthy Baker** (wholegrain bread). ANU procurement policy (revised 2024) requires all vending machine operators to give priority to suppliers within 100 kilometres of campus.

### Energy use and carbon emissions
The ANU Facilities and Services Division's 2024 report shows that campus vending machines consume about 85,000 kWh of electricity a year, equivalent to the annual usage of 20 average Australian households. All machines have been upgraded to LED lighting and energy-efficient refrigeration, cutting energy use by 32% compared with the 2019 baseline. A **solar-powered** pilot is running on 3 machines in the Kambri area, with plans to extend it to all vending machines in 2026.

## FAQ

### Q1: Do ANU campus vending machines support WeChat Pay or Alipay?
Not at present. All ANU vending machines only accept Visa/Mastercard contactless cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Samsung Pay, and the ANU ID card. According to a statement from the ANU Finance Division in March 2025, the University is evaluating options for integrating Alipay and WeChat Pay, but no timeline has been set. International students can transfer funds into their campus card balance through the Flywire tuition payment system and then use it for vending machine purchases.

### Q2: What is the cheapest item in campus vending machines?
The cheapest item is bottled water (600ml), at a uniform price of 2.50 Australian dollars, below the average of 3.20 Australian dollars at Canberra city convenience stores. Next come energy bars (3.20 Australian dollars) and tea bags (2.80 Australian dollars). According to the ANU Students' Association 2024 price survey, the price of items rated HSR 4.0 stars and above in vending machines is capped at 120% of the retail price at nearby supermarkets.

### Q3: What should I do if a vending machine takes my money but does not dispense the item?
You can apply for a refund through the following channels: call the ANU Facilities and Services Division 24-hour hotline (+61 2 6125 1234), or submit a request through the "Vending Machine Issue" channel in the ANU ID App. According to 2024 data from the ANU service desk, refunds take an average of 3-5 working days. Keep the transaction screenshot and the machine number (located in the top-right corner of the machine panel).
