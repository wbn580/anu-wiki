As of 2024, the Australian National University (ANU) main Acton campus has **27** bicycle parking facilities with capacity for more than **1,200** bikes, 12 of them secure points with CCTV or fencing. According to the Urban Commuting Patterns Report published by the Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (BITRE, 2023), about 5.7% of residents of the Canberra metropolitan area commute by bike — higher than the national average of 3.2% — and much of that is linked to the "Green Commuter Program" ANU has run since 2021. Through subsidies and campus parking fee waivers, the program lets eligible students get shared-bike memberships at 40% below market rates. Based on 2024 public data from ANU Facilities Management (FM) and ACT Government transport planning documents, this article sets out the location of on-campus shared-bike parking points, usage rules and current discount schemes.

## Parking Point Locations and Capacity

### Main campus core
ANU's **bike parking points** on the main campus cluster around Chifley Library, the Peter Karmel Building and Union Court. South of Chifley Library there is a covered two-tier rack (capacity 120 bikes) with CCTV monitoring and overnight lighting. The ground floor west of the Peter Karmel Building has a further 80 open racks, but no rain cover. Between 9 am and 3 pm on weekdays, these points typically run at over 85% occupancy (ANU FM internal usage statistics, February 2024).

### Student residence areas
The entrances of Bruce Hall, Ursula College and Wright Hall all have dedicated parking areas. Wright Hall's **secure bike shed** requires student card access, holds 60 bikes, and has ground lock rings and an air pump station. Fenner Hall and Burgmann College use open racks with a capacity of 40 bikes each. The 2023 ANU student accommodation services annual report shows overnight occupancy of 62% in residence-area parking — lower than the teaching areas — but above 90% at the end of semester (the luggage-moving peak).

### Campus edges and interchange points
The east side of the Acton campus (near the City Bus Interchange) has 50 temporary spaces, mainly serving commuter students transferring to light rail. Under the "last mile" project run jointly by ANU and the ACT Government, this location has **30 dedicated shared-bike docking stations** compatible with Canberra Metro's bike-share system. The point turns over an average of 3.2 times a day (ACT Government, 2023, *Canberra Active Travel Report*).

## Shared-Bike Operators and How to Access Them

### Main operator
The shared-bike operator on the ANU campus is **Canberra Metro Bike Share** (CMBS), authorised by the ACT Government and operated by a private company. As of 2024, CMBS has deployed 200 bikes across 15 stations within ANU. The bikes have GPS tracking and 3-speed gearing, suited to short on-campus trips (<5 km). Users unlock bikes through the "Canberra Bike" app; the base rate is AUD 2.00 per 30 minutes.

### Student-specific access
ANU students can apply for the **"Green Commuter" membership** through the university portal; linking their student ID unlocks discounted rates. The membership has no minimum spend and waives the AUD 5.00 registration fee. Once activated, students can pick up and return bikes at any CMBS station on campus, and cross-campus rides (such as Acton to Kambri) are billed per minute with a daily cap of AUD 8.00.

### Links to campus transport
Shared-bike parking points sit within 50 metres' walking distance of **ANU shuttle bus (Route 50)** stops. For example, the Union Court parking point is right next to the shuttle shelter, letting students switch seamlessly between bike and bus. A 2023 ACT Government survey found that 22% of ANU students use the "bike + shuttle" combo for commuting at least once a week.

## Discounts and Subsidies

### Semester pass discount
Under the annual agreement between ANU Student Affairs (SA) and CMBS, full-time students buying a **semester pass** (18 weeks) get a 40% discount, paying AUD 89.00 (full price AUD 148.00). The pass covers unlimited rides of up to 30 minutes; longer rides are billed at 50% of the standard rate. About 1,400 students had registered for the program by first semester 2024.

### Extra benefits for residents
Students living in **ANU student accommodation** who also buy a semester pass get an extra AUD 20.00 voucher for the campus store (usable on repair tools or bike lights). The benefit, launched jointly by ANU Accommodation Services and CMBS, saw 380 vouchers issued in 2023. The application link comes in the residence move-in email — no extra form needed.

### Hardship assistance
Students holding an **ANU Access Scholarship** can have the shared-bike membership fee waived in full. Applicants must submit supporting documents to the student services desk within two weeks before the start of each semester; approval takes 5 business days. In 2023, 62 students received free riding credits through this channel, saving an average of AUD 120.00 each.

## Safety and Maintenance Rules

### Parking rules
All shared bikes must be returned to a **designated docking station** or within a **geo-fenced area**, otherwise a AUD 5.00 relocation fee applies. ANU's campus geo-fence covers all teaching and residential areas, but has a blind spot at the boundary towards Mount Stromlo. The ACT Government updated the fence map in January 2024, adding 3 boundary alert points.

### Helmets and lighting
Under ACT law, all riders must wear a helmet that meets the **AS/NZS 2063 standard**. CMBS offers rental helmets at every station (AUD 1.00 per use), but ANU recommends bringing your own. Riding at night (sunset to sunrise) requires front and rear lights; CMBS bikes have automatic LED sensor lights, but users should check battery status in the app.

### Damage reporting and insurance
Damage must be reported through the app within 24 hours, or the user may be liable for repair costs (capped at AUD 150.00). ANU students who buy the semester pass automatically get **third-party liability cover** (AUD 10,000,000) underwritten by QBE Insurance on behalf of CMBS. The cover does not include personal property losses (such as phones or backpacks).

## Peak Times and Usage Data

### Usage peaks
The ANU bike-share system peaks in **weeks 3 to 8 of semester**, averaging 420 rides a day (ANU FM data, March 2024). Short trips (<10 minutes) between 1 pm and 2 pm (lunchtime) account for the largest share, at 38%. Weekend usage runs at just 45% of weekday levels, concentrated between the residence areas and supermarkets.

### Parking point turnover
The highest-turnover point is **Kambri Precinct** (4.1 uses per dock per day), followed by Chifley Library (3.8). Bruce Hall's point turns over slowly (2.0) because of its position near a slope. Based on 2023 data, ANU Facilities Management has added 20 temporary docks at Kambri to ease crowding.

### Seasonal variation
Riding drops about 35% in winter (June-August), but ANU's subsidy programs do not pause. In July 2023, CMBS deployed 30 **electric assist shared bikes (E-bikes)** on the Acton campus, free to upgrade to for semester pass holders (2 hours per ride). These E-bikes were used 22% more than regular bikes during winter.

## Application and Activation Process

### Registration steps
Students apply through the "Green Commuter" page on the ANU website, entering their student ID and ACT driver licence number (or passport number if they do not drive). **Approval takes 2 business days**, after which the "ANU Student" tag appears in the app. Students under 18 need a guardian's consent form, available from the ANU student services desk.

### Payment methods
Semester pass fees can be deducted directly from the student account (same channel as tuition) or paid online by credit/debit card. When paying tuition across borders, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E5%AE%BF%E8%88%8D%E5%85%B1%E4%BA%AB%E5%8D%95%E8%BD%A6%E5%81%9C%E6%94%BE%E7%82%B9%E4%B8%8E%E4%BD%BF%E7%94%A8%E4%BC%98%E6%83%A0) to complete the currency exchange. Refund policy: full refund within 14 days of semester start, then pro-rata refund for the remaining weeks.

### Activation and first ride
After registering, students activate their membership by **scanning the QR code** at any CMBS station. Before the first ride, the system plays a 90-second safety video (available in English/Chinese). A 2023 ACT Government survey found that 94% of ANU students had no technical issues with the process.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can ANU shared bikes be returned at a different campus?
Yes. All CMBS stations (including Acton main campus, Kambri and city stations) support cross-campus returns at no extra cost. But returning outside the campus geo-fence (such as the Mount Stromlo area) costs AUD 5.00 per return. The ACT Government reported the geo-fence covering 12.4 square kilometres in 2024.

### Q2: Can the semester pass be used during holidays?
Yes. The semester pass covers 18 consecutive weeks, including the mid-semester break (2 weeks) and the exam period. However, during the summer break (December-February) the ANU-CMBS agreement is suspended, and students pay standard rates unless they buy an annual pass (AUD 199.00, available only to ACT residents).

### Q3: Who is responsible if a bike is stolen on campus?
CMBS bears the theft and damage risk for shared bikes, so users are not liable. But ANU is not responsible if a student's own personal bike is parked in a non-secure area (such as open racks). The 2023 ANU security report records 14 bike thefts on campus, 11 of them from non-secure parking. Students are advised to use the secure shed with lock rings and register their bike's serial number.
