In the Australian National University (ANU) postgraduate degree system, the **Thesis Proposal** is the critical milestone that determines whether research can proceed smoothly. According to the Higher Degree by Research (HDR) Policy Handbook released by the ANU Graduate Research office in 2024, all PhD candidates must pass the Oral Confirmation within 12 months of enrolment, while MPhil candidates must complete it within 9 months. The document also notes that in 2023, about 15% of PhD students failed the proposal milestone and had to resubmit or revise their research direction [ANU, 2024, HDR Policy and Procedures]. In addition, a 2023 global survey by **Times Higher Education (THE)** found that more than 60% of postgraduate researchers considered "the depth and structure of the literature review" the most challenging part of writing a proposal [THE, 2023, Postgraduate Research Experience Survey]. Understanding ANU's specific requirements for proposal format, literature review standards and oral defence preparation is therefore directly relevant to reducing the risk of research delays and keeping your degree on track.

## Official Template and Structural Requirements

### Core Components

The ANU Graduate Research office provides a unified **Thesis Proposal Template** divided into five mandatory modules: the research title (no more than 30 English words), Research Questions and Aims, the **Literature Review**, the Methodology, and the Timeline. Word limits for each module vary by college: in the ANU College of Engineering and Computer Science (CECS), for example, the literature review section typically requires 2,000 to 3,000 words, while the College of Humanities and Social Sciences (CHL) may allow up to 4,000 words [ANU CECS, 2024, HDR Student Handbook].

### Format and Submission Rules

The template requires 12-point Times New Roman font, 1.5 line spacing and 2.54 cm margins. Citation style must follow APA 7th edition or the Chicago Manual of Style, as specified by the college. Submissions must be uploaded as a PDF through the **ANU HDR management system (HDR Hub)** together with a signed confirmation page from the Supervisor. Students who miss the deadline may have their scholarship (such as the ANU PhD Scholarship) suspended — 8 such cases were recorded in 2023 [ANU Graduate Research, 2024, HDR Hub User Guide].

## The Depth and Criticality of the Literature Review

### Moving from Description to Argument

ANU's expectations for the literature review go far beyond listing existing research. The official assessment criteria explicitly require students to demonstrate **Critical Analysis** — identifying research gaps, methodological limitations or theoretical contradictions in the literature. An analysis of ANU PhD thesis examination feedback from 2022 shows that more than 70% of examiners listed "literature review lacking criticality" as a common item in their revision comments [ANU, 2022, HDR Examination Reports Internal Analysis].

### Literature Search and Citation Strategy

Students must conduct systematic searches using **ANU Library databases** (such as Scopus, Web of Science and JSTOR). The proposal should list at least 50 to 80 core references (PhD usually requires 80 or more, MPhil 50), including literature published within the last 5 years. When citing, avoid over-reliance on a single source and demonstrate coverage of different theoretical schools. ## The Logical Chain of Research Questions and Aims

### Applying the SMART Principle to Research Questions

Research questions must meet the **SMART criteria**: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. In guidelines updated in 2023, the ANU Graduate Research office stresses that there should be no more than 3 research questions, and each question must map directly to a research aim. For example, if the question is "What is the impact of Australia's carbon trading policy on agricultural carbon emissions?", the aim must be specific enough to "quantify changes in emissions data between 2015 and 2023" [ANU Graduate Research, 2023, Thesis Proposal Writing Guide].

### Connecting to the Literature Review

Research questions must emerge naturally from the literature review. Examiners typically ask in the oral defence: "How does your research question fill the gap you identified in the literature?" Students should therefore ensure that every question has a corresponding "gap" in the literature review section. 2024 internal training materials note that about 40% of first-time proposals suffer from "disconnect between questions and literature" [ANU HDR Training, 2024, Proposal Workshop Materials].

## The Operability of the Research Methodology

### Data Collection and Analysis Plans

The methodology section must detail the data sources (such as ANU on-campus experimental data, the Australian Bureau of Statistics ABS public databases, or fieldwork data), the sample size or case selection criteria, and the data analysis tools (such as SPSS, NVivo, Python or R). For quantitative research, a **Power Analysis** must be conducted in advance to determine the minimum sample size; for qualitative research, the sampling strategy for interviews or observations (such as purposive sampling) must be explained. The ANU College of Social Sciences requires all research involving human participants to include the approval number or application status of the **Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC)** [ANU HREC, 2024, Ethics Application Guidelines].

### Timeline and Feasibility

The timeline in the template must be presented as a Gantt Chart covering the 36-month (PhD) or 24-month (MPhil) cycle from proposal to final submission. Each phase (data collection, data analysis, drafting) should be assigned specific months, with a 3 to 6-month buffer reserved for revisions and defence preparation. ANU 2023 data shows that PhD students take an average of 42 months to complete, about 16.7% beyond plan [ANU Planning & Performance, 2023, HDR Completion Statistics].

## Oral Defence Process and Assessment Criteria

### Format and Duration

The Oral Confirmation usually lasts 45 to 60 minutes, comprising a 15 to 20-minute student presentation and 30 to 40 minutes of questions from the examination panel. The panel has 3 members: the Chair (principal supervisor), an Independent Assessor from the same college, and an External Assessor from another college or institution. The defence is conducted in English, and the presentation must be supported by slides (such as PowerPoint or Prezi) [ANU, 2024, Oral Confirmation Guidelines].

### Assessment Dimensions and Common Questions

Scoring is based on four dimensions: Significance (25%), Rigour of methodology (30%), Mastery of literature (25%) and Communication (20%). Common questions include: "How does your research address potential ethical risks?", "If your data results do not match expectations, what is your fallback plan?" and "What specific contribution does your research make to policy or practice?" ANU internal statistics from 2023 show that about 12% of students were graded "Major Revision" at their first defence and had to re-defend within 3 months [ANU HDR Committee, 2023, Annual Review Report].

## Common Mistakes and Remedial Measures

### The "Checklist-Style" Literature Review Trap

The most common mistake is writing the literature review as a checklist of "author A said X, author B said Y" rather than an integrated analysis. The remedy is **Thematic Organization**: group the literature by theoretical school, methodology or time period, and close each group with a summary sentence that points directly to the research gap. The ANU Academic Skills Centre offers free literature review workshops; 320 people attended in the first quarter of 2024 [ANU Academic Skills, 2024, Workshop Attendance Data].

### Overly Optimistic Timelines

Many students underestimate the time needed for data collection or ethics approval. For example, research involving Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Communities can require 6 to 9 months for ethics approval. It is recommended to include "ethics approval" as a standalone phase in the timeline and to benchmark against the average duration of past cases in your college. The ANU Human Research Ethics Committee's average approval time in 2023 was 8.2 weeks [ANU HREC, 2024, Processing Times Report].

## FAQ

### Q1: Is there a word limit for the thesis proposal? Does it include the reference list?
The official ANU template sets no uniform word limit, but each college has its own rules. For example, the College of Engineering and Computer Science (CECS) requires the main text (excluding references and appendices) to be no more than 6,000 words, while the College of Humanities and Social Sciences (CHL) typically allows 8,000 words. The reference list does not count toward the word limit, but appendices (such as survey instruments) usually take up additional pages.

### Q2: If I fail the oral confirmation, how many re-defence attempts do I get?
Under ANU 2024 policy, students have a maximum of 2 re-defence attempts. After a first failure, students have 3 months to revise and resubmit; if the second attempt also fails, they may face a downgrade of their degree (for example, from PhD to MPhil) or termination of enrolment. In 2023, only 3.2% of students still failed after the second defence [ANU Graduate Research, 2024, HDR Policy and Procedures].

### Q3: How many references count as "enough" in the literature review?
For a PhD proposal, the ANU examination panel typically expects at least 80 core references; for MPhil, 50. The literature should span the last 10 years, with at least 40% published within the last 5 years. Sources should be predominantly peer-reviewed journal articles, and textbooks and grey literature (such as government reports) combined should not exceed 20% of the total.
