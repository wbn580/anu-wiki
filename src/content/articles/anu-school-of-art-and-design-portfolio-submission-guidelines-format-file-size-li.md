Applications for undergraduate and postgraduate programs at the Australian National University (ANU) School of Art and Design usually require a portfolio as the core assessment material. According to ANU's official 2025 admissions handbook, the portfolio accounts for about 60% to 70% of admission decisions, with academic results and English language ability making up the remainder. For the 2025 intake, applicants must submit by 30 November 2024 (round 1) or 28 February 2025 (round 2) [ANU 2024, Admissions Guide for International Undergraduate Applicants]. Based on the latest guidelines published by ANU, this article sets out the portfolio format requirements, file size limits, content structure and assessment criteria as a practical reference framework for applicants.

## Format Requirements and File Types

### Accepted File Formats
The ANU School of Art and Design requires all portfolios to be submitted **digitally**, uploaded through the university-designated Slidesroom platform. Supported still-image formats are JPEG, PNG, TIFF and BMP, with JPEG and PNG preferred for their balance of compression quality and compatibility. Video works must be submitted as MP4, with a recommended resolution of 1920×1080 pixels and a frame rate of at least 24 fps. Animation or interactive works may include an additional PDF explanatory document, but the core content must still be presented as video [ANU 2024, Portfolio Submission Guidelines].

### File Size and Naming Rules
Each file may be at most **20 MB**, and the entire portfolio must not exceed **200 MB** in total. If your work includes high-resolution video or large images, use compression tools (such as Adobe Media Encoder) to bring files into compliance. File names must follow the pattern "Surname_GivenName_WorkNumber_WorkTitle", for example "Wang_Ming_01_UrbanLandscape.jpg". Files named incorrectly may be rejected automatically by the system or cause delays in review.

## Portfolio Content Structure

### Core Works: Quantity and Types
The portfolio should contain **8 to 12 original works**, at least 4 of which were completed within the last two years. Works should span at least two media — for example painting, sculpture, digital media, photography or printmaking. For applicants to the Visual Arts stream, **3 observational drawing works** are recommended; for the Design stream, submit **2 projects** that show the design process from sketch to finished piece. The portfolio must open with a **personal statement of no more than 300 words** explaining your creative motivation and artistic direction.

### Work Descriptions and Supplementary Materials
Each work needs a written description of **50 to 100 words**, including the work's title, year of creation, materials used, dimensions (for three-dimensional works) and technical parameters (such as lens focal length or shutter speed). For collaborative works, clearly mark your individual contribution and attach a consent form from your collaborators. Extra materials such as exhibition certificates, publications or award proofs can be combined into a single PDF, but this file does not count toward the number of works and must not exceed 10 MB.

## Assessment Criteria and Weighting

### Technical Ability and Command of Media
The review panel first assesses your **technical proficiency**, worth **35%** of the scoring weight. This covers basic competence in your chosen media (such as brush control in oil painting or fluency in digital software) and the finish of the works. ANU explicitly wants the portfolio to show "the full process from conception to execution", so drafts, process shots and iteration versions earn extra credit. Applicants to digital media, for example, should include layer screenshots or timeline records to demonstrate a complete technical workflow.

### Conceptual Depth and Originality
**Conceptual innovation** carries **30%** of the scoring weight. Reviewers look for independent critical thinking rather than simple imitation of existing styles. ANU encourages applicants to explore themes tied to contemporary social issues, such as climate change, identity or technology ethics. At least one work in the portfolio must explicitly respond to the theme of "Australian local culture" or "artistic traditions of the Asia-Pacific region", reflecting the cross-cultural research direction added when the school revised its curriculum in 2024 [ANU 2024, School of Art and Design Curriculum Update].

### Narrative Logic and Portfolio Curation
The portfolio's **overall narrative structure** accounts for **20%** of the weight. Reviewers expect works ordered logically — for example, from early explorations to mature pieces, or grouped by theme. Transitions between works should feel natural, avoiding jarring stylistic leaps. Applicants should run at least three mock sequencing passes before submission and use Slidesroom's "preview mode" to check visual consistency.

### Personal Statement and Its Alignment with the Works
**The alignment between your personal statement and your works** carries **15%** of the weight. The creative ideas stated in the statement need concrete support in the portfolio. For example, if the statement stresses "attention to marginalised urban communities", the portfolio should include relevant portraits, scenes or documentary works. ANU's review panel noted in a 2023 internal report that about 40% of rejected applications were eliminated directly because the statement and the works did not connect [ANU 2023, Portfolio Review Internal Report].

## Submission Process and Deadlines

### Platform Registration and Upload Steps
Applicants log into Slidesroom through the ANU Admissions Portal, create a personal account and select the "Art and Design Portfolio" entry point. Before uploading: sort works into folders by category — "still images", "video" and "PDF" — with each folder no larger than 50 MB; use the platform's built-in **"annotation tool"** to tag each work (e.g. "life drawing" or "digital rendering"); and finally sign an electronic declaration confirming the works are original. The system sends a confirmation email within 48 hours of submission; if it doesn't arrive, check your spam folder or contact the admissions office.

### Deadlines and Extension Policy
The key deadlines for 2025 entry are: **round 1 — 30 November 2024** (23:59 Australian Eastern Daylight Time) and **round 2 — 28 February 2025**. Late applications require a written explanation and may be placed on a waitlist. ANU allows extensions for medical or family emergencies, provided supporting documents are supplied (such as a doctor's certificate or death certificate), and no later than 14 days after the deadline. International students should also allow for visa processing time and are advised to start their student visa application immediately after submitting the portfolio.

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

### Format and Size Violations
About **15%** of applications are automatically blocked by the system for wrong file formats or oversized files [ANU 2024, Technical Support FAQ]. Common problems include video in AVI or MOV format (convert to MP4 instead), images above 4000×4000 pixels (which load slowly), and total portfolio size over 200 MB. How to avoid them: batch-compress files with a "file checking tool" (such as ImageOptim) before uploading; cut long videos to under 5 minutes; and strip unnecessary metadata.

### Repetitive Content or Lack of Depth
Some applicants submit multiple variations of the same subject (for example, 10 sunset photos of the same scene), which reviewers read as **insufficient creative range**. Show a different technical or conceptual challenge in each work. Also avoid overused internet memes or template-driven designs (such as Instagram filter effects) — ANU reviewers noted at a 2024 workshop that such works tend to score low on "originality".

## FAQ

### Q1: Can the portfolio include class assignments or copies of existing works?
Yes, but they must be clearly labelled. Class assignments completed within the last two years can count as core works if you attach the course name and the tutor's feedback. Copies (such as reproductions of famous paintings) may make up at most **20%** of the portfolio (that is, 2 works), and their descriptions must state the source of the original and the purpose of the copy. ANU values originality most, so use class assignments as a supplement rather than the bulk of the portfolio.

### Q2: Do video works need subtitles or narration?
Subtitles are not mandatory, but adding text explanations for key visual elements is recommended. Videos are capped at **10 minutes**; anything longer should be cut to the essential parts. Narration may be in English or Chinese, but non-English narration must include English subtitles. Reviewers noted in a 2024 report that about 25% of video works lost marks for lacking a clear narrative structure, so write a script in advance and control the pacing.

### Q3: If my portfolio is rejected, can I resubmit?
Yes, but you must wait for the next application cycle. ANU allows the same applicant to submit a portfolio **twice** within one admissions year, but the second submission must be made **within 30 days** of the first result being announced and include at least **3 new works**. The most common reasons for first-time rejection are technical errors or disorganised content, so consider attending the school's free "portfolio feedback workshops" (bookable in March and September each year) before resubmitting.
