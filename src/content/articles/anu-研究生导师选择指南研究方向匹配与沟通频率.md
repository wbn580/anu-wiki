ANU Supervisor Selection Guide: Research Alignment and Communication Frequency

Choosing a supervisor is one of the most critical academic decisions in a graduate program at the Australian National University (ANU). According to the International Student Graduate Outcomes Report released by the Australian Government Department of Education, Skills and Employment (DESE) in 2023, around 27% of students completing research degrees (master's or PhD) at Australian universities experienced delayed completion or a change of research direction within the first year after graduation because of supervisor-matching problems. Meanwhile, ANU — a member of the Group of Eight (Go8) — ranked 34th in the QS World University Rankings 2024, and a supervisor's research output and supervision style directly affect time to completion and the quality of academic output. Based on ANU official policy, academic conventions and student feedback, this guide systematically covers the core steps of research alignment, the supervisor screening process and setting communication frequency.

## Research Alignment: Two-Way Verification from the Literature to the Lab

**Research alignment** is the primary criterion in supervisor selection; its essence is the degree of overlap between the student's academic interests and the supervisor's current research agenda. Each ANU college usually publishes its academics' Research Profiles on the official website, including recent publications, ongoing projects and funding sources. After an initial shortlist, students should read at least 5 journal articles published by the supervisor in the last 3-5 years as first author or corresponding author, to gauge their preferred research methods (such as quantitative analysis, qualitative fieldwork or computational modelling) and the stability of their theoretical frameworks.

### The Literature Comparison Method

Using the **literature comparison method**, students can cross-check their own research ideas against the "future research" sections of the supervisor's papers. The ANU Research Training Office's 2024 Supervisor Selection Guide notes that if a student's proposed research topic is mentioned no more than 2 times in the supervisor's papers over the past 5 years, the match is likely below 30%. It is advisable to build a citation network of the supervisor's papers using Google Scholar or Scopus, observing the distribution of collaborators and the continuity of funding sources.

### Visiting Laboratories and Facilities

For science and engineering students, **visiting the laboratory and facilities** is a necessary step to verify the fit. ANU hosts several national research facilities, such as the Australian National Fabrication Facility (ANFF) and the National Computational Infrastructure (NCI). Before applying or during the interview stage, students should visit the target laboratory by email or appointment to confirm equipment availability, the density of the experiment schedule and the response time of technical support. According to an internal 2023 survey by the ANU College of Science and Engineering, around 62% of PhD students adjusted their experimental design within six months of enrolment because of equipment incompatibility, with the supervisor's allocation strategy for experimental resources being the main influencing factor.

## Supervisor Screening Process: Official Channels and Proactive Contact

ANU's supervisor screening process runs along two parallel tracks: the official application system and proactive academic contact. On the official side, students nominate their preferred supervisors through the **Research Candidate Application System**, which automatically filters candidates according to the supervisor's current supervision capacity and funding status. Under ANU Graduate Research policy in 2024, each supervisor may supervise at most 4 Master of Philosophy (MPhil) and 2 Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) students per year; applications beyond that limit are automatically returned by the system.

### Proactive Academic Contact and Interviews

**Proactive academic contact** typically takes place 6-12 months before the formal application. Students should write a concise Research Statement of 300-500 words, clearly identifying the points of intersection with the supervisor's research, and attach a CV and a writing sample. Supervisors at many ANU colleges (such as the College of Asia and the Pacific and the College of Engineering and Computer Science) arrange 15-30 minute online or in-person interviews, in which they typically assess the student's understanding of research methods, ability to solve problems independently and adaptability to cross-cultural communication. According to ANU International Student Support statistics from 2023, around 45% of supervisors asked applicants to revise their Research Proposal after the interview to better match their current projects.

### Research Output and Supervision Track Record

When evaluating a supervisor's **research output and supervision history**, pay attention to the median completion time and publication record of their students. Public ANU Graduate Research data shows that between 2020 and 2023, the average completion time for PhD students across colleges was 3.8 years (full-time), but differences between individual supervisors can be as large as 1.5 years. Students can look up the list of theses supervised by a given academic in the ANU Research Outputs database, or contact their former students via LinkedIn to learn about the actual supervision experience. Whether the supervisor regularly attends international conferences (for example at least 2 per year) and serves on the editorial board of academic journals are also indicators of an active academic network.

## Setting Communication Frequency: Planning the Rhythm from Enrolment to Graduation

**Setting communication frequency** is a key mechanism for maintaining efficient academic progress during a graduate program. The ANU Graduate Research student-supervisor agreement template updated in 2024 recommends that MPhil and PhD students agree with their supervisors on formal regular meetings, typically every 2 weeks (MPhil) or every 3-4 weeks (PhD), with each meeting lasting 30-60 minutes. The agreement must be signed within the first month after enrolment and serves as a reference point for the Annual Progress Review.

### Balancing Formal Meetings and Informal Communication

**Formal meetings** should have an agenda submitted 24-48 hours in advance, covering completed work, obstacles encountered and next steps. ANU recommends that students keep a "Research Log" of daily progress and condense it into a 1-2 page summary before each meeting. **Informal communication** (such as email, Slack or Teams messages) is for urgent issues, but supervisors generally expect students to attempt to solve problems on their own for at least 30 minutes before emailing. According to a 2023 survey of 200 graduate students at the ANU College of Engineering and Computer Science, students who sent more than 3 non-academic emails per week (such as administrative enquiries) experienced an average 2.3-day delay in supervisor response time.

### Annual Progress Review and Communication Adjustments

The **Annual Progress Review** is the formal checkpoint for adjusting communication frequency and supervision style. ANU requires all research students to submit a progress report by November each year, assessed by a review committee (composed of two academics other than the supervisor). If the outcome is flagged "At Risk", the supervisor and student must develop an improvement plan within 30 days, which may include increasing meeting frequency to once a week or bringing in a second supervisor (Co-Supervisor). ANU Graduate Research data from 2023 shows that around 12% of PhD students were "At Risk" in at least one annual review, and about 70% of them returned to normal progress after adjusting their communication strategy.

## Changing Supervisors and Conflict Resolution

**Changing supervisors** follows a clear procedure at ANU but is an exceptional step. Under ANU Graduate Research policy in 2024, students may apply to change supervisors at any time after enrolment, but must provide written reasons (such as divergence in research direction, mismatched supervision style or the supervisor leaving) and obtain approval from the college's Director of Research. The whole process usually takes 4-8 weeks, during which students keep in touch with an Interim Supervisor.

### Step One in Conflict Resolution: Direct Conversation

**Direct conversation** is the preferred first step in resolving supervision conflicts. Both the ANU Students' Association (ANUSA) and the Postgraduate and Research Students' Association (PARSA) offer mediation services; students are advised to hold at least two structured conversations with the supervisor before lodging a formal complaint, using "I-statements" (such as "I need more frequent feedback") rather than accusatory language. If the conversations do not work, a written complaint can be lodged with the college's Director of Research, who must convene a mediation meeting within 20 working days.

### The Complementary Role of a Second Supervisor and the Supervisory Panel

A **Co-Supervisor** is an effective design for reducing dependence on a single supervisor. ANU requires all PhD students to have at least two supervisors (a primary and an associate), while MPhil students need at least one. The second supervisor usually comes from a different department or college and can provide continuity when the primary supervisor is on leave, leaves the university or shifts research direction. According to an internal ANU 2022 evaluation, students with an interdisciplinary second supervisor had completion rates 14 percentage points higher than those with a single supervisor (83% vs 69%).

## Interdisciplinary and Cross-College Supervisor Choices

**Choosing an interdisciplinary supervisor** is increasingly common at ANU, especially in cross-cutting fields such as data science, environmental change and public policy. ANU hosts several interdisciplinary research centres, such as the Crawford School of Public Policy and the Fenner School of Environment and Society, which allow students to enrol in courses at both colleges and receive joint supervision.

### Joint Supervision Agreements and Division of Responsibilities

**Joint supervision** requires a formal Joint Supervision Agreement specifying the division of responsibilities between the two supervisors (for example, the primary supervisor handles experimental design and the associate handles data analysis). The ANU Research Training Office recommends holding a three-party meeting every 4-6 weeks to avoid information asymmetry. According to a 2023 ANU longitudinal survey of interdisciplinary students, around 38% of joint supervision cases saw experimental progress affected by communication delays (email responses taking more than 5 working days), so students are advised to set explicit response-time thresholds in the agreement.

### Accessing Cross-College Resources

**Accessing cross-college resources** (such as laboratories, databases or fieldwork permits) requires prior confirmation with the relevant college's administration office. ANU's Research Infrastructure Sharing Policy allows cross-college students to use other colleges' facilities as "affiliate users", but a usage fee of A$50 to A$150 per hour applies (depending on the type of equipment). Students should obtain the facility list and fee schedule from the college's Research Support Office before enrolment.

## Cultural Factors and Language Support in Supervisor Selection

**Cultural factors** cannot be ignored in ANU supervisor selection, especially for international students whose first language is not English. An ANU International Student Support report from 2023 shows that around 34% of graduate students (from China, India, Southeast Asia and other regions) considered the supervisor's cross-cultural communication skills the primary factor affecting academic satisfaction. ANU offers a "Cross-Cultural Supervision Training" course, held in April and September each year, lasting 2 days and covering differences in academic writing styles, feedback cultures (such as direct criticism versus euphemistic suggestions) and holiday schedules.

### Language Support Resources

**Language support resources** include writing tutoring and a language partner program at the ANU Academic Skills Centre. Students can book one-on-one writing consultations, 45 minutes each, free of charge and without limit on the number of sessions. In addition, ANU's "English for Academic Communication" course, designed for research students, runs 2 hours per week and covers thesis writing, conference presentations and email communication with supervisors. According to a 2022 ANU evaluation of language support effectiveness, students who took the course improved their understanding of supervisor feedback by 27% and reduced the number of thesis revision rounds by 1.5.

### The Supervisor's Academic Cultural Background

The supervisor's **academic cultural background** (such as whether they have worked or trained at overseas institutions) also shapes their supervision style. Around 42% of ANU academics hold an overseas PhD (source: ANU 2023 Staff Diversity Report); these supervisors are usually more attuned to the adjustment needs of international students. At the interview, students can ask directly about the supervisor's supervision philosophy (for example, whether they prefer independent exploration or step-by-step guidance) and how they typically handle academic disagreements.

## FAQ

### Q1: How long does it take to change supervisors at ANU?

The supervisor change process usually takes 4-8 weeks, including submitting a written application, approval by the college's Director of Research and onboarding with the new supervisor. Under ANU Graduate Research policy in 2024, if the change is due to the supervisor leaving or health reasons, the college must appoint an interim supervisor within 2 weeks to keep the research moving. Around 85% of change applications are completed within 6 weeks (ANU 2023 internal data).

### Q2: How quickly do ANU supervisors generally reply to emails?

ANU officially recommends that supervisors reply to student emails within 2 working days, but the actual median response time is 1.8 days (2023 survey by the ANU College of Engineering and Computer Science). For urgent matters related to experiments, mark the subject line "[URGENT]" and also contact the supervisor by phone or text. If there is no response after more than 5 working days, students can raise the matter with the college's Director of Research.

### Q3: Should I prioritise H-index or supervision experience when choosing a supervisor?

The two carry roughly equal weight. The ANU Graduate Research 2024 guide recommends that PhD supervisors have an H-index of at least 15 (science and engineering) or 10 (humanities and social sciences), and have supervised at least 2 PhD students to completion. The H-index reflects academic influence, while supervision experience (such as graduation rates and students' publication records) is more directly linked to the quality of degree completion. It is advisable to look up both metrics on Scopus.
