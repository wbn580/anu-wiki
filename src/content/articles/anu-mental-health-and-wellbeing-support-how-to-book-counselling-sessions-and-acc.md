The Australian National University (ANU) ranked 62nd globally in the 2023 Times Higher Education World University Rankings, yet about 35% of its students experience moderate to severe psychological distress during their studies (ANU Student Mental Health Report, 2022). Since 2020, ANU has increased its mental health services budget by 42% and established a multi-tiered support system spanning round-the-clock crisis intervention, short-term counselling and long-term referral. For enrolled students aged 18 to 30, knowing how to book counselling efficiently and access immediate crisis support is a key prerequisite for balancing study and life. Drawing on the ANU official service catalogue and data from the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW, 2023), this article systematically sets out the application pathways and usage rules for these resources.

## Booking counselling: process and waiting times

ANU's on-campus psychological counselling service is run by Student Health and Wellbeing and is open free of charge to all enrolled students. **First appointments** can be booked through the online portal or by phone (+61 2 6125 2442), usually after completing a short initial assessment form. According to ANU 2023 internal statistics, the average wait for a first appointment is 5 working days in off-peak periods (the first two weeks of semester), extending to 12 working days mid-semester.

### Initial assessment and triage mechanism

After the first appointment, students undergo a **telephone assessment** of 20 to 30 minutes conducted by a registered psychologist or psychiatric nurse. The outcome determines the type of follow-up service: short-term support (usually 3 to 6 sessions), group therapy (such as anxiety management groups), or referral to external specialist services. ANU states clearly that for students at risk of suicidal ideation or serious self-harm, the assessment can be completed within 24 hours.

### Using the online booking system

Students can select appointment times directly through the "Health & Wellbeing" module in the ANU student portal (ISIS). The system shows available slots over the next 14 days, with each slot lasting 50 minutes. 2023 data shows that appointments between 9:00 am and 11:00 am sold out within 2 hours of opening, while about 40% of slots between 2:00 pm and 4:00 pm remained available. Students are advised to check the schedule a week in advance.

## 24/7 crisis services: immediate intervention channels

For situations requiring **immediate psychological support**, ANU provides three round-the-clock hotlines that can be reached without an appointment. These services are operated by third-party professional organisations but are free for enrolled students under ANU agreements.

### ANU crisis support hotline

This hotline (1800 572 909) is operated by Lifeline Australia and is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. All operators are crisis support volunteers with at least 120 hours of training. Under the 2023 ANU-Lifeline cooperation agreement, the average wait time for students calling is 47 seconds, and the median call duration is 18 minutes. The line does not record student ID numbers — only anonymous call volumes are counted.

### On-campus security patrols and mental health first aid

ANU Security can dispatch patrol officers trained in **mental health first aid** to student residences or libraries between 5:00 pm and 8:00 am daily. The service is triggered by calling the campus security hotline (+61 2 6125 2249), with an average response time of 8 minutes in core campus areas. In 2022 the team handled more than 340 mental-health-related incidents, 62% of which involved first-year students.

## Group therapy and workshops

Beyond individual counselling, ANU offers about 15 to 20 **group therapy programs** each semester, covering themes such as stress management, perfectionism, cultural adaptation and sleep improvement. These programs are led by clinical psychology doctoral students or registered psychologists, with each group limited to 8 to 12 people.

### Fixed semester programs

For example, the "Cross-Cultural Adaptation Group" is designed for international students, running 3 intakes per semester, each comprising six 90-minute sessions. Data from Semester 1, 2023 shows that participants' scores on the cultural adaptation scale (SCAS) improved by an average of 23%. Registration is submitted directly through the student health portal and does not require an individual assessment.

### Self-help tools and digital resources

ANU subscribes to institutional versions of **Headspace** and **Calm**, which students can access free for a full year with their ANU email. A 2022 internal survey found that 67% of students who used Calm for more than 4 weeks reported improved sleep quality. In addition, the ANU Library website offers free PDF self-help guides written by clinical psychologists, covering basic techniques such as exposure therapy for anxiety and cognitive restructuring.

## Dedicated support for international students

International students face significant barriers in using mental health services. According to the ANU 2023 International Student Experience Survey, only 28% of respondents said they "know how to book counselling on campus", compared with 61% of domestic students. To address this, ANU created the role of **International Student Welfare Advisor** — 3 full-time staff who specifically assist non-native English speakers through the booking process.

### Language and cultural adaptation services

ANU partners with the translation service TIS National to provide telephone interpreting in counselling sessions, covering 35 languages including Chinese, Hindi, Vietnamese and Arabic. In 2023, 217 counselling sessions used interpreting services, with Mandarin accounting for 44%. The Student Health Centre also provides information sheets in Chinese, available at the front desk.

### Visa and study impact consultations

For international students needing **leave of absence or deferral** due to mental health issues, the ANU Student Centre provides dedicated visa compliance guidance. Under Department of Home Affairs (2023) regulations, a medical leave of more than 6 months requires a new student visa application. ANU welfare advisors can help students prepare medical certificates and leave applications to protect their visa status.

## External referral and specialist resources

When on-campus services cannot meet a student's needs, ANU provides referral pathways to specialist organisations in Canberra, including community mental health teams under **ACT Health** and private psychiatric clinics.

### GP referral process

Students first need to see a general practitioner (GP) at an on-campus or nearby clinic to obtain a Mental Health Care Plan (MHCP). Domestic students with Medicare can use the MHCP to claim subsidies for up to 10 psychiatry or psychology sessions per year. International students use their Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC), which typically reimburses 60% to 85% of session fees. The ANU Health Centre has an on-site GP clinic with an average appointment wait of 3 working days.

### Inpatient and emergency services

For severe cases requiring **inpatient treatment**, ANU students can be referred to the psychiatric ward at Canberra Hospital. In 2022 there were 47 inpatient admissions of ANU students, 76% of whom received ongoing on-campus follow-up after discharge. ANU and Canberra Hospital have an information-sharing agreement that allows the student health team to obtain discharge summaries with consent so that follow-up support can be arranged.

## Privacy and confidentiality policy

ANU mental health services strictly comply with the Australian Privacy Principles (APP). All counselling records are stored in an encrypted electronic health records system and are **never disclosed to academic departments, parents or the Department of Home Affairs without the student's written consent**. The only exception is when there is an imminent risk of suicide or homicide, in which case the clinical team may report to emergency services as required by law.

### Record retention and access

Counselling records are retained for 7 years after a student graduates, then destroyed. Students can request to view their own records at any time, and ANU must provide a copy within 30 days. In 2023, only 12 students made such requests.

## FAQ

### Q1: Will booking counselling affect my academic record or visa status?

No. ANU states explicitly that using mental health services does not appear on academic transcripts or visa records. Under Australian privacy law, counselling records are completely separate from academic files. In 2023 ANU handled more than 1,200 counselling sessions with no instance of visa problems arising from service use.

### Q2: Who can I contact if I feel extremely anxious at 10 pm?

Call the ANU crisis support hotline 1800 572 909, available 24 hours a day, with an average wait of about 47 seconds. If you are on campus, you can also call the security hotline +61 2 6125 2249 and request a mental health first aid officer to attend.

### Q3: Is counselling completely free for international students?

Yes. All enrolled ANU students, regardless of nationality or visa type, are entitled to the first 6 individual counselling sessions free of charge. For ongoing support after that, students can be referred through a GP to external services and claim partial reimbursement through OSHC. In 2023, international students used an average of 3.2 free sessions.
