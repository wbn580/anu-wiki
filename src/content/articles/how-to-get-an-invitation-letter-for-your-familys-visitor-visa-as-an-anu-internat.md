The Australian National University (ANU) ranked 30th in the 2025 QS World University Rankings, attracting international students from more than 100 countries. For many international students at ANU, inviting family to visit Australia is a common need, and obtaining an Invitation Letter that meets the requirements of the Department of Home Affairs is a key step in applying for a Visitor Visa (subclass 600). According to the Migration and Citizenship Program report published by the Department of Home Affairs in 2024, visitor visa applications exceeded 4.5 million in the 2023-24 financial year, with family visits accounting for a significant share. Drawing on ANU's official policies and Australian immigration regulations, this article sets out the essentials of writing an invitation letter, the required documents, and answers to common questions, giving ANU international students a practical, actionable guide.

## The Legal Status of the Invitation Letter and Visa Categories

An **invitation letter** is not a mandatory document for an Australian visitor visa, but it can significantly boost the visa officer's confidence in the genuineness of the applicant's intended visit. According to the Visitor Visa Application Guidelines updated by the Department of Home Affairs in 2024, visa officers primarily assess whether the applicant meets the **"Genuine Student" (GS)** criterion. As supporting evidence, the invitation letter should clearly state the relationship between the inviter and the invitee, the purpose of the visit, the length of stay, and accommodation arrangements.

The **Sponsored Family Visitor Stream** of the Visitor Visa (subclass 600) allows Australian citizens, permanent residents, or eligible New Zealand citizens to sponsor relatives. However, international students can usually only apply under the ordinary Tourist Stream, in which case the invitation letter serves to demonstrate the relationship between the student and their family and the reasonableness of the visit. ANU's International Student Services recommends attaching the student's Enrolment Letter and a copy of the passport to corroborate the student's identity.

## Core Content of an Effective Invitation Letter

A valid invitation letter should include the following **6 key sections**, each providing specific information rather than vague statements.

**Inviter's details**: The student's full name (matching the passport), date of birth, ANU student ID number, current address, and contact details. The address should be the actual residential address in Canberra (such as an ANU on-campus residence or an off-campus rental), not the home address back in China.

**Invitee's details**: Each family member's full name, date of birth, and relationship to the inviter (e.g., father, mother, sibling). If inviting multiple people, list them separately and state whether they are travelling on the same itinerary.

**Purpose and itinerary of the visit**: Clearly explain the specific reason for the visit, such as attending a Graduation Ceremony, a semester-break family visit, or travelling together. It is advisable to attach the holiday dates from ANU's official **Academic Calendar** — for example, the 2025 Semester 2 break runs from 30 June to 18 July — to support the reasonableness of the proposed visit dates.

**Accommodation and financial arrangements**: Describe in detail where family members will stay (e.g., the address of student accommodation or a rental apartment) and how costs will be covered. If the student is covering the costs, provide bank statements or scholarship evidence; if family members are self-funding, explain their sources of income. ANU recommends attaching a lease agreement or residence confirmation letter as proof of accommodation.

**Length of stay**: State the planned entry and departure dates. Visitor visas generally allow a maximum stay of 3 months per visit, although visa officers may grant shorter periods depending on the itinerary. The letter should include a commitment that family members will depart on time and not breach visa conditions.

**Signature and date**: The invitation letter must be signed by the student, in handwriting or electronically, and dated. The signature should match the one on the passport.

## Required Supporting Documents

The invitation letter should be submitted together with the following **5 categories of documents** to form a complete chain of evidence. Missing documents are a common cause of visa refusal; according to 2023 case statistics from the Administrative Appeals Tribunal (AAT), about 35% of family visitor visa appeals involved incomplete documentation.

**Proof of student status**: An ANU Enrolment Letter, which can be generated through the ANU ISIS system and must show the course name, enrolment status, and expected completion date. Apply for it after the semester starts to demonstrate active student status. Also provide a copy of the student visa (subclass 500) and the passport bio-data page.

**Proof of relationship**: A notarial birth certificate, a notarised household register (hukou), or a notarised certificate of kinship. If inviting non-immediate relatives (such as grandparents), additional notarisation of the family relationship chain is required. Applicants from China need to obtain bilingual (Chinese-English) notarised certificates through a local notary office; such documents are accepted by the Department of Home Affairs.

**Proof of funds**: The student's Australian bank account statements for the last 3 months (showing a stable income or scholarship deposits), or family members' fixed deposit certificates (recommended at least AUD 5,000 per person). If family members are self-funding, provide their domestic payslips or pension statements.

**Proof of accommodation**: A confirmation letter from an ANU on-campus residence (such as a housing contract for Wright Hall or Bruce Hall), or a copy of an off-campus lease. If the accommodation is temporary (e.g., Airbnb), attach the booking confirmation.

**Itinerary**: Return flight booking records (no need to purchase tickets — airlines can hold a booking for 24 hours) and a brief travel plan. It is advisable to include Canberra and nearby attractions (such as Parliament House and the Australian War Memorial) to reflect the purpose of tourism.

## Language and Format Guidelines

The invitation letter should be written in **English**, in a formal and concise tone, avoiding subjective or emotional language. The Visa Application Language Requirements published by the Department of Home Affairs in 2024 state that non-English documents must be accompanied by NAATI-certified translations, but there is no mandatory translation requirement for the invitation letter itself — an English letter can be submitted directly.

**Format suggestions**: Use a standard business letter format, including the sender's address, date, recipient details (e.g., "To the Department of Home Affairs") and a subject line (e.g., "Invitation Letter for [Family Member Name]"). Keep clear paragraph spacing, with each paragraph focused on one topic. Keep the whole letter within one A4 page (about 300-400 words) to avoid unnecessary length.

**Tone control**: Use declarative sentences to state facts, such as "I am currently enrolled in the Bachelor of Commerce at the Australian National University" rather than "I hope you can come to visit me". Avoid guarantee-style wording such as "I guarantee" or "I promise"; instead, provide objective evidence (e.g., "I have attached my bank statement showing sufficient funds").

## How to Submit and the Visa Application Process

The invitation letter is usually uploaded to the Department of Home Affairs' ImmiAccount system as a **visa application attachment**. The applicant (the family member) should upload the invitation letter and supporting documents under the "Supporting Documents" section when lodging the visa application form (Form 1415).

**Timeline**: Start preparing the documents about **3 months** before the family's planned departure. According to 2024 Department of Home Affairs processing time data, the global average processing time for visitor visas (subclass 600) is 15-35 business days, and about 20-40 business days for applications from mainland China. During peak periods (such as the November-to-February graduation season), processing can extend to 50 business days.

**Special note**: If family members plan to extend their visitor visa within Australia, be aware of the "No Further Stay" condition (visa condition 8503). ANU International Student Services advises avoiding visas that carry this condition on the first application, so that itineraries can be adjusted flexibly later.

## Common Reasons for Refusal and How to Avoid Them

According to the Visa Refusal Trends Report published by the Department of Home Affairs in 2023, the refusal rate for family visitor visas is about 18%, concentrated in the following **3 categories**.

**Failed GS assessment**: The visa officer believes the family member may overstay or engage in activities inconsistent with the visa. How to avoid: clearly state in the invitation letter the family member's ties to their home country (such as employment, property, and family responsibilities) and provide supporting evidence (e.g., an employment certificate or a copy of the property title).

**Insufficient proof of funds**: The family member or student cannot demonstrate sufficient funds to cover the travel costs. Under the 2024 Department of Home Affairs cost-of-living standard, visitor visa applicants must demonstrate the ability to cover at least AUD 100 per person per day. When providing deposit certificates, cover the full cost of the trip and leave a 20% buffer.

**Inconsistent documents**: Information in the invitation letter contradicts the application form (e.g., the accommodation address does not match the hotel booking). How to avoid: cross-check all documents to ensure name spellings, dates, and addresses are completely consistent. When paying cross-border tuition fees, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/how-to-get-an-invitation-letter-for-your-familys-visitor-visa-as-an-anu-internat) to complete currency conversion; such payment records can also serve as supplementary evidence of fund flows.

## FAQ

### Q1: Does the invitation letter need to be issued by ANU officially?

No. The invitation letter is written and signed by the student (the inviter) themselves; no official ANU stamp or notarisation is required. However, it is advisable to attach an ANU Enrolment Letter as proof of student status — this letter is generated through the ANU ISIS system and shows the course name and enrolment status. Visa officers weigh the authenticity of the letter's content and the completeness of the documents more heavily than official endorsement.

### Q2: How far in advance should family members start preparing a visitor visa application?

Start preparing the documents **3 months** ahead and submit the application **2 months** before the planned departure. According to 2024 Department of Home Affairs data, the average processing time for visitor visa applications from mainland China is 20-40 business days, but during peak periods (such as December to February) it can extend to 50 business days. In addition, notarising relationship documents (such as birth certificates) usually takes 5-10 business days, so allow time for that.

### Q3: Can one invitation letter invite multiple family members?

Yes. The letter should list each invitee's full name, date of birth, and relationship to the inviter one by one, and state in the itinerary section whether they share the same flight and accommodation arrangements. If inviting both parents, it is advisable to provide separately notarised birth certificates for each. Visa officers assess each applicant individually, but submitting together can simplify the process. Note that each family member must complete a separate visa application form and pay the application fee (AUD 195 per person in 2024).
