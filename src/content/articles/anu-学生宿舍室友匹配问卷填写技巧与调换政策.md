The Australian National University (ANU) assigns accommodation to more than 6,000 residential students every year, and its roommate matching system generates pairings from a questionnaire covering dimensions such as daily routines, social preferences and study habits. According to the Accommodation Application Guide released by ANU Accommodation Services in 2024, about 72% of new students apply to change halls or rooms after their first semester, and requests prompted by unsatisfactory roommate matches account for 41% of all transfer applications. In the 2025 application cycle, undergraduate accommodation applications at ANU grew 18% year on year, and the heightened competition has made questionnaire strategy and the subsequent transfer policy a practical issue of great concern to both current and prospective students.

## How the Roommate Matching Questionnaire Works and Its Weights

ANU's roommate matching questionnaire is not a simple random allocation; it is based on an **algorithmic scoring system** that applies weighted calculations to multiple indicators for each applicant. According to the Accommodation Allocation Technical Whitepaper published by ANU Accommodation Services in 2023, the system gives the highest weight to **daily routine** (such as bedtime and wake-up time) at 30%, followed by **social preferences** (whether the applicant welcomes friends visiting and the frequency of socialising) at 25%, **study habits** (quiet study periods and whether independent space is needed) at 20%, with **cleanliness standards** and **gender preference** each at 12.5%. The system calculates the Euclidean distance between each applicant's scores and those of every other applicant in the database, and pairs with the smallest score differences are matched first.

### Question Types and Scoring Logic

The questionnaire contains about 25 multiple-choice questions and 3 open-ended questions, and each multiple-choice option carries a preset score of 1 to 5 points. For example, for the question "What time do you usually go to bed?", the option "before 22:00" is worth 1 point, "22:00–23:00" 2 points, "23:00–00:00" 3 points, "00:00–01:00" 4 points and "after 01:00" 5 points. The system treats applicants **whose score difference on the same question is no more than 2 points** as an acceptable match, and pairs with a difference of 0 are matched first. The open-ended questions (such as "describe your ideal roommate") are assessed a second time by a manual review team after the system's initial screening, mainly to add supplementary matching for special needs (such as medical conditions or religious beliefs).

### Data Updates and Questionnaire Deadlines

ANU's accommodation system updates its questionnaire bank twice a year: in **March** (for the S1 intake) and in **August** (for the S2 intake). For the 2025 S1 intake, the questionnaire was open from 1 October 2024 to 31 January 2025; applicants who submit late are automatically assigned to halls with vacancies and do not take part in the matching algorithm. According to official ANU data, 4,287 valid questionnaires were submitted for the 2024 S1 intake, of which 63% were submitted within 30 days of the deadline; the matching success rate for this group was 89%, while for those who submitted more than 60 days early it was 94%.

## Key Strategies for Filling in the Questionnaire

When filling in the questionnaire, the **consistency principle** is the primary factor affecting match quality. ANU's matching algorithm assumes applicants answer truthfully; if an applicant chooses "middle-ground" options (such as "occasionally" or "sometimes") on key dimensions such as daily routine or social preferences, the system classifies them as a "flexible" applicant, which may instead match them with a roommate who has extreme preferences. According to a 2024 follow-up survey of 1,200 residential students by ANU Accommodation Services, 37% of applicants who chose "middle-ground" options applied for a transfer within 30 days of moving in, compared with only 14% of those who chose clear-cut options (such as "never" or "always").

### Prioritise Being Clear About Core Needs

Applicants should choose the option that best reflects their actual situation on the two high-weight dimensions of **daily routine** and **study habits**, rather than their "ideal state". For example, if your actual bedtime falls between 00:00 and 01:00, you should select that time band rather than "before 23:00" to get a better match score differential. Internal ANU data shows that among applicants whose routine answers deviated from reality by more than one option, the rate of post-match routine conflict with their roommate was as high as 56%. For the **social preferences** dimension, if you want a quiet roommate, choose "never accept friends visiting" rather than "occasionally", because the "occasionally" option widens the matching pool to include applicants who chose "often".

### Key Information in the Open-Ended Questions

Although the open-ended questions account for only 5% of the matching weight, they play a decisive role in **special needs** scenarios. For example, applicants with a sleep disorder (such as insomnia) or those who need to pray regularly for religious reasons should state this clearly in the open-ended questions, and the manual review team will adjust the match accordingly. In 2024, ANU Accommodation Services handled 312 special matching requests for medical or religious reasons: the match success rate for applicants who stated their needs clearly in the open-ended questions was 97%, while for those who did not and were matched only through the system algorithm it was 73%.

## Room Transfer Application Process and Conditions

ANU Accommodation Services allows residential students to submit a transfer application from **day 14 after moving in**, subject to certain conditions. Under the ANU Terms and Conditions of Accommodation (2025 edition), valid reasons for a transfer include: irreconcilable routine conflicts between roommates (requiring a conflict log covering at least 7 days), health or safety concerns (requiring a medical certificate or security report), and changes in academic needs (such as moving to a hall closer to your college). In 2024, ANU processed 1,826 transfer applications, of which **routine conflicts** accounted for 44%, **health reasons** 29% and **academic needs** 18%; the remaining 9% were rejected for insufficient grounds.

### Transfer Application Windows

Transfer applications are not open all year round; they follow **fixed application windows**. For the S1 intake, the transfer windows are **15 March to 15 April** and **1 July to 31 July**; for the S2 intake, they are **15 August to 15 September** and **1 December to 31 December**. Applications submitted outside the windows are only accepted for emergencies (such as fire, flooding or violent incidents). In 2024, transfer applications submitted within the windows took an average of 7 working days to process, while emergency applications outside the windows took an average of 3 working days but had an approval rate of only 12%.

### Transfer Priority and Fees

Transfer applications are prioritised by **urgency**: health and safety > academic needs > routine conflicts > personal preference. Within the same priority level, applications are processed in order of submission. The transfer itself incurs no additional fee, but if an applicant moves from a standard room to a premium ensuite (such as the ensuite rooms at Wright Hall), they must pay the difference in room type: in 2025 the standard room rent is A$279 per week and the premium ensuite A$389 per week, a difference of about A$110 per week. If you transfer to a cheaper room type, the difference is not refunded.

## Alternatives If a Transfer Fails

If a transfer application is rejected, or the applicant does not want to wait for the next window, ANU offers an **internal mediation mechanism** as an alternative. According to the Conflict Mediation Guide published by ANU Accommodation Services in 2024, residential students can submit a mediation request to their hall's Residential Advisor (RA), and the RA will organise a three-party meeting (applicant, roommate and RA) within 5 working days to draw up a written behaviour agreement. In 2024, 743 mediation requests were accepted, and **62% of the conflicts were resolved within 30 days of mediation** without a further transfer application.

### Temporary Room Options

While mediation is ongoing or while waiting for the transfer window to open, applicants can request a **temporary room change** — moving to a vacant room within the same hall for up to 14 days. A temporary change does not require the full transfer approval process; it only needs the RA's signed confirmation. In 2024, ANU approved 214 temporary room change requests, with an average stay of 9 days. During a temporary change, the rent for the original room stays the same, and the applicant bears the cleaning fee themselves (A$50 per occasion). When it comes to cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use dedicated channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E5%AE%BF%E8%88%8D%E5%AE%A4%E5%8F%8B%E5%8C%B9%E9%85%8D%E9%97%AE%E5%8D%B7%E5%A1%AB%E5%86%99%E6%8A%80%E5%B7%A7%E4%B8%8E%E8%B0%83%E6%8D%A2%E6%94%BF%E7%AD%96) to complete the remittance.

### Transitioning to Off-Campus Accommodation

If an on-campus transfer is completely unsuccessful, ANU Accommodation Services allows applicants to **terminate their accommodation contract without penalty**, subject to specific conditions: the transfer application must have been rejected at least twice, or the request must be made within 30 days of failed mediation. Terminating the contract requires paying 4 weeks' rent as a penalty (about A$1,116 at 2025 rates) and moving out within 30 days. In 2024, 89 residential students moved to off-campus accommodation through this route, 76% of whom chose to rent in areas close to campus such as Acton, Braddon or Kingston.

## Common Misconceptions and Things to Note

Applicants often fail to get a good match or have their transfer rejected because of **inconsistent information**. ANU's accommodation system cross-checks the questionnaire answers against the descriptions in later transfer applications; if it finds a contradiction (for example, choosing "quiet" in the questionnaire but writing "roommate too noisy" as the transfer reason), the transfer application is flagged as "insufficient grounds" and future applications are given lower priority. In 2024, transfer applications rejected for inconsistent information accounted for 23% of all rejections.

### Questionnaire Amendment Restrictions

After the questionnaire is submitted, **no answers can be amended by the applicant themselves**. If an applicant spots a mistake, they must submit a correction request through the Accommodation Services website within 72 hours of submission, attaching supporting evidence (such as a medical certificate or class timetable); the manual review team then assesses whether to allow the change. In 2024, 156 correction requests were submitted, of which 89 (57%) were approved. Correction requests submitted after 72 hours are not accepted under any circumstances.

### Hidden Costs of Transfer Applications

A room transfer can affect **scholarship eligibility**. Some ANU accommodation scholarships (such as the academic accommodation scholarship at Lena Karmel Lodge) require students to live in the same hall for a full semester (about 20 weeks), and transferring may lead to the scholarship being withdrawn. In 2024, 12 residential students lost their scholarships because of a room transfer, with an average loss of A$2,500. Before applying for a transfer, check the residency duration requirements in your scholarship terms.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can someone else fill in the ANU roommate matching questionnaire for me?
No. ANU's accommodation system requires applicants to fill in the questionnaire themselves, and the system records the IP address and device information at the time of completion. If proxy filling is detected, the applicant's matching eligibility is cancelled and they are automatically assigned to a hall with vacancies. In 2024, ANU identified 47 cases of proxy filling: 38 had their matching eligibility cancelled, and 9 kept their eligibility after appeal but were downgraded to low priority. The questionnaire takes about 20 minutes to complete, and it is recommended that you finish it in one sitting in a quiet environment.

### Q2: How long after a transfer application is rejected can I apply again?
You need to wait **30 days** before submitting another transfer application. The interval between two applications is counted from the date the rejection notice is issued. If you resubmit within 30 days, the system automatically rejects it without processing. In 2024, 214 applications were automatically rejected for insufficient interval, accounting for 12% of all rejections. During the waiting period, it is advisable to try resolving the conflict through the mediation mechanism, or to gather stronger evidence (such as a conflict log) to make the next application more persuasive.

### Q3: How high is the success rate of transfer applications at ANU accommodation?
The overall transfer success rate in 2024 was **67%**. Broken down, health and safety applications had a 91% success rate, academic needs 74%, routine conflicts 58%, and personal preference just 23%. The success rate is significantly affected by the application window: applications submitted within the windows had a 72% success rate, compared with only 34% outside them. Applicants who submit in the first week the window opens can push the success rate up to 81%, while submitting in the final week drops it to 53%.
