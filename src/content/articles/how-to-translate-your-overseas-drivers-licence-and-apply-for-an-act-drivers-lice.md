For ANU international students holding an overseas driver's licence, converting it to an official Australian Capital Territory (ACT) licence is an essential step towards driving legally. According to the *Australian Driver Licence Guidelines* published by Austroads in 2024, international students on temporary visas (such as the subclass 500 student visa) may drive in the ACT with a valid overseas licence, provided the licence is in English or accompanied by an officially recognised translation. ACT Government data shows that in the 2023-2024 financial year, more than 2,300 international licence holders completed a licence conversion or translation certification at Access Canberra. With the 2025 update to ACT road rules now explicitly requiring temporary residents who have held an overseas licence for more than 3 months to complete the conversion, this topic is especially urgent for ANU students.

## Official Requirements for Translating an Overseas Licence

**Translating your overseas licence** is the first step towards applying for an ACT licence. Under ACT Government rules, a non-English licence must be translated by a translator accredited by the **National Accreditation Authority for Translators and Interpreters (NAATI)**, or by a translation service provider designated by the ACT Government. In its 2024 policy update, Access Canberra makes clear that self-translations and unaccredited online translation tools are not accepted.

### The NAATI-Accredited Translation Process

Translations produced by NAATI-accredited translators carry legal effect. ANU international students can search the directory of accredited translators on the NAATI website (naati.com.au); translation fees typically range from 30 to 60 AUD, depending on the number of pages in the licence. The translation must include the translator's signature, NAATI number and certification date, and its validity matches that of the licence itself. Students can also lodge a translation application at an Access Canberra service counter, where it is handled by an officially partnered agency — processing takes roughly 5 to 10 working days.

### The International Driving Permit (IDP) Alternative

Students holding an **International Driving Permit (IDP)** can skip the separate translation step. The IDP is issued by an authorised agency in your home country (China, for example, cannot issue IDPs directly — you would need to apply through a third country), and is usually valid for 1 year. The ACT Government recognises the IDP as a valid translation, provided you also hold a valid original overseas licence. According to 2023 statistics from the Australian Department of Home Affairs, around 68% of international students choose NAATI-accredited translation over the IDP for their licence conversion, because the IDP application process is complicated and some countries are not party to the Geneva Convention on Road Traffic.

## ACT Licence Eligibility and Conditions

**ACT licence eligibility** depends on your visa type and how long you have lived in the Territory. As temporary visa holders, ANU international students must meet the following conditions: hold a valid overseas licence or NAATI-accredited translation, be at least 16 years old, and have lived in the ACT for more than 3 months. Under the ACT *Road Transport (Driver Licensing) Regulation 2023*, temporary visa holders who have lived in the ACT for less than 3 months may drive directly on their overseas licence, but once the 3-month mark passes they must apply for an ACT licence.

### Proof of Residence and Identity Verification

Applicants must provide **proof of residence**, including: a bank statement at an ACT address (dated within 30 days of the application), a utility bill, or an ACT lease agreement. Identity verification requires your passport, the Visa Grant Notice for your student visa, and your ANU student card. In its 2024 *Licence Application Guide*, Access Canberra stresses that all documents must be originals or certified copies — screenshots and electronic versions are not accepted.

### Vision Testing and Health Declarations

All ACT licence applicants must pass a **vision test**. Access Canberra service counters offer free vision screening, and applicants are required to meet the 6/12 standard (equivalent to 0.5) with or without corrective lenses. If you have a particular medical condition (such as epilepsy or diabetes), you must submit a Medical Certificate signed by a doctor; this certificate is valid for 6 months. The ANU Health Centre can carry out the relevant checks for students, at a cost of roughly 50 to 80 AUD.

## The Driver Knowledge Test (DKT) and Driving Test

The **Driver Knowledge Test (DKT)** is the centrepiece of the ACT licence application. The test consists of 45 multiple-choice questions covering road rules, traffic signs and driving behaviour, and the pass mark is 35 (78%). The DKT can be booked at an Access Canberra counter or online, and costs 45 AUD (2025 rate). Online tests use the dedicated system provided by the ACT Government, and results are generated instantly.

### Booking the Driving Test and What It Involves

After passing the DKT, applicants must book the **Driving Test**. The test lasts around 45 minutes and assesses: vehicle control, observation skills, intersection handling, reversing and parallel parking. The ACT driving test uses a demerit scoring system: more than 12 demerit points, or any dangerous behaviour (such as running a red light or speeding), means an automatic fail. The test fee is 60 AUD, and you must wait at least 7 days before retaking it. According to 2023 ACT Government data, the pass rate for international students is around 62%, below the 71% for local applicants.

### Exemptions and Direct Conversion

Holders of licences from certain countries can be **exempt from the driving test**. The ACT Government has mutual recognition agreements with specific countries (such as the United Kingdom, Germany and Japan), so licences from those countries can be converted directly to an ACT licence without the DKT or driving test. Chinese licence holders are not on the exemption list and must complete the full testing process. However, applicants holding a Hong Kong or Macau licence can apply for an exemption, provided the licence has been held for at least 2 years.

## Application Fees and Timeline

**ACT licence application costs** add up across several stages: translation (30-60 AUD), the DKT (45 AUD), the driving test (60 AUD) and the licence card itself (79 AUD for 5-year validity, 119 AUD for 10-year validity). The total comes to roughly 214 to 304 AUD, depending on your translation method and the licence validity period you choose.

### Processing Times and Booking Wait Periods

**Processing times** vary by application method. After submitting your translation at a counter, the DKT can be booked the same day, and the wait for a driving test is usually 2 to 4 weeks depending on the schedule at each Access Canberra service centre (such as Belconnen, Tuggeranong and Civic). Access Canberra's 2024 annual report shows that the average wait for a driving test was 18 days at the Civic centre, compared with 23 days at Belconnen. Students are advised to book early and apply during semester breaks (such as June or November) to avoid peak periods.

### The Learner Licence Bridge

If you do not pass the driving test, you can apply for a **Learner Licence**, which is valid for 12 months and costs 45 AUD. Learner licence holders must be accompanied by a full licence holder and cannot drive alone. You may practise on ACT roads, but must obey the speed limit (maximum 80 km/h) and a zero-alcohol standard.

## Test Preparation Resources and Tips

**Test preparation resources** can significantly improve your chances of passing. The ACT Government offers a free DKT practice test (accesscanberra.act.gov.au) with a bank of 200 questions that can be retaken unlimited times. ANU students can also use on-campus resources, such as the driving information sessions run by the ANU Students' Association (ANUSA), or obtain the official study handbook through the ACT Road Safety Centre.

### Driving Instructors and Practice Strategies

Hiring a **driving instructor** is an effective way to prepare for the test. Registered ACT instructors charge around 60 to 80 AUD per hour, and lessons usually include mock driving tests and analysis of where you lose points. Students are advised to complete at least 5 to 10 hours of instructor-led lessons, focusing on the routes commonly used in ACT tests (such as the Civic roundabout and multi-lane merging in Belconnen). According to a 2024 survey by the ACT Driving Instructors' Association, the pass rate for international students who received professional training rose to 78%.

### Practice Tests and Common Mistakes

**Practice tests** help you identify your weak points. Common mistakes include: failing to fully check blind spots, inaccurate stopping positions at intersections, and speeding (especially in 40 km/h school zones). ANU students can access practice test videos through online platforms (such as DriveTest ACT) for around 20 AUD per session. ACT Government 2023 driving test statistics show that the most heavily penalised items are "intersection handling" (average 4.2 points deducted) and "speed control" (average 3.8 points deducted).

## What to Watch Out for After Converting Your Licence

Once your **licence conversion is complete**, international students must follow ACT driving laws. ACT licence holders must renew their licence within 90 days before it expires, at the same cost as the initial application. If your student visa expires or changes, your licence status may be affected, and you must notify Access Canberra within 30 days.

### The Demerit Point System and Fines

The ACT operates a **demerit point system**: full licence holders start with 12 points and learner licence holders with 4 points. Speeding (more than 10 km/h over the limit) costs 1 point, running a red light costs 3 points, and drink driving (blood alcohol concentration above 0.05) costs 4 points. 2024 ACT Government data shows that traffic fines issued to international students for being unfamiliar with local rules account for around 15% of all fines, with common violations including failing to come to a complete stop at stop signs and not using indicators.

### How Your Licence Relates to Your Visa Status

**Licence validity** is directly tied to your visa status. If your student visa is cancelled or expires, your ACT licence automatically becomes invalid and must be returned within 30 days. ANU international students who change visa categories (for example, from a student visa to a temporary graduate visa, subclass 485) must re-apply for a licence conversion. The ACT Government advises students to contact Access Canberra within 14 days of a visa change to update their licence details.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I use my Chinese licence directly in the ACT? Do I need a translation?

A Chinese licence cannot be used directly in the ACT. ANU international students holding a Chinese licence must complete a NAATI-accredited translation within 3 months of arrival, otherwise they are treated as driving unlicensed. Translation costs around 30 to 60 AUD and must be done by a NAATI-accredited translator — self-translation is not permitted. Under the ACT *Road Transport Regulation 2023*, offenders face a fine of up to 2,200 AUD.

### Q2: Is the ACT driving test hard? What is the pass rate?

The ACT driving test is moderately difficult; the average pass rate for international students in 2023 was 62%. The exam focuses on intersection handling, speed control and blind-spot observation. Students are advised to complete at least 5 hours of professional instructor-led lessons and to familiarise themselves with the common test routes in Civic and Belconnen. The test fee is 60 AUD, and you must wait 7 days before a retake.

### Q3: How long does the licence conversion take? Can it be expedited?

The standard process takes 2 to 4 weeks, including translation (5-10 working days), the DKT (can be sat the same day) and the driving test booking (2-4 week wait). Access Canberra does not offer an express service, but students can choose to book at the Civic centre, which has a shorter average wait (18 days). The total cost is roughly 214 to 304 AUD.
