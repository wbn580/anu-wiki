The public kitchen rules and locker allocation system in the Australian National University's (ANU) on-campus residences and partner apartment blocks form part of a standardised management framework set by ANU Accommodation Services under the 2019 ANU Accommodation Agreement Terms and the 2024 Student Accommodation Code of Conduct. As of 2024, ANU has about 6,200 on-campus beds across 21 residence halls and several partner apartment buildings, and more than 85% of accommodation units come with shared kitchen facilities [ANU Accommodation Services, 2024, *Residential Halls and Lodges Handbook*]. The kitchen rules and locker allocation exist to balance limited space (each shared kitchen serves, on average, 8 to 15 students) against day-to-day hygiene and safety, while reducing cross-cultural friction over food storage, cooking times and cleaning duties. According to the 2023 ANU student accommodation satisfaction survey, disputes over kitchens and storage were the single largest category of accommodation complaints, accounting for about 37% of all of them [ANU Student Living, 2023, *Annual Resident Feedback Report*]. This article sets out ANU public kitchen access requirements, the locker application process, the cleaning roster system and the penalty regime, giving current and prospective residents a practical reference.

## Access Requirements and Hours for Public Kitchens

ANU residence halls set clear thresholds for access to public kitchens. Before moving in, all residents must complete the **Kitchen Safety Induction** online training — about 45 minutes long, covering the location of fire alarms, how to use fire blankets and how to handle grease fires. Students who do not complete the training cannot activate their access card for kitchen areas [ANU Accommodation Services, 2024, *Online Induction Module*].

**Hours**: most halls (such as Bruce Hall, Ursula Hall and Fenner Hall) keep their public kitchens open 24 hours, but between 22:00 and 7:00 the use of kitchen equipment that produces noticeable noise (including, but not limited to, high-speed blenders, food processors and deep fryers) is prohibited. Wright Hall and Davey Lodge enforce a stricter timed policy: kitchens are fully closed from 23:00 to 6:00 and the door access system locks automatically. Breaches of the hours rule are recorded as a **Level 1 violation**; a first offence draws a warning, and a second results in a 72-hour suspension of kitchen access.

### Visitor restrictions
Non-residents of the building may not use the public kitchen alone. If a resident wants to bring a visitor into the kitchen, they must register at the front desk, and the resident must accompany the visitor at all times. Each resident may bring at most 2 visitors into the kitchen area per visit, and visitors may stay no longer than 2 hours. The rule stems from several incidents in 2022 in which visitors left stovetops on and triggered false smoke alarms [ANU Accommodation Services, 2023, *Incident Log Summary*].

### Special dietary needs applications
Residents who need a separate cooking area for religious or medical reasons can submit a **Special Dietary Needs Form** to their Residential Advisor, with supporting proof from a doctor or a religious organisation. ANU Accommodation Services usually arranges a dedicated microwave or induction cooktop area within 72 hours, although this arrangement does not extend to separate locker allocations.

## Locker Allocation and the Application Process

ANU locker allocation follows the principle of "apply first, then allocate, then clear regularly". The number of lockers in each public kitchen varies by hall: newer buildings (such as the Lodge complex in the Kambri Precinct) have 12 to 16 individual lockers per kitchen, while historic buildings (such as the old wing of Bruce Hall) have only 6 to 8. Locker size is uniform at 30 cm × 30 cm × 45 cm, and each locker comes with one removable shelf.

**Application process** — three steps: within 7 days of moving in, residents submit a locker application through the ANU Accommodation Portal, choosing a preferred kitchen area; the system assigns locker numbers by random draw within 48 hours; residents must buy a combination lock (recommended: Master Lock 1500D or equivalent) and secure the locker within 72 hours of the allocation notice. Lockers not locked in time are reclaimed and reassigned. First-semester 2024 data shows an average locker wait of 11 days, stretching to 23 days at popular halls such as Kinloch Lodge [ANU Accommodation Services, 2024, *Storage Allocation Dashboard*].

### Locker tenure and clearance cycles
Lockers are held for the same period as the accommodation contract, meaning everything must be cleared at the end of each semester (end of June and end of November). ANU Accommodation Services posts clearance notices by email and on hall noticeboards 14 days before the end of semester. Items not cleared in time are treated as **abandoned property**, removed by the hall and held in lost property for 30 days. In the end-of-semester-2 2023 clearance, 217 uncleared lockers were emptied, and about 63% of their contents were never claimed within 30 days [ANU Accommodation Services, 2024, *End-of-Semester Clearance Report*].

### Transfers and sharing restrictions
Lockers may not be transferred or shared under any circumstances. If a resident checks out mid-semester, their locker must be emptied on the day they check out and may not be privately passed on to another resident. Residents who breach this rule face a **AUD 50 administrative fine** and lose locker eligibility for the next semester. The fine amount is set under clause 4.2 of the 2023 ANU Accommodation Fees and Fines Regulation.

## Cleaning Rosters and Hygiene Standards

Cleaning in public kitchens runs on a two-track system: "resident roster + professional cleaners". Each kitchen has a **Cleaning Roster Group** made up of residents from that floor, usually 5 to 8 people, rotating weekly. The roster task list is set by the Residential Advisor and includes: wiping down stovetops and the inside and outside of microwaves, mopping floors, cleaning sink strainers, emptying shared bins, and checking for expired food in the fridge.

**Hygiene inspections** are carried out twice a week by the Residential Advisor (Mondays and Thursdays), with results graded "pass", "needs improvement" or "fail". A kitchen that records two consecutive "fail" grades requires the whole group to do a catch-up clean within 24 hours, otherwise the group faces a **collective fine** of AUD 15 per person. According to 2023 data, about 22% of kitchens received at least one "fail" rating during the semester, rising to 31% in halls where more than 12 people use the kitchen [ANU Accommodation Services, 2023, *Cleaning Compliance Report*].

### Fridge and freezer management
Shared fridges are allocated by floor zone: each kitchen has 2 to 3 fridges, each labelled with its floor or room-number range. Residents must label their food packaging with their name and storage date; unlabelled food is treated as **unclaimed food** and cleared by the Residential Advisor every Friday. Freezers follow similar rules, but the clearance cycle runs fortnightly. In first semester 2024, ANU Accommodation Services cleared about 430 kg of expired food from shared fridges, with dairy products and ready-to-eat salads the largest share (about 47%) [ANU Accommodation Services, 2024, *Food Waste Audit*].

### Shared crockery and cookware rules
Public kitchens in the halls provide basic crockery (bowls, plates, cups, cutlery) and cookware (pots, spatulas, chopping boards), but quantities are limited — on average, each kitchen has 8 place settings. Residents must wash and put away anything they use within 30 minutes. Items left unwashed are moved to the "dirty dishes bin" and washed centrally by the Residential Advisor at 22:00 each day. Residents who deliberately damage or lose shared crockery must pay replacement cost, ranging from AUD 5 (a single cup) to AUD 35 (a full set of pots).

## Penalty System for Violations

ANU applies a three-tier penalty system for violations in public kitchens and lockers. **Level 1 violations** include: entering the kitchen without completing kitchen safety training, using noisy equipment outside permitted hours, and failing to clean up your own cooking area. The penalty is a written warning; three accumulated Level 1 violations escalate to a Level 2 violation. **Level 2 violations** include: smoking or using open flames in the kitchen, storing flammable items in a locker, and using someone else's locker without permission. The penalty is a 7 to 14 day suspension of kitchen access plus a AUD 30 fine. **Level 3 violations** include: deliberately triggering a fire alarm, being drunk or violent in the kitchen, and stealing others' food or locker contents. The penalty is immediate revocation of kitchen access and initiation of accommodation contract termination [ANU Accommodation Services, 2024, *Student Conduct Policy*].

### Complaints and appeals
Residents who disagree with a penalty can submit a written appeal to the floor Residential Advisor within 5 business days of receiving the penalty notice. If the appeal is dismissed, they can ask the Accommodation Services Manager for a review. The review decision, usually delivered within 10 business days, is final. In 2023, ANU Accommodation Services handled 127 kitchen-related appeals, and about 34% were partially or fully upheld [ANU Accommodation Services, 2024, *Appeals Summary*].

### Cross-cultural kitchen etiquette guide
With international students making up about 42% of ANU's accommodation residents (2024 data), ANU Accommodation Services has published the *Cross-Cultural Kitchen Etiquette Guide*, available in Chinese, Hindi, Indonesian and Arabic. Key reminders: avoid long cooking sessions with strong-smelling spices (such as fish sauce or durian) in shared areas; wipe down oil splashes immediately after using a shared stovetop; and do not store unsealed raw meat in the fridge. Behaviours that go against the guide's advice do not directly trigger penalties, but if they draw a formal complaint from another resident they are treated as a Level 2 violation.

## Food Storage and Labelling Rules

Food stored in shared fridges and lockers must follow strict **labelling rules**. Every item placed in a shared fridge must be labelled with: the resident's name, room number and storage date (format DD/MM/YYYY). Labels can be written with a waterproof marker or on the free food-label stickers available at the front desk. Food that is unlabelled or hard to read may be thrown out by the Residential Advisor during the Friday clearance without prior notice.

**Expiry management**: refrigerated food may be stored for no more than 14 days, and frozen food no more than 60 days. The Residential Advisor checks expiry dates during the weekly clearance, and expired food is photographed, recorded and discarded. In 2023, ANU Accommodation Services received 214 resident complaints about food labelling non-compliance, and about 58% involved "unlabelled food thrown away by mistake" [ANU Accommodation Services, 2024, *Labeling Compliance Report*].

### Allergen and dietary restriction labels
Residents with food allergies (such as peanuts, dairy or gluten) can stick an allergen warning sticker (red, 5 cm × 5 cm) on the inside of their locker door, available from the front desk. Residential Advisors skip lockers with allergen labels during clearances. ANU Accommodation Services also recommends storing allergen foods in sealed containers in shared fridges, marked "ALLERGEN" on the outside.

### Food sharing and donations
Some halls (such as Fenner Hall and Wright Hall) have a "community food sharing basket" where residents can leave unopened, in-date surplus food for others to take free. The baskets are checked and cleared of expired items by the Residential Advisor every Monday. In 2023, about 1,200 items of food were shared through the scheme across ANU halls, cutting food waste by about 320 kg [ANU Accommodation Services, 2024, *Food Sharing Program Report*].

## Equipment Repairs and Maintenance

Repairs for public kitchen equipment (ovens, microwaves, induction cooktops, fridges, rangehoods, etc.) must be requested through a work order on the ANU Accommodation Portal. Emergency faults (such as gas leaks or electrical shorts) can be reported on the accommodation services 24-hour emergency line (+61 2 6125 1234), with response usually within 30 minutes. Non-urgent repairs (such as a fridge not cooling enough or a microwave turntable fault) have a 2 to 5 business day response time.

**Maintenance records** show that 843 repair work orders were raised for public kitchens across ANU halls in 2023, with fridge faults (29%), unresponsive microwave buttons (22%) and uneven induction cooktop heating (18%) the most common problems [ANU Accommodation Services, 2024, *Maintenance Log*]. While repairs are under way, residents can use backup equipment (usually on the "backup equipment shelf" in the kitchen corner) or temporarily use the kitchen on an adjacent floor.

### Fire alarms and firefighting equipment
Every public kitchen has at least one smoke alarm, one fire blanket and one ABC dry-powder fire extinguisher. Smoke alarms are tested monthly by the Residential Advisor, with 24 hours' email notice of the test time. Residents must not cover or remove smoke alarms; doing so is a Level 3 violation. Fire blankets and extinguishers are inspected quarterly by ANU Safety and Emergency Services. In 2023, there were 156 false smoke alarm activations in ANU hall kitchens, about 71% of them caused by excessive cooking fumes [ANU Safety and Emergency Services, 2024, *Incident Report*].

### Energy saving and environmental requirements
Since 2023, ANU Accommodation Services has required residents to switch induction cooktops to "standby" mode after use rather than powering them down completely, to reduce the energy spike when the appliance restarts. Each kitchen also has colour-coded bins (general waste, recycling, food waste), and residents must sort their waste by label. Mis-sorting draws a warning the first time and a AUD 10 fine per offence from the second time on.

## FAQ

### Q1: What should I do if someone else has taken my locker?
First check that the number on the locker door matches your allocation notice. If it is indeed occupied, take photos as evidence and report it to the floor Residential Advisor right away. The Residential Advisor will contact the occupant within one business day and ask them to empty it; if there is no response within 48 hours, the hall will cut the lock and remove the items. In 2023 these incidents took an average of 3.2 days to resolve. Never cut the lock yourself — that is a Level 2 violation.

### Q2: Can I store raw meat in the shared kitchen fridge?
Yes, but it must be in a sealed container or wrapped in plastic wrap, labelled "RAW MEAT" on the outside with the storage date. Raw meat must go on the bottom shelf of the fridge so juices cannot drip onto other food. Storage is limited to 7 days. Contaminating other food or drawing complaints because of this rule is a Level 2 violation with a AUD 30 fine. Using red containers to tell raw meat apart from cooked food is recommended.

### Q3: Can I use a rice cooker or air fryer in the residence kitchen?
Yes, but only in the public kitchen area — high-power cooking appliances (over 1,200 watts) are banned in personal rooms. After use, clean the appliance within 30 minutes and store it in your locker. If a rice cooker or air fryer is left out on the shared bench, the Residential Advisor may move it to lost property and hold it there for 72 hours.
