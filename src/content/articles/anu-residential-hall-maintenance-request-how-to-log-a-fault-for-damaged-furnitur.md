The Australian National University (ANU) operates 18 residential buildings on campus, ranging from traditional college-style halls to modern apartments, with a total bed capacity of more than 6,600 (ANU Accommodation, 2024 Annual Report). When a resident finds damaged furniture, a leaking pipe or a faulty circuit, how efficiently they can log a repair request and track its progress directly affects their living experience and their studies. According to the Resident Handbook published by ANU Accommodation Services in 2024, an average of more than 4,200 maintenance jobs are logged each academic year; about 35% involve damage to furniture and fittings, and a further 28% are classed as urgent repairs (such as power cuts and gas leaks). This article sets out ANU's maintenance request process — submission channels, classification criteria, emergency response and progress tracking — citing official ANU policy and common industry practice, as a practical fault-reporting guide for current students.

## How to submit a maintenance request and what you need first

ANU Accommodation Services offers three main ways to raise a job: the online portal (ANU Accommodation Portal), a paper form at the front desk, and an emergency phone line. Under section 4.2 of the 2024 Resident Handbook, the **online portal** is the recommended channel; requests logged there are given "Standard" priority, with an average response time of 2 working days. The **paper form** is for urgent situations where there is no internet access, but the request must be confirmed by phone within 24 hours of submission. The **emergency phone line** (02 6125 1234) is reserved for situations that threaten personal safety or could cause major property damage, such as gas leaks, major flooding or signs of fire.

Before lodging a request, students must be **legitimate occupants** — that is, they have signed the accommodation agreement and completed check-in. The job must be submitted by the occupant themselves, unless someone else is acting for them for medical reasons (an authorisation letter is required). ANU Accommodation Services 2024 data shows around 12% of jobs were rejected because the submitter's identity did not match, delaying processing by an average of 3.7 days. Before submitting, take at least 2 photos of the damaged area (one wide shot and one close-up) and attach them to speed up the assessment.

## Classifying and reporting damaged furniture

ANU Accommodation Services splits furniture damage into three categories: **normal wear**, **repairable damage** and **irreparable damage**. Normal wear (faded curtains, light carpet indentations) is maintained at the accommodation provider's expense, at no cost to the student. Repairable damage (a drawer slide coming off, a broken bed board) requires the cause to be noted when reporting; if it resulted from accidental use, the student may have to pay a repair fee of between 50 AUD and 150 AUD (ANU Accommodation, 2024 Fee Schedule). Irreparable damage (a structurally cracked bed frame, a shattered desk top) usually means the whole item is replaced, with the cost apportioned pro rata against the item's original price, up to a maximum of 350 AUD.

When reporting, students need to give the **furniture ID number** (the label code assigned to each item at check-in) and select the damage type code (F1–F5). ANU Accommodation Services' 2024 technical report shows about 62% of furniture jobs were delayed because the ID number was missing or the code was wrong. It is a good idea to check the Furniture Inventory List within 24 hours of moving in and photograph everything as evidence for later disputes. Damage in shared areas (a lounge sofa, a kitchen table) must be reported jointly by all residents of that floor; otherwise the job is marked "Pending Confirmation".

## What counts as an urgent repair and how fast it gets fixed

ANU Accommodation Services defines an urgent repair as an event that "**directly threatens personal safety or health, or causes major property damage**", including: gas leaks, complete power failure, burst pipes, a smoke alarm that will not stop false-alarming, and lock failure (cannot lock or cannot open). Under the 2024 revision of ANU's Emergency Response Protocol, urgent jobs must be responded to by the on-call maintenance team within **30 minutes** of submission, with work on site starting within 2 hours. Non-urgent repairs (a light bulb change, a dripping tap) are responded to by the next working day.

Students should be careful to distinguish **urgent repairs** from **priority repairs**. Priority repairs (a broken fridge spoiling food, a hot water system failure) affect quality of life but are not emergencies; the response time is within 4 hours. ANU Accommodation Services' 2024 figures show that about 18% of urgent jobs were actually priority repairs, which lengthened students' waiting times. When you call, say clearly that "there is a risk to personal safety" or "property damage is getting worse", and have your room number, a description of the damage and photos ready. If you cannot describe the problem clearly in English, ask the operator to put you through to the Chinese interpreting service (available on ANU's multilingual support line during working hours).

## Tracking repair progress and reading job statuses

After submitting a job, students can check its status in real time on the ANU Accommodation Portal. The system uses four status labels: **Submitted**, **Assigned**, **In Progress** and **Closed**. According to ANU Accommodation Services' 2024 user guide, the average time from "Submitted" to "Assigned" is 15 minutes for urgent jobs and 1.8 working days for standard ones. If a status has not changed after 2 working days, students can use the Escalate function in the Portal to send a reminder, and the system automatically notifies the regional manager.

Once a job is closed, students have 48 hours to check the quality of the repair. If the issue is unresolved or a new one has appeared, select "Reopen" in the Portal and the job is promoted to next priority. ANU Accommodation Services 2024 data shows around 9% of closed jobs were reopened, most commonly because repair parts had not arrived (43%) or the repair had caused follow-on problems (31%). Take photos of the repaired area after closure and compare them with the originals — they will be useful if you need to dispute anything later.

## Who pays for repairs and how to dispute charges

Who pays is set out in clause 7.3 of the ANU Accommodation Agreement: **normal wear** is covered by the accommodation provider; for **accidental damage** (spilling a drink and staining the carpet, say) the student pays a cleaning fee of 65 AUD per incident; for **deliberate damage** (kicking in a door panel, smashing glass) the student pays the full repair cost and may also face disciplinary action. The ANU Accommodation Services 2024 fee schedule lists common jobs: replacing a light bulb (free), unblocking a toilet (45 AUD), replacing a mattress (120–250 AUD) and patching a wall (80–200 AUD).

If you disagree with a charge, you can lodge a written appeal with the ANU Accommodation Services Dispute Resolution Office within 14 days of the job closing. The appeal must include before-and-after photos, an itemised bill and a written explanation. According to ANU's 2024 accommodation disputes annual report, around 32% of appeals resulted in partial or full waivers, with an average processing time of 11 working days. Keep every job number, email exchange and call recording (tell the other party you are recording first) as your evidence chain. ## Special cases: shared areas and cross-building repairs

Maintenance requests for shared areas (laundries, gyms, communal kitchens) must be submitted by the **Residence Manager** — individual students cannot raise them directly. Under ANU Accommodation Services' 2024 Shared Areas Maintenance Protocol, students can tell the manager via the building's WeChat group or the front desk, and the manager logs the job within 24 hours. If a shared facility (a washing machine or dryer, say) breaks down and affects more than 10 residents, the manager must activate an emergency plan within 2 hours (arranging replacement equipment or temporary compensation vouchers).

Cross-building repairs (a central air-conditioning failure affecting several halls) are coordinated by ANU Facilities Management, with a response time of 4 hours. ANU's 2024 infrastructure report shows cross-building jobs take an average of 3.5 days to resolve — 1.8 days longer than single-building jobs. If you spot a cross-building problem (a whole building without power, hot water off building-wide), call the emergency line rather than raising a Portal job, so the building-level emergency plan can be activated.

## Preventive maintenance and resident responsibilities

ANU Accommodation Services runs **Quarterly Preventive Maintenance**, with routine checks of furniture, water and electrical fittings in every room. Under ANU's 2024 maintenance plan, each room is inspected once per semester, covering more than 95% of rooms in all residential buildings. Students must make sure their room is accessible on inspection day; otherwise it is marked "Not Inspected" and a catch-up inspection must be booked (possibly incurring a 50 AUD service fee). Preventive maintenance cuts the chance of sudden breakdowns by about 40% (ANU Accommodation, 2024 Internal Data).

Resident responsibilities include keeping furniture in good condition, reporting small problems promptly (a loose screw, for example) and never using open flames or corrosive chemicals on furniture. Clause 8.1 of the ANU Accommodation Agreement is explicit: if unreported small problems lead to large-scale damage, the resident bears the full repair cost. Check your furniture's stability every couple of weeks and raise a job the moment something looks wrong, however minor. A 2024 ANU Accommodation Services case study describes a loose bookshelf that was never reported and eventually collapsed entirely, costing 780 AUD to repair — paid in full by the resident.

## FAQ

### Q1: How long does an ANU hall maintenance job usually take?

According to ANU Accommodation Services 2024 data, a standard maintenance job takes an average of 3.2 working days to complete — 2.8 working days for furniture jobs and 4.1 for plumbing and electrical jobs. Urgent jobs get on-site attention within 2 hours, but a full fix (such as replacing parts) can take an extra 1–3 working days.

### Q2: What if the problem is still there after the repair?

Within 48 hours of the job closing, select "Reopen" in the ANU Accommodation Portal and the job is promoted to next priority. ANU's 2024 figures show reopened jobs take an average of 1.5 working days — 54% faster than the first time round. It is also worth calling the emergency line (02 6125 1234) to confirm verbally.

### Q3: Who pays for repairs, and when do students have to pay?

Normal wear (faded curtains, light carpet indentations) is covered by ANU. For accidental damage (spilling a drink and staining the carpet) the student pays a 65 AUD cleaning fee per incident. Deliberate damage (kicking in a door panel) means the student pays the full repair cost and may face disciplinary action. If you disagree with a charge, lodge a written appeal with the Dispute Resolution Office within 14 days.
