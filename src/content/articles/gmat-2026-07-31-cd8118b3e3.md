## Understanding Weaken, Strengthen, and Assumption Questions

GMAT Critical Reasoning questions often ask you to weaken, strengthen, or identify an assumption in an argument. A reliable approach is logic-chain analysis, which breaks the argument into premises and conclusion, then examines the gap. Weaken questions target that gap by introducing new information that makes the conclusion less likely. Strengthen questions do the opposite, making the conclusion more probable. Assumption questions require you to identify an unstated premise that must be true for the argument to hold.

Consider an official GMAT question: "Installing scrubbers in smokestacks reduces air pollution. Therefore, requiring scrubbers will significantly improve air quality." The logic chain moves from installing scrubbers to reduced pollution, then to improved air quality. To weaken, we point out a factor that undermines this chain—for example, if scrubbers are too expensive for widespread adoption. To strengthen, we provide evidence supporting the link, such as data from early adopters showing measurable air quality gains. An assumption might be that scrubbers are the only cost-effective means available.

## Applying Logic-Chain Analysis to Real GMAT Questions

Another official example: "A study found that people who drink green tea daily have lower cholesterol. Therefore, green tea reduces cholesterol." The logic chain jumps from correlation to causation. A weaken answer could introduce an alternative cause, like those people also exercising more. A strengthen answer could show that the effect remains even when other factors are controlled. An assumption is that no third factor accounts for both tea drinking and lower cholesterol.

A third example: "Company X plans to cut costs by outsourcing customer service. Thus, Company X will increase profits." The logic chain assumes cost savings directly boost profits. To weaken, you might cite evidence that outsourced service leads to customer loss, offsetting savings. To strengthen, you could show that similar companies improved profits after outsourcing. An essential assumption is that customer satisfaction won't drop enough to reduce revenue.

These patterns repeat across GMAT Critical Reasoning. By practicing with official questions and mapping out the logic chain for each, you build the skill to quickly identify what a weaken, strengthen, or assumption answer must do.

## Key Takeaways for Exam Preparation

When working through GMAT practice questions, always articulate the logic chain before looking at the answer choices. State the premises, then the conclusion, and identify any leap in reasoning. For weaken and strengthen, anticipate what additional fact would break or reinforce that leap. For assumption, apply the negation test: if the negation of an answer choice destroys the argument, it’s the correct assumption. Consistent use of logic-chain analysis turns abstract critical reasoning into a systematic process.
