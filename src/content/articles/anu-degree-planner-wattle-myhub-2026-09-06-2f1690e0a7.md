## Understanding Course Codes and Prerequisites

At ANU, each course has a unique code that indicates its level and subject area. For example, courses numbered 1000-level are typically introductory, while 2000-level and 3000-level are more advanced. Prerequisites are listed in the course description and must be completed before enrolling in higher-level courses. Always check the prerequisite requirements before adding a course to your enrolment.

## Using Degree Planner to Map Your Degree

Degree Planner is an online tool that helps you plan your degree progress. It allows you to see which courses are required for your degree, track your completed courses, and check that you are on track to meet all requirements. You can use it to map out your semester-by-semester plan and ensure you don't miss any compulsory courses.

## Timing Strategies to Avoid Full Classes

Popular courses can fill up quickly. To secure a place, enrol as early as possible during the enrolment period. If a course is full, you may be able to join a waitlist. Monitor your enrolment status regularly and be prepared to select an alternative course if needed.

## Tips for Using Wattle and MyHub

Wattle is ANU's learning management system, where you access course materials and submit assignments. MyHub is the student portal for enrolment and administrative tasks. Familiarise yourself with both systems: check Wattle for announcements and weekly content, and use MyHub to manage your enrolment and view your timetable.

## Common Course Selection Mistakes to Avoid

- **Ignoring credit conversion**: If you are transferring credits from another institution, ensure they are correctly converted to ANU units. Contact the ANU admissions office if you are unsure.
- **Missing deadlines**: Keep track of enrolment and census dates. Missing the enrolment deadline can result in having to pay late fees or being unable to add courses.
- **Overlooking prerequisites**: Taking a course without completing prerequisites can lead to enrolment being cancelled or poor performance.
- **Not checking timetable clashes**: Use the timetable to ensure your selected courses do not overlap in time. Clashing classes will cause scheduling problems.

By understanding the course codes, using Degree Planner effectively, enrolling early, and being aware of common pitfalls, you can plan your semester smoothly and keep your studies on track.
