The Australian National University (ANU) has offered an electricity subsidy policy for students living in on-campus accommodation since 2023, designed to ease the financial pressure of rising energy costs. According to the Australian Energy Regulator's (AER) 2023 annual report, residential electricity prices in the ACT rose by 18.7% in the 2022-23 financial year. ANU responded by launching the **"Student Energy Support Plan"** in Semester 1, 2023, which provides every student living in on-campus residences with a $150-per-semester electricity subsidy, credited directly against their accommodation bill. The plan is also paired with an **"Energy-Saving Rewards"** mechanism, under which students can earn up to $50 in bonus points by taking part in energy-saving challenges. The policy was continued in 2024 and covers around 6,000 on-campus residents, making it Australia's first comprehensive, university-led student electricity subsidy scheme.

## Policy Background and Reasons for Launch

ANU's electricity subsidy policy did not emerge in isolation; it was a response to multiple pressures. The Australian Government's 2022 Australian Energy Market Report noted that national wholesale electricity prices rose by 141% in the 2021-22 financial year, reaching $87 per megawatt hour. Although the ACT benefits from renewable energy accounting for more than 80% of its supply, retail prices still felt the pass-through of wholesale costs. ANU's on-campus residential facilities (including 12 halls such as Bruce Hall and Wright Hall) saw average annual electricity expenditure grow by 34% in 2022, with part of that cost passed on to students through accommodation fees.

The **Australian National University Students' Association (ANUSA)** conducted a survey in the fourth quarter of 2022 showing that 72% of on-campus residents said rising electricity prices were placing significant strain on their personal budgets. ANUSA subsequently submitted a formal petition to the university calling for a dedicated subsidy. The university approved the plan at the Student Welfare Committee meeting in February 2023, with an initial budget of $900,000 drawn from the university's operating reserves. When the policy was renewed in 2024, the budget was increased to $1.1 million and coverage was extended from undergraduates to all on-campus residents, including postgraduate and exchange students.

## Subsidy Disbursement Mechanism and Eligibility

ANU's electricity subsidy uses an **"automatic deduction"** model, so students do not need to apply separately. The subsidy is $150 per semester and is applied directly to reduce the electricity line item on students' accommodation bills. Eligibility is reviewed centrally by ANU Accommodation Services and requires: holding a valid ANU student ID, and having signed an on-campus accommodation contract of at least one semester. For students who move in mid-semester, the subsidy is prorated at $5.77 per week.

The subsidy is disbursed in the third week of each semester, synchronised with the accommodation billing cycle. The Semester 1, 2024 subsidy was credited on 11 March. **ANU's finance systems** push a notification through the student portal (ISIS), where students can view the deduction details under the "Accommodation Bills" module. If a student receives a duplicate subsidy because of an early checkout or room transfer, the university has the right to claw back the overpayment on a later bill. The policy does not apply to students renting off campus, but ANU has partnered with the ACT Government to offer off-campus students a separate "Energy Relief Payment" application channel, providing a one-off grant of up to $200.

### Types of Accommodation Covered

The subsidy covers **ANU's 12 fully university-operated residential halls**, including Bruce Hall, Wright Hall, Fenner Hall, Burgmann College and John XXIII College. Burgmann College and John XXIII College are affiliated colleges, but their electricity billing is managed through the university's system. **Not covered** are: on-campus apartments (such as University House), privately leased off-campus residences (such as some properties managed by UniLodge), and student apartments that do not have a direct electricity supply contract with ANU.

### Eligibility Review and Dispute Resolution

Students who disagree with an eligibility decision can lodge an appeal with ANU Accommodation Services within 14 business days of receiving their bill. In 2023, 47 appeals were lodged in total, of which 89% were resolved; the main cause was delays in system data synchronisation. Appeals must include the accommodation contract number, student ID number and a screenshot of the bill, and processing takes 5-7 business days.

## Energy-Saving Rewards Program Details

The **Energy-Saving Rewards** program is a complementary module to ANU's electricity subsidy policy, designed to encourage students to actively reduce their electricity use. Run by the ANU Sustainability Office, it uses a **"points-for-redemption"** mechanism. Students earn points by taking part in energy-saving challenges (such as cutting monthly electricity use by 10%, attending energy-saving seminars, or submitting energy-saving suggestions); every 100 points can be redeemed for a $10 reward, up to a cap of 500 points (i.e. $50). Rewards are issued as top-ups to the campus card, which can be used at on-campus supermarkets, canteens and printing services.

The 2024 challenges include: switching off non-essential appliances in the room during "Earth Hour" (20:30-21:30 on 23 March) for 50 points; and keeping the room air conditioner set above 24°C for four consecutive weeks for 100 points. **ANU's energy monitoring system** tracks the electricity data of every room in real time, and students can view their personal energy ranking through the "Green ANU" mobile app. Across 2023, a total of 1,342 students took part in the program, earning an average of 280 points each and saving a combined 187,000 kWh of electricity — equivalent to a reduction of 78 tonnes of CO2 emissions.

### How to Join the Energy-Saving Challenges

Students must register an account in the "Green ANU" app and link their personal accommodation number. Challenges are updated monthly, and completion is verified automatically by the system. For example, the air-conditioning temperature challenge requires students to sync their smart thermostat data with the system. Rooms without a smart thermostat (such as some rooms in Fenner Hall) can take part by submitting photos of the temperature readings manually. The **ANU Sustainability Office** publishes a challenge leaderboard every quarter, and students in the top 10% receive an additional 100 points.

### Reward Redemption Limits

Reward points are valid for 12 months and expire after that. Redemptions must be made at the campus card top-up terminals (located in the Union Court and Kambri areas), with a minimum redemption of 100 points per transaction. Rewards are non-transferable and cannot be exchanged for cash. In 2023, 207 students lost points by failing to redeem them within the validity period, with a total value of around $4,140.

## Policy Outcomes and Data Feedback

According to **ANU Finance's Q1 2024 report**, two years after implementation, average electricity bills for on-campus residents fell by 12.3%, from $215 to $188.50 per semester. Subsidy coverage reached 98.7%, with only 0.5% of students rejected on eligibility grounds. A student satisfaction survey (November 2023) showed that 85% of respondents felt the subsidy had effectively reduced cost-of-living pressure.

On the energy-saving side, the rooms of participating students used on average 14.6% less electricity than those of non-participants. In the Campus Energy White Paper published in February 2024, the ANU Sustainability Office noted that the program had cut campus carbon emissions by 142 tonnes in total, equivalent to the annual carbon absorption of 2,130 mature eucalyptus trees. In March 2024, the **ACT Government** listed ANU's energy-saving program as a "community carbon-reduction best practice" case and allocated $250,000 to scale the program up.

## Comparison with Other Universities

Nationally, ANU is one of the few universities offering a direct electricity subsidy. The **University of Melbourne** only provided a one-off emergency grant of $100 in 2023, and students had to apply for it themselves. The **University of Sydney** covers energy costs indirectly through its "Student Living Support Grant" of up to $500, but the assessment process takes up to 4 weeks. The **University of Queensland** only launched a similar program in January 2024, offering $120 per semester exclusively to students from households with an annual income below $60,000.

In the Student Financial Wellbeing Report published in October 2023, **Universities Australia** named ANU a "best practice in energy subsidies" case, noting that its automatic-deduction model lowered administrative costs and enabled 92% of subsidy funds to go directly to students. By contrast, the application-based models at other universities spend an average of 18% of subsidy funds on administrative assessment. This difference prompted 5 universities to consult ANU about the policy details in 2024.

## Application Process and Common Steps

Students do not need to apply for the subsidy proactively, but they must ensure their accommodation contract matches the information in ANU's student systems. **Key steps** include: submitting a copy of a valid student ID to ANU Accommodation Services at check-in; confirming in ISIS that the electricity line item on the accommodation bill is ticked for "automatic deduction"; and checking the bill in the third week of each semester to confirm the $150 subsidy has been credited.

For the energy-saving rewards, students first download the "Green ANU" app (available on iOS and Android) and register with their ANU student email. After registration, the system automatically syncs the accommodation number and electricity data. When joining challenges, the app shows the task list and completion status. For example, during "Energy Saving Week" (held every August), students must keep room electricity use below 85% of the baseline value for 7 consecutive days, after which the system automatically awards 100 points.

For cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use professional channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%AD%A6%E7%94%9F%E5%AE%BF%E8%88%8D%E7%94%B5%E8%B4%B9%E8%A1%A5%E8%B4%B4%E6%94%BF%E7%AD%96%E4%B8%8E%E8%8A%82%E8%83%BD%E5%A5%96%E5%8A%B1) to complete currency settlement, ensuring funds arrive on time and avoiding exchange-rate losses.

## Future Policy Directions

At the Student Welfare Committee meeting in May 2024, ANU announced it would assess raising the subsidy to $180 per semester in 2025. The adjustment is based on the **Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) Q1 2024 Consumer Price Index**, in which electricity prices rose by 9.2% year on year. If approved, the annual budget would increase to $1.3 million. In addition, the university is considering extending the subsidy to some off-campus apartments, which would require negotiating sub-metering arrangements with the property managers.

For the energy-saving rewards, the university plans to introduce a "team challenge" mode that lets students from the same residence hall compete as a team. The **ANU Energy Research Centre** is testing an AI-based energy-consumption prediction model that could one day provide students with personalised energy-saving advice. If the trials succeed, a "smart thermostat subsidy" program could launch in 2026, replacing the thermostats of participating students free of charge.

## FAQ

### Q1: Do I need to apply for the ANU electricity subsidy?

No. The subsidy uses an automatic-deduction model; students only need to make sure their accommodation contract matches the information in ANU's student systems. The subsidy is credited automatically in the third week of each semester — Semester 1, 2024 was paid on 11 March. If you have not received it, log in to ISIS and check the "Accommodation Bills" module, or lodge an appeal with ANU Accommodation Services within 14 business days.

### Q2: How do I convert energy-saving reward points into cash?

Points cannot be converted directly into cash, but they can be redeemed for campus card top-ups. Every 100 points is worth $10, up to a cap of 500 points ($50). Redemptions are made at the campus card top-up terminals in the Union Court or Kambri areas, with a minimum of 100 points per redemption. Points are valid for 12 months; in 2023, 207 students lost points through expiry.

### Q3: Can students renting off campus enjoy this subsidy?

No. The subsidy is only available to residents of ANU's 12 fully university-operated halls. However, students renting off campus can apply for the "Energy Relief Payment" offered by the ACT Government, a one-off grant of up to $200. Applications must include the rental contract, electricity bill and student ID, and are submitted through the ACT Government website; assessment takes about 10 business days.
