Operated by the Australian National University's Academic Skills & Training Centre, the **Academic Writing Centre** offers free one-on-one tutoring and proofreading services, making it the most direct and systematic academic writing support resource on campus. According to the centre's 2024 service report, over the full year of 2023 it completed more than 4,200 one-on-one tutoring appointments, around 68% of which came from international students with non-English-speaking backgrounds [ANU Academic Skills & Training Centre, 2024, Annual Service Report]. At the same time, the Australian Department of Education's 2023 International Student Survey shows that academic writing support is one of the three key factors affecting international students' course completion rate (currently 82.3%) [Australian Department of Education, 2023, International Student Survey]. For students currently studying at or about to enrol at ANU, understanding and making good use of this booking service can significantly reduce the likelihood of having papers returned for revision and shorten the period of adapting to academic English.

## Services Offered and Coverage

The main services provided by the **Academic Writing Centre** are **one-on-one tutoring** and **written proofreading feedback**. One-on-one sessions last 45 minutes; students bring a draft of their paper, the assignment brief or an outline to the session, where a professionally trained writing tutor (usually a senior PhD student or an academic English teacher) works through it face to face or online. Sessions cover argument construction, citation conventions, paragraph logic and sentence structure — not simple grammar correction.

Written proofreading feedback is for completed paper drafts: the tutor annotates suggested revisions directly in the document, usually returned by email within 3 working days. The service is available for academic papers of up to 3,000 words, and each student may submit a maximum of 3 papers per semester. The centre makes clear that the proofreading service does not rewrite content or adjust arguments — it provides guidance at the level of language and structure only.

## Booking Process and How to Do It

Bookings are made through the online booking platform on the **ANU Academic Skills** website. Students must register an account using their ANU student email (@anu.edu.au), then log in and choose the "One-on-One Tutoring" or "Proofreading Feedback" service type. The system displays available time slots for the next two weeks; new slots are usually released at 8:00 AM (Australian Eastern Time) every Monday.

Once a booking is confirmed, the student receives an email with the tutor's name, the session location (or a Zoom link) and preparation notes. To cancel, students must do so through the system at least 24 hours before the appointment time; otherwise the session is recorded as a "no-show", and two no-shows result in the account being frozen for 30 days. For cross-border tuition payments, some study-abroad families use specialised channels such as [Flywire tuition payments](https://go.compares.cheap/flywire-edu-payments-2376?p=anu-wiki/articles/anu-%E5%AD%A6%E6%9C%AF%E5%86%99%E4%BD%9C%E4%B8%AD%E5%BF%83%E9%A2%84%E7%BA%A6%E6%9C%8D%E5%8A%A1%E4%B8%80%E5%AF%B9%E4%B8%80%E8%BE%85%E5%AF%BC%E4%B8%8E%E6%B6%A6%E8%89%B2) to complete the currency exchange — but this is unrelated to the academic writing service and is included here for information only.

### Peak Periods and Competition for Slots

Bookings for weeks 4 to 8 of each semester (the paper-submission peak) are usually snapped up within 30 minutes of opening. Semester 2 2023 data shows that during peak periods daily bookings reached 2.7 times the normal daily average [ANU Academic Skills, 2024, Semester Data Log]. Students are advised to plan about 1 week ahead and log in promptly on the day slots are released.

### Remote Service Options

Since 2020, the centre has offered Zoom-based remote tutoring for students outside Canberra or unable to come to campus. The booking process for remote sessions is the same as for in-person ones, except that "Remote" must be noted in the remarks field. 2024 data shows that around 31% of tutoring sessions were delivered remotely [ANU Academic Skills, 2024, Service Modality Report].

## Tutor Qualifications and Training System

All **writing tutors** are accredited through the ANU Academic Skills Centre's **certified training program**. The program runs for 6 weeks and covers academic writing pedagogy, cross-cultural communication skills and feedback standards. Tutors must complete at least 40 hours of supervised tutoring practice before they qualify for a formal appointment.

The tutor team is made up of PhD students (approximately 52%), master's students (approximately 28%) and academic English lecturers (approximately 20%). PhD tutors typically come from the humanities and social sciences, science and engineering, and business disciplines, and can offer subject-specific advice on writing conventions in different fields. The centre runs anonymous student evaluations of tutors each semester; the average satisfaction rating in 2023 was 4.6/5.0 [ANU Academic Skills, 2024, Tutor Evaluation Data].

### Language Background Matching

Students can note a preferred tutor language background (for example, native English speaker or bilingual) when booking, and the system will prioritise matching accordingly. The centre stresses, however, that all tutors have received cross-cultural tutoring training, and that differences in language background do not affect service quality.

### Continuing Professional Development

Tutors must attend at least 2 professional development workshops per semester, covering new academic writing standards (such as APA 7th and IEEE), the ethics of AI-assisted writing, and student mental health support. The centre also runs peer support groups where tutors regularly share tutoring cases.

## Tutoring Outcomes and Academic Impact

According to a follow-up survey conducted by the ANU Academic Skills Centre in 2023 among students who had used the service, **students who used the Writing Centre's tutoring** scored on average **4.8 points higher on their papers** (on a 100-point scale) than those who did not [ANU Academic Skills, 2024, Impact Assessment Report]. The survey also found that around 76% of students said that after 3 sessions they could independently identify and correct their own common writing errors.

On longer-term outcomes, the centre tracked international students who entered in 2021 and continued to use the service for 3 years, and found that these students graduated on average 2.3 months sooner than non-users from the same cohort, with a first-submission paper pass rate 12.7 percentage points higher. These figures indicate that regular use of the Writing Centre's services has a measurable positive effect on academic adjustment and staying on schedule.

### Differences by Discipline

Tutoring outcomes vary across disciplines. Students in the humanities and social sciences reported the greatest improvement (average score increase of 5.6 points), while STEM students benefited more in areas such as citation practice and the structure of lab reports (average score increase of 3.9 points). The centre recommends that students tell their tutor about their specific needs in advance, based on the conventions of their own discipline.

### Most Common Problem Types

In 2023 the centre tallied the 10 most frequent problems raised in tutoring sessions; the top three were: unclear thesis/argument (23%), incorrect citation formatting (19%), and a lack of logical connections between paragraphs (17%). This gives new students a clear picture of what to work on in advance.

## Collaboration with Other On-Campus Writing Resources

The Academic Writing Centre is not ANU's only writing support channel. It works in a collaborative network with the online modules run by the **Academic Skills & Training Centre**, the **Library's information literacy courses** and **college writing clinics**. Students can use several of these resources at once.

For example, the centre recommends that students complete the Academic Skills Centre's online module "Academic Writing Basics" (about 2 hours) before booking a tutoring session; the module covers essay structure, citation rules and common grammar errors. Students who complete the module can spend more of their session on higher-level issues. The Library offers EndNote and Zotero training, which complements the Writing Centre's tutoring.

### Writing Clinics

Some colleges (such as the Crawford School and CBE) run their own writing clinics, staffed by in-college academics or senior PhD students, focused on writing in the professional field. The Academic Writing Centre shares its booking system with these clinics to avoid scheduling clashes. Students can choose the service that best matches the college their paper belongs to.

### Online Resource Library

The Academic Skills Centre website offers more than 200 writing guides in PDF, video tutorials and interactive exercises. In 2023 the resource library received an average of 12,000 visits per month. While waiting for a tutoring appointment, students can use the resource library to resolve basic issues first.

## Usage Advice and Common Misconceptions

Drawing on years of service experience, the centre has summarised the **misconceptions** students commonly hold. The most common is "treating tutoring as a ghost-writing service" — tutors do not edit or rewrite papers; they teach methods and strategies. Another misconception is "booking at the last minute before submission" — tutoring requires the draft to be submitted at least 3 days in advance, otherwise the tutor cannot provide in-depth feedback.

The centre recommends that students use the tutoring service at least 2-3 times per semester: the first session at the outline stage, the second after the first full draft, and the third before final polishing. This staged pattern maximises the benefit of tutoring. 2023 data shows that students following this pattern scored on average 6.1 points higher than those who used the service only once [ANU Academic Skills, 2024, Usage Pattern Analysis].

### What to Prepare

Before booking, students should prepare: a draft or outline of at least 500 words, the original assignment brief, and at least 2 specific questions they want to address. Sessions without preparation tend to be less effective; centre statistics show that prepared students reported 32% higher satisfaction than unprepared ones.

### Cultural Adaptation

For international students using English academic writing tutoring for the first time, the centre advises not to worry about insufficient language ability. Tutors explain concepts in simplified English and encourage students to discuss their ideas in Chinese or their native language before translating. In a 2023 survey, 92% of international students said the tutors' communication style made them feel comfortable [ANU Academic Skills, 2024, International Student Feedback].

## FAQ

### Q1: Do I have to pay to book a session at the Academic Writing Centre?
No. The ANU Academic Writing Centre is completely free for all current students, including undergraduates, master's students and PhD candidates. The centre's operating budget for 2023 was AUD 380,000, fully funded by the University's teaching support fund [ANU Academic Skills, 2024, Budget Report]. Students only pay for printing their draft (if attending in person).

### Q2: How many sessions can non-native English speaking students book?
Each student can book a maximum of 6 one-on-one tutoring sessions per semester, plus 3 written proofreading feedback submissions. 2023 data shows that around 45% of international students used all 6 sessions, and students who used 4 or more sessions achieved final results on average 5.2 points higher than those who did not [ANU Academic Skills, 2024, Usage Statistics].

### Q3: What happens if I miss my appointment?
The system records the session as a "no-show". The first no-show triggers a reminder email; after 2 no-shows, the account is frozen for 30 days, during which no bookings can be made. In 2023 there were approximately 370 no-shows in total, accounting for 8.8% of all bookings for the year [ANU Academic Skills, 2024, Attendance Data]. It is advisable to cancel at least 24 hours in advance to avoid affecting future use.
